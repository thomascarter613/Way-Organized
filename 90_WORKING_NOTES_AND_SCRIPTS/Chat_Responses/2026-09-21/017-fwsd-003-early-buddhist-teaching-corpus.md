## Substantive result

The Early Buddhist Teaching Corpus remains **CAND-3 — Evaluated** and is a serious future **CAND-4 — Foundational Candidate**, but it is **not canonically admitted**. Any FWC-A decision is deferred until the first cross-candidate comparison cycle is complete.

The dossier deliberately evaluates shared early Buddhist teaching recoverable across multiple school traditions rather than the Pāli Canon en bloc. It uses cross-recensional comparison among Pāli Nikāyas, Chinese Āgamas, early Vinaya traditions, Sanskrit/Gāndhārī witnesses, and early manuscript evidence.

The strongest recoverable core includes:

- dukkha / unsatisfactoriness;
- craving and ignorance as causal conditions;
- cessation/liberation;
- a disciplined path of ethics, concentration, mindfulness, and insight;
- the Middle Way;
- dependent arising / conditionality;
- impermanence;
- non-self analysis of the aggregates;
- intentional karma and rebirth as early teaching;
- ethical restraint and non-harming;
- meditation/jhāna;
- and awakening as direct experiential knowledge rather than mere inherited assent.

The dossier explicitly separates the historical presence of rebirth, karma, devas, nirvāṇa, and related teachings from their metaphysical truth status under EPC-001.

It also prevents two opposite retrojections:

1. later Buddhist philosophy must not be silently attributed to Gautama;
2. modern secularized mindfulness must not be projected backward as though early Buddhism lacked rebirth, renunciation, cosmology, concentration, and liberation.

No composite truth score was assigned.
