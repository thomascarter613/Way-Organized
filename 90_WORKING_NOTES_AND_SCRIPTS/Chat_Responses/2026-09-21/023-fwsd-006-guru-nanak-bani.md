## Substantive result

Guru Nanak Bani remains **CAND-3 — Evaluated** and is a serious future **CAND-4 — Foundational Candidate**, but it is **not canonically admitted**. Any FWC-A decision is deferred until the first cross-candidate comparison cycle is complete.

The dossier source-freezes the candidate to founder-associated Nanak bani and early Sikh textual transmission rather than Sikhism as a whole. It distinguishes historical Nanak, early oral/liturgical bani, early pothi traditions, the 1604 Adi Granth, later Janamsakhi biography, later Guru-period institutional development, Khalsa identity, rahit literature, and modern Sikh systematization.

The strongest recoverable core includes:

- Ik Oankar / divine Oneness;
- hukam;
- haumai;
- nam;
- sabad;
- truthful living / sachiara;
- grace;
- critique of hollow religious status and ritual formalism;
- spiritual equality over inherited status;
- householder-compatible spiritual transformation;
- rebirth and liberation as part of the early Sikh worldview;
- and formation of a concrete community rather than merely private mystical teaching.

The dossier also treats early Sikh manuscript transmission as a major strength while preserving uncertainty: scholarly work supports the possibility of written bani during Nanak's lifetime or very soon afterward, but no founder autograph is treated as securely extant. The Goindval and other early pothi traditions, concurrent oral repertoires, and Guru Arjan's 1604 editorial/canonical work remain distinct stages.

No composite truth score was assigned.
