# FWSD-007 — Torah / Pentateuchal Witness Strata Foundational Witness Selection Dossier

**Date:** 2026-09-21  
**Status:** CAND-3 — Evaluated; not canonically admitted  
**Authoritative dossier:** `../../../../01_FOUNDATIONAL_SOURCE/source/foundational-witness-dossiers/FWSD-007-torah-pentateuchal-witness-strata.md`

FWSD-007 evaluates source-controlled witness strata within Genesis–Deuteronomy rather than treating the final Pentateuch as one uniform Mosaic transcript.

Key findings:

- current Pentateuch scholarship agrees on substantial literary layering but not on one universally accepted replacement for classical J/E/D/P;
- the dossier therefore privileges Priestly, non-Priestly, Deuteronomic, Holiness, named legal collections, and identifiable editorial strata where stronger than speculative labels;
- MT, Samaritan Pentateuch, Septuagint, and Dead Sea Scroll evidence establish both substantial continuity and real Second Temple textual plurality;
- Genesis 1–11 contains Priestly/non-Priestly development and strong ancient Near Eastern intertextuality;
- patriarchal narratives are best treated as cultural memory with some potentially old traditions, not verified biography;
- El/YHWH divine-name traditions preserve historical development rather than one flat nomenclature;
- Exodus remains foundational cultural memory with an unresolved historical core rather than either fully verified modern history or demonstrated total fabrication;
- the Decalogue, Covenant Code, Deuteronomic law, Priestly law, and Holiness material must be evaluated as related but developing legal witnesses;
- Torah itself preserves legal revision, making internal reinterpretation of authority a major historical finding;
- slavery, conquest/ḥerem, patriarchy, religious coercion, capital punishment, and collective punishment are explicitly retained in the adverse-evidence register and cannot become modern binding commands merely through canonical status;
- the candidate remains CAND-3 with a strong recommendation for CAND-4 only after first-cycle comparison.
