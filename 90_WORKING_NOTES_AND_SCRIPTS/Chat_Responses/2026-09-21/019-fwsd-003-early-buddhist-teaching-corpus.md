## Substantive result

The Early Buddhist Teaching Corpus remains **CAND-3 — Evaluated** and is a serious future **CAND-4 — Foundational Candidate**, but it is **not canonically admitted**. Any FWC-A decision is deferred until the first cross-candidate comparison cycle is complete.

The dossier evaluates shared early Buddhist teaching recoverable across multiple school traditions rather than the Pāli Canon en bloc. It uses cross-recensional comparison among Pāli Nikāyas, Chinese Āgamas, early Vinaya traditions, Sanskrit/Gāndhārī witnesses, and early manuscript evidence.

The strongest recoverable core includes dukkha, craving and ignorance as causal conditions, cessation/liberation, the structured path, the Middle Way, dependent arising, impermanence, non-self analysis, karma and rebirth as early teaching, ethical restraint, non-harming, meditation/jhāna, and awakening as direct experiential knowledge rather than mere inherited assent.

The dossier separates the historical presence of rebirth, karma, devas, nirvāṇa, and related teachings from their metaphysical truth status under EPC-001.

It also prevents two opposite retrojections: later Buddhist philosophy must not be silently attributed to Gautama, and modern secularized mindfulness must not be projected backward as though early Buddhism lacked rebirth, renunciation, cosmology, concentration, and liberation.
