I’ve now converted the idea into a research system rather than a loose comparative-religion exercise. The result below is **Prophetic/Revelatory Corpus v0.1**: a framework designed to test the hypothesis that human beings in different cultures may have encountered the same transcendent reality, different transcendent realities, or primarily human religious phenomena—without building the desired conclusion into the method.

## 1. The governing research rule

The corpus will distinguish four propositions that are often accidentally collapsed:

**Historical:** “This person existed, and this text/tradition can plausibly be connected to them.”

**Phenomenological:** “This person or community reported an encounter, vision, audition, awakening, revelation, or other extraordinary experience.”

**Attributional:** “This particular teaching probably derives from the claimed recipient rather than later followers.”

**Metaphysical:** “The claimed divine/transcendent reality actually exists and caused the experience.”

Historical scholarship can sometimes give us considerable confidence regarding the first three. It cannot, by historical method alone, prove the fourth.

Accordingly, there will be **no overall truth score**.

## 2. Evidence model

Every corpus entry receives separate ratings:

| Code  | Dimension              | Question                                                                                      |
| ----- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **H** | Historical attestation | How confidently can we place the person/community in history?                                 |
| **P** | Source proximity       | How close are surviving sources to the claimed event/person?                                  |
| **A** | Attribution            | How confidently can particular teachings be attributed to the figure?                         |
| **T** | Textual stability      | How securely can we recover the relevant text?                                                |
| **E** | Encounter directness   | Are we reading something close to a first-person encounter report, or a much later biography? |
| **X** | External corroboration | Is there independent inscriptional, archaeological, documentary, or cross-source evidence?    |
| **I** | Independence           | How independent is this witness from the traditions with which we are comparing it?           |

Ratings are **VH = very high, H = high, M = moderate, L = low, VL = very low, U = indeterminate**.

Importantly, `T = VH` would mean “we know very well what the text says,” **not** “what it says is metaphysically true.”

# 4. A major result already follows

Our earlier category **“Abrahamic prophetic stream” is too narrow for the research stage**.

Ancient Near Eastern prophecy demonstrably existed outside Israel. The Mari evidence includes more than a hundred prophetic documents from Mesopotamian, Levantine, and related contexts, while Deir ʿAlla gives us a named non-Israelite seer independently of the biblical account. ([jstor.org][1])

So the proper super-category is now:

> **Human Claims of Contact With, Disclosure From, or Direct Apprehension of Transcendent Reality**

Underneath it we can distinguish:

**Revelation → prophecy → vision → audition → dream → oracle/divination → mystical apprehension → inspired perception → awakening/illumination.**

Those phenomena may overlap, but we should not equate them in advance.

That change is important. Otherwise we would make Muhammad, Zarathustra, Buddha and the ṛṣis look more like Hebrew prophets merely because Hebrew prophecy supplied our terminology.

# 5. The source-distance problem is now explicit

The traditions are strikingly unequal in what historians can recover.

At one extreme is **Mari**: the documents themselves come from the society reporting the prophetic events.

**Muhammad/Qur'an** is also unusually strong for antiquity: whatever conclusion is ultimately reached about revelation, historians possess a text very close to the movement that produced it, while later Islamic biographies need separate treatment. Early manuscript work has substantially weakened older proposals that placed Qur'anic formation very late. ([Cambridge University Press][13])

**Guru Nanak** is better still chronologically: hymnic material entered Sikh textual transmission within generations, and the 1604 canonical compilation is only about sixty-five years after Nanak's death. ([OUP Academic][14])

**Jesus** occupies an intermediate position. We have first-century Christian evidence and texts within decades, but no writings authored by Jesus, and the preserved traditions already belong to communities interpreting him. Paul's letters demonstrate that sayings traditions existed before the Gospels but also demonstrate how little of Jesus' actual teaching Paul reproduces. ([Cambridge University Press][15])

**Buddha** is historically recoverable at a broad level, but the literary gap is substantially greater. ([Cambridge Assets][16])

**Abraham and Moses** present the greatest difficulty. This does not mean “Abraham was fictional” or “Moses did not exist.” It means that present historical methods do not permit us to recover them with anything like the confidence available for Muhammad, Jesus, Nanak, Amos or even the extra-biblical Balaam tradition. Ronald Hendel's formulation is particularly useful: old historical memories may survive in the patriarchal traditions even though we cannot locate an original Abraham in recoverable history. ([OUP Academic][2])

That distinction will be foundational to this project.

# 6. First blind-convergence analysis

When names such as YHWH, Ahura Mazdā, Allah and Akal Purakh are temporarily removed, some recurring propositions become conspicuous.

Across several otherwise different witnesses we repeatedly encounter a claim that ordinary human life is answerable to a reality or moral order greater than the individual. Zarathustra structures this around **aša**, Truth/order, against the Lie. Amos associates fidelity to YHWH with justice rather than mere religious performance. Jesus' earliest recoverable tradition places unusual weight on repentance, love, mercy, the Kingdom of God and moral transformation. The Qur'an repeatedly connects God-consciousness, judgment, generosity, justice and treatment of the vulnerable. Nanak describes one ultimate reality while attacking ego, empty religious formalism and social hierarchy. These are not identical teachings, but neither are the parallels trivial. ([Iranica Online][7])

The same comparison also immediately falsifies any simplistic claim that “all religions teach the same thing.”

The witnesses disagree profoundly about the **ontology behind the experience**. Mari assumes multiple divine beings. Zarathustra gives Ahura Mazdā extraordinary supremacy while retaining a complex spiritual ontology. Israelite prophecy moves within the YHWH tradition. Islam makes uncompromising divine unity central. Sikh teaching centers the one transcendent and immanent reality. Early Buddhism does not make revelation from a creator God the basis of its path at all.

They likewise disagree about **human destiny**: resurrection, judgment, cosmic restoration, rebirth, nirvāṇa and other models cannot simply be translated into one another without losing their meaning.

That disagreement is evidentially valuable.

# 7. The research hypothesis is therefore now split into four live models

Instead of trying to prove one proposition, the corpus will continuously test four competing explanations:

| Model                               | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **U — Unified-source model**        | One transcendent reality is encountered through culturally conditioned human cognition and therefore acquires different names, imagery and conceptual forms.                                     |
| **M — Multiple-intelligence model** | Human beings encounter multiple non-human intelligences or levels of transcendent reality; not every “god,” spirit, messenger or revelation has the same source.                                 |
| **N — Naturalistic model**          | Religious encounter phenomena arise principally from human neuropsychology, dreams, altered states, social processes, memory, charisma and cultural evolution.                                   |
| **H — Hybrid model**                | The historical corpus contains mixtures: genuine transcendence, ordinary psychology, cultural borrowing, imaginative interpretation, institutional development, error and sometimes fabrication. |

No entry will be forced into one model prematurely.

At this stage, **H—the hybrid model—is the appropriate methodological null position**, because the corpus is already too diverse for either blanket acceptance or blanket dismissal.

That is not a theological conclusion. It is the posture least likely to contaminate the evidence.

# 8. Canon formation is postponed

This is especially important for the religion we have been considering constructing.

We should **not yet** declare:

Torah = canonical
Jesus = canonical
Qur'an = canonical
Zarathustra = maybe canonical
Nanak = maybe canonical
Buddha = wisdom literature

Doing so now would reverse the investigation: we would decide our theology first and then grade the evidence according to how comfortably it fits.

The research sequence should instead be:

**Encounter → earliest recoverable testimony → attribution → proposition extraction → comparison → contradiction analysis → historical-development analysis → metaphysical evaluation → only then canonical classification.**

That means even the Torah itself will eventually be decomposed rather than evaluated as one undifferentiated object. Genesis patriarchal tradition, Exodus tradition, covenant law, priestly material, Deuteronomic material and later editorial layers have different historical problems.

Likewise “Jesus” must eventually mean hundreds of individual units.

Likewise “Muhammad” must distinguish the Qur'an from sīra, early historical reports, individual hadith families and later jurisprudence.

# 9. The most interesting anomaly so far: Balaam

Balaam deserves promotion from a curiosity to one of our primary research cases.

The Deir ʿAlla evidence gives us something extraordinarily useful: a **named revelatory figure appearing independently in two religious traditions**.

The inscription calls Balaam son of Beor a seer and describes gods coming to him at night with a message associated with El. The biblical tradition also remembers Balaam son of Beor as an outsider capable of receiving genuine divine communication—but restructures the encounter around YHWH. ([TheTorah.com][4])

That creates a testable historical problem:

**Did Israel appropriate an already famous non-Israelite seer and reinterpret his revelation within Yahwistic theology?**

Or:

**Do the biblical and Deir ʿAlla traditions descend from a still older common Balaam tradition?**

Or:

**Was Balaam a historical visionary whose reputation crossed religious boundaries?**

Notice how valuable this is for our larger hypothesis. Balaam may provide a miniature laboratory for studying exactly how an alleged encounter can move through:

**experience → oral reputation → competing communities → competing theologies → scripture.**

That mechanism could eventually help us understand much larger processes.

---

# 10. Another major result: textual proximity and theological compatibility are independent

Zarathustra is especially instructive.

His Gāthās may preserve remarkably early material associated with the founding religious vision, but his chronology is uncertain and some scholarship even questions traditional assumptions about authorship and historicity. ([Iranica Online][17])

Yet conceptually, the Gāthic world contains several themes extraordinarily relevant to our investigation: supreme Ahura Mazdā, Truth/order, moral agency, the Lie, judgment and eventual cosmic restoration. ([Iranica Online][7])

So Zarathustra could eventually turn out to be **philosophically closer to biblical prophecy but historically less secure than Muhammad**, while Buddha could be **historically secure but metaphysically quite different from both**.

That is exactly why a one-dimensional ranking would have failed.

# 11. Provisional architecture of the complete corpus

The database should eventually contain approximately **30 primary cases**, with three classes.

**Class A — Revelatory/prophetic core:** Mari prophets, Abraham/Melchizedek, Moses, Balaam, Samuel traditions, Elijah/Elisha traditions, Amos, Hosea, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Zarathustra, Jesus, Muhammad and Guru Nanak.

**Class B — alternative transcendent-access traditions:** Ṛgvedic ṛṣis, Upanishadic seers, Buddha, Mahavira, Delphi/Pythia and selected early Daoist revelation traditions.

**Class C — controls and later high-documentation revelations:** Mani, the Báb/Bahá'u'lláh, Joseph Smith and carefully selected African and Indigenous revelatory movements; Confucius, Socrates and other non-revelatory sages can function as controls so that profound ethical insight is not automatically mistaken for claimed revelation.

The point of the controls is crucial. If essentially the same moral propositions appear in someone who **never claimed revelation**, then ethical convergence alone cannot establish common divine origin.

# 13. Where the investigation stands

We have already learned something substantive.

There **is** a broad, historically demonstrable human phenomenon of people claiming communication with gods, God, spirits, cosmic reality or transcendent truth. It predates Christianity and Islam by many centuries and is not confined to Israel. Mari alone prevents us from treating prophetic revelation as an exclusively biblical phenomenon. ([JSTOR][1])

There is also a recurrent tendency for these encounters to become increasingly mediated:

**encounter → proclamation → followers → oral transmission → written transmission → edited corpus → canon → commentary → institution → doctrine.**

And the reliability problem changes at each transition.

Our original intuition therefore survives, but in a more rigorous form:

> **It is historically reasonable to investigate whether apparently separate human revelatory traditions may represent culturally conditioned encounters with a common transcendent reality. The historical evidence does not presently establish that hypothesis, but neither can it be responsibly tested by simply comparing the finished doctrines of modern religions. The proper objects of comparison are the earliest recoverable encounters, teachings and textual strata.**

That is now the governing research program.

The next execution boundary is **PRC Phase II: Deep Dossiers**. The highest-value sequence is **Mari → Balaam → Abraham/Melchizedek → Moses → Zarathustra → Amos/Isaiah → Ṛgvedic seers → Buddha → Jesus → Muhammad → Guru Nanak**. For each, we can reconstruct the earliest recoverable corpus proposition-by-proposition and eventually place the anonymous propositions side-by-side without revealing which religion produced them. That is where the genuinely interesting test of the “one transcendent source” hypothesis begins.
