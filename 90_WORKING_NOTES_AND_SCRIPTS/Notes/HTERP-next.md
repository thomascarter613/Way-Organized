From here, I would **stop expanding the framework for a moment and stress-test it on a small set of anchor cases**. We now have enough architecture. The next risk is building an enormous catalog without discovering whether our method actually discriminates between competing explanations.

The best next phase is a **Calibration Cycle** using six very different cases: **Mari prophecy, Zarathustra, Jesus, Muhammad, controlled mystical experiences, and fraud/neurological controls**. That gives us ancient and later material, first- and third-person evidence, personal-deity revelation, ethical prophecy, founder traditions, experimentally induced experiences, and adversarial controls.

For each one, we should go all the way down to the primary or earliest recoverable sources and build a complete dossier: exactly what the source says, what is later interpretation, what can be historically established, what the reported phenomenology was, what naturalistic explanations fit, what transcendent explanations fit, and—most importantly—what observations would actually distinguish those hypotheses.

Then we build our first **cross-case matrix**. I want columns such as: entity self-identification, auditory communication, visual form, noetic certainty, ethical imperative, unexpected information, prediction, altered consciousness, physiological effects, recurrence, voluntariness, cultural conformity, subsequent transformation, and later doctrinal expansion. That will tell us whether apparently different traditions are actually reporting the same kind of phenomenon.

After that, we do something especially important: **content-stripping**. Remove the religious names and compare experiences blind. Instead of “Gabriel appeared to Muhammad” or “YHWH spoke to Isaiah,” the analytical representation becomes something like:

> A perceived noncorporeal agent communicated propositional content during an unusual state of consciousness, identified itself or was subsequently identified as X, issued an ethical imperative, and produced enduring behavioral change.

Then we compare cases without letting familiar religious labels predetermine our judgment.

The next major stage would be **dependency analysis**. We build an influence graph showing which traditions plausibly knew about which others. Similarities between Jesus and Isaiah, for example, are not independent evidence because Jesus operated within Jewish scripture and culture. Similarities between ancient Israel and Mesopotamia likewise require Near Eastern transmission analysis. Similarities between Shang China and Mari potentially carry more comparative weight because direct textual dependence is much harder to establish.

Only after dependency analysis do we search seriously for **independent convergence**.

That is where things could become genuinely interesting.

Suppose, for example, culturally independent traditions repeatedly report some highly specific combination such as:

**luminous but noncorporeal presence + overwhelming moral authority + loss of ordinary temporal perception + communication experienced as knowledge rather than ordinary sound + subsequent radical ethical transformation.**

That would deserve considerably more attention than the generic observation that many religions have “visions.”

At the same time, we should construct the **Contradiction Corpus** rather than only looking for similarities. We need to ask where purported revelations make mutually incompatible claims:

**one divine intelligence vs many;
personal vs impersonal ultimate reality;
reincarnation vs resurrection;
eternal self vs no-self;
exclusive revelation vs universal revelation;
animal sacrifice required vs rejected;
particular foods forbidden vs permitted.**

Then we examine whether those contradictions occur primarily at the level of **raw encounter**, **human interpretation**, or **later institutional doctrine**.

That may turn out to be one of the project's biggest discoveries.

### Then comes the highest-value investigation

We need to search specifically for what I would call **anomalous-information cases**.

Most religious experiences cannot distinguish transcendence from psychology because an experience of light, love, unity, terror, presence or a voice can plausibly arise through ordinary human mechanisms.

A much stronger case would look like:

**information was obtained → information was specific → the experiencer apparently couldn't know it → the report was documented beforehand → independent verification followed → fraud and information leakage can reasonably be excluded.**

That becomes a separate HTERP subproject:

**HTERP-AI — Anomalous Information Corpus.**

Prophecy, precognitive dreams, mediumship, divination, visions and modern controlled experiments all become candidates.

Most will probably fail stringent controls. That's useful. We want the filter to be brutal.

Similarly, we should create **HTERP-PRED**, a prospective-prediction corpus. Retrospective prophecy is extremely vulnerable to reinterpretation. A serious prediction should be timestamped before the outcome, specific enough to fail, and evaluated according to an explicit criterion.

Another subproject should be **HTERP-NEURO**, comparing religious experiences with known neurological and pharmacological states. Not to “explain religion away,” but to identify which phenomenological features humans can produce without any demonstrated external agent.

Eventually we will have something like:

**historical corpus**

* **phenomenology corpus**
* **neuroscience controls**
* **fraud controls**
* **dependency graph**
* **anomalous-information corpus**
* **contradiction matrix**

and only then should we return to theology.

At that point we can ask progressively stronger questions:

**Level 1:** Is ordinary waking materialism sufficient to describe all reported human experience?

**Level 2:** Are unusual religious states reducible to known cognitive mechanisms?

**Level 3:** Is there evidence for an objective transcendent reality?

**Level 4:** If so, does it appear personal, impersonal, or both?

**Level 5:** Is there evidence for one intelligence or multiple intelligences?

**Level 6:** Can any historical prophetic/revelatory stream be identified as unusually reliable?

**Level 7:** What teachings survive historical and comparative scrutiny?

**Level 8:** What practices reliably produce beneficial contact or transformation?

**Level 9:** What should humans actually believe, practice and organize around?**

Only **Level 9** begins constructing the religion.

And I would deliberately postpone giving that religion a name.

The immediate next work package should therefore be:

> **HTERP-WP-001 — Calibration Corpus**

Complete full primary-source-level dossiers for **Mari, Zarathustra, Jesus, Muhammad, experimental mystical experience, neurological experience and documented fraud**, then produce the first blinded phenomenology matrix, dependency analysis, contradiction matrix and hypothesis update.

That one work package will tell us whether the research system we've designed actually works before we scale it across the remaining forty-plus traditions.

That is where I would go next.
