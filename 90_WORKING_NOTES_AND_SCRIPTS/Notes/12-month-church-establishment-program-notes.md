# CEP-001 — Twelve-Month Church Establishment and IRS Recognition Program

**Version:** 1.0
**Program period:** September 21, 2026–September 19, 2027
**Primary objective:** Establish a genuine, operational religious body capable of substantiating classification as a church under IRC §§ 501(c)(3), 509(a)(1), and 170(b)(1)(A)(i).
**Jurisdictional assumption:** Tennessee nonprofit religious corporation; United States federal tax law.
**Target federal filing:** Full Form 1023 with Schedule A, not Form 1023-EZ.
**Operating principle:** Religious beliefs and practices are developed because they are genuinely held and actually practiced; IRS evidence documents those facts rather than manufacturing religious characteristics for tax qualification.

---

# 1. Program objective

At the end of this program, the organization should be able to demonstrate through contemporaneous records that it possesses, to the extent genuinely applicable, the characteristics the IRS traditionally considers when determining whether a religious organization is a “church.”

Current IRS guidance identifies characteristics including distinct legal existence, creed and worship, ecclesiastical government, doctrine and discipline, religious history, membership, ordained ministers, prescribed ministerial study, religious literature, established places of worship, regular congregations, regular religious services, youth religious education, and preparation of ministers. The IRS considers the combination of these characteristics and the organization's overall facts and circumstances rather than requiring every characteristic mechanically. ([IRS][1])

Most importantly for this program, current Form 1023 instructions say an established congregation or other religious membership group generally consists of people who regularly attend and participate in religious services at an established location; a congregation ordinarily is not merely one family. An organization without such a congregation may qualify as a religious §501(c)(3) organization without yet qualifying specifically as a church. ([IRS][2])

---

# 2. What success looks like

The program does **not** define success as simply obtaining an EIN, incorporating, creating a website, or drafting a creed.

Success means there is an actual institution behind the paperwork.

By the filing gate, the record should show:

| Domain             | Target condition                                                           |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Legal existence    | Active Tennessee nonprofit corporation                                     |
| Federal identity   | EIN issued and organizational records consistent                           |
| Religious identity | Written statement of beliefs, doctrine, history, worship, and discipline   |
| Governance         | Functioning ecclesiastical and civil governance                            |
| Congregation       | Genuine body of participants from multiple households                      |
| Membership         | Defined membership process and maintained membership register              |
| Worship            | Regular recurring religious services                                       |
| Place              | Established physical worship location or recurring established locations   |
| Ministry           | Genuine ministers with defined qualifications and duties                   |
| Formation          | Prescribed substantive ministerial education                               |
| Literature         | Body of original religious/educational material                            |
| Religious rites    | Defined and actually practiced where appropriate                           |
| Education          | Continuing adult instruction and youth instruction when applicable         |
| Finances           | Separate bank account, books, contributions, budgets, controls             |
| Evidence           | Service records, attendance, minutes, teaching archives, financial records |
| Compliance         | §501(c)(3) private-benefit, lobbying, and campaign restrictions addressed  |
| IRS package        | Form 1023 + Schedule A + consolidated evidentiary PDF ready for filing     |

Churches that actually satisfy §501(c)(3) can be tax exempt without filing Form 1023, but obtaining a determination letter provides formal IRS recognition. ([IRS][2])

# 5. Permanent operating records

Once worship begins, five record series become continuous rather than one-time deliverables.

| Record series              | Identifier    | Created                     |
| -------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------- |
| Worship Service Record     | SR-YYYY-MM-DD | Every service               |
| Governing Body Minutes     | GB-YYYY-MM-DD | Every formal meeting        |
| Religious Education Record | ER-YYYY-MM-DD | Every instructional session |
| Rite Record                | RR-YYYY-NNN   | Every qualifying rite       |
| Financial Month-End Record | FR-YYYY-MM    | Monthly                     |

A normal Worship Service Record should document the date, location, starting and ending time, officiating minister, service type, physical attendance, synchronous remote attendance separately, readings, teaching or sermon, religious practices or rites, offering, and associated bulletin or program.

Physical attendance, live remote participation, and later video views should never be combined into one attendance number.

# 8. Congregational evidence standard

This program deliberately does not invent a statutory minimum congregation size because the IRS publishes no such numerical minimum.

The internal maturity target is instead a congregation that is demonstrably broader than one household and is capable of behaving institutionally.

A healthy early target would be roughly 10–20 regularly participating adults across several unrelated households, but that is a **program goal, not an IRS rule**.

The stronger the following evidence, the less significance any arbitrary headcount has:

| Factor        | Strong evidence                                      |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Continuity    | People repeatedly return                             |
| Independence  | Multiple unrelated households                        |
| Participation | Congregants worship rather than merely consume media |
| Membership    | Defined community affiliation                        |
| Interaction   | Members associate with one another                   |
| Worship       | Recurring communal religious practice                |
| Ministry      | Religious leadership serves congregants              |
| Place         | Recurring identifiable gathering location            |
| Duration      | Sustained pattern over time                          |

# 9. Hybrid congregation model

The recommended architecture is:

**Religious Body → Local Congregation → Online Community → Future Local Congregations**

Online participation is treated as a genuine extension of ministry where it actually functions that way, but it is separately recorded from physical congregational attendance.

The program therefore preserves four different concepts:

| Category            | Meaning                                           |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Physical congregant | Person attending the established gathering        |
| Remote congregant   | Member participating synchronously from elsewhere |
| Online participant  | Nonmember participating live                      |
| Media audience      | Person consuming recorded material later          |

This avoids treating YouTube views, website visitors, newsletter subscribers, or social-media followers as though they were automatically congregants.

---

# 10. Minimum content of a regular worship service

The religion itself ultimately determines worship.

For recordkeeping purposes, a service can contain whichever practices authentically express the community's faith, such as gathering, invocation, prayer, meditation, sacred or religious reading, teaching, music or chant, communal response, rites, offering, intercession, fellowship, or dismissal.

The IRS asks what occurs during services and how those activities further the organization's religious purposes; it does not require a Protestant Sunday-service template. ([IRS][2])

# 11. Evidence maturity scoring

Each quarter assign every domain an evidentiary condition rather than a numerical legal score.

| Condition      | Meaning                                                     |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established    | Genuine practice + strong contemporaneous evidence          |
| Developing     | Genuine practice exists but history/evidence remains thin   |
| Planned        | Institution intends to establish it but has not yet done so |
| Not applicable | Not part of this religious tradition                        |
| Deficient      | Claimed activity cannot presently be substantiated          |

No characteristic should be called “Established” merely because a policy says it exists.

| Condition                                                                                     | Action                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |

Current Form 8940 instructions expressly contemplate requests involving church classification and repeat the established-congregation analysis. ([IRS][12])

# 14. Things the program must never do

The organization must not fabricate congregants, backdate attendance, invent worship services, issue meaningless ministerial credentials, manufacture rites never practiced, characterize media viewers as worshippers without basis, intermingle founder and church money, create sham independent directors, or write doctrine solely because an IRS characteristic mentions it.

A church qualification application should describe a religious reality already coming into existence.

# 15. First-year target state

By September 19, 2027, a successful program should have approximately one year of institutional records and perhaps roughly nine months of recorded regular worship, depending upon when the congregation was genuinely ready to begin.

The resulting evidence repository should contain dozens of service records, an attendance history, a living membership register, a sermon/teaching archive, governance minutes, financial statements, ministerial formation records, educational records, worship-location documentation, actual religious literature and a coherent record of institutional continuity.

At that point, the IRS application becomes primarily an exercise in accurately describing an institution that can already be observed in operation.

# 17. Program completion definition

CEP-001 is complete only when one of two outcomes occurs.

**Outcome A — Church filing ready:** The organization possesses sufficient genuine congregational and institutional evidence to submit Form 1023 and Schedule A seeking classification under §§509(a)(1) and 170(b)(1)(A)(i).

**Outcome B — Religious organization, church classification deferred:** The organization is genuinely operating for religious §501(c)(3) purposes but has not yet established the congregational facts necessary to make a strong church-classification case. It continues operating without misrepresenting its maturity and seeks church classification later when the facts support it.

Either outcome is preferable to filing prematurely or creating artificial facts.

That gives us the **master execution program**, not merely another planning outline. The next work boundary is now clear: **CH-001 through CH-006 are the Phase I production set**, followed immediately by CH-007 through CH-012 for the religious-identity layer. Assuming the Tennessee religious nonprofit we have already been designing is the entity we're using, I would carry those forward as the same governance project rather than creating a second organization.
