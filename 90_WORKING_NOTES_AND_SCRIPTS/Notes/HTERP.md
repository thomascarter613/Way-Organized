# Human–Transcendent Encounter Research Program

**Working abbreviation:** HTERP
**Version:** 0.1
**Primary question:** *What, if anything, can the global historical record of claimed human contact with transcendent reality tell us about the nature of reality, consciousness, revelation, and religion?*

The project begins **before theology**. Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Zoroastrianism, Buddhism, Vedic religion, Indigenous traditions, and modern revelatory movements enter as evidence to be examined—not as authorities whose conclusions are assumed.

## I. Foundational rule

The basic unit of investigation will not be **a religion**.

It will be:

> **a reported encounter + the earliest recoverable report of that encounter + the teachings attributed to it + the subsequent transmission history.**

That gives us this model:

**possible reality/event**
→ **human experience**
→ **interpretation by experiencer**
→ **spoken report**
→ **first hearers**
→ **oral transmission**
→ **written record**
→ **editing/redaction**
→ **commentary**
→ **canon**
→ **institution**
→ **doctrine**

We will never automatically treat the bottom of that chain as identical with the top.

This matters enormously. For example, scholarship on Hebrew prophecy emphasizes that the biblical prophetic books are literary products shaped through scribal transmission rather than stenographic transcripts of ancient prophets. ([OUP Academic][1]) The Rigveda poses a different problem because its hymns were orally composed and transmitted for long periods, although with extraordinary conservatism. ([OUP Academic][2]) Early Buddhism presents another transmission structure again: a long oral phase preceded our earliest surviving manuscripts. ([OUP Academic][3])

These differences must become part of the evidence rather than inconveniences we ignore.

# II. What counts as an encounter?

We will distinguish at least these classes rather than calling everything “revelation.”

| Code    | Encounter class             | Basic form                                                                |
| ------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **MSG** | Message/revelation          | “A transcendent agent communicated information.”                          |
| **VIS** | Vision                      | “I perceived something normally unseen.”                                  |
| **AUD** | Auditory encounter          | “I heard a voice or communication.”                                       |
| **DRM** | Sacred/revelatory dream     | Information experienced during dreaming                                   |
| **POS** | Possession/mediumship       | An apparently other intelligence communicates through a person            |
| **DIV** | Divination/oracle           | Information sought through an established procedure                       |
| **JRN** | Otherworld journey          | Experiencer reports entering another realm                                |
| **UNI** | Unitive mystical experience | Distinction between self and ultimate reality diminishes or disappears    |
| **PRS** | Presence encounter          | Powerful perception of an unseen personal presence                        |
| **ILL** | Illumination/awakening      | Direct apprehension of reality rather than communication by an entity     |
| **INS** | Inspired composition        | Speech, poetry, music, or writing attributed to inspiration               |
| **APP** | Apparition                  | An apparently external being becomes perceptible                          |
| **CHA** | Channeling                  | Extended communication attributed to a distinct noncorporeal intelligence |

A single case can occupy several categories.

Muhammad's traditional revelation accounts, for example, involve dreams, visions and communication; a newly published Cambridge treatment shows that even the early transmitted accounts contain multiple recensions of the first revelatory experience. ([Cambridge University Press][4])

The Buddha belongs much more naturally under **ILL** than **MSG**.

That difference must remain visible.

# III. Source hierarchy

Every source enters with a provenance classification.

| Grade  | Source relationship                                               |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **P1** | First-person material produced by the experiencer                 |
| **P2** | Contemporary or nearly contemporary record from direct associates |
| **P3** | Early community tradition within approximately a generation       |
| **P4** | Substantially later transmitted tradition                         |
| **P5** | Retrospective hagiography, legend, or theological reconstruction  |
| **PX** | Date/provenance too uncertain for confident placement             |

This is **not a truth score**.

A P1 source proves that someone *reported* an experience extremely well. It does not prove that their interpretation of the experience was correct.

Conversely, a P4 tradition could preserve genuine ancient information.

We simply know less confidently.

This is why the Qur'an, later hadith/sīra material and later Islamic theology cannot initially be collapsed together. The Qur'an is an exceptionally important seventh-century source for the emergence of Islam, while much detailed narrative tradition about Muhammad is transmitted through later literary sources. ([Cambridge University Press][5])

Likewise, early Sikhism provides unusually good opportunities because substantial early manuscript evidence survives comparatively close to the foundational generations. ([OUP Academic][6])

# V. No single “credibility score”

I don't want to reduce these cases to something like:

**Moses 78/100; Muhammad 86/100; Zarathustra 72/100.**

That would create fake precision.

Instead we will maintain separate evidentiary dimensions:

**source proximity | textual stability | independence | historical context | transmission transparency | phenomenological detail | external corroboration | cultural dependency**

A case could therefore have excellent textual evidence but no external verification of its supernatural interpretation.

That's a far more informative result.

# VII. Abraham, Moses and Melchizedek receive a special methodological designation

They remain extremely important, but they **cannot serve as our evidentiary baseline**.

We do not possess contemporary Abrahamic or Mosaic writings.

Therefore:

**Abraham ≠ documented encounter from Abraham's lifetime.**

What we possess is an **ancient Israelite tradition about Abrahamic encounters**.

Likewise:

**Melchizedek ≠ independently documented priest of El Elyon.**

We possess an extraordinarily interesting Genesis tradition concerning him.

The distinction does not dismiss the traditions.

It prevents us from claiming more historical certainty than the evidence permits.

The first baseline cases should instead be encounters for which reasonably early documentation survives.

# VIII. The competing hypotheses

We will keep six hypotheses alive throughout the project.

| Hypothesis                       | Explanation                                                                               |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **H0 — Constructed**             | Reports arise through invention, legend, social formation or literary creation            |
| **H1 — Cognitive**               | Human neuropsychology generates the experiences                                           |
| **H2 — Cultural**                | Existing religious concepts substantially generate or shape experiences                   |
| **H3 — Transcendent impersonal** | Humans sometimes encounter an underlying nonpersonal reality                              |
| **H4 — Transcendent personal**   | Humans sometimes encounter one or more noncorporeal intelligences                         |
| **H5 — Mixed**                   | Different cases have different causes, with several of the above operating simultaneously |

At present **H5 is the methodologically safest working hypothesis**, not because it has been established, but because it doesn't prematurely force thousands of years of heterogeneous phenomena into one explanation.

# IX. The most important comparative distinction

For every report we will attempt to separate:

### Phenomenology

What was actually experienced?

> brilliant light
> voice
> presence
> loss of self
> vision of a figure
> overwhelming love
> terror
> timelessness
> communicated propositions

from:

### Interpretation

What did the person conclude?

> “This was YHWH.”

> “This was Gabriel.”

> “This was Apollo.”

> “This was an ancestor.”

> “I apprehended Brahman.”

> “I experienced nirvāṇa.”

Two people could potentially undergo phenomenologically similar experiences while interpreting them through completely different conceptual systems.

Or apparently similar language could disguise fundamentally different experiences.

That is precisely what the research must determine.

# X. Transmission analysis

Every tradition also gets a **layer map**.

For Jesus, for example:

**Jesus event/experience**
→ remembered sayings/actions
→ early oral Jesus traditions
→ individual Gospel traditions
→ canonical Gospels
→ patristic interpretation
→ creedal Christianity.

The history of the Gospel tradition itself demonstrates the importance of textualization and subsequent manuscript culture. ([OUP Academic][11])

For Buddhism:

**Buddha's awakening/teaching**
→ disciples
→ oral formulaic transmission
→ schools
→ written collections
→ later canonical systems.

Early Buddhism was fundamentally oral before texts were eventually committed to writing, and comparative scholarship warns that surviving discourses contain both preserved teachings and literary development. ([OUP Academic][12])

For Muhammad:

**reported revelatory encounters**
→ proclamations/recitations
→ oral + written preservation
→ Qur'anic corpus
→ early biographical traditions
→ hadith corpora
→ tafsir/fiqh/theology.

Research on Qur'anic textual history indeed distinguishes the early proclamation/text from later interpretive tradition and the development of canonical readings. ([OUP Academic][13])

This exact analysis will be repeated for everyone.

# XI. Convergence will have to pass a dependency test

Suppose Zarathustra, Isaiah, Jesus and Muhammad all teach something resembling final judgment.

That is interesting.

But it cannot simply count as four independent witnesses because later traditions could have inherited ideas from earlier ones.

So every apparent convergence gets classified as:

**possibly independent**

**probably historically connected**

**demonstrably inherited**

or

**relationship unknown**.

The strongest evidence would be a highly specific recurring pattern among cultures for which plausible historical transmission is weak.

That is why Shang China, Indigenous traditions, African traditions and ancient Australian traditions are methodologically invaluable.

# XIII. Wave One

We should not start with Abraham.

We should start where the evidence becomes unusually tangible.

The first comparative wave will be:

**HTE-001 Mari prophecy → HTE-006 Shang divination → HTE-007 Vedic seers → HTE-008 Zarathustra → HTE-014–017 Hebrew prophecy → HTE-009 Greek oracles → HTE-019 Buddha → HTE-023 Jesus → HTE-024 Paul → HTE-028 Muhammad → HTE-030 Guru Nanak → HTE-031 Yoruba Ifá → HTE-033 Aboriginal Dreaming → HTE-034 Maya traditions → one modern control case.**

This order isn't a ranking of religious importance or truth. It deliberately alternates different cultures and modes of encounter so we don't unconsciously define “real revelation” to look like biblical prophecy.

The Rigveda is particularly valuable here because it is the oldest Sanskrit corpus, was composed by numerous poets belonging to identifiable poetic lineages, and gives us a very different model of human-divine encounter from messenger prophecy. ([OUP Academic][14])

Zarathustra is equally valuable because the Gathas repeatedly address Ahura Mazda and appear to preserve material unusually close to Zarathustra's religious activity, while still presenting substantial interpretive difficulties. ([Iranica Online][15])

And Greek prophecy gives us an excellent methodological warning: even for Delphi, probably antiquity's most famous oracle, no original oracle report survives; much of what we know comes through later literary sources. By contrast, sites such as Dodona provide epigraphic evidence. ([OUP Academic][16])

That is exactly the sort of distinction HTERP needs to preserve.

## HTE-001 is now the starting case

Our first dossier will be:

# **HTE-001 — Prophetic Communication at Mari, c. 18th–17th century BCE**

Mari is ideal because it predates biblical literary prophecy, survives on contemporary cuneiform tablets, includes both male and female prophetic intermediaries, identifies claimed divine speakers, records messages transmitted to political authorities, and allows comparison between prophecy and other forms of divination. Oxford's comparative work treats this material as one of our principal sources for reconstructing ancient Near Eastern prophecy. ([OUP Academic][7])

From this point forward, we can work through the corpus case by case and accumulate the evidence rather than repeatedly rebuilding our methodology.

**The project has therefore moved from “design a synthetic religion” to “investigate the global human–transcendent encounter, then follow the evidence wherever it leads.”** That is now the governing direction.
