# Financial Stewardship, Property, and Fiduciary Controls Framework
## [[CORP_NAME]]

---

# PREAMBLE

Property held by a religious institution is not the personal property of its Founder, spiritual leaders, directors, ministers, members, donors, or officers.

It is property entrusted to the Corporation for the lawful religious and charitable purposes for which the Corporation exists.

Financial governance therefore rests upon stewardship rather than ownership.

The Religious Body may determine:

- its mission;
- its ministries;
- its religious priorities;
- its sacred purposes;
- the theological significance of property;
- the religious importance of proposed activities.

The civil Corporation, acting through its legally competent fiduciaries, remains responsible for determining whether corporate assets may lawfully and prudently be:

- received;
- held;
- invested;
- obligated;
- spent;
- transferred;
- borrowed against;
- sold;
- encumbered.

Neither sphere may improperly absorb the other.

Accordingly:

> **Ecclesiastical authority determines religious purpose within its jurisdiction; fiduciary authority determines whether and how corporate resources may lawfully and prudently be committed.**

And:

> **Religious approval of an undertaking does not compel an unlawful or financially imprudent expenditure.**

Likewise:

> **Control of the treasury does not confer authority to determine doctrine, worship, ministry qualifications, or other ecclesiastical questions.**

Where a decision is simultaneously religiously fundamental and financially consequential, neither sphere shall unilaterally convert its legitimate authority into supremacy over the other.

Such matters shall proceed through **concurrent jurisdiction**.

The financial system shall be designed around several additional principles:

1. no person possesses personal ownership of charitable assets;
2. no office carries unlimited spending authority;
3. no person should authorize and conceal the same transaction;
4. conflicts shall be disclosed before decisions are made;
5. compensation shall be reasonable and independently determined;
6. restricted gifts shall be used according to lawful restrictions;
7. significant commitments require deliberation proportionate to risk;
8. records shall permit reconstruction of material transactions;
9. financial transparency shall increase with responsibility;
10. institutional mission shall not become a justification for financial disorder;
11. financial caution shall not become a covert mechanism for controlling religion;
12. stewardship belongs to the institution across generations rather than to the current occupants of office.

---

# ARTICLE I
## Authority

### Section 1.1 — Governing Instruments

This Framework is adopted pursuant to:

1. the Charter of the Corporation;
2. REL-CON-001 — Foundational Religious Constitution;
3. ECC-CON-001 — Ecclesiastical Constitution;
4. the Civil Bylaws;
5. applicable Board charters;
6. applicable ecclesiastical governing instruments;
7. applicable federal and state law.

### Section 1.2 — Hierarchy

This Framework shall be interpreted consistently with instruments having superior legal or constitutional authority.

### Section 1.3 — Implementation

Detailed financial procedures may be adopted by policy provided that such policies do not reduce protections established by this Framework.

---

# ARTICLE II
## Objectives

The financial governance system shall seek to:

1. protect charitable and religious assets;
2. preserve tax-exempt purposes;
3. prevent private inurement and impermissible private benefit;
4. protect donor restrictions;
5. ensure reliable accounting;
6. maintain operational solvency;
7. control fraud and misuse;
8. support responsible ministry;
9. distinguish civil and ecclesiastical authority;
10. provide scalable financial controls;
11. preserve institutional independence;
12. ensure continuity beyond individual leaders;
13. protect sacred and mission-critical property;
14. permit responsible investment;
15. maintain trustworthy financial records;
16. support meaningful oversight.

---

# ARTICLE III
## Fundamental Ownership Principle

All assets held by the Corporation belong to the Corporation or are otherwise held in a legally recognized fiduciary capacity.

No person acquires a proprietary interest in corporate assets by reason of:

- founding the organization;
- serving as PSL;
- serving as a minister;
- serving as director;
- donating funds;
- holding Covenant Membership;
- volunteering;
- occupying religious office.

No doctrine of spiritual leadership shall be interpreted as personal ownership of corporate property unless a particular asset is demonstrably owned outside the Corporation under independently valid legal arrangements.

---

# ARTICLE IV
## Fiduciary Authority

The Civil Board shall exercise or supervise the Corporation's financial and property authority except where lawful delegation has been made.

The Board remains responsible for appropriate oversight of delegated authority.

Delegation does not constitute abandonment of fiduciary responsibility.

---

# ARTICLE V
## Fiduciary Duties

Persons exercising financial authority shall act:

1. in good faith;
2. with appropriate care;
3. in the interests of the Corporation and its exempt purposes;
4. within their delegated authority;
5. without improper personal benefit;
6. with reasonable attention to risk;
7. according to applicable restrictions.

---

# ARTICLE VI
## Competence

Financial authority shall be distributed according to competence.

A person does not become financially qualified merely because the person possesses:

- spiritual authority;
- theological knowledge;
- founder status;
- popularity;
- seniority.

Likewise, financial expertise does not create ecclesiastical authority.

---

# ARTICLE VII
## Separation of Domains

### Section 7.1 — Ecclesiastical Domain

Ecclesiastical authorities may determine questions such as:

- whether a ministry should exist;
- whether property possesses sacred or religious significance;
- whether a proposed program furthers religious mission;
- whether a religious community should be established;
- religious priorities among ministries.

### Section 7.2 — Civil Financial Domain

The Civil Board determines questions such as:

- affordability;
- financial risk;
- contracts;
- financing;
- insurance;
- taxation;
- accounting;
- legal title;
- property liability;
- investment;
- internal control.

### Section 7.3 — Non-Encroachment

Neither authority shall use its own jurisdiction merely as a pretext to usurp the other's legitimate jurisdiction.

---

# ARTICLE VIII
## Concurrent Jurisdiction

A matter shall require both ecclesiastical and civil approval when it materially combines:

1. substantial religious significance; and
2. substantial corporate financial or property consequence.

Examples may include:

- acquisition of principal worship property;
- sale of principal sacred property;
- creation of a major monastery or religious community;
- closure of a principal religious institution;
- major restricted endowment for a doctrinally defined ministry;
- merger with another religious body;
- transaction materially altering the institution's religious continuity.

---

# ARTICLE IX
## Effect of Concurrent Approval

Where concurrent approval is required:

- ecclesiastical approval means the undertaking is religiously authorized;
- Board approval means the undertaking is corporately authorized.

Neither approval substitutes for the other.

Failure of either required approval means the Corporation shall not proceed.

---

# ARTICLE X
## No Ecclesiastical Compulsion of Fiduciary Breach

No ecclesiastical body may require a director or officer to approve a transaction that the person reasonably believes would violate:

- law;
- fiduciary duty;
- valid donor restriction;
- tax-exempt requirements;
- binding contractual obligation.

The matter may instead be:

- reconsidered;
- restructured;
- referred for independent advice;
- deferred;
- abandoned.

---

# ARTICLE XI
## No Financial Veto Over Doctrine

The Civil Board may decline to fund a proposal for legitimate financial or legal reasons.

It shall not declare:

> “Because the Board controls the money, the Board therefore determines the doctrine.”

Budget authority shall not become doctrinal authority.

---

# ARTICLE XII
## Financial Authority Schedule

The Corporation shall maintain a written **Financial Authority Schedule** establishing approval thresholds for categories such as:

- ordinary purchases;
- contracts;
- grants;
- capital expenditures;
- hiring commitments;
- borrowing;
- leases;
- property transactions;
- investments;
- emergency expenditures.

Dollar thresholds should ordinarily be established by policy rather than permanently embedded in this constitutional Framework.

---

# ARTICLE XIII
## Scaling of Controls

Controls shall scale according to:

- transaction value;
- total organizational resources;
- risk;
- reversibility;
- related-party involvement;
- duration;
- legal consequence;
- religious significance.

A small routine purchase should not require the same process as a property acquisition.

A major irreversible commitment should not receive the same process as a small routine purchase.

---

# ARTICLE XIV
## Segregation of Duties

Where organizational scale reasonably permits, no single person should control all of:

1. initiation;
2. authorization;
3. custody;
4. payment;
5. accounting;
6. reconciliation;
7. review

for the same material transaction.

---

# ARTICLE XV
## Small-Organization Compensating Controls

Where staffing is too small for full segregation of duties, compensating controls shall be used.

These may include:

- secondary review;
- Board review of bank statements;
- transaction notifications;
- dual authorization above thresholds;
- external bookkeeping;
- periodic independent reconciliation;
- enhanced documentation.

Small size is a reason to adapt controls, not eliminate them.

---

# ARTICLE XVI
## Budget

The Corporation shall maintain an annual operating budget or equivalent financial plan.

The budget should identify:

- anticipated revenue;
- ordinary operating expense;
- ministry allocations;
- personnel expense;
- capital expenditures;
- debt obligations;
- reserves;
- restricted activities;
- significant planned commitments.

---

# ARTICLE XVII
## Budget Development

Budget development should integrate:

1. ecclesiastical priorities;
2. operational planning;
3. financial projections;
4. legal obligations;
5. cash requirements;
6. reserve needs;
7. risk.

---

# ARTICLE XVIII
## Budget Approval

The Civil Board shall approve the final corporate budget.

Where the budget includes material ecclesiastical priorities, the appropriate ecclesiastical authority should first establish or affirm those priorities.

Board approval shall remain necessary before corporate funds are committed.

---

# ARTICLE XIX
## Budget Does Not Appropriate Unlimited Authority

An approved budget does not automatically authorize every expenditure within a category.

Transactions must also satisfy:

- Financial Authority Schedule;
- conflict rules;
- contracting procedures;
- restricted-fund rules;
- other applicable controls.

---

# ARTICLE XX
## Budget Amendments

Material deviations from the approved budget shall require appropriate authorization.

Policy shall define:

- materiality;
- reforecasting;
- budget-transfer authority;
- supplemental appropriations.

---

# ARTICLE XXI
## Financial Forecasting

The responsible financial officers should periodically maintain forecasts of:

- cash;
- revenue;
- expenses;
- liabilities;
- reserves;
- significant commitments.

The purpose is not prediction for its own sake but early detection of material financial risk.

---

# ARTICLE XXII
## Bank Accounts

Corporate money shall be maintained in accounts:

- titled to the Corporation;
- appropriately authorized;
- institutionally controlled.

Corporate receipts shall not ordinarily be deposited into personal accounts.

---

# ARTICLE XXIII
## Account Opening and Closing

Opening or closing a financial account requires authorization established by Board policy.

The Corporation shall maintain a current register of:

- institutions;
- accounts;
- authorized persons;
- account purpose.

---

# ARTICLE XXIV
## Signatory Authority

No person acquires permanent control over corporate accounts merely because of religious office.

Signatory authority:

- is institutional;
- is revocable;
- should correspond to role;
- shall terminate promptly when authorization ends.

---

# ARTICLE XXV
## Payment Authorization

Material disbursements shall require approval according to the Financial Authority Schedule.

Higher-risk payments may require:

- two approvals;
- Board approval;
- committee approval;
- documented contract;
- supporting invoice;
- other verification.

---

# ARTICLE XXVI
## No Blank Authority

The following should ordinarily be prohibited:

- pre-signed blank checks;
- unrestricted signature stamps;
- shared banking passwords;
- unlogged cash withdrawals;
- undocumented transfers.

---

# ARTICLE XXVII
## Cash

Cash receipts and disbursements shall receive heightened control because cash is difficult to reconstruct after misuse.

Controls should include appropriate:

- counting;
- documentation;
- custody;
- deposit;
- reconciliation.

---

# ARTICLE XXVIII
## Offerings and Collections

Where offerings or collections are received:

1. more than one person should ordinarily participate in counting;
2. counts should be recorded;
3. receipts should be deposited promptly;
4. deposit records should reconcile with count records;
5. designated gifts should be separately identified where practical.

---

# ARTICLE XXIX
## Electronic Payments

Electronic payment systems shall use:

- individual credentials;
- role-based access;
- multi-factor authentication where available;
- authorization limits;
- transaction records;
- periodic access review.

---

# ARTICLE XXX
## Payment Cards

Corporate payment cards:

- remain corporate property;
- shall have defined custodians;
- shall have reasonable limits;
- shall not be used for personal expenditures;
- require supporting documentation.

---

# ARTICLE XXXI
## Reimbursements

Reimbursements shall be limited to legitimate organizational expenditures and supported by reasonable documentation.

Policy should define:

- allowable expenses;
- documentation;
- approval;
- timing;
- travel rules.

---

# ARTICLE XXXII
## Personal Expenses

Corporate funds shall not be used to pay personal expenses except where the payment constitutes:

- properly approved compensation;
- legitimate employee benefit;
- documented reimbursement;
- another lawful organizational expenditure.

Improper personal use must be corrected and may result in discipline.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIII
## Advances

Reasonable advances for anticipated organizational expenses may be permitted under written procedures.

Unspent amounts shall be returned.

Documentation shall be required.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIV
## Insider Loans

As an institutional policy, the Corporation shall not ordinarily make personal loans or guarantee personal obligations of:

- directors;
- officers;
- Founder;
- PSL;
- other senior insiders.

This prohibition may be stricter than exemptions otherwise available to a religious corporation under state law.

Ordinary reimbursable expense advances and lawful employee-benefit arrangements are not intended to be treated as personal loans when properly structured.

---

# ARTICLE XXXV
## Compensation

The Corporation may reasonably compensate persons for bona fide services.

Religious service is not required to be uncompensated merely because it is religious.

Compensation must not become a mechanism for:

- private inurement;
- disguised profit distribution;
- founder enrichment;
- excessive insider benefit.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVI
## Compensation Components

Compensation review shall consider the total economic arrangement, including as applicable:

- salary;
- wages;
- bonuses;
- housing;
- retirement contributions;
- insurance;
- allowances;
- vehicles;
- education;
- deferred compensation;
- personal use of property;
- other material benefits.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVII
## Independent Compensation Approval

A person shall not determine or vote upon their own compensation.

Compensation for a person capable of materially influencing the Corporation shall be determined by disinterested decision-makers.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVIII
## Compensation Comparability

Material compensation decisions should be supported by appropriate comparability information considering such factors as:

- duties;
- experience;
- organization size;
- geographic area;
- comparable organizations;
- market conditions;
- total compensation.

The record should identify information relied upon.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIX
## Compensation Record

Minutes or equivalent records should document:

- persons participating;
- conflicts and recusals;
- comparability data considered;
- decision;
- basis for determining reasonableness.

---

# ARTICLE XL
## Founder Compensation

Founder status neither:

- prohibits reasonable compensation;
nor
- creates entitlement to compensation.

Any compensation for the Founder shall follow the same independent review standards applicable to another substantial insider.

---

# ARTICLE XLI
## PSL Compensation

PSL compensation shall be determined by a body independent of the PSL.

The PSL shall not:

- vote on personal compensation;
- control comparability data;
- threaten ecclesiastical consequences to influence compensation.

---

# ARTICLE XLII
## Board Compensation

Directors may be compensated only where:

- lawful;
- expressly authorized;
- reasonable;
- independently approved;
- consistent with tax-exempt purposes.

Reimbursement of legitimate Board expenses is distinct from compensation.

---

# ARTICLE XLIII
## Conflict of Interest

A conflict exists where personal, family, financial, professional, or other interests could materially impair or reasonably appear to impair independent judgment on behalf of the Corporation.

---

# ARTICLE XLIV
## Duty to Disclose

Persons participating in material financial decisions shall disclose actual or potential conflicts before participating in deliberation or decision.

---

# ARTICLE XLV
## Related Persons

Conflict procedures shall address material interests involving persons related to a decision-maker through relationships such as:

- spouse;
- domestic partner;
- ancestor;
- descendant;
- sibling;
- controlled entity;
- substantial business relationship;
- other relationship defined by policy or law.

---

# ARTICLE XLVI
## Recusal

A person with a material conflict shall ordinarily:

1. disclose the conflict;
2. provide relevant information if requested;
3. leave deliberation when appropriate;
4. abstain from voting;
5. not improperly influence other decision-makers.

---

# ARTICLE XLVII
## Related-Party Transactions

A transaction involving an insider or related party is not automatically prohibited.

It shall receive heightened review and may proceed only where the competent disinterested body determines that the transaction is:

- lawful;
- in the Corporation's interests;
- fair and reasonable;
- consistent with exempt purposes.

---

# ARTICLE XLVIII
## Alternatives

Before approving a substantial related-party transaction, the responsible body should consider reasonably available alternatives.

Where appropriate, competitive quotations, appraisals, or market comparisons should be obtained.

---

# ARTICLE XLIX
## Founder-Owned Vendors

The Corporation shall not contract with a business owned or controlled by the Founder merely because the Founder created the organization.

Such a transaction requires:

- full disclosure;
- independent review;
- fair-market analysis;
- recusal;
- documented institutional benefit.

---

# ARTICLE L
## Clergy-Owned Vendors

The same principles apply to businesses owned or controlled by:

- PSL;
- ministers;
- directors;
- officers;
- their related persons.

Religious office does not create an exception to conflict review.

---

# ARTICLE LI
## Annual Conflict Disclosure

Directors, principal officers, and other designated persons shall periodically disclose material interests that could create conflicts.

At minimum, an annual disclosure process is recommended.

---

# ARTICLE LII
## Gifts to Insiders

Corporate assets shall not be transferred to insiders merely as personal gifts except for reasonable:

- compensation;
- recognition;
- benevolence available under an objective charitable program;
- another legitimate exempt-purpose expenditure.

---

# ARTICLE LIII
## Benevolence

The Corporation may operate benevolence and charitable-assistance programs.

Such programs should use objective standards designed to further exempt purposes rather than personally benefit insiders.

---

# ARTICLE LIV
## Insider Benevolence

An insider should not approve personal participation in a benevolence program.

Assistance involving insiders or their families requires independent review and documentation sufficient to demonstrate legitimate charitable purpose.

---

# ARTICLE LV
## Donations

The Corporation may accept lawful gifts including:

- cash;
- securities;
- personal property;
- real property;
- intellectual property;
- digital assets;
- testamentary gifts;
- trusts;
- other lawful assets.

Acceptance may be conditioned upon review.

---

# ARTICLE LVI
## Gift Acceptance Policy

The Board shall adopt a Gift Acceptance Policy addressing assets that may create unusual:

- liability;
- restrictions;
- valuation issues;
- tax consequences;
- maintenance expense;
- reputational concerns;
- doctrinal conflicts.

---

# ARTICLE LVII
## Restricted Gifts

A valid donor restriction accepted by the Corporation shall be honored according to its terms and applicable law.

Restricted assets shall not be diverted merely because another use later appears more desirable.

---

# ARTICLE LVIII
## Acceptance Before Obligation

A donor's proposed restriction does not bind the Corporation merely because the donor stated it.

The Corporation must first accept the gift and its restriction through an authorized process.

---

# ARTICLE LIX
## Unacceptable Restrictions

The Corporation may refuse a gift whose conditions:

- conflict with its religious purposes;
- violate law;
- impose unreasonable administrative burden;
- compromise institutional independence;
- create unacceptable liability;
- conflict with tax-exempt requirements.

---

# ARTICLE LX
## Donor Control

A donor may impose valid conditions on a gift.

A donor shall not obtain general governance authority merely by contributing money unless such authority independently exists under a valid governing instrument.

---

# ARTICLE LXI
## Donor Influence Over Doctrine

No donor shall purchase:

- doctrinal authority;
- ordination authority;
- disciplinary immunity;
- ecclesiastical office;
- Board position

through financial contribution.

---

# ARTICLE LXII
## Designated Funds

The accounting system shall distinguish, as appropriate:

- unrestricted funds;
- internally designated funds;
- donor-restricted funds;
- endowment funds;
- agency or custodial funds;
- other legally distinct categories.

Internal designation by the Board is not the same as donor restriction.

---

# ARTICLE LXIII
## Endowments

Endowment assets shall be managed consistently with:

- donor intent;
- applicable law;
- prudent investment principles;
- institutional purposes.

---

# ARTICLE LXIV
## Endowment Spending

The Board shall adopt a prudent spending policy for endowment funds.

Spending decisions should consider:

- donor terms;
- fund duration;
- preservation;
- inflation;
- expected return;
- organizational needs;
- applicable law.

---

# ARTICLE LXV
## Modification of Restrictions

The Corporation shall not unilaterally disregard a donor restriction merely because compliance becomes inconvenient.

Modification shall occur only through:

- donor consent where legally sufficient;
- judicial process;
- statutory process;
- another lawful mechanism.

---

# ARTICLE LXVI
## Fundraising

Fundraising shall be:

- truthful;
- consistent with religious identity;
- reasonably transparent concerning purpose;
- compliant with applicable law.

---

# ARTICLE LXVII
## Solicitation Representations

Funds solicited for a represented purpose shall not knowingly be used inconsistently with that representation where the representation creates a restriction or legally enforceable obligation.

---

# ARTICLE LXVIII
## Fundraising Costs

Fundraising compensation and costs shall be reasonable.

The Corporation shall avoid misleading donors concerning the amount or proportion of funds devoted to particular purposes.

---

# ARTICLE LXIX
## Professional Fundraisers

Use of professional fundraising services requires:

- appropriate due diligence;
- written agreement;
- legal compliance;
- reasonable compensation;
- access to necessary records.

---

# ARTICLE LXX
## Grants Made by the Corporation

Grants shall further the Corporation's lawful exempt purposes.

The Corporation should maintain:

- eligibility criteria;
- approval records;
- grant terms;
- monitoring proportionate to risk.

---

# ARTICLE LXXI
## Grants to Insiders

Grants or financial assistance benefiting insiders shall receive heightened scrutiny and shall not be used to disguise:

- compensation;
- distributions;
- private benefit.

---

# ARTICLE LXXII
## Grants Received

Grant restrictions accepted by the Corporation shall be tracked and honored.

Material reporting obligations shall be assigned to responsible officers.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIII
## Reserves

The Board shall establish a reserve philosophy appropriate to:

- operating volatility;
- fixed obligations;
- ministry continuity;
- emergency needs;
- capital requirements;
- donor restrictions.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIV
## Operating Reserve

The Corporation should seek to maintain an operating reserve appropriate to its circumstances.

The reserve target may evolve with:

- organizational size;
- revenue reliability;
- property ownership;
- staffing;
- debt;
- risk.

---

# ARTICLE LXXV
## Reserve Use

Use of designated reserves shall require authorization appropriate to the purpose and amount.

Emergency reserve use shall be reported promptly to the Board.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVI
## Debt

Borrowing shall be undertaken deliberately.

Before incurring material debt, the Board should consider:

- purpose;
- repayment source;
- interest rate;
- collateral;
- covenants;
- liquidity;
- downside risk;
- effect on future ministry.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVII
## Ecclesiastical Approval Does Not Create Debt Authority

A religious body's decision that a project is spiritually desirable does not itself authorize the Corporation to borrow money.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVIII
## Encumbrance of Property

Mortgaging, pledging, or otherwise materially encumbering significant real property requires Board approval.

Sacred or principal religious property may additionally require ecclesiastical concurrence under Article VIII.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIX
## Personal Guarantees

Directors, ministers, Founder, PSL, and members should not ordinarily be expected to personally guarantee institutional obligations.

Any proposed personal guarantee arrangement requires legal review and explicit informed consent.

---

# ARTICLE LXXX
## Investments

Corporate investments shall be managed according to a written Investment Policy appropriate to:

- time horizon;
- liquidity needs;
- risk tolerance;
- purposes;
- donor restrictions;
- applicable law.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXI
## Prudence

Investment decisions shall be evaluated in context rather than solely according to whether an individual investment later gains or loses value.

The process should consider:

- diversification;
- liquidity;
- risk and return;
- costs;
- organizational needs;
- restricted purposes.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXII
## Speculation

The Corporation shall not treat charitable assets as a vehicle for speculative personal theories or insider financial interests.

Higher-risk strategies require proportionately higher:

- expertise;
- due diligence;
- limits;
- oversight.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIII
## Digital and Cryptocurrency Assets

The Corporation may receive or hold digital assets when approved under policy.

Controls should address:

- custody;
- private keys;
- valuation;
- volatility;
- conversion;
- cybersecurity;
- accounting;
- tax reporting.

No single individual should possess unreviewable control of material institutional digital assets.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIV
## Investment Conflicts

Investment decisions involving:

- director-owned businesses;
- insider funds;
- related investment managers;
- personal financial interests

shall receive related-party review.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXV
## Real Property

Acquisition of real property requires appropriate review of:

- title;
- condition;
- valuation;
- financing;
- zoning;
- insurance;
- environmental risk;
- maintenance obligations;
- intended use;
- financial sustainability.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVI
## Sacred Property

The Religious Body may designate certain property as **Sacred Property** because of its enduring religious significance.

Possible examples include:

- principal sanctuary;
- monastery;
- shrine;
- cemetery;
- sacred archive;
- consecrated land;
- materially significant religious objects.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVII
## Effect of Sacred Designation

Sacred designation does not:

- transfer legal title from the Corporation;
- eliminate lawful fiduciary responsibilities;
- place property beyond civil law.

It creates additional internal ecclesiastical protections governing decisions materially affecting the property's religious use or disposition.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVIII
## Disposition of Sacred Property

Material sale, transfer, abandonment, secularization, destruction, or long-term repurposing of designated Sacred Property should require:

1. Civil Board approval;
2. Ecclesiastical Council approval;
3. any CMA reserved-power concurrence required by superior law;
4. satisfaction of applicable donor or trust restrictions.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIX
## Principal Religious Property

The governing instruments may designate one or more properties as **Principal Religious Property**.

Disposition or material encumbrance may receive a higher approval threshold than ordinary property.

---

# ARTICLE XC
## Property Cannot Be Held Hostage

Neither a departing faction nor an individual leader shall:

- remove;
- transfer;
- conceal;
- pledge;
- retain control of

corporate property contrary to valid institutional authority.

---

# ARTICLE XCI
## Schism and Property

Property rights in a religious disagreement shall be determined by:

- valid legal title;
- Charter;
- constitutional asset provisions;
- applicable restrictions;
- governing law.

Control shall not automatically pass to whichever faction has:

- more supporters;
- the Founder;
- PSL;
- more directors

unless governing law and instruments produce that result.

---

# ARTICLE XCII
## Personal Property Used by the Institution

Where personal property is loaned to the Corporation, the arrangement should be documented sufficiently to distinguish:

- corporate ownership;
- personal ownership;
- donated property;
- temporary loan.

This is especially important for valuable:

- religious objects;
- books;
- art;
- equipment;
- intellectual property.

---

# ARTICLE XCIII
## Intellectual Property

The Corporation shall maintain clear ownership and licensing arrangements for material:

- publications;
- curriculum;
- liturgical materials;
- recordings;
- trademarks;
- websites;
- software;
- databases;
- media.

Founder or author involvement shall not be left ambiguous.

---

# ARTICLE XCIV
## Founder-Created Intellectual Property

Where intellectual property predates the Corporation or is personally owned by the Founder, its relationship to the Corporation should be established through explicit:

- assignment;
- license;

- domains;

- credentials;

Commitments extending substantially beyond the current budget period shall receive enhanced review.

Creation or control of:

- subsidiaries;

Material procurement should seek reasonable value through procedures proportionate to transaction size.

# ARTICLE CIX

The Board shall establish accounting practices appropriate to organizational size and reporting requirements.

The Board may establish a Finance Committee.

# ARTICLE CXVII
## Audit Committee

As scale warrants, an Audit Committee or equivalent independent oversight function should be established.

Persons responsible for preparing financial records should not exclusively supervise their own external review.

---

# ARTICLE CXVIII

The Corporation shall use a risk-based approach to independent financial examination.

Depending upon size and circumstances, this may include:

- internal review;
- independent reconciliation;
- compilation;
- review engagement;
- financial audit;
- forensic examination.

# ARTICLE CXIX
## Audit Triggers

Regardless of ordinary schedule, enhanced independent review should be considered upon:

- suspected fraud;
- major unexplained discrepancy;
- rapid organizational growth;
- major borrowing;
- significant donor requirement;
- leadership transition;
- major property transaction;
- credible financial whistleblower allegation.

---

# ARTICLE CXX
## External Auditor Independence

An external auditor or reviewer should possess appropriate independence from:

- management;
- Founder;
- PSL;
- persons whose transactions are under review.

# ARTICLE CXXI
## Management Letter

Material control deficiencies identified by external accountants or auditors shall be presented to the Board and tracked to resolution.

# ARTICLE CXXII
## Fraud

Suspected financial fraud shall be reported through channels that bypass any person implicated in the allegation.

# ARTICLE CXXIII
## Financial Misconduct

Financial misconduct may include:

- theft;
- embezzlement;
- falsification;
- unauthorized expenditure;
- intentional misuse of restricted funds;
- concealed conflicts;
- kickbacks;
- bribery;
- false reimbursement;
- record destruction;
- material financial misrepresentation.

---

# ARTICLE CXXIV
## Fraud Response

A material fraud allegation may require:

1. protection of assets;
2. preservation of records;
3. suspension of relevant access;
4. independent investigation;
5. insurer notification;
6. legal review;
7. external reporting where required;
8. disciplinary referral under DISC-001.

---

# ARTICLE CXXV
## No Quiet Repayment Rule

Repayment of misused funds does not automatically resolve misconduct.

Repayment may be relevant to remediation but shall not erase:

- underlying facts;
- breach of trust;
- disciplinary consequences;
- reporting obligations.

---

# ARTICLE CXXVI
## Financial Whistleblowers

Good-faith reports of suspected financial misconduct shall be protected from retaliation.

A reporting pathway shall exist that does not require reporting solely to the person accused.

---

# ARTICLE CXXVII
## Record Preservation

Material financial records shall be retained according to REC-001 and applicable law.

Records should permit reconstruction of:

- authorization;
- payment;
- purpose;
- recipient;
- accounting treatment

for material transactions.

# ARTICLE CXXVIII
## No Destruction During Investigation

Ordinary destruction schedules shall be suspended for records relevant to:

- investigation;
- litigation;
- audit;
- government inquiry;
- material dispute.

## Financial Access Controls

Access to:

- banking;
- accounting;
- payroll;
- payment systems;
- investment platforms

shall be role-based and periodically reviewed.

Departed or removed personnel shall have access terminated promptly.

Reasonable controls shall protect financial systems against:

Material changes to vendor banking instructions should be independently verified through a trusted channel before payment.

Payroll shall be administered through documented processes governing:

- authorized compensation;

# ARTICLE CXXXIII

Housing allowances or other clergy-specific tax arrangements shall be established and documented according to applicable law.

Religious office alone does not eliminate documentation requirements.

# ARTICLE CXXXIV

Founder, PSL, and other senior leaders shall not possess unreviewable expense accounts.

Their expenses shall receive independent review.

---

# ARTICLE CXXXV
## Financial Information Rights

Board members shall receive reasonable access to financial information necessary for fiduciary duties.

Ecclesiastical bodies shall receive financial information necessary for decisions within their jurisdiction.

Neither entitlement creates unlimited access to confidential information unrelated to legitimate responsibility.

Financial transparency shall be tiered.

The Covenant community should periodically receive a meaningful stewardship report appropriate to organizational scale.

# ARTICLE CXXXVIII
## Donor Privacy

Donor information shall be protected according to:

- law;
- donor expectations;
- organizational policy.

Large donations shall not secretly purchase institutional authority.

---

# ARTICLE CXXXIX
## Anonymous Gifts

Anonymous gifts may be accepted subject to:

- legal compliance;
- source-risk considerations;
- internal knowledge where necessary;
- anti-money-laundering or banking requirements where applicable.

Anonymous donor status shall not be used to disguise insider transactions.

---

# ARTICLE CXL
## Naming Rights

Naming buildings, programs, scholarships, or funds after donors shall require appropriate approval.

Naming shall not:

- confer governance power;
- create doctrinal authority;
- prevent lawful future changes unless contractually agreed.

---

# ARTICLE CXLI
## Financial Dependency Risk

The Board should monitor excessive dependence upon:

- one donor;
- one congregation;
- one business;
- one revenue stream;
- one leader.

Financial concentration can become governance concentration.

---

# ARTICLE CXLII
## Founder Funding

Founder financing may legitimately help establish the institution.

The nature of each contribution shall be documented as:

- donation;
- reimbursable advance;
- lawful loan if permissible and approved;
- property license;
- capital contribution not creating ownership;
- another defined arrangement.

Ambiguity between “gift” and “money the Founder expects back later” shall be avoided.

---

# ARTICLE CXLIII
## Contributions Do Not Create Equity

No donor, including the Founder, acquires equity in the nonprofit Corporation through contributions.

---

# ARTICLE CXLIV
## Emergency Expenditures

Emergency financial authority may be granted for circumstances requiring action before ordinary approval can reasonably occur.

---

# ARTICLE CXLV
## Emergency Limits

Emergency authority shall be:

- limited in amount or subject matter;
- exercised only for genuine urgency;
- documented;
- reported promptly;
- reviewed after use.

It shall not become a permanent bypass around ordinary controls.

---

# ARTICLE CXLVI
## Emergency Sacred-Property Action

Where immediate action is necessary to prevent serious:

- injury;
- destruction;
- legal forfeiture;
- catastrophic loss

temporary protective action concerning Sacred Property may be taken without ordinary concurrence where delay would materially worsen harm.

Permanent disposition shall still require the ordinary constitutional approvals.

---

# ARTICLE CXLVII
## Crisis Liquidity

During a severe financial crisis, the Board may adopt a temporary stabilization plan addressing:

- liquidity;
- expenditures;
- reserves;
- staffing;
- property;
- obligations.

The plan shall preserve ecclesiastical jurisdiction over religious questions.

---

# ARTICLE CXLVIII
## Insolvency Risk

Where the Corporation faces serious insolvency risk, directors shall obtain appropriate professional advice.

No religious leader may require continuation of financially impossible commitments merely by asserting spiritual necessity.

---

# ARTICLE CXLIX
## Dissolution

Upon dissolution, assets shall be distributed according to:

- the Charter;
- valid restrictions;
- applicable law;
- tax-exempt asset-dedication requirements.

No remaining charitable assets shall be distributed to insiders as personal ownership.

A proposed merger involving substantial religious or financial consequences shall receive both:

# ARTICLE CLVII

Suspected financial misconduct shall be referred under DISC-001 when appropriate.

Financial investigation and ecclesiastical discipline may proceed in coordinated but distinct processes.

# ARTICLE CLVIII
## Board Accountability

Failure by directors to exercise meaningful financial oversight may itself constitute governance failure even where the directors did not personally misuse assets.

---

# ARTICLE CLIX
## Ecclesiastical Accountability

A religious leader who improperly uses spiritual authority to pressure financial decision-makers may be subject to ecclesiastical accountability.

Examples include demanding that fiduciaries:

- conceal transactions;
- ignore conflicts;
- misuse restricted funds;
- approve unreasonable compensation;
- transfer assets to insiders.

---

# ARTICLE CLX
## Treasurer and Officer Accountability

Financial officers are accountable for:

- truthful reporting;
- following authorization;
- preserving records;
- disclosing material irregularities.

They shall not conceal a material concern merely because a senior religious leader requested silence.

---

# ARTICLE CLXI
## Board Removal Is Not a Financial Remedy

Ordinary disagreements over a prudent financial judgment should not automatically become disciplinary cases.

Discipline is appropriate where conduct involves:

- dishonesty;
- bad faith;
- abuse;
- serious neglect;
- conflict concealment;
- unauthorized action;
- other misconduct.

---

# ARTICLE CLXII
## Financial Decisions and Religious Dissent

A director's good-faith refusal to approve a financially imprudent transaction shall not itself constitute religious disloyalty.

Likewise, an ecclesiastical leader's advocacy for a ministry priority shall not itself constitute fiduciary misconduct.

The system should distinguish legitimate institutional tension from wrongdoing.

---

# ARTICLE CLXIII
## Transaction Classification

Material decisions should be classified as:

1. **Ecclesiastical-only**
2. **Civil-financial-only**
3. **Concurrent**

Examples:

| Matter | Ecclesiastical | Civil Board | Classification |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| adopt teaching curriculum | ✓ | only budget implementation | primarily ecclesiastical |
| routine utility contract | — | ✓ | civil |
| determine minister's doctrinal qualification | ✓ | employment interface only | ecclesiastical |
| approve payroll | — | ✓ | civil |
| acquire principal sanctuary | ✓ | ✓ | concurrent |
| sell designated Sacred Property | ✓ | ✓ | concurrent |
| decide investment allocation | — | ✓ | civil |
| define religious use of sanctuary | ✓ | operational interface | primarily ecclesiastical |
| incur mortgage on sanctuary | religious concurrence where protected | ✓ | concurrent |
| establish reasonable PSL compensation | no self-control | ✓ | civil fiduciary |

---

# ARTICLE CLXIV
## Concurrent Decision Record

A material concurrent transaction should produce a record identifying separately:

1. ecclesiastical approval;
2. fiduciary approval;
3. financial analysis;
4. conflict review;
5. applicable restrictions;
6. final authorization.

This prevents later confusion concerning which body actually decided what.

---

# ARTICLE CLXV
## Jurisdictional Conflict

If the Civil Board and ecclesiastical authorities disagree over which body has jurisdiction:

1. neither shall simply declare itself supreme;
2. the governing instruments shall be consulted;
3. temporary preservation measures may continue;
4. unresolved constitutional jurisdiction may be referred to the CCG.

---

# ARTICLE CLXVI
## No Financial Capture

The governance system shall guard against financial capture in which control over resources becomes effective control over religion.

Indicators may include:

- donors dictating doctrine;
- Board withholding ordinary resources to coerce doctrine;
- Founder controlling all accounts personally;
- one financial officer controlling records and payments;
- ministers directing undisclosed transactions.

---

# ARTICLE CLXVII
## No Ecclesiastical Capture of Finance

The system shall equally guard against ecclesiastical capture in which spiritual authority eliminates meaningful fiduciary oversight.

Indicators may include:

- leader approval replacing invoices;
- “faith” being invoked against accounting;
- questions about spending treated as insubordination;
- insider transactions concealed from directors;
- unrestricted leader access to treasury.

---

# ARTICLE CLXVIII
## Institutional Continuity

Financial systems shall be designed so that death, resignation, incapacity, or removal of any one individual does not render the Corporation unable to:

- access funds;
- pay obligations;
- identify assets;
- recover credentials;
- reconstruct records.

---

# ARTICLE CLXIX
## Leadership Transition

Upon transition of a:

- Founder;
- PSL;
- Treasurer;
- executive;
- finance officer

the Corporation shall promptly review:

- account access;
- cards;
- passwords;
- contracts;
- assets;
- pending obligations;
- financial records.

At least every three years, the Board should review this financial governance system.

3. Are related-party transactions controlled?
4. Is compensation independently determined?
5. Are restricted gifts traceable?
6. Are reserves adequate?
7. Are investments prudent?
8. Are sacred-property protections functioning?
9. Is the Board receiving reliable information?
10. Are financial systems excessively dependent upon one person?
11. Are financial and ecclesiastical jurisdictions clear?
12. Are audits or reviews proportionate to risk?
13. Are cybersecurity controls adequate?
14. Are policies actually followed?

# ARTICLE CLXXI
## Financial Control Failure Modes

The Corporation shall specifically guard against:

### 1. Founder Treasury

The Founder treats corporate funds as personal funds.

### 2. Spiritual-Leader Exemption

The PSL is exempt from normal financial controls.

### 3. Rubber-Stamp Board

Directors approve decisions without understanding them.

### 4. Accountant Sovereignty

Financial staff effectively control institutional policy because others cannot understand the records.

### 5. Restricted-Fund Diversion

Restricted donations are quietly repurposed.

### 6. Related-Party Patronage

Insider businesses receive favorable contracts without independent review.

### 7. Compensation Capture

A leader effectively sets personal compensation.

### 8. Single-Person Banking

One individual controls authorization, payment, records, and reconciliation.

### 9. Donor Capture

Major donors purchase governance or doctrine.

### 10. Sacred-Property Hostage

One faction gains control of spiritually central property through ordinary corporate procedure alone.

### 11. Mission Excuse

Poor financial controls are defended as evidence of spiritual faith.

### 12. Fiduciary Supremacy

Budget control is used to seize ecclesiastical authority.

### 13. Permanent Emergency

Temporary emergency spending becomes ordinary governance.

### 14. Institutional Amnesia

Nobody can reconstruct why a major transaction occurred.

### 15. Unfunded Vision

Leadership repeatedly creates commitments without sustainable resources.

# ARTICLE CLXXII
## Financial Decision Quality Standard

Before approving a material financial commitment, the responsible authority should be able to answer:

1. What is being approved?
2. Who possesses authority?
3. What exempt purpose does it serve?
4. Is ecclesiastical concurrence required?
5. What will it cost?
6. How will it be funded?
7. What future obligations arise?
8. Is a conflict present?
9. Is the price or compensation reasonable?
10. Are donor restrictions implicated?
11. Is liquidity adequate?
12. What risks exist?
13. What alternatives were considered?
14. What approvals are required?
15. How will the transaction be documented?
16. Who will monitor performance?
17. Can the institution exit?
18. What happens under an adverse scenario?
19. Would the same transaction be approved if no insider benefited?
20. Will a future Board be able to understand why this decision was made?

# ARTICLE CLXXIX

The financial order of the Religious Body shall therefore rest upon the following rule:

The PSL does not own it.

The Board does not own it.

The donors do not own it.

The congregation does not own it.

Those who administer it hold authority only in trust for the purposes to which the assets are lawfully devoted.

# FINANCIAL STEWARDSHIP COVENANT

We shall treat institutional property as a trust rather than a possession.

We shall not confuse spiritual authority with ownership.

We shall not confuse financial authority with doctrinal authority.

We shall not allow the Founder to become the treasury.

We shall not allow the Board to become the religion.

We shall not allow donors to purchase governance.

We shall not allow religious rank to defeat financial accountability.

We shall disclose conflicts.

We shall recuse where independence requires it.

We shall compensate fairly without enriching insiders improperly.

We shall honor lawful restrictions placed upon gifts we knowingly accept.

We shall preserve records sufficient to explain what we have done.

We shall not hide transactions merely because disclosure would be embarrassing.

We shall distinguish ordinary expenses from consequential commitments.

We shall protect Sacred Property without pretending that it exists outside civil law.

We shall not mortgage the future casually.

We shall maintain reserves where prudence requires them.

We shall investigate suspected misuse.

We shall correct errors.

We shall protect those who report financial wrongdoing in good faith.

We shall preserve sufficient separation that no single person can quietly control authorization, custody, accounting, and review.

We shall build systems that continue after the current leaders are gone.

And whenever a financial decision is difficult, we shall ask not merely:

> **“Can we spend this money?”**

but:

> **“By what authority, for what purpose, under whose stewardship, with what risk, under what restrictions, with what record, and on whose behalf are we acting?”**
