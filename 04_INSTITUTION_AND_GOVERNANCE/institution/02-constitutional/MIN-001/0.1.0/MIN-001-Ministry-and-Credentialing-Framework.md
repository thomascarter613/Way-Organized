# Ministry and Credentialing Framework
## [[CORP_NAME]]

---

# PREAMBLE

Religious ministry entrusts persons with forms of authority capable of reaching deeply into the lives of others.

A minister may teach.

A spiritual guide may influence conscience.

A chaplain may enter moments of crisis.

A celebrant may preside over significant rites.

A teacher may shape religious understanding.

A pastoral caregiver may receive deeply private information.

A senior religious officer may exercise authority over entire communities.

Such authority should therefore never arise merely because:

- a person desires a title;
- a leader likes the person;
- the person makes substantial donations;
- the person has charisma;
- the person possesses academic credentials;
- the person has served for many years;
- the person claims a spiritual experience;
- the person has gathered followers;
- or an institution needs someone immediately.

Religious authority shall be entrusted deliberately.

Qualification shall precede authorization.

Authorization shall be defined.

Authority shall be bounded.

Credentials shall reflect actual standing.

Supervision shall correspond to responsibility.

Continuing authority shall depend upon continuing fitness.

And no credential shall become a means of manufacturing legal, tax, professional, or governmental status that does not independently exist.

This Framework therefore establishes a ministry system founded upon the principle:

> **Calling may initiate the path to ministry, but calling alone does not complete the institutional discernment necessary to entrust religious authority.**

And:

> **A credential records authority already validly entrusted through the religious order; the paper itself does not create unlimited authority.**

# ARTICLE II
## Foundational Principles of Ministry

### Section 2.1 — Ministry as Trust

Religious ministry is a trust rather than personal property.

### Section 2.2 — No Right to Ministry

No person possesses an inherent right to:

- ordination;
- commissioning;
- licensing;
- appointment;
- religious office;
- leadership;
- public teaching authority.

### Section 2.3 — Human Dignity Distinguished

Denial, suspension, or loss of religious office does not establish lesser human dignity.

### Section 2.4 — Authority According to Competence

The scope of religious authority shall correspond to demonstrated:

- formation;
- competence;
- character;
- responsibility;
- supervision;
- constitutional authorization.

### Section 2.5 — Greater Authority, Greater Accountability

Higher forms of ministry authority shall receive correspondingly greater:

- qualification requirements;
- scrutiny;
- accountability;
- review.

### Section 2.6 — Credential Integrity

A credential shall describe actual ecclesiastical standing accurately.

### Section 2.7 — No Credential Inflation

The Religious Body shall not issue a higher credential merely:

- to create prestige;
- to obtain tax treatment;
- to satisfy an external application;
- to create apparent civil authority;
- to improve fundraising;
- to manufacture professional status.

### Section 2.8 — Continuing Fitness

Initial qualification does not permanently establish fitness.

### Section 2.9 — Office and Credential Distinguished

A person may possess a ministry credential without holding a particular governing office.

### Section 2.10 — Employment Distinguished

A credential does not automatically establish:

- employment;
- salary;
- benefits;
- employee status;
- independent-contractor status.

### Section 2.11 — Corporate Agency Distinguished

A religious credential does not independently authorize a person to:

- sign contracts;
- bind the Corporation;
- incur debt;
- control corporate funds;
- hire employees;
- transfer property.

# ARTICLE III
## Core Terminology

### Section 3.1 — Ministry

**Ministry** means religious service undertaken under the authority or recognition of the Religious Body.

### Section 3.2 — Ministry Candidate

A **Ministry Candidate** is a person formally undergoing discernment or formation for potential religious ministry.

### Section 3.3 — Credential

A **Credential** is an official ecclesiastical record confirming defined religious standing or authority.

### Section 3.4 — Commission

A **Commission** is a defined authorization to perform specified religious functions.

### Section 3.5 — License

A **Ministerial License** is an internal ecclesiastical credential authorizing defined ministry under the Religious Body.

It is not a governmental professional license.

### Section 3.6 — Ordination

**Ordination**, where adopted by the Religious Body, is the highest ordinary general recognition that a person has been entrusted with the principal religious functions assigned by the tradition to ordained ministry.

### Section 3.7 — Endorsement

An **Endorsement** is institutional confirmation that a credentialed minister is recommended or authorized for a particular external ministry context.

### Section 3.8 — Religious Office

A **Religious Office** is a constitutionally or institutionally defined position possessing specified authority.

### Section 3.9 — Appointment

**Appointment** is placement into a particular ministry assignment or office.

### Section 3.10 — Standing

**Ministerial Standing** is the person's current recognized credential status.

# ARTICLE IV
## Ministry Status Architecture

The ministry system shall distinguish at least:

1. **Ministry Aspirant**
2. **Ministry Candidate**
3. **Authorized Religious Worker**
4. **Commissioned Minister**
5. **Licensed Minister**
6. **Ordained Minister**, if ordination is adopted
7. **Specialized Ministry Endorsement**
8. **Religious Officer**
9. **Emeritus Minister**

Not every tradition-specific ministry must use every category.

---

# ARTICLE V
## Ministry Aspirant

### Section 5.1 — Nature

A Ministry Aspirant is a person exploring possible religious vocation before formal candidacy.

### Section 5.2 — No Credential

Aspirant status is not a ministry credential.

### Section 5.3 — Activities

An Aspirant may participate in:

- discernment;
- study;
- supervised service;
- vocational conversation.

### Section 5.4 — No Representation

An Aspirant shall not represent themselves as a credentialed minister of the Religious Body.

---

# ARTICLE VI
## Ministry Candidate

### Section 6.1 — Nature

A Ministry Candidate is formally accepted into preparation for one or more ministry credentials.

### Section 6.2 — Candidate Status

Candidacy does not itself constitute ordination, licensing, or commissioning.

### Section 6.3 — Candidate Responsibilities

Candidates shall:

- participate in formation;
- complete required study;
- comply with safeguarding;
- undergo evaluation;
- disclose material conflicts;
- cooperate with legitimate qualification review.

### Section 6.4 — Candidate Service

A candidate may perform supervised ministry functions expressly delegated to them.

### Section 6.5 — No Guaranteed Credential

Completion of time in candidacy does not guarantee credentialing.

---

# ARTICLE VII
## Authorized Religious Worker

### Section 7.1 — Purpose

The Religious Body may recognize persons authorized to perform limited religious functions without conferring general ministerial standing.

### Section 7.2 — Examples

Functions may include:

- religious education;
- music ministry;
- ritual assistance;
- community service;
- youth ministry;
- visitation;
- administrative ministry;
- specified liturgical functions.

### Section 7.3 — Scope

Authorization shall specify permitted functions.

### Section 7.4 — No Ministerial Representation

Limited authorization shall not be represented as full ministerial credentialing.

---

# ARTICLE VIII
## Commissioned Minister

### Section 8.1 — Nature

A Commissioned Minister is entrusted with a defined field or category of religious ministry.

### Section 8.2 — Possible Commissions

A commission may authorize service such as:

- teaching;
- missionary work;
- chaplaincy;
- spiritual formation;
- contemplative guidance;
- community leadership;
- pastoral visitation;
- religious education;
- charitable ministry.

### Section 8.3 — Defined Scope

Every commission shall identify:

- authorized ministry;
- supervision;
- duration;
- renewal requirements;
- limitations.

### Section 8.4 — Not Automatically Full Ministry

Commissioning shall not be represented as equivalent to the highest general ministry credential unless the Religious Body actually treats it as such.

---

# ARTICLE IX
## Licensed Minister

### Section 9.1 — Nature

A Licensed Minister is a person authorized ecclesiastically to exercise a substantial range of ministerial functions under defined conditions.

### Section 9.2 — Provisional or Developmental Character

The Religious Body may use ministerial licensing as:

- a developmental stage;
- renewable authorization;
- supervised ministry status;
- permanent category for defined ministry.

### Section 9.3 — Internal License

A Ministerial License is issued by the Religious Body.

It does not imply governmental licensure.

### Section 9.4 — Scope

The credential shall state whether the minister may perform functions including:

- teaching;
- worship leadership;
- pastoral ministry;
- rites or ordinances;
- spiritual direction;
- local leadership;
- other recognized functions.

---

# ARTICLE X
## Ordained Minister

### Section 10.1 — Adoption of Ordination

If the Religious Body adopts ordination as a religious institution, an Ordained Minister shall possess the highest ordinary general ministerial standing recognized under this Framework.

### Section 10.2 — Nature

Ordination signifies enduring ecclesiastical recognition of:

- religious vocation;
- formation;
- qualification;
- character;
- authority;
- responsibility.

### Section 10.3 — Ordination Does Not Mean Irrevocable Office

Ordained standing may remain subject to:

- restriction;
- suspension;
- revocation;
- disqualification

through valid process.

### Section 10.4 — No Automatic Governance Seat

Ordination does not automatically confer:

- EC membership;
- PSL eligibility without additional qualification;
- Civil Board membership;
- CCG membership;
- EAC membership.

### Section 10.5 — No Automatic Employment

Ordination does not create an employment relationship.

---

# ARTICLE XI
## Specialized Ministry Credentials

The Religious Body may create specialized credentials including:

- Religious Teacher;
- Chaplain;
- Spiritual Director;
- Pastoral Care Minister;
- Missionary;
- Celebrant;
- Contemplative Guide;
- Youth Minister;
- Community Minister;
- Religious Educator;
- Institutional Chaplain;
- other defined ministries.

### Section 11.1 — Specialized Competence

Each specialized credential shall have written qualification requirements.

### Section 11.2 — Scope

A specialized credential confers only the authority defined for it.

### Section 11.3 — Additional Credential

A specialized endorsement may supplement rather than replace a general ministry credential.

---

# ARTICLE XII
## Religious Office Distinguished From Credential

### Section 12.1 — Credential

A credential answers:

> **What category of religious authority has this person been recognized as qualified to exercise?**

### Section 12.2 — Office

A religious office answers:

> **What institutional role does this person presently hold?**

### Section 12.3 — Assignment

A ministry assignment answers:

> **Where and in what context is the person presently authorized to exercise ministry?**

### Section 12.4 — Employment

Employment answers:

> **What civil work relationship exists with the Corporation?**

### Section 12.5 — Agency

Agency answers:

> **What legal authority does the person possess to bind or represent the Corporation?**

These questions shall not be collapsed.

---

# ARTICLE XIII
## General Ministry Qualifications

A candidate for substantial ministry credentialing shall ordinarily demonstrate:

1. religious commitment;
2. knowledge appropriate to the ministry;
3. moral and ethical fitness;
4. spiritual maturity appropriate to the tradition;
5. understanding of constitutional governance;
6. safeguarding competence;
7. capacity to respect personal boundaries;
8. willingness to receive supervision;
9. ability to distinguish religious office from personal ownership;
10. capacity to work within legitimate authority;
11. capacity to respect lawful civil authority;
12. truthfulness;
13. appropriate communication skills;
14. fitness for the particular ministry sought.

---

# ARTICLE XIV
## Covenant Membership and Ministry

### Section 14.1 — Ordinary Requirement

Substantial ministerial credentials should ordinarily require Covenant Membership.

### Section 14.2 — Exceptions

The EC may establish exceptions for:

- external chaplains;
- visiting ministers;
- scholars;
- specialized collaborators;
- temporary ministry.

### Section 14.3 — No Automatic Credential

Covenant Membership alone does not establish qualification for ministry.

---

# ARTICLE XV
## Vocational Discernment

### Section 15.1 — Personal Discernment

A candidate should examine whether they experience a genuine call or compelling reason toward ministry.

### Section 15.2 — Community Discernment

The Religious Body shall independently discern whether the person should be entrusted with authority.

### Section 15.3 — Dual Recognition

The preferred principle is:

> **personal vocation + communal recognition**

rather than either alone.

### Section 15.4 — No Claim of Calling as Command

A person's assertion that they are spiritually called does not compel the institution to credential them.

---

# ARTICLE XVI
## Admission to Candidacy

Admission to candidacy may require:

1. application;
2. Covenant Membership where required;
3. initial interview;
4. background screening;
5. safeguarding review;
6. references;
7. disclosure of prior ministry history;
8. disclosure of prior credential discipline;
9. preliminary formation plan;
10. approval by the competent credentialing body.

---

# ARTICLE XVII
## Prior Ministry and Credential History

Applicants shall disclose material prior:

- ordinations;
- licenses;
- commissions;
- ministry employment;
- credential suspensions;
- credential revocations;
- disciplinary proceedings;
- safeguarding findings.

### Section 17.1 — No Concealment

Intentional material concealment may itself affect qualification.

### Section 17.2 — Fair Evaluation

Prior adverse action shall be evaluated according to:

- reliability;
- seriousness;
- relevance;
- procedural fairness;
- present fitness.

---

# ARTICLE XVIII
## Background Screening

### Section 18.1 — Purpose

Background screening may be required according to the responsibilities sought.

### Section 18.2 — Sensitive Ministry

Enhanced screening should ordinarily apply to ministry involving:

- minors;
- vulnerable persons;
- pastoral counseling;
- residential ministry;
- financial access;
- confidential records.

### Section 18.3 — No Automatic Rule Without Context

Background information shall be evaluated according to:

- relevance;
- seriousness;
- age of information;
- evidence;
- rehabilitation where relevant;
- safeguarding implications;
- applicable law.

### Section 18.4 — Mandatory Disqualification

SAFE-001 may identify conduct creating mandatory or presumptive disqualification from specified ministries.

---

# ARTICLE XIX
## Formation Program

Ministerial formation shall ordinarily address:

1. religious foundations;
2. doctrine;
3. authoritative sources;
4. history;
5. ethics;
6. spiritual formation;
7. pastoral practice;
8. safeguarding;
9. abuse of spiritual authority;
10. boundaries;
11. constitutional governance;
12. ministry authority;
13. religious pluralism and external relations;
14. conflict;
15. discipline;
16. institutional accountability;
17. civil–ecclesiastical distinctions;
18. practical ministry.

---

# ARTICLE XX
## Individual Formation Plan

Each Ministry Candidate should receive an **Individual Formation Plan** identifying:

- credential sought;
- required study;
- supervised practice;
- mentor;
- evaluations;
- expected competencies;
- anticipated review points;
- unresolved development needs.

### Section 20.1 — Individualization

Equivalent prior learning or experience may be recognized where verified.

### Section 20.2 — No Meaningless Waiver

Requirements shall not be waived merely because a favored candidate seeks accelerated advancement.

---

# ARTICLE XXI
## Academic and Intellectual Formation

The Religious Body may require study in areas including:

- religious texts;
- theology;
- philosophy;
- ethics;
- religious history;
- comparative religion;
- hermeneutics;
- pastoral studies;
- psychology appropriate to ministry;
- sociology of religion;
- nonprofit governance;
- other fields.

### Section 21.1 — Degree Not Automatically Required

A formal academic degree need not be universally required unless the applicable credential standard establishes it.

### Section 21.2 — Competence Required

Absence of a degree does not eliminate the need for demonstrated competence.

---

# ARTICLE XXII
## Spiritual and Character Formation

Formation shall consider capacities such as:

- honesty;
- humility;
- self-awareness;
- emotional maturity;
- accountability;
- responsible use of authority;
- ability to receive criticism;
- ability to maintain boundaries;
- capacity for service;
- resistance to manipulation and exploitation.

### Section 22.1 — No Demand for Perfection

Ministerial fitness does not require human perfection.

### Section 22.2 — Serious Character Concerns

Substantial concerns may justify:

- extended formation;
- restricted credential;
- deferral;
- denial.

---

# ARTICLE XXIII
## Safeguarding Formation

Before substantial ministerial authority is granted, candidates shall receive formation concerning:

- child protection;
- vulnerable adults;
- mandatory reporting interfaces;
- sexual boundaries;
- pastoral power;
- grooming;
- coercive control;
- spiritual abuse;
- confidentiality limits;
- retaliation;
- reporting channels;
- emergency protection.

---

# ARTICLE XXIV
## Constitutional Formation

Candidates shall understand:

- REL-CON-001;
- ECC-CON-001;
- ministry jurisdiction;
- EC authority;
- PSL authority;
- EAC authority;
- CCG authority;
- civil–ecclesiastical boundaries;
- due process;
- protected reporting;
- recusal;
- anti-capture principles.

A minister shall not be permitted to treat the Constitution as an obstacle applicable only to other persons.

---

# ARTICLE XXV
## Supervised Ministry

### Section 25.1 — Requirement

Substantial ministry credentials should ordinarily require meaningful supervised practice.

### Section 25.2 — Supervisor

The supervisor shall be qualified for the ministry being evaluated.

### Section 25.3 — Evaluation

Supervision should assess:

- competence;
- judgment;
- interpersonal conduct;
- boundaries;
- teaching;
- pastoral practice;
- reliability;
- accountability.

### Section 25.4 — No Exploitative Apprenticeship

Supervised ministry shall not become a pretext for extracting indefinite uncompensated labor.

---

# ARTICLE XXVI
## Mentorship

### Section 26.1 — Mentor

Candidates may be assigned a ministry mentor.

### Section 26.2 — Mentor Is Not Sole Gatekeeper

A mentor's recommendation is relevant but shall not automatically determine credentialing.

### Section 26.3 — Conflict

A mentor with a material conflict shall disclose it.

---

# ARTICLE XXVII
## Examination

### Section 27.1 — Purpose

Examination determines whether required competencies are actually present.

### Section 27.2 — Forms

Examination may include:

- written assessment;
- oral examination;
- teaching demonstration;
- case analysis;
- supervised practice;
- portfolio;
- doctrinal examination;
- practical ministry assessment.

### Section 27.3 — Higher Credential, Higher Examination

Ordination or equivalent senior credentialing should receive more substantial examination than limited authorization.

---

# ARTICLE XXVIII
## Examination Panel

### Section 28.1 — Composition

A substantial ministerial examination should ordinarily involve at least three qualified evaluators.

### Section 28.2 — Independence

The panel should not consist solely of persons personally dependent upon the candidate.

### Section 28.3 — Expertise

Panel composition should match the credential sought.

### Section 28.4 — Record

Material examination outcomes shall be documented.

---

# ARTICLE XXIX
## Evaluation Standard

The examination process shall determine whether the candidate:

1. satisfies required knowledge;
2. understands doctrine;
3. understands ministry limits;
4. can reason responsibly;
5. demonstrates practical competence;
6. demonstrates safeguarding understanding;
7. demonstrates constitutional understanding;
8. demonstrates ethical fitness;
9. possesses appropriate judgment.

---

# ARTICLE XXX
## Possible Candidacy Outcomes

The credentialing body may determine:

1. **Approved**
2. **Approved With Conditions**
3. **Additional Formation Required**
4. **Deferred**
5. **Not Approved**
6. **Disqualified for Specified Credential**

### Section 30.1 — Reasons

Material adverse outcomes should ordinarily include sufficient explanation for meaningful review.

### Section 30.2 — No Public Humiliation

Credential denial shall not be announced publicly merely to embarrass the candidate.

---

# ARTICLE XXXI
## Credentialing Authority

### Section 31.1 — EC Ultimate Authority

The Ecclesiastical Council retains ultimate ecclesiastical authority over ministry credentials.

### Section 31.2 — Credentialing Body

Routine administration may be delegated to a **Ministerial Formation and Credentialing Council**, abbreviated **MFCC**.

### Section 31.3 — EC Reserved Matters

The EC shall retain direct approval or confirmation for:

- ordination;
- the highest general ministry credential;
- senior religious officers;
- PSL credential standing;
- restoration following the most serious credential revocations.

### Section 31.4 — No PSL Unilateral Credentialing

The PSL may recommend and participate but shall not possess unrestricted unilateral power to create ministerial credentials.

---

# ARTICLE XXXII
## Ministerial Formation and Credentialing Council

### Section 32.1 — Establishment

The EC should establish an MFCC when the volume of candidates justifies a permanent credentialing body.

### Section 32.2 — Composition

The mature MFCC should ordinarily contain five members.

### Section 32.3 — Competence

Collectively it should possess competence in:

- religious teaching;
- ministry;
- formation;
- safeguarding;
- ethics;
- governance.

### Section 32.4 — Independence

The MFCC shall not become merely the personal credentialing office of the PSL.

### Section 32.5 — Charter

A future MFCC-001 may establish detailed operations if organizational scale warrants.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIII
## Credential Decision

A credential decision shall identify:

1. person;
2. credential;
3. effective date;
4. scope;
5. restrictions;
6. duration;
7. renewal requirements;
8. supervising authority;
9. issuing authority;
10. credential identifier.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIV
## Commissioning

### Section 34.1 — Formal Act

Commissioning should ordinarily occur through a public or appropriately witnessed religious act.

### Section 34.2 — Meaning

Commissioning recognizes and authorizes a defined ministry.

### Section 34.3 — Instrument

A Commissioning Instrument shall identify the actual authority entrusted.

---

# ARTICLE XXXV
## Licensing

### Section 35.1 — Ecclesiastical License

A ministerial license is an internal religious authorization.

### Section 35.2 — Duration

A license may be:

- annual;
- multi-year;
- continuing subject to periodic review.

### Section 35.3 — Renewal

Where licenses expire, renewal shall depend upon continuing fitness rather than automatic payment of a fee.

### Section 35.4 — No Governmental Representation

Official documents shall not imply that an ecclesiastical ministerial license constitutes governmental professional licensure.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVI
## Ordination

### Section 36.1 — Extraordinary Recognition

Ordination should not be used merely as an administrative shortcut.

### Section 36.2 — Minimum Process

Ordination should ordinarily require:

1. Covenant Membership;
2. substantial formation;
3. supervised ministry;
4. examination;
5. safeguarding clearance;
6. character review;
7. MFCC recommendation;
8. EC approval;
9. formal ordination act;
10. permanent record.

### Section 36.3 — EC Threshold

Ordination should ordinarily require at least two-thirds approval of the EC members participating and eligible to vote unless a higher doctrinal rule is adopted.

### Section 36.4 — PSL Participation

The PSL should ordinarily participate in the ordination act but cannot ordain contrary to a valid EC decision.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVII
## Ordination Instrument

The formal record should identify:

- minister;
- credential identifier;
- date;
- religious body;
- authority;
- ministers or officers participating;
- scope;
- status;
- signatures or authentication.

### Section 37.1 — Permanent Record

Ordination records shall receive permanent retention.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVIII
## Credential Identifier

Every substantial credential shall receive a stable identifier.

Recommended format:

> **MIN-CRED-YYYY-#####**

The identifier should survive:

- name changes;
- local-community transfers;
- role changes;
- credential reissuance.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIX
## Credential Certificate

A certificate may be issued as evidence of standing.

It should identify:

- legal or recorded name;
- credential category;
- issuing Religious Body;
- effective date;
- credential identifier;
- current limitations where appropriate;
- verification mechanism.

### Section 39.1 — Certificate Is Evidence

Possession of a paper certificate does not override the canonical Credential Registry.

---

# ARTICLE XL
## Credential Registry

### Section 40.1 — Canonical Registry

The Religious Body shall maintain a canonical **Ministerial Credential Registry**.

### Section 40.2 — Registry Fields

The Registry should record:

- stable minister identifier;
- credential identifier;
- credential category;
- effective date;
- current standing;
- restrictions;
- expiration where applicable;
- endorsements;
- credentialing authority;
- status history.

### Section 40.3 — Sensitive Information

Detailed disciplinary information shall remain in appropriately restricted records rather than the ordinary credential record.

---

# ARTICLE XLI
## Credential Standing

A credential may have one of the following statuses:

1. **Candidate**
2. **Active**
3. **Active With Conditions**
4. **Inactive**
5. **Restricted**
6. **Protectively Suspended**
7. **Disciplinarily Suspended**
8. **Revoked**
9. **Resigned**
10. **Retired**
11. **Emeritus**
12. **Deceased**

---

# ARTICLE XLII
## Active Standing

A minister is in Active Standing when:

- credential remains valid;
- required review is current;
- safeguarding requirements are current;
- no restriction or suspension prevents ordinary ministry.

---

# ARTICLE XLIII
## Active With Conditions

Conditions may require:

- supervision;
- limited ministry scope;
- continuing education;
- specified mentorship;
- periodic reporting;
- another proportionate requirement.

Conditions shall be documented.

---

# ARTICLE XLIV
## Inactive Standing

Inactive standing may apply where a minister:

- voluntarily pauses ministry;
- is not presently serving;
- has allowed renewable credentials to lapse administratively;
- is temporarily unavailable.

Inactive status is not itself disciplinary.

---

# ARTICLE XLV
## Restricted Standing

A credential may remain valid while specified functions are restricted.

Examples include prohibition or limitation concerning:

- minors;
- counseling;
- finances;
- independent preaching;
- sacraments or rites;
- supervision of others;
- public representation.

---

# ARTICLE XLVI
## Protective Suspension

### Section 46.1 — Nature

Protective suspension temporarily removes or limits ministry authority before final adjudication where credible risk requires protection.

### Section 46.2 — Not Final Finding

Protective suspension is not by itself a finding of misconduct.

### Section 46.3 — Review

Protective suspension shall receive prompt review under DISC-001, SAFE-001, or ACCOUNT-001.

---

# ARTICLE XLVII
## Disciplinary Suspension

Disciplinary suspension follows a valid accountability or disciplinary determination.

The suspension order shall identify:

- duration;
- conditions;
- prohibited functions;
- restoration requirements;
- review rights.

---

# ARTICLE XLVIII
## Revocation

### Section 48.1 — Nature

Revocation terminates the religious body's recognition of the credential.

### Section 48.2 — Serious Action

Revocation requires substantial due process.

### Section 48.3 — Possible Grounds

Grounds may include:

- serious abuse;
- exploitation;
- predatory conduct;
- severe safeguarding misconduct;
- serious fraud;
- persistent abuse of spiritual authority;
- fundamental doctrinal disqualification where doctrine is relevant to credential;
- serious constitutional defiance;
- deliberate credential fraud;
- other conduct fundamentally incompatible with ministry.

---

# ARTICLE XLIX
## Permanent Disqualification

Certain findings may establish permanent ineligibility for:

- all ministry;
- specified ministry;
- ministry involving vulnerable persons;
- senior religious leadership;
- pastoral counseling;
- fiduciary religious functions.

### Section 49.1 — Restoration Distinguished

Spiritual or relational restoration does not automatically remove permanent ministry disqualification.

---

# ARTICLE L
## Resignation of Credentials

### Section 50.1 — Voluntary Resignation

A minister may resign a credential.

### Section 50.2 — No Permission Required

The Religious Body shall not claim permanent ownership of the minister's religious identity.

### Section 50.3 — Pending Proceedings

Credential resignation does not automatically terminate:

- safeguarding review;
- misconduct investigation;
- institutional findings;
- historical record.

### Section 50.4 — Accurate Record

A credential resigned during an unresolved serious matter shall not be falsely represented as having ended in ordinary good standing if material proceedings were pending.

---

# ARTICLE LI
## Retirement

A minister may retire from active ministry while retaining an appropriate historical credential status.

Retirement does not automatically confer Emeritus status.

---

# ARTICLE LII
## Emeritus Recognition

### Section 52.1 — Nature

Emeritus status is honorary recognition of past service.

### Section 52.2 — No Automatic Authority

Emeritus status does not automatically confer:

- governance vote;
- executive authority;
- pastoral jurisdiction;
- employment;
- authority over current leaders.

### Section 52.3 — Fitness

Emeritus recognition should not be granted merely to conceal circumstances that would otherwise require disciplinary treatment.

---

# ARTICLE LIII
## Continuing Formation

Credentialed ministers shall engage in continuing formation appropriate to their ministry.

Subjects may include:

- doctrine;
- ethics;
- safeguarding;
- governance;
- pastoral care;
- professional development;
- cultural competence;
- ministry practice.

---

# ARTICLE LIV
## Periodic Ministerial Review

### Section 54.1 — Review Cycle

Substantial ministerial credentials should receive formal review at least every three years.

### Section 54.2 — Review Areas

Review may consider:

- continued religious fitness;
- ethical conduct;
- ministry effectiveness;
- safeguarding compliance;
- continuing education;
- complaints or concerns;
- constitutional compliance;
- supervision.

### Section 54.3 — Higher Office

Senior leaders may receive more frequent or more comprehensive review.

---

# ARTICLE LV
## Annual Standing Confirmation

The credential administration function should ordinarily confirm annually:

- current contact information;
- current ministry assignment;
- safeguarding status;
- material conflicts;
- credential status.

This administrative confirmation is distinct from comprehensive ministerial review.

---

# ARTICLE LVI
## Continuing Safeguarding Compliance

Credentialed ministers shall maintain applicable:

- training;
- screening;
- reporting knowledge;
- role-specific safeguarding qualifications.

Failure to maintain mandatory safeguards may result in automatic temporary restriction from affected ministry functions pending correction.

---

# ARTICLE LVII
## Ministerial Ethics

Credentialed ministers shall comply with a future **Ministerial Ethics Code** or equivalent provisions of MIN-COV-001.

Core principles shall include:

1. respect for dignity;
2. truthful representation;
3. protection of vulnerable persons;
4. sexual and relational boundaries;
5. financial integrity;
6. confidentiality;
7. conflict disclosure;
8. non-retaliation;
9. appropriate use of spiritual authority;
10. respect for civil law;
11. respect for constitutional authority;
12. avoidance of exploitation.

---

# ARTICLE LVIII
## Abuse of Spiritual Authority

A minister shall not materially misuse religious authority to:

- control conscience for improper purposes;
- obtain sexual or romantic access;
- extract money;
- extract unreasonable labor;
- suppress reporting;
- isolate persons improperly;
- compel personal loyalty;
- threaten invented spiritual consequences for personal benefit;
- obstruct lawful accountability.

Legitimate religious teaching, moral standards, correction, and discipline remain permissible when exercised within proper authority and process.

---

# ARTICLE LIX
## Sexual and Romantic Boundaries

### Section 59.1 — Power Differential

Ministers shall recognize that pastoral relationships can create substantial power differentials.

### Section 59.2 — Exploitation

A minister shall not exploit:

- pastoral dependence;
- counseling;
- spiritual direction;
- confession-like disclosure;
- supervisory authority

for sexual or romantic benefit.

### Section 59.3 — Detailed Policy

SAFE-001 and the Ministerial Ethics Code shall establish specific boundaries.

---

# ARTICLE LX
## Financial Integrity

A minister shall not use religious office to:

- divert donations;
- obtain improper gifts;
- conceal conflicts;
- borrow improperly from persons under pastoral influence;
- use restricted funds personally;
- pressure vulnerable persons financially.

### Section 60.1 — Honoraria and Gifts

The Corporation may establish policies governing:

- honoraria;
- gifts;
- love offerings;
- speaking fees;
- reimbursements.

---

# ARTICLE LXI
## Confidentiality and Pastoral Information

### Section 61.1 — Respect for Confidentiality

Ministers shall preserve legitimate pastoral confidentiality.

### Section 61.2 — No Absolute Promise

Ministers shall not promise absolute confidentiality where disclosure may become:

- legally required;
- necessary for safeguarding;
- necessary to prevent serious harm;
- otherwise required by governing policy.

### Section 61.3 — No Concealment

Pastoral confidentiality shall not be used knowingly to facilitate continuing abuse.

---

# ARTICLE LXII
## Professional Boundaries

A religious credential does not authorize a minister to present themselves falsely as a:

- physician;
- psychologist;
- licensed counselor;
- attorney;
- financial adviser;
- social worker;
- other regulated professional

unless independently qualified.

### Section 62.1 — Referral

Ministers shall refer matters beyond their competence appropriately.

---

# ARTICLE LXIII
## Civil Marriage Authority

### Section 63.1 — Separate Question

The Religious Body may determine ecclesiastically who is authorized to officiate its religious marriage rites.

Civil authority to solemnize a legally recognized marriage is a separate matter governed by applicable civil law.

### Section 63.2 — No Guarantee

Issuance of an internal religious credential does not constitute a guarantee that the credential holder satisfies the civil requirements of any jurisdiction.

### Section 63.3 — Verification

A minister intending to solemnize a civil marriage is responsible, with appropriate institutional assistance, for verifying applicable legal requirements.

---

# ARTICLE LXIV
## Federal Tax Classification

### Section 64.1 — Separate Legal Determination

The Religious Body may identify persons as ministers according to its religion.

Federal, state, or local tax treatment is determined independently under applicable law.

### Section 64.2 — No Tax-Motivated Credential Inflation

A person shall not be ordained, licensed, or commissioned merely to obtain a tax benefit.

### Section 64.3 — Compensation Advice

Ministers receiving compensation should obtain qualified tax guidance appropriate to their circumstances.

---

# ARTICLE LXV
## Housing and Ministerial Benefits

Any housing allowance, parsonage arrangement, retirement benefit, or other ministerial compensation arrangement shall be established only:

- through proper corporate authority;
- according to applicable law;
- with appropriate documentation.

A ministry credential alone does not create entitlement to a particular tax treatment or benefit.

---

# ARTICLE LXVI
## Employment Status

### Section 66.1 — Separate Determination

Whether a minister is:

- employee;
- independent contractor;
- volunteer;
- officeholder

shall depend upon the actual relationship and applicable law.

### Section 66.2 — Credential Does Not Decide Employment Status

The credentialing body shall not make legal employment classifications merely by changing religious titles.

---

# ARTICLE LXVII
## Corporate Authority

A credentialed minister shall not bind the Corporation unless separately authorized through:

- civil office;
- employment authority;
- written delegation;
- Board resolution;
- applicable corporate policy.

### Section 67.1 — Apparent Authority Risk

Public titles and role descriptions should be administered carefully to avoid misleading third parties concerning corporate authority.

---

# ARTICLE LXVIII
## Ministry Assignment

### Section 68.1 — Separate Assignment

A credential establishes standing.

A ministry assignment establishes where that standing is exercised institutionally.

### Section 68.2 — Assignment Instrument

Material assignments should identify:

- ministry;
- role;
- supervisor;
- authority;
- duration;
- reporting relationship.

### Section 68.3 — Removal From Assignment

Removal from a particular assignment does not necessarily revoke the person's credential.

---

# ARTICLE LXIX
## Local Ministry Authority

Local communities may be authorized to:

- recommend candidates;
- supervise candidates;
- appoint local ministry assignments;
- evaluate local ministers

within authority delegated by the EC.

### Section 69.1 — Credentialing Remains Common

A local community shall not independently issue credentials represented as credentials of the entire Religious Body unless authorized.

---

# ARTICLE LXX
## Transfer Between Local Communities

Credentialed ministers may transfer ministry assignments without losing general credential standing where:

- credential remains active;
- no restriction applies;
- receiving community accepts the assignment;
- required records transfer.

### Section 70.1 — No Status Laundering

Transfer shall not erase:

- safeguarding restrictions;
- disciplinary status;
- credential conditions.

---

# ARTICLE LXXI
## Ministers From Other Religious Bodies

### Section 71.1 — External Credentials

The Religious Body may recognize prior external formation or credentials.

### Section 71.2 — No Automatic Reciprocity

External ordination, licensing, or commissioning does not automatically create internal standing.

### Section 71.3 — Review

Recognition may require:

- doctrinal compatibility;
- credential verification;
- ministry history;
- safeguarding review;
- formation supplementation;
- constitutional orientation.

---

# ARTICLE LXXII
## Transfer of Ministerial Standing Into the Religious Body

Possible outcomes include:

1. full recognition;
2. provisional recognition;
3. equivalent credential;
4. specialized recognition;
5. additional formation required;
6. denial of recognition.

### Section 72.1 — Historical Integrity

The person's external credential history shall remain distinguished from credentials issued internally.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIII
## Transfer Out

A minister leaving for another religious institution may request appropriate confirmation of:

- credential history;
- current standing;
- relevant public or lawfully disclosable status.

### Section 73.1 — No Misrepresentation

The Religious Body shall not knowingly provide a false letter of good standing.

### Section 73.2 — Safeguarding

Serious substantiated safeguarding restrictions may be disclosed where lawful, truthful, and reasonably necessary for protection.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIV
## Letters of Good Standing

### Section 74.1 — Nature

A Letter of Good Standing confirms ministerial status as of a specified date.

### Section 74.2 — Verification

It should identify:

- credential;
- current standing;
- issuing authority;
- verification contact.

### Section 74.3 — No False Silence

A letter shall not state unrestricted good standing where a material credential restriction is presently operative.

---

# ARTICLE LXXV
## Chaplaincy Endorsement

### Section 75.1 — Separate Endorsement

External chaplaincy may require institutional endorsement in addition to general ministry standing.

### Section 75.2 — Endorsement Standard

Endorsement may consider:

- credential;
- formation;
- institutional representation;
- professional environment;
- external requirements;
- continuing accountability.

### Section 75.3 — Withdrawal

Endorsement may be withdrawn independently from general credential standing where the person no longer satisfies endorsement requirements.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVI
## Specialized External Ministries

Similar endorsements may be established for:

- military chaplaincy;
- hospital chaplaincy;
- prison ministry;
- hospice ministry;
- campus ministry;
- disaster-response ministry;
- institutional spiritual care.

External organizations retain authority over their own qualification requirements.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVII
## Ministers Serving Independently

### Section 77.1 — Independent Ministry

A credentialed minister may, where authorized, conduct ministry not directly operated by the Corporation.

### Section 77.2 — Representation

The minister shall clearly distinguish:

- personal ministry;
- affiliated ministry;
- official ministry of [[CORP_NAME]].

### Section 77.3 — Credential Accountability

Independent service does not eliminate applicable credential ethics.

### Section 77.4 — Corporate Liability

Credentialing does not automatically make every act of an independently serving minister an act of the Corporation.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVIII
## Affiliated Ministries

Where another ministry seeks formal affiliation, the relationship shall identify:

- credential recognition;
- supervision;
- branding;
- governance;
- safeguarding;
- financial independence;
- authority;
- termination.

Affiliation shall not be inferred merely because a credentialed minister leads the external ministry.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIX
## Ministerial Complaints

Concerns concerning ministers may be submitted through:

- ordinary supervisory channels;
- grievance systems;
- safeguarding channels;
- EAC channels;
- credentialing channels

according to seriousness and jurisdiction.

### Section 79.1 — No Single Gatekeeper

A minister shall not control the only complaint channel concerning themselves.

---

# ARTICLE LXXX
## Credentialing and Accountability

### Section 80.1 — EAC Findings

Valid EAC findings materially affecting ministerial fitness shall be referred to the credentialing authority.

### Section 80.2 — Weight

The credentialing authority shall give substantial appropriate weight to final EAC findings.

### Section 80.3 — Distinct Question

The EAC may establish misconduct.

The credentialing authority determines credential consequences where that authority has been assigned separately.

### Section 80.4 — No Relitigation Without Basis

Credential review shall not become an unrestricted retrial of properly adjudicated facts.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXI
## Credential Restriction Pending Investigation

Where credible allegations raise substantial ministry risk, temporary credential restrictions may be imposed through authorized protective procedure.

Restrictions shall identify:

- reason;
- scope;
- effective date;
- review process.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXII
## Due Process for Credential Sanctions

Before substantial final credential restriction, suspension, or revocation, the minister shall ordinarily receive:

1. notice;
2. governing standard;
3. factual basis;
4. meaningful opportunity to respond;
5. impartial decision-maker;
6. reasoned determination;
7. appeal information.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIII
## Credential Appeals

### Section 83.1 — Right

Serious credential sanctions shall be appealable under DISC-001.

### Section 83.2 — Grounds

Appeal may address:

- jurisdiction;
- procedural error;
- material factual error;
- disqualifying conflict;
- disproportionate sanction;
- constitutional violation;
- newly discovered material evidence.

### Section 83.3 — No Self-Appeal

The original final decision-maker shall not be the sole appellate authority.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIV
## Restoration of Credentials

### Section 84.1 — Possible Restoration

Some suspended or revoked credentials may be eligible for restoration.

### Section 84.2 — Not Guaranteed

Restoration is not a right merely because:

- time has passed;
- apology occurred;
- forgiveness was extended;
- the person desires ministry again.

### Section 84.3 — Evaluation

Restoration may consider:

- original conduct;
- harm;
- accountability;
- changed circumstances;
- safeguarding;
- formation;
- current competence;
- trust;
- recurrence risk.

### Section 84.4 — Permanent Disqualification

A finding of permanent disqualification is not subject to ordinary restoration.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXV
## Restoration Process

Where restoration is permitted, it may include:

1. application;
2. review of prior record;
3. updated background review;
4. evidence of corrective action;
5. formation;
6. evaluation;
7. supervised reentry;
8. conditions;
9. EC approval where required.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVI
## Recredentialing After Resignation

A former minister who voluntarily resigned credentials may apply again.

The process may consider:

- prior standing;
- time away;
- current doctrine;
- current safeguarding standards;
- current competency;
- reasons for resignation.

Prior credentialing does not guarantee automatic restoration.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVII
## Credential Fraud

Credential fraud includes knowingly:

- fabricating ordination;
- altering certificates;
- using another person's credential;
- misrepresenting standing;
- concealing revocation;
- falsely claiming authorization from [[CORP_NAME]].

Credential fraud may constitute serious misconduct.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVIII
## Public Credential Verification

The Religious Body should provide a reliable method through which appropriate parties can verify whether a substantial credential is:

- active;
- inactive;
- restricted;
- suspended;
- revoked.

### Section 88.1 — Privacy

Verification need not disclose confidential reasons.

### Section 88.2 — Accuracy

Public verification shall not describe a suspended or revoked credential as active.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIX

Credential records shall include as appropriate:

The Religious Body shall ordinarily retain permanently:

- ordination records;

# ARTICLE XCI

Credential records shall not be altered deceptively to:

- improve a minister's apparent history;

Corrections shall preserve appropriate traceability.

# ARTICLE XCII

# ARTICLE XCIII
## Use of Titles

### Section 93.1 — Authorized Titles

The EC shall determine which religious titles correspond to credentials and offices.

### Section 93.2 — Clarity

Titles should not imply authority the person does not possess.

### Section 93.3 — Professional Titles

A religious title shall not be presented as a regulated professional title where that would be misleading.

### Section 93.4 — Former Titles

Former officeholders may use historical titles only according to applicable policy.

---

# ARTICLE XCIV
## Honorary Titles

### Section 94.1 — Permitted

The Religious Body may confer honorary recognition.

### Section 94.2 — No Authority

An honorary title shall not confer:

- ministerial standing;
- religious office;
- governance authority;
- professional qualification;
- corporate authority.

### Section 94.3 — Clarity

Honorary status shall be labeled clearly.

---

# ARTICLE XCV
## Ministerial Compensation

### Section 95.1 — Credential Does Not Set Pay

Credentialing bodies shall not ordinarily determine compensation.

### Section 95.2 — Civil Authority

Compensation shall be established through proper corporate processes.

### Section 95.3 — Conflict

A minister shall not participate in the final determination of their own compensation where prohibited by governing rules.

---

# ARTICLE XCVI
## Volunteer Ministry

A person may possess a valid ministry credential while serving without compensation.

Volunteer status does not reduce:

- ethical duties;
- safeguarding duties;
- credential accountability.

---

# ARTICLE XCVII
## Ministerial Intellectual Property

Ministry credentials do not automatically determine ownership of:

- sermons;
- teachings;
- books;
- curricula;
- recordings;
- liturgical materials;
- software;
- artistic works.

Ownership shall be determined through:

- applicable law;
- employment terms;
- written agreements;
- intellectual-property policy.

---

# ARTICLE XCVIII
## Ministerial Political Activity

Credentialed ministers acting personally shall distinguish personal political activity from official institutional religious communication.

Nothing in ministerial authority authorizes use of the Corporation in violation of applicable federal tax restrictions.

---

# ARTICLE XCIX
## Candidates Who Are Related to Leaders

### Section 99.1 — No Automatic Disqualification

Family relationship to a leader does not automatically disqualify a candidate.

### Section 99.2 — Heightened Conflict Review

Where a candidate is closely related to:

- PSL;
- Founder;
- EC member;
- credentialing officer

the process shall include heightened independence.

### Section 99.3 — No Dynasty by Default

Ministerial authority does not pass through family inheritance.

---

# ARTICLE C
## Founder and Credentialing

### Section 100.1 — Founder May Be Credentialed

The Founder may receive or hold a religious credential if otherwise qualified.

### Section 100.2 — Founder Does Not Automatically Credential Others

Founder status alone does not create permanent unilateral credentialing authority.

### Section 100.3 — Founding Period

Temporary Founder involvement shall be governed by ECC-CON-001 and FOUND-TRANS-001.

---

# ARTICLE CI
## PSL and Credentialing

### Section 101.1 — Teaching and Formation Role

The PSL should ordinarily play a substantial role in ministerial formation.

### Section 101.2 — Recommendation

The PSL may recommend candidates.

### Section 101.3 — No Sole Control

The PSL shall not possess unilateral final authority over all substantial credentials.

### Section 101.4 — PSL Credential Accountability

The PSL's own credential standing remains subject to the same constitutional accountability principles applicable to senior leadership.

---

# ARTICLE CII
## EC Members and Credentialing

Where an EC member is personally affected by a credential decision, applicable conflict and recusal rules apply.

An EC member shall not use Council office to secure credentials improperly for:

- themselves;
- family;
- close associates.

---

# ARTICLE CIII
## Credentialing-Body Conflicts

Credentialing decision-makers shall disclose conflicts involving:

- close family;
- employment;
- financial relationship;
- supervisory dependence;
- personal animosity;
- candidate sponsorship;
- prior disciplinary involvement.

Material conflicts require recusal.

---

# ARTICLE CIV
## Minority Evaluation

Where a substantial credentialing panel disagrees, a minority evaluator may preserve a written concern.

### Section 104.1 — No Automatic Veto

Minority concern does not automatically defeat credentialing.

### Section 104.2 — Serious Safeguarding Concern

A material safeguarding objection shall not be overridden casually by majority vote.

---

# ARTICLE CV
## Candidate Access to Decision Information

A candidate receiving a material adverse credential decision should ordinarily receive:

- outcome;
- principal basis;
- unmet requirements;
- reapplication path where available;
- appeal rights.

Confidential third-party information may be protected.

---

# ARTICLE CVI
## Reapplication

A candidate denied a credential may ordinarily reapply after:

- additional formation;
- remediation;
- changed circumstances;
- applicable waiting period.

Some disqualifications may be permanent for specified ministry.

---

# ARTICLE CVII
## Ministerial Leave of Absence

A credentialed minister may request temporary leave because of:

- health;
- family;
- study;
- sabbatical;
- vocational discernment;
- another substantial reason.

Leave shall specify whether credential standing remains active or inactive.

---

# ARTICLE CVIII
## Incapacity

Temporary or permanent incapacity may affect:

- ministry assignment;
- office;
- credential status

differently.

Medical or other sensitive information shall be handled appropriately.

Credential status shall not be revoked merely because a person becomes disabled where the credential itself remains meaningful and no essential qualification has been lost.

---

# ARTICLE CIX
## Death

Upon death:

- active ministry assignments end;
- credential status becomes historical;
- permanent credential records remain preserved.

Posthumous discipline should ordinarily occur only where necessary for:

- safeguarding;
- historical truth;
- institutional accountability.

---

# ARTICLE CX
## Ministry Across Multiple Jurisdictions

A credential issued by [[CORP_NAME]] shall not be represented as automatically satisfying:

- foreign law;
- another state's law;
- military requirements;
- hospital credentialing;
- prison requirements;
- professional licensing rules;
- another denomination's standards.

External eligibility must be verified separately.

# ARTICLE CXI
## Foreign and Cross-Border Ministry

- immigration;
- work authorization;

- financial controls.

# ARTICLE CXII
## Ministry Involving Minors

Additional requirements may include:

- background screening;
- safeguarding education;

- two-adult practices;
- transportation rules;
- communication rules;
- reporting obligations.

# ARTICLE CXIII
## Spiritual Direction and Pastoral Counseling

The Religious Body may authorize spiritual direction or pastoral counseling as religious functions.

### Section 113.1 — Scope

Religious pastoral care shall be distinguished from regulated mental-health treatment where applicable.

### Section 113.2 — Competence

Ministers shall remain within their actual competence.

### Section 113.3 — Referral

Professional referral shall occur when appropriate.

### Section 113.4 — Power and Boundaries

Pastoral dependence shall receive heightened ethical safeguards.

---

# ARTICLE CXIV
## Sacramental, Ritual, or Ceremonial Authority

Where the developing religious tradition recognizes rites, sacraments, ceremonies, or equivalent sacred acts, doctrine shall determine:

- who may preside;
- required credential;
- delegation;
- validity;
- records.

No general assumption shall be made that every credential authorizes every rite.

---

# ARTICLE CXV
## Teaching Authority

The EC shall define levels of teaching authority.

Possible categories include:

1. personal religious reflection;
2. authorized teaching;
3. institutional teaching;
4. Formal Doctrine;
5. Foundational Principle.

### Section 115.1 — Ministerial Speech

A minister's sermon or teaching does not automatically become Formal Doctrine.

### Section 115.2 — Misrepresentation

A minister shall not represent personal theological opinion as binding institutional doctrine where it is not.

---

# ARTICLE CXVI
## Ministry Supervision

Every significant ministry assignment shall identify an appropriate supervisory relationship.

Supervision shall consider:

- doctrinal accountability;
- ministry practice;
- ethical conduct;
- safeguarding;
- performance;
- continuing formation.

No senior minister should remain wholly unsupervised merely because of prestige.

---

# ARTICLE CXVII
## Supervision of the PSL

The PSL is supervised constitutionally rather than through an ordinary employee-minister chain of command.

Relevant institutions include:

- EC;
- EAC;
- CCG;
- Civil Board within civil jurisdiction;
- CMA within reserved powers.

---

# ARTICLE CXVIII
## Ministry Teams

Where ministers operate collectively:

- responsibilities shall be defined;
- reporting relationships shall be clear;
- safeguarding shall remain individual and institutional;
- collective ministry shall not eliminate personal accountability.

---

# ARTICLE CXIX
## Delegation

A credentialed minister may delegate only authority that:

- is delegable;
- remains within the minister's own jurisdiction;
- is entrusted to a qualified person.

Delegation does not eliminate supervisory responsibility.

---

# ARTICLE CXX
## Unauthorized Ministry

The Religious Body may direct a person to cease representing themselves as an authorized minister where:

- no credential exists;
- credential expired;
- credential is suspended;
- claimed authority exceeds actual standing.

---

# ARTICLE CXXI
## Independent Religious Expression

Nothing in this Framework claims ownership over a former minister's personal religious beliefs or speech after departure.

The Religious Body may nevertheless protect against false claims that the person currently represents or is credentialed by [[CORP_NAME]].

---

# ARTICLE CXXII
## Credential Portability

Credentials are ecclesiastical recognitions of [[CORP_NAME]].

They are not property that another religious body must recognize.

A receiving institution may determine independently whether to recognize them.

---

# ARTICLE CXXIII
## Credential Fees

### Section 123.1 — Administrative Fees

Reasonable administrative fees may be charged where appropriate.

### Section 123.2 — No Sale of Ministry

Payment shall not purchase approval.

### Section 123.3 — Hardship

Financial hardship should not automatically prevent an otherwise qualified person from pursuing ministry.

---

# ARTICLE CXXIV
## Donations by Candidates

Candidate donations shall not influence credential decisions.

Material donations by a candidate or close related party shall be disclosed where they create a reasonable appearance of influence.

---

# ARTICLE CXXV
## Credentialing Metrics

The Religious Body shall not evaluate credentialing success merely by:

- number ordained;
- speed of credentialing;
- ministry growth;
- revenue generated.

Quality measures should include:

- candidate preparation;
- safeguarding quality;
- retention of healthy ministers;
- accountability;
- community trust;
- credential integrity.

---

# ARTICLE CXXVI
## Periodic System Review

At least every three years, the EC should evaluate:

1. whether credential categories remain useful;
2. whether requirements correspond to actual authority;
3. whether formation is sufficient;
4. whether examinations are meaningful;
5. whether safeguarding works;
6. whether credentialing has become politicized;
7. whether credentials are being inflated;
8. whether transfer procedures work;
9. whether records are reliable;
10. whether ministers understand civil–ecclesiastical distinctions;
11. whether restoration practices remain appropriate;
12. whether excessive bureaucracy has developed.

---

# ARTICLE CXXVII
## Ministry-System Failure Modes

The Religious Body shall guard against:

### 1. Credential Mill

Titles are issued rapidly without meaningful formation.

### 2. Founder Patronage

Credentials become rewards for personal loyalty.

### 3. Academic Elitism

Degrees substitute entirely for spiritual, ethical, and practical fitness.

### 4. Anti-Intellectual Credentialing

Claims of calling substitute entirely for formation and competence.

### 5. Celebrity Ministry

Charisma substitutes for accountability.

### 6. Tax Credentialing

Religious titles are manufactured primarily to seek legal or tax advantages.

### 7. Hidden Restriction

A minister is publicly represented as unrestricted while substantial internal restrictions exist.

### 8. Quiet Transfer

A minister facing serious misconduct is moved elsewhere rather than investigated.

### 9. Permanent Informality

No one can determine who is actually authorized to minister.

### 10. Credential as Ownership

A minister treats ordination as permanent entitlement to office or property.

### 11. Unreviewable Senior Clergy

Higher ministers are effectively exempt from credential discipline.

### 12. Paper Supremacy

Possession of a certificate is treated as more authoritative than the canonical credential record.

# ARTICLE CXXVIII
## Credential Decision Quality Standard

Before granting a substantial credential, the responsible body should be able to answer:

1. What credential is being granted?
2. What authority will it confer?
3. What authority will it not confer?
4. What qualification standard applies?
5. Has the candidate completed formation?
6. Has practical competence been demonstrated?
7. Has safeguarding review been completed?
8. Are material conflicts disclosed?
9. Has prior ministry history been reviewed?
10. Has the candidate demonstrated constitutional literacy?
11. Has the candidate demonstrated ethical fitness?
12. Has the candidate exercised ministry under supervision?
13. Is the credential level proportionate to demonstrated competence?
14. Is the credential being sought principally for an external advantage unrelated to religious vocation?
15. What supervision will exist afterward?
16. What continuing review applies?
17. What happens if concerns arise?
18. Is the decision adequately documented?
19. Would we grant this same credential if the candidate lacked wealth, family connection, charisma, or Founder support?
20. Would we trust this person with religious authority over someone vulnerable to them?

---

# ARTICLE CXXIX
## Credential Sanction Quality Standard

Before imposing a major credential sanction, the responsible body should ask:

1. What authority permits the sanction?
2. What facts have been validly established?
3. What standard applies?
4. Is safeguarding implicated?
5. Has civil reporting been addressed where applicable?
6. Is the decision-maker independent?
7. Has the minister had meaningful opportunity to respond?
8. Is the sanction proportionate?
9. Is temporary protection being confused with final guilt?
10. Is restoration possible?
11. Is permanent disqualification appropriate?
12. What appeal exists?
13. What record must be preserved?
14. What should be disclosed externally?
15. Would the process remain fair if the respondent were the PSL or Founder?

# ARTICLE CXXXV
## Institutional Objective

The ministry system must make it possible to say:

> **“This person is spiritually called.”**

without pretending that personal conviction alone establishes institutional qualification.

It must make it possible to say:

> **“This person is ordained.”**

without meaning:

> **“This person may never be removed from authority.”**

It must make it possible to say:

> **“This person is a minister.”**

without meaning:

> **“This person owns the ministry, controls the corporation, or may bind it legally.”**

It must make it possible to say:

> **“This minister has been forgiven.”**

without requiring:

> **“This minister must therefore be restored to authority.”**

It must distinguish:

> vocation from qualification,

> qualification from credential,

> credential from office,

> office from assignment,

> assignment from employment,

> employment from agency,

> religious recognition from civil authority,

> spiritual restoration from fitness for leadership,

> certificate from canonical standing.

The institutional purpose of this Framework is therefore:

> **to entrust religious authority only through processes sufficiently serious to justify the trust that members, participants, families, vulnerable persons, other institutions, and future generations may place in those who minister in the name of the Religious Body.**

# MINISTERIAL ATTESTATION

Religious authority is entrusted rather than owned.

Calling does not exempt us from formation.

Knowledge does not exempt us from character.

Charisma does not exempt us from accountability.

Ordination does not exempt us from safeguarding.

Spiritual office does not confer corporate ownership.

Religious credential does not create professional competence we do not possess.

A certificate does not stand above truth.

We shall exercise only the ministry entrusted to us.

We shall honor boundaries.

We shall protect vulnerable persons.

We shall report wrongdoing where required.

We shall disclose conflicts.

We shall receive supervision.

We shall remain open to correction.

We shall not manipulate conscience for personal benefit.

We shall not use spiritual authority to prevent lawful accountability.

We shall relinquish authority when our lawful service ends.

And we shall understand every religious title we receive not as evidence that we stand above the community, but as evidence that the community has entrusted us with greater responsibility to serve it.
