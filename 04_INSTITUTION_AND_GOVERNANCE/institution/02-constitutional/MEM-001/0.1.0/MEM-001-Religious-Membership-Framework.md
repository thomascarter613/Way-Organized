# Religious Membership Framework
## [[CORP_NAME]]

---

# PREAMBLE

A religious community consists of more than those who hold office and more than those who have entered its highest form of formal membership.

Some persons encounter a tradition briefly.

Some explore it.

Some participate regularly.

Some come to identify themselves with it.

Some voluntarily undertake a deeper covenant with the community.

Some are separately entrusted with religious office.

These relationships should not be collapsed into a single category.

Nor should participation in religious community be confused with ownership of the institution, corporate voting power, spiritual rank, financial influence, or fitness to govern.

This Framework therefore establishes a graded system of religious affiliation designed to preserve simultaneously:

- openness to exploration;
- meaningful belonging;
- voluntary commitment;
- freedom of conscience;
- responsible community standards;
- meaningful Covenant Member participation;
- fair process;
- safeguarding;
- institutional accountability;
- accurate membership records;
- and the constitutional distinction between religious membership and civil corporate membership.

The guiding principle is:

> **Belonging may deepen through commitment, but increasing commitment does not establish increasing human worth.**

A Visitor is not spiritually inferior to a Covenant Member merely by reason of status.

A Covenant Member does not acquire ownership of the Religious Body.

A Minister does not become more human than a lay participant.

A donor does not purchase membership.

A leader does not own the membership.

And no governing body may manipulate who belongs merely to manufacture a desired constitutional outcome.

# ARTICLE I
## Authority and Scope

### Section 1.1 — Authority

This Framework is adopted pursuant to:

1. REL-CON-001 — Foundational Religious Constitution;
2. ECC-CON-001 — Ecclesiastical Constitution;
3. applicable Foundational Principles;
4. applicable Formal Doctrine.

### Section 1.2 — Purpose

This Framework establishes:

- religious affiliation categories;
- Covenant Membership;
- admission standards;
- membership rights;
- membership responsibilities;
- governance eligibility;
- status changes;
- withdrawal;
- suspension;
- termination;
- restoration;
- membership administration;
- records;
- local-community relationships;
- civil–ecclesiastical distinctions.

### Section 1.3 — Subordination

This Framework may not:

- amend REL-CON-001;
- amend ECC-CON-001;
- create statutory corporate membership;
- alter CMA reserved powers established constitutionally;
- eliminate constitutionally protected member rights.

### Section 1.4 — Implementing Instruments

Detailed implementation may be established through:

- MEM-COV-001 — Covenant Membership Covenant;
- MEM-PROC-001 — Membership Admission, Status, Withdrawal, and Restoration Procedure;
- MEM-RIGHTS-001 — Covenant Member Rights and Responsibilities Statement;
- CMA-001 — Covenant Member Assembly Charter;
- CMA-PROC-001 — Covenant Member Assembly Procedure Manual;
- DISC-001 — Ecclesiastical Discipline and Appeals Code;
- GRIEV-001 — Community Grievance and Concern Procedure;
- applicable safeguarding instruments.

# ARTICLE II
## Nature of Religious Belonging

### Section 2.1 — Voluntary Relationship

Religious affiliation is a voluntary ecclesiastical relationship.

Except for obligations independently arising under civil law or valid contract, no person shall be compelled to remain affiliated with the Religious Body.

### Section 2.2 — No Ownership

No religious status creates:

- ownership of the religion;
- ownership of the Corporation;
- ownership of charitable assets;
- ownership of ministries;
- a distributive financial interest;
- a proprietary claim upon religious office.

### Section 2.3 — No Purchase of Status

Religious status shall not be purchased through:

- donations;
- dues;
- gifts;
- loans;
- property transfers;
- volunteer labor;
- business relationships;
- political influence.

### Section 2.4 — No Automatic Authority

Religious affiliation does not itself confer:

- ministerial authority;
- teaching authority;
- disciplinary authority;
- fiduciary authority;
- corporate authority;
- constitutional office.

### Section 2.5 — No Hierarchy of Human Dignity

The affiliation framework defines relationships, commitments, functions, and governance rights.

It does not establish a hierarchy of inherent human dignity.

# ARTICLE III
## Ecclesiastical and Statutory Membership Distinguished

### Section 3.1 — No Statutory Corporate Members

The Corporation associated with the Religious Body has no statutory members under its Charter.

### Section 3.2 — Covenant Member Is an Ecclesiastical Term

The term **Covenant Member** describes religious membership only.

It shall not by itself constitute corporate membership.

### Section 3.3 — No Civil Board Election Right

Covenant Members shall not, merely by virtue of Covenant Membership, possess a recurring right to:

- elect Civil Board directors;
- appoint Civil Board directors;
- remove Civil Board directors;
- vote upon ordinary corporate governance.

### Section 3.4 — Ecclesiastical Reserved Powers

Rights exercised through the Covenant Member Assembly are ecclesiastical constitutional rights.

They are not statutory corporate membership rights.

### Section 3.5 — Separate Corporate Action

Where a CMA decision concerns a matter requiring corporate action, the CMA decision constitutes only the ecclesiastical concurrence assigned to the CMA.

The competent Civil Board or other lawful corporate authority must separately perform any required civil act.

### Section 3.6 — Terminological Accuracy

Official governance documents shall distinguish carefully among:

- Covenant Member;
- statutory corporate member;
- director;
- officer;
- minister;
- participant.

# ARTICLE IV
## Affiliation Structure

The ordinary affiliation structure shall be:

1. **Visitor**
2. **Participant**
3. **Adherent**
4. **Covenant Candidate**
5. **Covenant Member**

Religious office exists separately from this affiliation ladder.

### Section 4.1 — Nonlinear Participation

A person need not necessarily progress through every category in precisely the same manner where an approved procedure permits equivalent formation.

### Section 4.2 — No Forced Progression

No person shall be pressured to advance to a deeper status merely to:

- satisfy attendance goals;
- increase voting numbers;
- improve institutional statistics;
- satisfy fundraising objectives;
- please a leader.

### Section 4.3 — No Automatic Progression

Time alone does not automatically transform one status into another.

Formal statuses require the applicable admission process.

---

# ARTICLE V
## Visitor

### Section 5.1 — Definition

A **Visitor** is a person who:

- attends;
- observes;
- explores;
- receives publicly available teaching;
- participates in activities open to the public

without having undertaken formal religious affiliation.

### Section 5.2 — No Affirmation Required

A Visitor need not:

- affirm doctrine;
- identify with the religion;
- make a financial contribution;
- enter a covenant.

### Section 5.3 — Access

Visitors should ordinarily have reasonable access to activities designated as open.

### Section 5.4 — Conduct

Visitors remain subject to reasonable:

- safety requirements;
- facility rules;
- behavioral expectations;
- safeguarding restrictions.

### Section 5.5 — No Governance Rights

Visitor status creates no governance vote or constitutional office eligibility.

---

# ARTICLE VI
## Participant

### Section 6.1 — Definition

A **Participant** is a person who participates with some regularity in the religious or community life of the Religious Body but has not entered formal religious affiliation.

### Section 6.2 — Participation

Participants may engage in activities including:

- worship;
- study;
- education;
- service;
- fellowship;
- public spiritual practice;
- community events.

### Section 6.3 — Volunteer Roles

Participants may serve in approved volunteer roles appropriate to their:

- qualifications;
- safeguarding status;
- training;
- degree of responsibility.

### Section 6.4 — Restricted Roles

Participation alone does not qualify a person for:

- senior religious office;
- confidential pastoral roles;
- governance positions requiring Covenant Membership;
- sensitive service involving vulnerable persons without required screening.

### Section 6.5 — No Assumed Religious Identity

Regular participation shall not automatically be represented as formal identification with the religion.

---

# ARTICLE VII
## Adherent

### Section 7.1 — Definition

An **Adherent** is a person who voluntarily identifies with the Religious Body or developing religious tradition but has not entered Covenant Membership.

### Section 7.2 — Recognition

Adherent status may ordinarily require:

- voluntary request or acknowledgment;
- familiarity with the Religious Body's basic identity;
- willingness to be recorded as affiliated.

### Section 7.3 — No Full Covenant Required

An Adherent is not required to undertake every commitment required of Covenant Members.

### Section 7.4 — Participation

Adherents may receive opportunities for:

- formation;
- consultation;
- community service;
- educational participation;
- nonreserved community dialogue.

### Section 7.5 — No Reserved-Power Vote

Adherent status does not confer voting authority in the CMA.

---

# ARTICLE VIII
## Covenant Candidate

### Section 8.1 — Purpose

A **Covenant Candidate** is a person formally preparing for Covenant Membership.

### Section 8.2 — Candidate Period

The candidate period exists to promote:

- informed commitment;
- mutual understanding;
- formation;
- questions;
- discernment;
- voluntary consent.

### Section 8.3 — No Presumption of Admission

Entering the candidate process does not guarantee admission.

### Section 8.4 — No Stigma for Withdrawal

A candidate may discontinue the process without being treated as disloyal.

### Section 8.5 — Candidate Participation

Candidates should receive meaningful opportunity to:

- study governing principles;
- learn religious commitments;
- ask questions;
- understand membership rights;
- understand membership responsibilities;
- understand the disciplinary and grievance systems;
- understand the right of withdrawal.

---

# ARTICLE IX
## Covenant Member

### Section 9.1 — Definition

A **Covenant Member** is a person formally admitted into the principal ordinary category of ecclesiastical membership.

### Section 9.2 — Covenant Nature

Covenant Membership represents a voluntary commitment between:

- the member;
- the religious community;
- the constitutional order.

### Section 9.3 — Not a Property Right

Covenant Membership is neither:

- transferable property;
- inheritable property;
- a financial asset;
- a vested right to office.

### Section 9.4 — Significant Status

Because Covenant Membership carries constitutional participation rights and corresponding responsibilities, admission shall be deliberate rather than casual.

---

# ARTICLE X
## General Eligibility for Covenant Membership

An applicant shall ordinarily:

1. satisfy the minimum age established by this Framework;
2. demonstrate sufficient familiarity with the Religious Body;
3. complete required introductory formation;
4. understand the applicable membership covenant;
5. voluntarily request membership;
6. affirm the commitments designated as necessary for Covenant Membership;
7. agree to respect the governing constitutional order;
8. agree to comply with safeguarding obligations;
9. agree to the applicable membership accountability process;
10. provide information reasonably required to administer membership.

### Section 10.1 — No Financial Eligibility Requirement

No minimum:

- donation;
- tithe;
- contribution;
- membership fee;
- property ownership;
- income level

shall be required for Covenant Membership merely as a condition of religious standing.

### Section 10.2 — No Social Prestige Requirement

Membership shall not depend upon:

- wealth;
- profession;
- political influence;
- social class;
- donor status;
- personal friendship with leadership.

### Section 10.3 — Additional Religious Qualifications

MEM-COV-001 may identify additional religious commitments once substantive Foundational Principles and membership doctrine have been established.

---

# ARTICLE XI
## Age and Governance Eligibility

### Section 11.1 — Ordinary Covenant Membership Age

Ordinary Covenant Membership shall require the applicant to be at least **eighteen years of age**.

### Section 11.2 — Youth Religious Participation

Persons under eighteen may participate through age-appropriate categories, programs, formation, and religious commitments established by policy.

### Section 11.3 — Future Youth Covenant Category

The EC may create a distinct **Youth Covenant** or comparable religious status.

Such status shall not itself confer CMA voting eligibility.

### Section 11.4 — No Diminished Religious Worth

The absence of formal governance membership for minors shall not be interpreted as diminishing their:

- dignity;
- importance;
- spiritual participation;
- access to appropriate religious life.

### Section 11.5 — Safeguarding

All youth participation remains subject to applicable child-protection requirements.

---

# ARTICLE XII
## Doctrinal Standard for Membership

### Section 12.1 — Defined Standard Required

No person shall be denied or disciplined for failing to affirm a doctrinal proposition unless the governing corpus identifies that proposition, category, or commitment as applicable to Covenant Membership.

### Section 12.2 — Different Levels of Teaching

Covenant Membership shall distinguish among:

- Foundational Principles;
- required membership commitments;
- Formal Doctrine;
- Authoritative Interpretation;
- permitted theological opinion.

### Section 12.3 — No Required Pretended Certainty

A person shall not be required to claim certainty they do not possess.

### Section 12.4 — Good-Faith Questions

A Covenant Member may:

- ask questions;
- disclose uncertainty;
- request clarification;
- examine evidence;
- study alternative interpretations;
- respectfully disagree within permitted doctrinal boundaries.

### Section 12.5 — Membership Incompatibility

Where a person's beliefs become materially incompatible with commitments expressly required for Covenant Membership, the matter should ordinarily begin with:

- conversation;
- clarification;
- discernment;
- voluntary reassessment

rather than immediate punitive discipline.

---

# ARTICLE XIII
## Membership Formation

### Section 13.1 — Purpose

Formation shall prepare a candidate to understand what they are voluntarily undertaking.

### Section 13.2 — Required Subjects

Formation should ordinarily address:

1. religious identity and mission;
2. Foundational Principles when adopted;
3. principal religious practices;
4. constitutional governance;
5. meaning of Covenant Membership;
6. rights of members;
7. responsibilities of members;
8. safeguarding;
9. accountability;
10. grievance and reporting channels;
11. discipline and appeal;
12. freedom to withdraw;
13. civil–ecclesiastical distinction.

### Section 13.3 — Questions Encouraged

Candidates shall be encouraged to ask substantial questions.

### Section 13.4 — No Recruitment Pressure

Formation personnel shall not:

- manufacture urgency;
- conceal material obligations;
- promise governance influence;
- condition social belonging upon admission.

---

# ARTICLE XIV
## Application

### Section 14.1 — Voluntary Application

Covenant Membership begins with a voluntary application or equivalent affirmative request.

### Section 14.2 — Application Information

The application may reasonably request:

- name;
- contact information;
- date of birth;
- affiliation history;
- local-community affiliation;
- formation completion;
- covenant acknowledgment;
- other information genuinely necessary to administer membership.

### Section 14.3 — Sensitive Information

Application requirements shall not demand irrelevant intimate information merely because the institution possesses religious authority.

### Section 14.4 — Privacy

Application information shall be handled under applicable privacy and records standards.

---

# ARTICLE XV
## Membership Interview or Discernment Conversation

### Section 15.1 — Purpose

A candidate should ordinarily participate in a membership conversation.

### Section 15.2 — Scope

The conversation may address:

- understanding of the covenant;
- questions;
- readiness;
- religious commitments;
- community expectations;
- concerns affecting eligibility.

### Section 15.3 — Not an Interrogation

The membership conversation shall not be used to:

- coerce disclosures unrelated to membership;
- demand political conformity;
- extract improper confessions;
- evaluate social status;
- obtain financial commitments.

### Section 15.4 — Multiple Reviewers

Where reasonably practicable, at least two qualified persons should participate in final membership evaluation.

---

# ARTICLE XVI
## Admission Authority

### Section 16.1 — EC Oversight

The EC retains ultimate authority over Covenant Membership standards.

### Section 16.2 — Delegation

Routine admissions may be delegated to a:

- Membership Committee;
- local council;
- designated Membership Steward;
- other body authorized under MEM-PROC-001.

### Section 16.3 — No Unilateral PSL Control

The PSL shall not possess unrestricted unilateral authority to admit or exclude Covenant Members for governance purposes.

### Section 16.4 — Admission Decision

A properly delegated membership body may:

- approve;
- defer;
- request additional formation;
- deny.

### Section 16.5 — Reasons

A denial or substantial deferral should ordinarily identify sufficient reasons for meaningful review.

---

# ARTICLE XVII
## Membership Covenant

### Section 17.1 — Written Instrument

Covenant Members shall affirm MEM-COV-001 or another validly adopted membership covenant.

### Section 17.2 — Covenant Content

The covenant should address:

- religious commitments;
- community responsibilities;
- constitutional respect;
- conduct;
- safeguarding;
- accountability;
- freedom of conscience;
- freedom of withdrawal.

### Section 17.3 — No Hidden Terms

No material membership obligation shall be intentionally concealed from applicants.

### Section 17.4 — Subordinate Status

The Covenant may not contradict:

- REL-CON-001;
- ECC-CON-001;
- this Framework.

---

# ARTICLE XVIII
## Effective Date of Membership

Membership becomes effective when:

1. applicable eligibility requirements are satisfied;
2. admission is validly approved;
3. the membership covenant is affirmed;
4. the membership record is entered in the Membership Registry.

### Section 18.1 — No Retroactive Admission

Membership should not ordinarily be backdated for the purpose of changing governance eligibility.

### Section 18.2 — Administrative Error

A clerical delay in recording an otherwise completed admission may be corrected without unjustly depriving the member of status.

---

# ARTICLE XIX
## Good Standing

### Section 19.1 — Definition

A Covenant Member is in **Good Standing** when:

- membership remains active;
- the member is not under a governance-affecting suspension;
- no final status action has terminated membership.

### Section 19.2 — Good Standing Not Moral Perfection

Good Standing does not imply:

- moral perfection;
- doctrinal omniscience;
- absence of ordinary disagreement;
- uninterrupted attendance.

### Section 19.3 — Administrative Inactivity Distinguished

A member may become administratively inactive without being disciplined.

---

# ARTICLE XX
## Governance-Eligible Covenant Member

### Section 20.1 — Definition

A **Governance-Eligible Covenant Member** is a Covenant Member who satisfies the qualifications for formal CMA voting on a particular matter.

### Section 20.2 — Ordinary Requirements

The person must ordinarily:

1. be at least eighteen years old;
2. be in Good Standing;
3. have held Covenant Membership continuously for at least ninety days;
4. not be validly suspended from governance participation;
5. satisfy applicable record-date requirements.

### Section 20.3 — Ninety-Day Rule

The ninety-day period exists principally to prevent rapid manipulation of the electorate.

### Section 20.4 — Initial Formation Exception

During the first year following initial implementation of Covenant Membership, the EC may establish transitional eligibility rules because no member could otherwise satisfy a mature membership-duration requirement.

Any exception shall be:

- documented;
- neutral;
- temporary;
- equally applicable.

### Section 20.5 — Participation Distinguished From Voting

A Covenant Member who is not yet governance-eligible may still:

- attend appropriate CMA sessions;
- receive reports;
- submit questions;
- participate in nonvoting dialogue;
- use grievance and petition mechanisms where otherwise eligible.

---

# ARTICLE XXI
## Covenant Member Rights

Subject to legitimate procedural and confidentiality limitations, Covenant Members shall ordinarily possess rights including:

1. participation in the CMA;
2. governance voting when eligible;
3. nomination rights established by governing instruments;
4. petition rights;
5. appropriate access to governance information;
6. participation in constitutionally required consultations;
7. grievance procedures;
8. safeguarding reporting;
9. fair membership discipline;
10. appeal of serious membership sanctions;
11. protection from retaliation;
12. voluntary withdrawal;
13. appropriate privacy;
14. appropriate pastoral access;
15. good-faith religious inquiry;
16. equal application of membership rules.

---

# ARTICLE XXII
## Right to Participate in the Covenant Member Assembly

### Section 22.1 — Assembly Membership

Covenant Members constitute the CMA subject to eligibility classifications established by ECC-CON-001 and CMA-001.

### Section 22.2 — Reserved Powers

Governance-Eligible Covenant Members may exercise the reserved powers defined by ECC-CON-001.

### Section 22.3 — No Expansion by Framework

This Framework does not create additional CMA reserved powers beyond those validly established by constitutional authority.

### Section 22.4 — No Ordinary Executive Authority

CMA participation does not create a right to administer ordinary:

- ministries;
- staff;
- finances;
- operations;
- contracts;
- pastoral decisions.

---

# ARTICLE XXIII
## Right of Nomination

Where a governing instrument provides for Covenant Member nominations, eligible members may nominate qualified candidates.

### Section 23.1 — Nomination Is Not Appointment

Nomination creates no entitlement to office.

### Section 23.2 — Qualification Review

All nominees remain subject to applicable:

- qualifications;
- due diligence;
- safeguarding;
- conflicts;
- selection procedures.

---

# ARTICLE XXIV
## Right of Petition

Covenant Members possess the petition rights established by ECC-CON-001.

### Section 24.1 — Subjects

Petitions may concern:

- governance;
- doctrine review;
- leadership concerns;
- community practices;
- constitutional matters;
- other subjects within legitimate ecclesiastical jurisdiction.

### Section 24.2 — Good-Faith Protection

A good-faith petition shall not itself constitute:

- insubordination;
- disloyalty;
- factional misconduct.

### Section 24.3 — Abuse

Petition rights do not authorize:

- harassment;
- knowing falsehoods;
- disclosure of protected information;
- repetitive bad-faith obstruction.

---

# ARTICLE XXV
## Right to Information

### Section 25.1 — Responsible Information Access

Covenant Members should receive information reasonably necessary to exercise their constitutional responsibilities.

### Section 25.2 — Information May Include

Appropriate information may include:

- governing documents;
- leadership structure;
- major ecclesiastical decisions;
- annual reports;
- proposed constitutional amendments;
- information necessary for reserved-power votes.

### Section 25.3 — Confidentiality

Membership does not create unrestricted access to:

- pastoral records;
- safeguarding records;
- personnel files;
- legal advice;
- private donor information;
- confidential investigations;
- private member information.

### Section 25.4 — No Information Manipulation

Leadership shall not knowingly withhold material nonconfidential information primarily to manipulate a reserved-power decision.

---

# ARTICLE XXVI
## Right to Grievance and Concern Processes

Covenant Members shall have access to a process for raising:

- governance concerns;
- relational concerns;
- membership concerns;
- leadership concerns;
- institutional complaints.

### Section 26.1 — Appropriate Routing

Not every concern is disciplinary.

The system shall route matters to the appropriate:

- pastoral;
- administrative;
- membership;
- safeguarding;
- accountability;
- constitutional

process.

### Section 26.2 — No Chain-of-Command Trap

A member shall have an alternative reporting path where the ordinary recipient is materially implicated.

---

# ARTICLE XXVII
## Right to Report Wrongdoing

No membership covenant, doctrine, policy, or leader instruction shall prohibit lawful reporting of suspected:

- abuse;
- exploitation;
- violence;
- fraud;
- criminal conduct;
- safeguarding failure;
- other serious wrongdoing

to competent authorities.

### Section 27.1 — No Permission Requirement

A member need not first obtain ecclesiastical permission to make a lawful external report.

### Section 27.2 — Internal Reporting Also Protected

Good-faith use of internal reporting systems is likewise protected.

---

# ARTICLE XXVIII
## Freedom From Retaliation

No person shall retaliate against a Covenant Member merely for:

- filing a good-faith grievance;
- making a safeguarding report;
- submitting a constitutional petition;
- appealing discipline;
- raising a legitimate conflict;
- participating as a witness;
- expressing protected good-faith disagreement.

Retaliation may itself constitute misconduct.

---

# ARTICLE XXIX
## Freedom of Conscience

### Section 29.1 — Religious Commitment Preserved

Covenant Membership may legitimately require defined religious commitments.

### Section 29.2 — Conscience Not Owned

No leader or institution owns a member's conscience.

### Section 29.3 — Protected Conduct

Members may ordinarily:

- ask honest questions;
- investigate teachings;
- disclose doubt;
- seek independent advice;
- decline religious office;
- refuse improper demands;
- withdraw from membership.

### Section 29.4 — Doctrinal Boundaries

Freedom of conscience does not require the Religious Body to represent every belief as compatible with Covenant Membership.

### Section 29.5 — Honest Resolution

Where conscience and membership commitments become irreconcilable, separation should occur honestly rather than through coerced false profession.

---

# ARTICLE XXX
## Covenant Member Responsibilities

A Covenant Member shall ordinarily be expected to:

1. honor the applicable membership covenant;
2. respect the dignity of others;
3. comply with safeguarding requirements;
4. participate honestly in community discernment;
5. respect legitimate confidentiality;
6. refrain from abuse or exploitation;
7. avoid knowingly false allegations;
8. respect valid governance processes;
9. disclose relevant conflicts when exercising governance rights;
10. avoid manipulation of membership or voting processes;
11. comply with lawful and constitutionally valid discipline;
12. contribute reasonably to community life according to ability and circumstance.

---

# ARTICLE XXXI
## Participation and Attendance

### Section 31.1 — Participation Encouraged

Covenant Membership ordinarily contemplates meaningful continuing relationship with the religious community.

### Section 31.2 — Attendance Not Absolute

Temporary or extended reduced participation may occur because of:

- illness;
- disability;
- caregiving;
- employment;
- military service;
- travel;
- family circumstances;
- geographic relocation;
- spiritual discernment;
- other legitimate reasons.

### Section 31.3 — No Automatic Expulsion

Absence alone shall not ordinarily trigger punitive termination.

### Section 31.4 — Administrative Review

Prolonged lack of contact may result in administrative inactive status after reasonable efforts at communication.

---

# ARTICLE XXXII
## Financial Support

### Section 32.1 — Voluntary Support

Members may be encouraged to support the religious mission financially according to:

- ability;
- conscience;
- applicable religious teaching.

### Section 32.2 — No Purchase of Standing

A person's donation history shall not determine:

- Good Standing;
- CMA voting weight;
- access to grievance procedures;
- disciplinary outcome;
- eligibility for ordinary religious care.

### Section 32.3 — No Weighted Voting

No member shall receive additional governance votes because of:

- donations;
- wealth;
- volunteer hours;
- office;
- seniority.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIII
## Volunteer Service and Labor

### Section 33.1 — Voluntary Service

Members may serve voluntarily.

### Section 33.2 — No Exploitation

Religious authority shall not be used to extract unreasonable uncompensated labor through:

- spiritual threats;
- manipulation;
- false emergency;
- promises of status;
- threats to membership.

### Section 33.3 — Sensitive Roles

Volunteer roles involving:

- minors;
- vulnerable persons;
- finances;
- confidential information;
- pastoral care

may require additional qualification and screening.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIV
## Membership and Religious Office

### Section 34.1 — Separate Categories

Religious office is not merely a higher membership tier.

### Section 34.2 — Membership Requirement

MIN-001 may require Covenant Membership for specified religious offices.

### Section 34.3 — Office Does Not Expand Membership Vote

A minister who is also a Covenant Member ordinarily receives the same CMA vote as another Governance-Eligible Covenant Member.

### Section 34.4 — Loss of Office

Loss of ministerial office does not automatically terminate Covenant Membership unless:

- the underlying reason independently warrants membership action;
- governing law expressly provides otherwise.

### Section 34.5 — Loss of Membership

Loss of Covenant Membership may make a person ineligible to continue in an office for which membership is required.

---

# ARTICLE XXXV
## Membership and Employment

### Section 35.1 — Separate Relationships

Covenant Membership and employment are distinct.

### Section 35.2 — Employment Does Not Confer Membership

An employee does not become a Covenant Member merely through employment.

### Section 35.3 — Membership Does Not Guarantee Employment

Membership creates no right to:

- employment;
- promotion;
- compensation;
- contract renewal.

### Section 35.4 — Religious Positions

Where lawfully appropriate, certain positions may require religious qualifications or membership.

### Section 35.5 — Separate Actions

Termination of employment shall not automatically terminate membership unless a separate membership basis exists.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVI
## Membership and the Civil Board

### Section 36.1 — Board Independence

Covenant Members do not elect Civil Board directors merely by virtue of Covenant Membership.

### Section 36.2 — Candidate Suggestions

Covenant Members may suggest or nominate potential directors where BYL-001 permits.

### Section 36.3 — Board Election Remains Corporate

Final election of Civil Board directors remains with the authority established by the Charter and BYL-001.

### Section 36.4 — No Indirect Election Scheme

The membership system shall not be used to create a recurring de facto Covenant Member election of Civil Board directors while claiming that no statutory corporate members exist.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVII
## Membership Registry

### Section 37.1 — Registry

The Religious Body shall maintain a canonical **Membership Registry**.

### Section 37.2 — Purpose

The Registry establishes reliable ecclesiastical records concerning:

- affiliation status;
- admission;
- governance eligibility;
- local affiliation;
- status changes;
- withdrawal;
- restoration.

### Section 37.3 — Stable Member Identifier

Each Covenant Member should receive a stable internal membership identifier.

### Section 37.4 — Minimum Record

The Registry should ordinarily identify:

- member identifier;
- name;
- admission date;
- current status;
- local-community affiliation where any;
- governance eligibility;
- relevant status effective dates;
- withdrawal or termination date where applicable.

### Section 37.5 — Sensitive Reasons Separated

Detailed disciplinary, pastoral, or safeguarding information should not ordinarily be stored in the general Membership Registry.

Such records shall be maintained in appropriately restricted systems.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVIII
## Accuracy of Membership Records

### Section 38.1 — Duty of Accuracy

Membership records shall be maintained accurately and in good faith.

### Section 38.2 — No Secret Electorate

No hidden or unofficial member list shall control CMA eligibility.

### Section 38.3 — Corrections

A member may request correction of material administrative errors.

### Section 38.4 — Historical Integrity

Corrections shall not erase significant prior status changes deceptively.

### Section 38.5 — Annual Review

The membership administration function shall periodically review Registry accuracy.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIX
## Governance Record Date

### Section 39.1 — Need for Record Date

A formal CMA vote shall use a defined membership record date established under CMA-001 or CMA-PROC-001.

### Section 39.2 — Neutral Application

The record date shall be applied neutrally.

### Section 39.3 — Post-Record Admission

A person admitted after the applicable record date may become a Covenant Member without thereby becoming eligible to vote retroactively on that matter.

### Section 39.4 — No Strategic Delay

Leadership shall not deliberately:

- delay valid admissions;
- accelerate questionable admissions;
- suspend members

for the primary purpose of altering the electorate.

---

# ARTICLE XL
## Anti-Packing Rule

### Section 40.1 — Prohibition

No leader, council, faction, local community, or other person shall manipulate admissions primarily to predetermine:

- PSL confirmation;
- PSL removal;
- constitutional amendment;
- doctrinal concurrence;
- affiliation;
- disaffiliation;
- dissolution;
- another reserved-power decision.

### Section 40.2 — Indicators

Potential manipulation may include:

- mass expedited admissions immediately before a vote;
- waiver of ordinary requirements selectively;
- coordinated backdating;
- admissions conditioned on promised votes;
- targeted exclusion of otherwise qualified applicants because of expected voting preference.

### Section 40.3 — CCG Review

Material allegations of electorate manipulation may be submitted to the CCG.

---

# ARTICLE XLI
## Anti-Purging Rule

### Section 41.1 — Prohibition

Membership discipline shall not be used primarily to remove persons from the electorate because leadership expects them to vote contrary to leadership preference.

### Section 41.2 — Independent Violations

Membership misconduct remains subject to ordinary discipline even near an important vote.

### Section 41.3 — Heightened Documentation

Status actions materially affecting governance eligibility during a pending Class D or E matter shall receive heightened documentation and review.

### Section 41.4 — Emergency Safeguarding Preserved

Nothing in this Article prevents immediate protective action required for genuine safeguarding concerns.

---

# ARTICLE XLII
## Administrative Inactive Status

### Section 42.1 — Nature

**Inactive** is an administrative rather than punitive status.

### Section 42.2 — Possible Grounds

Inactive status may arise from:

- prolonged absence;
- inability to contact;
- extended relocation;
- voluntary request;
- another neutral administrative reason.

### Section 42.3 — No Misconduct Finding

Inactive status does not itself imply misconduct.

### Section 42.4 — Governance Eligibility

An inactive member may be temporarily ineligible for formal CMA voting under CMA-001.

### Section 42.5 — Return to Active Status

Reactivation should ordinarily be straightforward where no disciplinary or eligibility concern exists.

---

# ARTICLE XLIII
## Membership Restriction

### Section 43.1 — Nature

A **Restriction** limits specified membership privileges without fully suspending membership.

### Section 43.2 — Examples

Restrictions may concern:

- access to specified activities;
- volunteer roles;
- contact with specified persons;
- governance participation;
- leadership eligibility.

### Section 43.3 — Basis

Material restrictions require:

- valid authority;
- stated reasons;
- proportionate scope;
- applicable due process.

### Section 43.4 — Safeguarding Restrictions

Temporary safeguarding restrictions may be imposed before final adjudication where authorized.

---

# ARTICLE XLIV
## Membership Suspension

### Section 44.1 — Nature

Suspension temporarily limits the exercise of substantial membership rights while membership status remains unresolved or subject to discipline.

### Section 44.2 — Grounds

Suspension may be:

- protective;
- disciplinary;
- procedural

as authorized by DISC-001 or safeguarding rules.

### Section 44.3 — No Automatic Guilt

Protective suspension is not a final finding of misconduct.

### Section 44.4 — Governance Rights

A valid suspension may temporarily remove CMA voting eligibility where the governing decision expressly provides.

### Section 44.5 — Review

Material suspension shall be subject to the review required by applicable disciplinary rules.

---

# ARTICLE XLV
## Termination of Covenant Membership

### Section 45.1 — Forms

Covenant Membership may end through:

1. voluntary withdrawal;
2. death;
3. final ecclesiastical removal;
4. other status expressly recognized by governing law.

### Section 45.2 — Removal Distinguished From Withdrawal

A member who voluntarily withdraws shall not be falsely recorded as expelled.

A member who is removed through final discipline shall not be falsely recorded as having resigned merely to conceal the action.

### Section 45.3 — Serious Action

Involuntary termination is a serious ecclesiastical action and shall require fair process under DISC-001.

---

# ARTICLE XLVI
## Voluntary Withdrawal

### Section 46.1 — Right

A Covenant Member may withdraw voluntarily.

### Section 46.2 — No Permission Required

Withdrawal does not require approval by:

- PSL;
- EC;
- local leader;
- Membership Committee;
- CMA.

### Section 46.3 — Method

Withdrawal may be communicated through any reliable method established by MEM-PROC-001.

### Section 46.4 — Effective Date

Withdrawal becomes effective upon:

- receipt;
- or a later date expressly chosen by the member where administratively feasible.

### Section 46.5 — No Spiritual Coercion

A person shall not be threatened with improper:

- spiritual consequences;
- family exclusion;
- loss of personal property;
- retaliation;
- public humiliation

merely to prevent withdrawal.

### Section 46.6 — Record

The Registry shall record withdrawal accurately.

---

# ARTICLE XLVII
## Withdrawal During a Pending Matter

### Section 47.1 — Withdrawal Still Effective

A pending grievance, investigation, or discipline does not prevent a member from withdrawing.

### Section 47.2 — Institutional Review May Continue

The Religious Body may continue a matter where reasonably necessary for:

- safeguarding;
- ministerial credentials;
- institutional learning;
- protection of others;
- preservation of accurate records.

### Section 47.3 — Jurisdictional Limit

After withdrawal, the Religious Body shall not claim general disciplinary authority over the former member's subsequent private life.

### Section 47.4 — Record Accuracy

Any continuing institutional finding shall distinguish:

- former membership status;
- conduct occurring during the period of affiliation.

---

# ARTICLE XLVIII
## Membership Discipline

### Section 48.1 — Governing Code

Serious membership discipline shall proceed under DISC-001.

### Section 48.2 — Possible Purposes

Membership discipline may seek:

- protection;
- accountability;
- correction;
- restoration;
- integrity of the covenant.

### Section 48.3 — No Discipline for Protected Conduct

A person shall not be disciplined merely for:

- good-faith questions;
- lawful reporting;
- filing a grievance;
- petitioning;
- appealing;
- protected disagreement.

### Section 48.4 — Known Standard

Serious discipline should rely upon:

- a governing standard in effect;
- a Foundational Principle;
- constitutional obligation;
- safeguarding requirement;
- another reasonably knowable obligation.

### Section 48.5 — No Retroactive Punishment

A member should not ordinarily be punished for conduct solely because a new rule adopted later would now prohibit it.

---

# ARTICLE XLIX
## Grounds Potentially Warranting Serious Membership Discipline

Subject to DISC-001 and applicable doctrine, serious grounds may include:

- abuse;
- exploitation;
- violence;
- serious harassment;
- safeguarding violations;
- deliberate fraud;
- serious retaliation;
- substantial abuse of religious authority;
- knowing material falsification in governance;
- repeated serious violation of the membership covenant;
- conduct fundamentally incompatible with Covenant Membership;
- persistent disruption intended to make ordinary community life impossible.

### Section 49.1 — Mere Disagreement Excluded

Theological or governance disagreement does not become serious misconduct merely because leadership considers the disagreement inconvenient.

---

# ARTICLE L
## Membership Due Process

Before involuntary termination or comparable serious membership sanction, a member shall ordinarily receive:

1. notice;
2. identification of the issue;
3. applicable standard;
4. meaningful opportunity to respond;
5. impartial consideration;
6. a reasoned outcome;
7. available appeal information.

### Section 50.1 — Emergency Measures

Immediate protective restrictions may occur before completion of ordinary process where necessary for safety.

### Section 50.2 — Recusal

A materially conflicted person shall not act as final adjudicator.

---

# ARTICLE LI
## Membership Appeals

### Section 51.1 — Right

A member may appeal:

- involuntary termination;
- long-term suspension;
- another serious sanction specified by DISC-001.

### Section 51.2 — Grounds

Appeal may include:

- procedural error;
- jurisdictional error;
- material factual error;
- bias;
- conflict;
- disproportionate sanction;
- newly discovered evidence;
- constitutional violation.

### Section 51.3 — Independent Review

The final appellate reviewer shall not be identical to the original decision-maker.

---

# ARTICLE LII
## Restoration to Covenant Membership

### Section 52.1 — Possibility

A former member may seek restoration where applicable.

### Section 52.2 — Restoration After Withdrawal

A person who voluntarily withdrew may ordinarily apply for readmission without being treated as having committed misconduct merely by leaving.

### Section 52.3 — Restoration After Removal

Where membership was terminated through discipline, restoration may require:

- completion of applicable conditions;
- evidence of changed circumstances;
- safeguarding assessment;
- renewed covenant;
- appropriate review.

### Section 52.4 — Restoration Is Not Erasure

Restoration does not require falsification or destruction of legitimate historical records.

### Section 52.5 — Governance Eligibility

A restored member may be subject to a new governance-eligibility period before resuming reserved-power voting.

---

# ARTICLE LIII
## Reapplication After Denial

### Section 53.1 — No Permanent Bar by Default

Denial of an application does not automatically create permanent ineligibility.

### Section 53.2 — Reapplication

MEM-PROC-001 may establish reasonable periods or conditions for reapplication.

### Section 53.3 — Appeal

Where denial allegedly resulted from:

- procedural irregularity;
- prohibited discrimination under applicable internal standards;
- retaliation;
- constitutional violation;
- material factual error,

a review process shall be available.

---

# ARTICLE LIV
## Local Community Affiliation

### Section 54.1 — General and Local Membership

Covenant Membership ordinarily belongs to the broader Religious Body.

A member may additionally be affiliated with a particular local community.

### Section 54.2 — No Duplicate Membership Required

Transfer between recognized local communities should not ordinarily require re-admission into Covenant Membership.

### Section 54.3 — Local Records

Local communities may maintain appropriate local affiliation records synchronized with the central Membership Registry.

### Section 54.4 — Local Standards

A local community may establish additional participation expectations where authorized but may not redefine Covenant Membership contrary to this Framework.

### Section 54.5 — Local Admission Delegation

Local bodies may administer Covenant Membership admissions only through authority expressly delegated by the EC.

---

# ARTICLE LV
## Transfer Between Local Communities

### Section 55.1 — Transfer

A Covenant Member may request transfer of primary local affiliation.

### Section 55.2 — Continuity

Valid transfer shall ordinarily preserve:

- Covenant Membership status;
- original admission date;
- governance eligibility;
- applicable disciplinary status.

### Section 55.3 — No Status Laundering

A member may not escape a valid safeguarding restriction or disciplinary process merely by transferring local affiliation.

---

# ARTICLE LVI
## Relocation Outside an Established Community

### Section 56.1 — Continued Membership

A Covenant Member may remain affiliated while living outside the geographic reach of a local community.

### Section 56.2 — Participation

Remote or regional participation may be established as reasonably practicable.

### Section 56.3 — Governance Eligibility

CMA-001 may establish neutral participation requirements that account reasonably for geographically dispersed members.

---

# ARTICLE LVII
## Affiliation and Disaffiliation of Communities

### Section 57.1 — Community Change Distinguished

The affiliation or disaffiliation of a local community does not automatically determine the personal religious status of each individual associated with it.

### Section 57.2 — Individual Choice

Members affected by institutional separation should ordinarily receive a meaningful opportunity to determine their continuing personal affiliation.

### Section 57.3 — No Automatic Property Interest

Individual members acquire no personal interest in institutional property through a community disaffiliation.

### Section 57.4 — Records

Membership records shall be reconciled carefully during institutional separation.

---

# ARTICLE LVIII
## Member Death

### Section 58.1 — Status

Membership terminates administratively upon death.

### Section 58.2 — Historical Record

The person's historical membership record should be preserved appropriately.

### Section 58.3 — Privacy

Posthumous records remain subject to appropriate confidentiality and historical-record policies.

---

# ARTICLE LIX
## Couples and Families

### Section 59.1 — Individual Membership

Covenant Membership belongs to individuals rather than households.

### Section 59.2 — No Automatic Spousal Membership

A person's spouse does not become a Covenant Member automatically.

### Section 59.3 — Independent Governance Rights

Each Governance-Eligible Covenant Member possesses their own vote.

### Section 59.4 — No Household Vote

No family head, spouse, parent, or household representative may cast another adult member's vote merely by familial role.

---

# ARTICLE LX
## Membership and Donors

### Section 60.1 — Donation Does Not Confer Membership

Donors are not Covenant Members merely because they donate.

### Section 60.2 — Membership Does Not Confer Donor Privilege

A member who gives substantially shall not receive additional ecclesiastical governance authority.

### Section 60.3 — Anonymous Donors

Donor privacy shall not be confused with membership anonymity for governance purposes.

The institution must still know who is eligible to exercise formal CMA rights.

---

# ARTICLE LXI
## Membership and Political Activity

### Section 61.1 — No Organizational Political Qualification by Default

Covenant Membership shall not ordinarily require affiliation with a political party or candidate.

### Section 61.2 — Religious Ethical Teaching Preserved

Nothing prevents the Religious Body from teaching moral or social principles within its religious mission and applicable law.

### Section 61.3 — Corporate Compliance

Member political activity undertaken personally shall not automatically be attributed to the Corporation.

---

# ARTICLE LXII
## Equality of Vote

Where Covenant Members exercise a formal reserved-power vote:

> **one eligible member shall possess one vote.**

No additional vote shall arise from:

- office;
- wealth;
- donation;
- seniority;
- ministerial status;
- Founder status;
- family position;
- educational attainment.

### Section 62.1 — Officeholders

A PSL, minister, EC member, Board director, or Founder who is separately eligible as a Covenant Member receives no additional CMA vote merely because of office.

---

# ARTICLE LXIII
## Proxy and Delegated Voting

### Section 63.1 — No Proxy Presumption

CMA voting shall not use proxies unless expressly authorized by CMA-001 after careful constitutional review.

### Section 63.2 — Independent Judgment

The preferred rule is personal voting by each eligible member.

### Section 63.3 — Accessibility

Remote or absentee voting mechanisms may be established without constituting proxy voting.

---

# ARTICLE LXIV
## Membership Confidentiality

### Section 64.1 — Membership Status

The fact of Covenant Membership may be treated according to reasonable community and privacy practices.

### Section 64.2 — Registry Access

The complete Membership Registry shall not be public merely because membership has governance consequences.

### Section 64.3 — Governance Verification

Enough information shall be available to authorized election or CMA administrators to verify eligibility.

### Section 64.4 — No Public Exposure as Punishment

A person's membership status shall not be publicized for the purpose of humiliation.

---

# ARTICLE LXV
## Sensitive Membership Information

Information concerning:

- pastoral care;
- discipline;
- safeguarding;
- family circumstances;
- financial hardship;
- health;
- private religious questions

shall receive heightened confidentiality appropriate to applicable law and institutional policy.

### Section 65.1 — Separation of Records

Sensitive case records should ordinarily be separated from the ordinary Membership Registry.

---

# ARTICLE LXVI
## Member Access to Personal Records

### Section 66.1 — Administrative Information

A Covenant Member may request confirmation of significant administrative information recorded concerning their own membership status.

### Section 66.2 — Limitations

Access may be limited appropriately concerning:

- confidential third-party information;
- safeguarding;
- privileged materials;
- protected investigations;
- other legitimate restrictions.

### Section 66.3 — Correction

Members may request correction of objective administrative errors.

---

# ARTICLE LXVII
## No Secret Membership Discipline

### Section 67.1 — Status Notice

A member whose formal status has been materially:

- restricted;
- suspended;
- terminated

shall ordinarily receive notice.

### Section 67.2 — Covert Governance Exclusion Prohibited

Leadership shall not secretly remove a person's voting eligibility while publicly representing that person's membership as unchanged, except for temporary protective confidentiality where authorized and promptly reviewable.

---

# ARTICLE LXVIII
## Membership Statistics and Public Reporting

### Section 68.1 — Accuracy

Public or community reports concerning membership shall use reasonable and consistent definitions.

### Section 68.2 — Categories Distinguished

Reports should distinguish, where material, among:

- attendance;
- participation;
- adherence;
- Covenant Membership;
- governance-eligible membership.

### Section 68.3 — No Inflated Claims

The organization shall not count Visitors or mailing-list subscribers as Covenant Members merely to exaggerate institutional size.

---

# ARTICLE LXIX
## Membership Review

### Section 69.1 — Periodic Administrative Review

Membership administration should periodically review:

- registry accuracy;
- inactive records;
- governance eligibility;
- local affiliations;
- unresolved administrative inconsistencies.

### Section 69.2 — No Loyalty Purge

Periodic review shall not become an ideological loyalty-purge process.

### Section 69.3 — Governance Freeze

Administrative cleanup occurring near a major reserved-power vote shall be handled under neutral rules designed to avoid electorate manipulation.

---

# ARTICLE LXX
## Changes to Membership Standards

### Section 70.1 — Prospective Clarity

Material changes to Covenant Membership standards shall be communicated clearly.

### Section 70.2 — Existing Members

Existing members should ordinarily receive a reasonable transition period where new requirements materially change prior membership commitments.

### Section 70.3 — Foundational Change

Where a constitutional Refounding substantially changes the religious identity of the community, existing members shall not be falsely represented as having assented automatically to beliefs they have not adopted.

### Section 70.4 — Opportunity to Withdraw

A materially affected member shall retain the right to withdraw.

---

# ARTICLE LXXI
## Member Discipline During Doctrinal Change

### Section 71.1 — No Manufactured Retroactivity

A member shall not ordinarily be disciplined for historical beliefs or statements that were permitted when made solely because doctrine later changed.

### Section 71.2 — Continuing Membership

Where newly adopted doctrine becomes a future condition of Covenant Membership, the institution should provide:

- explanation;
- reasonable discernment;
- opportunity for questions;
- opportunity for voluntary transition.

### Section 71.3 — Serious Independent Misconduct

Nothing in this Article protects conduct that violated an independent ethical, safeguarding, or constitutional obligation already applicable at the time.

---

# ARTICLE LXXII
## Membership Administration

### Section 72.1 — Membership Function

The EC shall establish a reliable membership-administration function.

### Section 72.2 — Administrator

A Membership Steward, committee, or administrative office may maintain routine membership operations.

### Section 72.3 — Authority Limits

Administrative personnel may not:

- invent new eligibility standards;
- impose disciplinary termination without authority;
- alter governance eligibility for political purposes;
- create secret membership categories.

### Section 72.4 — Auditability

Material status changes shall identify:

- authority;
- date;
- basis;
- responsible decision-maker.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIII
## Membership Committee

### Section 73.1 — Establishment

The EC may establish a **Membership Committee**.

### Section 73.2 — Functions

The Committee may:

- oversee formation;
- review applications;
- coordinate admissions;
- maintain standards;
- supervise membership administration;
- recommend procedural improvements.

### Section 73.3 — No Discipline Tribunal by Default

The Membership Committee shall not automatically become the final tribunal for serious discipline.

### Section 73.4 — Conflicts

Committee members shall disclose and manage conflicts.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIV
## Local Membership Stewards

Local communities may designate qualified persons to assist with:

- formation;
- applications;
- pastoral integration;
- local records;
- transfer requests.

### Section 74.1 — Central Standards

Local administration remains subject to common Covenant Membership standards.

### Section 74.2 — No Local Electoral Manipulation

A local leader may not manipulate central membership status to increase local influence in the CMA.

---

# ARTICLE LXXV
## Membership Decisions and Reasons

### Section 75.1 — Ordinary Admission

Routine approvals need not contain extensive written findings.

### Section 75.2 — Adverse Decisions

Material adverse membership decisions should ordinarily identify:

- action taken;
- authority;
- principal reason;
- effective date;
- review rights.

### Section 75.3 — Confidentiality

Reasons may be phrased in a way that protects:

- third parties;
- safeguarding information;
- legitimate confidentiality.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVI
## Membership Decision Validity

A significant membership decision shall be valid only where applicable requirements are satisfied concerning:

1. jurisdiction;
2. authorized decision-maker;
3. applicable standard;
4. notice where required;
5. conflicts;
6. due process;
7. required approval;
8. accurate record.

### Section 76.1 — Invalid Manipulation

A membership decision primarily manufactured to manipulate a constitutional vote may be invalid.

### Section 76.2 — Constitutional Review

Material constitutional validity disputes may be submitted to the CCG.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVII
## Emergency Membership Measures

### Section 77.1 — Temporary Measures

Where urgent safety concerns exist, authorized personnel may impose temporary restrictions before completion of ordinary proceedings.

### Section 77.2 — Narrow Scope

Emergency measures shall be no broader than reasonably necessary.

### Section 77.3 — Prompt Review

Emergency measures shall receive prompt review under applicable safeguarding or disciplinary rules.

### Section 77.4 — No Permanent Emergency Status

A member shall not remain indefinitely under an unreviewed emergency restriction.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVIII
## Institutional Duty Toward Members

The Religious Body shall seek to provide Covenant Members with:

- truthful representation of membership obligations;
- usable governance processes;
- appropriate religious formation;
- safe participation;
- fair process;
- accessible grievance channels;
- responsible recordkeeping;
- leadership accountability;
- freedom from improper spiritual coercion.

### Section 78.1 — No Guarantee of Agreement

The institution does not guarantee that every member will agree with every valid decision.

### Section 78.2 — No Guarantee of Office

Membership does not guarantee status, appointment, or leadership opportunity.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIX
## Member Duty Toward the Institution

Covenant Membership carries responsibility not to misuse membership rights in ways fundamentally contrary to their purpose.

This includes refraining from:

- deliberate governance fraud;
- vote buying;
- intimidation;
- knowingly false petitions;
- harassment of nominees;
- disclosure of protected information for factional advantage;
- attempts to seize charitable property personally.

### Section 79.1 — Vigorous Disagreement Preserved

This Article shall not be interpreted to prohibit vigorous good-faith disagreement or constitutional opposition.

---

# ARTICLE LXXX
## Factions and Organized Advocacy

### Section 80.1 — Association

Members may communicate and organize around governance concerns consistent with applicable conduct rules.

### Section 80.2 — No Official Factional Entitlement

A faction does not acquire guaranteed representation merely by organizing.

### Section 80.3 — Coercion Prohibited

Organized advocacy shall not involve:

- threats;
- bribery;
- spiritual intimidation;
- false information knowingly distributed as fact;
- harassment.

### Section 80.4 — Leadership Neutrality

Institutional leadership shall distinguish legitimate institutional communication from use of official resources merely to suppress lawful internal opposition.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXI
## Membership and Institutional Separation

Where serious institutional division arises:

1. members remain individuals with personal religious agency;
2. no leader owns the membership;
3. no faction may compel personal affiliation merely through control of property;
4. individual status shall be handled according to AFF-001;
5. charitable property remains governed separately from personal membership preference.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXII
## Membership and External Religious Affiliations

### Section 82.1 — Multiple Affiliations

Whether Covenant Members may simultaneously affiliate formally with another religious body shall be governed by future doctrine and membership covenant.

### Section 82.2 — No Assumption

No incompatibility shall be presumed solely from ordinary attendance at or relationship with another religious community unless governing doctrine establishes it.

### Section 82.3 — Leadership Roles Distinguished

Ministers and senior religious officers may be subject to stricter affiliation standards than ordinary Covenant Members.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIII
## Membership and Pastoral Care

### Section 83.1 — Access

Covenant Membership may include access to forms of pastoral or spiritual care offered by the community.

### Section 83.2 — No Ownership of Caregiver

Membership creates no right to demand unlimited access to a particular leader.

### Section 83.3 — Boundaries

Pastoral relationships remain subject to:

- capacity;
- ethics;
- safeguarding;
- professional referral where appropriate.

### Section 83.4 — Withdrawal From Pastoral Relationship

A member may decline a particular pastoral relationship without automatically losing membership.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIV
## Safeguarding Responsibilities of Members

Members shall comply with applicable safeguarding policies.

### Section 84.1 — Reporting

Members entrusted with roles subject to reporting obligations shall comply with those obligations.

### Section 84.2 — Screening

Membership alone does not satisfy screening requirements for sensitive roles.

### Section 84.3 — Protective Restrictions

A member may be restricted from a sensitive ministry even where Covenant Membership continues.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXV
## Protected Persons and Membership

No person shall be denied access to:

- safeguarding reporting;
- emergency assistance;
- grievance intake concerning abuse

merely because the person is:

- not a Covenant Member;
- inactive;
- suspended;
- a former member;
- a Visitor;
- a Participant.

Accountability systems protect persons, not merely formal members.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVI
## Membership Disputes Involving Leaders

Where a membership dispute materially involves:

- PSL;
- EC member;
- Membership Committee member;
- local leader

that person shall not control the final adjudication of the dispute where materially conflicted.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVII
## Annual Membership Report

The responsible membership body shall ordinarily provide the EC and CMA with an annual nonconfidential report addressing:

- Covenant Member count;
- admissions;
- withdrawals;
- inactive status;
- geographic/local distribution;
- significant administrative issues;
- formation activity;
- systemic membership concerns.

Sensitive individual information shall be protected.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVIII
## Periodic Review of the Membership System

At least every three years, the EC should review whether:

1. affiliation categories remain useful;
2. admission is understandable;
3. membership requirements remain appropriate;
4. membership rights are usable;
5. records are accurate;
6. governance eligibility is fair;
7. anti-packing protections are working;
8. inactive-member procedures are healthy;
9. discipline is being misused;
10. members understand the statutory/corporate distinction;
11. local-community processes remain consistent;
12. unnecessary barriers to meaningful belonging have arisen.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIX
## Membership-System Failure Modes

The Religious Body shall guard against:

### 1. Inflated Membership

Treating every attendee as a formal member.

### 2. Closed-Circle Membership

Using admission to preserve a leadership faction.

### 3. Electoral Packing

Rapidly admitting expected supporters before major votes.

### 4. Electoral Purging

Disciplining expected opponents to alter a vote.

### 5. Financial Membership

Equating donations with religious standing.

### 6. Loyalty-Test Membership

Requiring personal loyalty to leaders rather than legitimate religious commitments.

### 7. Pretended Assent

Pressuring persons to claim beliefs they do not hold.

### 8. Permanent Inactivity

Keeping thousands of unreachable persons on voting rolls indefinitely.

### 9. Administrative Discipline

Using clerical status changes to accomplish punishment without due process.

### 10. Membership as Ownership

Treating members as owners of charitable property.

### 11. Membership as Clergy

Assuming formal membership automatically qualifies a person for religious authority.

### 12. Statutory Drift

Accidentally creating corporate membership rights while claiming to have no statutory members.

---

# ARTICLE XC
## Membership Decision Quality Standard

Before taking a materially adverse membership action, the responsible body should ask:

1. What membership status is involved?
2. What rule applies?
3. Who has jurisdiction?
4. Is this administrative or disciplinary?
5. Is safeguarding implicated?
6. Is the decision-maker conflicted?
7. Has the person received fair notice where required?
8. Has the person's response been considered?
9. Is the action proportionate?
10. Is protected conduct involved?
11. Does the action affect CMA eligibility?
12. Is a major CMA vote pending?
13. Could the action reasonably appear designed to manipulate the electorate?

# ARTICLE XCV
## Institutional Objective

The membership system must make it possible for a person to say:

> **“I belong to this religious community.”**

without thereby saying:

> **“I own part of the corporation.”**

It must permit the community to say:

> **“Covenant Membership has real commitments.”**

without saying:

> **“You must surrender your conscience to leadership.”**

It must permit members to exercise meaningful constitutional power without converting every aspect of religious life into electoral politics.

It must permit leaders to maintain genuine religious standards without allowing membership discipline to become a method of political voter control.

It must permit the tradition to preserve its identity while remaining capable of honest inquiry, correction, and voluntary departure.

It must distinguish:

> attendance from participation,

> participation from adherence,

> adherence from covenant,

> covenant from office,

> religious membership from corporate membership,

> disagreement from misconduct,

> inactivity from discipline,

> restoration from historical erasure.

Its ultimate constitutional purpose is therefore:

> **to create a form of religious belonging that is voluntary enough to respect conscience, substantial enough to carry meaningful commitments, participatory enough to create shared responsibility, bounded enough to preserve competent governance, and protected enough that neither leaders nor factions can casually manipulate who counts as the community.**
