# Covenant Member Assembly Charter
## [[CORP_NAME]]

---

# PREAMBLE

A religious community should not be governed as though its members were merely an audience.

Nor should every question of doctrine, administration, finance, ministry, discipline, personnel, or religious practice become a referendum.

The Covenant Member Assembly therefore exists between two opposite errors.

It rejects:

> **governance in which the religious community possesses no meaningful constitutional voice.**

It also rejects:

> **governance in which every exercise of competent leadership is subordinated to continuous plebiscitary politics.**

The Covenant Member Assembly is the principal participatory institution of the Religious Body.

It provides a constitutional means through which Covenant Members may:

- receive information;
- question leadership;
- participate in communal discernment;
- nominate qualified persons;
- submit petitions;
- raise concerns;
- affirm designated leaders;
- exercise specifically reserved powers;
- participate in extraordinary constitutional decisions;
- and hold the governing order accountable to the community it serves.

The Assembly does not possess the religious community.

It does not own charitable assets.

It does not elect the Civil Board merely because it represents the Covenant Members.

It does not determine religious truth merely by counting votes.

It does not become the ordinary executive government.

It does not replace qualified councils, ministers, fiduciaries, investigators, or constitutional guardians.

The Assembly's constitutional purpose is instead:

> **to ensure that the religious community possesses meaningful voice, genuine responsibility, protected participation, and defined powers at precisely those points where broader communal concurrence is necessary for legitimate and durable governance.**

---

# ARTICLE I
## Establishment and Authority

### Section 1.1 — Establishment

The **Covenant Member Assembly**, abbreviated **CMA**, is established pursuant to REL-CON-001 and ECC-CON-001.

### Section 1.2 — Institutional Character

The CMA is an ecclesiastical participatory assembly.

### Section 1.3 — Governing Authority

The CMA operates subject to:

1. REL-CON-001 — Foundational Religious Constitution;
2. ECC-CON-001 — Ecclesiastical Constitution;
3. MEM-001 — Religious Membership Framework;
4. this Charter;
5. applicable constitutional interpretations;
6. applicable ecclesiastical procedure.

### Section 1.4 — Subordination

The CMA possesses only authority granted by the governing corpus.

### Section 1.5 — No Residual Sovereignty

Authority not expressly assigned to the CMA does not belong to the CMA merely because Covenant Members constitute the broader religious community.

---

# ARTICLE II
## Ecclesiastical Rather Than Statutory Character

### Section 2.1 — No Statutory Corporate Membership

The Corporation has no statutory members under its Charter.

### Section 2.2 — Religious Assembly

Participation in the CMA arises from ecclesiastical Covenant Membership rather than statutory corporate membership.

### Section 2.3 — No Recurring Director Election Right

The CMA shall not, merely through this Charter, possess a recurring right to:

- elect Civil Board directors;
- appoint Civil Board directors;
- remove Civil Board directors.

### Section 2.4 — No Corporate Ownership

CMA participation creates no:

- ownership interest;
- proprietary interest;
- distributive right;
- interest in corporate assets.

### Section 2.5 — Ecclesiastical Reserved Powers

Votes cast through the CMA constitute exercises of internal religious constitutional authority.

### Section 2.6 — Separate Corporate Action

Where a CMA decision concerns a matter also requiring civil corporate action, the competent corporate body must separately perform that act.

### Section 2.7 — No Statutory Representation

The CMA shall not publicly represent itself as the statutory membership meeting of the Corporation while the Charter states that the Corporation has no statutory members.

---

# ARTICLE III
## Mission

The CMA exists to:

1. give Covenant Members meaningful constitutional participation;
2. provide structured communication between leaders and members;
3. enable communal discernment;
4. exercise reserved powers;
5. protect extraordinary decisions from narrow institutional capture;
6. receive governance and accountability reports;
7. provide legitimate channels for petitions;
8. provide nomination and affirmation functions;
9. protect members from manufactured consent;
10. create institutional legitimacy without making popularity the measure of religious truth.

---

# ARTICLE IV
## Nature of Assembly Authority

CMA authority may be:

1. **informational**;
2. **consultative**;
3. **deliberative**;
4. **nominating**;
5. **affirmative**;
6. **petitionary**;
7. **reserved-power authority**.

### Section 4.1 — Informational

The CMA may receive reports and information.

### Section 4.2 — Consultative

The CMA may provide views without possessing final authority.

### Section 4.3 — Deliberative

The CMA may participate in structured communal consideration.

### Section 4.4 — Nominating

The CMA may nominate candidates where governing instruments authorize.

### Section 4.5 — Affirmative

The CMA may affirm selections made by another competent body where required.

### Section 4.6 — Petitionary

The CMA or qualifying groups of members may require formal consideration of specified matters.

### Section 4.7 — Reserved-Power Authority

For constitutionally designated matters, CMA approval is required for valid ecclesiastical action.

---

# ARTICLE V
## Matters Outside Ordinary CMA Authority

The CMA shall not ordinarily:

1. administer staff;
2. manage finances;
3. approve routine expenditures;
4. negotiate contracts;
5. control bank accounts;
6. direct individual employees;
7. determine individual pastoral assignments;
8. adjudicate factual misconduct;
9. conduct safeguarding investigations;
10. create Formal Doctrine by ordinary majority vote;
11. appoint Civil Board directors by recurring election;
12. manage ordinary ministry programming;
13. substitute itself for the EC, EAC, CCG, or Civil Board.

---

# ARTICLE VI
## Composition

### Section 6.1 — Assembly Body

The CMA consists of Covenant Members as defined by MEM-001.

### Section 6.2 — Voting Body

Formal reserved-power voting is exercised only by **Governance-Eligible Covenant Members**.

### Section 6.3 — Nonvoting Covenant Members

Covenant Members not presently governance-eligible may ordinarily:

- attend;
- receive reports;
- listen;
- submit questions;
- participate in designated deliberative portions;
- exercise nonvoting rights granted elsewhere.

### Section 6.4 — Other Attendees

Visitors, Participants, Adherents, candidates, advisers, staff, or guests may attend portions designated as open.

### Section 6.5 — Closed Sessions

Sensitive portions may be restricted to:

- Covenant Members;
- Governance-Eligible Covenant Members;
- designated officers;
- authorized advisers.

---

# ARTICLE VII
## Governance Eligibility

A person is governance-eligible when the requirements of MEM-001 are satisfied.

These ordinarily include:

1. age eighteen or older;
2. Covenant Membership;
3. Good Standing;
4. at least ninety days of continuous Covenant Membership;
5. absence of a valid governance-participation suspension;
6. inclusion on the applicable certified voter roll.

### Section 7.1 — No Leadership Discretion

Governance eligibility shall not depend upon whether leadership approves of a member's anticipated vote.

### Section 7.2 — Equal Vote

Each eligible member receives one vote.

---

# ARTICLE VIII
## Governance-Eligible Roll

### Section 8.1 — Certified Roll

Every formal CMA vote shall use a certified **Governance-Eligible Roll**.

### Section 8.2 — Contents

The Roll shall contain sufficient information to identify each eligible voter reliably.

### Section 8.3 — Privacy

The Roll shall not unnecessarily disclose:

- addresses;
- phone numbers;
- private disciplinary information;
- pastoral information;
- sensitive personal information.

### Section 8.4 — Official Status

No unofficial membership list shall control voting eligibility.

---

# ARTICLE IX
## Record Date

### Section 9.1 — Record Date Required

Every formal reserved-power vote shall have a defined **Record Date**.

### Section 9.2 — Effect

Only persons satisfying governance eligibility on the applicable Record Date may vote on that matter, except to correct administrative error.

### Section 9.3 — Ordinary Default

Unless another governing rule applies, the Record Date shall ordinarily be **thirty days before voting begins**.

### Section 9.4 — Leadership Confirmation

A PSL confirmation vote may use a Record Date twenty-one to thirty days before voting.

### Section 9.5 — Constitutional Matters

For Class D or E matters, an earlier Record Date may be established to protect electorate integrity.

### Section 9.6 — Neutrality

Record dates shall be established under neutral rules rather than after leaders know which particular members support an expected outcome.

---

# ARTICLE X
## Electorate Freeze

### Section 10.1 — Freeze

Once the Record Date passes, the electorate for that particular decision is ordinarily fixed.

### Section 10.2 — Later Admission

A person may still become a Covenant Member after the Record Date.

Such admission does not confer voting eligibility retroactively for that matter.

### Section 10.3 — Withdrawal

A member who voluntarily withdraws before casting a ballot ceases to be eligible to vote.

### Section 10.4 — Death or Incapacity

A deceased person cannot cast or retain a vote.

### Section 10.5 — Status Changes

Post-Record-Date status changes shall not ordinarily alter the electorate unless:

- required for urgent safeguarding;
- required by a final disciplinary action;
- necessary to correct administrative error.

### Section 10.6 — Heightened Review

Any governance-disqualifying status action occurring after the Record Date for a Class D or E matter shall receive heightened documentation and constitutional review if challenged.

---

# ARTICLE XI
## Anti-Packing Protection

No person or institution shall manipulate admissions principally to determine the outcome of a CMA vote.

Potential indicators include:

- expedited mass admissions;
- selective waiver of formation;
- admissions conditioned upon promised votes;
- coordinated backdating;
- unusual admission surges immediately before a Record Date.

### Section 11.1 — Challenge

Credible allegations may be referred to the CCG.

### Section 11.2 — Remedy

The CCG may order appropriate remedies including:

- corrected voter roll;
- delayed vote;
- investigation;
- invalidation of improperly manufactured eligibility.

---

# ARTICLE XII
## Anti-Purging Protection

No person or institution shall manipulate:

- suspension;
- inactivity;
- discipline;
- transfer;
- termination;
- record correction

principally to remove expected opposition from the electorate.

### Section 12.1 — Legitimate Accountability Preserved

Actual misconduct remains subject to valid safeguarding and disciplinary process.

### Section 12.2 — Heightened Scrutiny

Governance-affecting adverse actions taken near an extraordinary vote may receive heightened constitutional scrutiny.

---

# ARTICLE XIII
## Eligibility Certification

### Section 13.1 — Administrator

The Membership Administrator shall prepare the preliminary Governance-Eligible Roll.

### Section 13.2 — Election Steward Review

For major reserved-power votes, an independent Election Steward shall review or certify the Roll.

### Section 13.3 — Certification Date

The certified Roll should ordinarily be completed sufficiently before voting for eligibility challenges to be resolved.

### Section 13.4 — No Secret Changes

Material post-certification changes shall be logged.

---

# ARTICLE XIV
## Eligibility Challenges

### Section 14.1 — Personal Challenge

A person may challenge their exclusion from the Governance-Eligible Roll.

### Section 14.2 — Third-Party Challenge

A member may challenge another person's eligibility only upon a stated good-faith basis.

### Section 14.3 — Privacy

Challenges shall not become a means of exposing confidential information publicly.

### Section 14.4 — Administrative Resolution

Ordinary clerical errors may be resolved administratively.

### Section 14.5 — Disputed Constitutional Question

Material unresolved disputes may be referred to the CCG.

### Section 14.6 — No Vote Delay for Triviality

Minor challenges affecting no plausible outcome need not delay an entire Assembly unnecessarily.

---

# ARTICLE XV
## Annual Assembly

### Section 15.1 — Required Assembly

The CMA shall convene at least once each calendar year.

### Section 15.2 — Purposes

The annual Assembly should ordinarily include:

1. state-of-the-religious-body report;
2. EC report;
3. PSL report;
4. EAC accountability report;
5. CCG constitutional report;
6. membership report;
7. appropriate Civil Board report;
8. question-and-response period;
9. petitions;
10. nominations where scheduled;
11. community discernment;
12. reserved-power matters if pending.

### Section 15.3 — Not Merely Ceremonial

The annual Assembly shall include meaningful opportunity for member participation.

---

# ARTICLE XVI
## Annual Accountability Session

### Section 16.1 — Requirement

At least annually, senior ecclesiastical leadership shall participate in a structured **Community Accountability Session**.

### Section 16.2 — Participants

The session should ordinarily include representatives of:

- PSL;
- EC;
- EAC;
- CCG;
- Civil Board where appropriate.

### Section 16.3 — Questions

Members shall have a reasonable mechanism to submit questions.

### Section 16.4 — Unscreened Question Opportunity

A reasonable portion of the session should permit questions not chosen solely by senior leadership.

### Section 16.5 — Moderation

Questions may be moderated for:

- relevance;
- duplication;
- confidentiality;
- safety;
- harassment;
- time.

### Section 16.6 — No Retaliation

Good-faith difficult questions shall not constitute misconduct.

---

# ARTICLE XVII
## Special Assemblies

A special CMA may be called by:

1. the EC;
2. the PSL;
3. the CCG for a constitutional matter;
4. the EAC for a matter within its constitutional role;
5. a valid Covenant Member petition.

### Section 17.1 — Defined Purpose

The notice shall identify the principal purpose.

### Section 17.2 — Limited Scope

Binding action at a special Assembly should ordinarily be limited to matters reasonably identified in the notice.

---

# ARTICLE XVIII
## Member-Called Special Assembly

### Section 18.1 — Petition Threshold

A member petition for a special Assembly requires signatures from at least:

> **ten percent of Governance-Eligible Covenant Members or twenty Governance-Eligible Covenant Members, whichever is fewer.**

### Section 18.2 — Minimum Support

Where the total governance-eligible electorate is fewer than twenty persons, at least three eligible members shall ordinarily be required unless the matter concerns the petitioner's personal constitutional rights.

### Section 18.3 — Verification

Signatures or authenticated endorsements shall be verified neutrally.

### Section 18.4 — Scheduling

A valid petition shall ordinarily result in an Assembly within sixty days unless:

- a higher constitutional study period applies;
- emergency conditions require earlier action;
- the petition requests a later date.

### Section 18.5 — No Leadership Veto

A valid petition shall not be rejected merely because leadership opposes its subject.

---

# ARTICLE XIX
## Petitions for Formal Consideration

### Section 19.1 — Right

Covenant Members may petition competent governing bodies.

### Section 19.2 — Threshold

The threshold in ECC-CON-001 applies:

> ten percent of eligible Covenant Members or twenty eligible Covenant Members, whichever is fewer.

### Section 19.3 — Effect

A valid petition requires **formal consideration**.

It does not automatically enact the requested action.

### Section 19.4 — Required Response

The competent body shall ordinarily:

- consider;
- refer;
- adopt;
- reject;
- request clarification;
- initiate study.

### Section 19.5 — Reasoned Disposition

A substantial petition should receive a recorded disposition and brief reason.

---

# ARTICLE XX
## Petition Subject Matter

Petitions may concern:

- doctrine review;
- governance;
- leadership concerns;
- constitutional interpretation;
- membership administration;
- institutional policy;
- safeguarding systems;
- local-community concerns;
- affiliation;
- proposed amendments;
- other matters within ecclesiastical competence.

### Section 20.1 — Inappropriate Petition Uses

A petition shall not be used to:

- adjudicate criminal guilt;
- publicly expose confidential safeguarding records;
- direct unlawful conduct;
- bypass individual due process;
- seize private property;
- compel disclosure of protected pastoral information.

---

# ARTICLE XXI
## No Direct Legislation by Petition Except Where Reserved

A petition requires consideration.

It does not ordinarily become binding merely because sufficient signatures were collected.

Direct CMA enactment is limited to:

- constitutionally reserved powers;
- matters expressly delegated to the CMA;
- internal CMA procedural matters.

---

# ARTICLE XXII
## Assembly Officers

The CMA shall have:

1. a **Convener**;
2. a **Deputy Convener**;
3. an **Assembly Clerk**;
4. an **Election Steward** for formal ballots where required.

---

# ARTICLE XXIII
## Convener

### Section 23.1 — Role

The Convener facilitates the Assembly.

### Section 23.2 — Selection

The mature CMA shall elect or affirm a Convener for a two-year term through a procedure established by CMA-PROC-001.

### Section 23.3 — Interim Convener

Before mature election procedures exist, the EC may designate an interim Convener.

### Section 23.4 — Duties

The Convener shall:

- convene sessions;
- facilitate deliberation;
- protect fair participation;
- enforce legitimate procedural rules;
- preserve neutrality in voting administration;
- coordinate with the Clerk.

### Section 23.5 — No Ecclesiastical Supremacy

The Convener possesses no general religious governing authority.

---

# ARTICLE XXIV
## Convener Neutrality

When presiding over a reserved-power vote, the Convener shall distinguish:

- procedural facilitation;
- personal advocacy.

### Section 24.1 — Advocacy

A Convener may possess personal views.

If engaging substantially in advocacy, another neutral presider should ordinarily facilitate that portion.

### Section 24.2 — No Manipulation

The Convener shall not use procedural control to:

- silence one side;
- shorten debate selectively;
- exclude eligible voters;
- misstate a motion;
- manipulate vote timing.

---

# ARTICLE XXV
## Deputy Convener

The Deputy Convener shall:

- act when the Convener is absent or conflicted;
- assist with Assembly administration;
- perform duties delegated by the CMA.

---

# ARTICLE XXVI
## Assembly Clerk

The Clerk shall:

1. maintain Assembly records;
2. receive formal petitions;
3. coordinate notices;
4. maintain agendas;
5. preserve vote certifications;
6. maintain minutes;
7. preserve resolutions;
8. coordinate records transfer.

### Section 26.1 — Clerk Neutrality

The Clerk shall not alter official records to favor a faction.

---

# ARTICLE XXVII
## Election Steward

### Section 27.1 — Purpose

The Election Steward protects integrity of formal CMA voting.

### Section 27.2 — Independence

For Class D or E votes and PSL selection or removal, the Steward should ordinarily be independent of:

- the candidate;
- principal advocates;
- Membership Administrator;
- PSL office.

### Section 27.3 — Duties

The Steward may:

- certify electorate;
- supervise ballots;
- protect ballot secrecy;
- count votes;
- coordinate observers;
- certify results;
- preserve election records;
- administer recounts.

### Section 27.4 — External Steward

A qualified external election administrator may be used for especially sensitive votes.

---

# ARTICLE XXVIII
## Assembly Administrative Committee

### Section 28.1 — Establishment

The CMA may maintain a small **Assembly Administrative Committee**.

### Section 28.2 — Functions

It may coordinate:

- logistics;
- accessibility;
- communication;
- petition administration;
- nominations;
- agenda administration;
- election support.

### Section 28.3 — No General Governance Authority

The Committee shall not exercise reserved powers on behalf of the CMA.

### Section 28.4 — Balanced Composition

Its composition should not be controlled entirely by one governing office.

---

# ARTICLE XXIX
## Regular Notice

### Section 29.1 — Annual Assembly

At least thirty days' notice should ordinarily be provided.

### Section 29.2 — Ordinary Special Assembly

At least fourteen days' notice should ordinarily be provided.

### Section 29.3 — PSL Confirmation

At least twenty-one days' notice should ordinarily be provided after the nominee is formally presented.

### Section 29.4 — PSL Removal

At least thirty days' notice should ordinarily be provided after the matter becomes eligible for CMA consideration, except where protective suspension remains separately effective.

### Section 29.5 — Class D Matter

At least sixty days' notice should ordinarily precede final CMA voting.

### Section 29.6 — Class E Matter

At least one hundred twenty days' notice should ordinarily precede final CMA voting, in addition to any longer constitutional study period.

### Section 29.7 — Higher Rule Controls

Where REL-CON-001 or ECC-CON-001 requires a longer process, the longer period controls.

---

# ARTICLE XXX
## Notice Contents

Formal notice shall identify:

1. date;
2. time;
3. location or remote-access method;
4. matters to be considered;
5. whether voting will occur;
6. Record Date;
7. eligibility requirements;
8. voting period;
9. participation threshold;
10. approval threshold;
11. availability of explanatory materials;
12. challenge procedures.

### Section 30.1 — Plain Language

Notice should be understandable without specialized legal knowledge.

---

# ARTICLE XXXI
## Notice Methods

Notice may be provided through one or more reliable methods including:

- email;
- member portal;
- postal mail;
- secure electronic communication;
- personal delivery.

### Section 31.1 — Redundancy for Major Votes

For Class D or E matters, at least two reasonable communication methods should ordinarily be used where practicable.

### Section 31.2 — Contact Responsibility

Members should maintain reasonably current contact information.

---

# ARTICLE XXXII
## Agenda

### Section 32.1 — Published Agenda

An agenda shall ordinarily be distributed before each Assembly.

### Section 32.2 — Member-Proposed Items

Members may propose agenda items through procedure established by CMA-PROC-001.

### Section 32.3 — Petitioned Matters

A valid petitioned matter shall receive appropriate agenda placement.

### Section 32.4 — Confidential Matters

Confidential matters may be listed generically where disclosure would cause legitimate harm.

### Section 32.5 — No Surprise Extraordinary Votes

A Class D or E decision shall not be introduced and finally approved without required notice.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIII
## Assembly Materials

For substantial decisions, members should receive enough information to exercise responsible judgment.

Materials may include:

- question presented;
- governing authority;
- proposal;
- reasons;
- significant alternatives;
- supporting analysis;
- contrary analysis;
- institutional implications;
- required threshold;
- relevant reports.

### Section 33.1 — Information Neutrality

Official information should distinguish factual institutional information from advocacy.

### Section 33.2 — Material Omission

Leadership shall not knowingly omit a material nonconfidential fact for the purpose of producing a desired vote.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIV
## Member Access to Competing Views

### Section 34.1 — Major Decisions

For a disputed Class D or E matter, members should ordinarily have reasonable access to serious supporting and opposing analysis.

### Section 34.2 — No False Equivalence

The institution need not manufacture an opposing argument where none exists responsibly.

### Section 34.3 — Minority Reports

Formal EC minority reports concerning a matter submitted to the CMA should ordinarily be made available in appropriate form.

---

# ARTICLE XXXV
## Deliberative Purpose

The Assembly shall seek:

- understanding;
- clarification;
- community wisdom;
- responsible exercise of constitutional authority.

It shall not be conducted merely as a political rally before predetermined voting.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVI
## Discernment Process

For significant matters, the CMA should ordinarily proceed through:

1. statement of the question;
2. explanation of constitutional authority;
3. presentation of relevant information;
4. presentation of significant alternatives;
5. member questions;
6. discussion;
7. reflection;
8. articulation of areas of agreement and disagreement;
9. formal vote where required.

### Section 36.1 — Time Proportionate to Importance

More consequential decisions should receive greater deliberative opportunity.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVII
## Sense of the Assembly

### Section 37.1 — Nonbinding Discernment Tool

The Convener may attempt to articulate the emerging **Sense of the Assembly**.

### Section 37.2 — Testing

Members may indicate whether the articulation accurately reflects the discussion.

### Section 37.3 — No Substitution for Required Vote

Where the Constitution requires a formal vote, an asserted Sense of the Assembly cannot replace it.

### Section 37.4 — No Manufactured Consensus

The Convener shall not declare unanimity merely because dissenters ceased speaking.

---

# ARTICLE XXXVIII
## Debate and Participation

### Section 38.1 — Fair Opportunity

Members shall have meaningful opportunity to participate.

### Section 38.2 — Time Limits

Reasonable time limits may be used to protect fairness.

### Section 38.3 — Repetition

Repeated arguments may be limited after fair opportunity has been provided.

### Section 38.4 — Personal Attack

Participants shall not engage in:

- threats;
- harassment;
- demeaning personal attacks;
- disclosure of protected private information.

### Section 38.5 — Vigorous Disagreement

Strong disagreement remains permissible.

---

# ARTICLE XXXIX
## Spiritual Coercion in Governance

No leader or member shall use religious authority improperly to compel a vote.

Improper coercion may include:

- threatening spiritual condemnation merely for a lawful vote;
- claiming that voting against a leader is inherently rebellion against the sacred;
- conditioning pastoral access upon political support;
- threatening ministry retaliation;
- using confidential pastoral information to pressure a voter.

### Section 39.1 — Religious Teaching Distinguished

This Article does not prohibit leaders from sincerely teaching religious principles relevant to the decision.

The distinction concerns improper personal coercion rather than religious reasoning.

---

# ARTICLE XL
## Organized Advocacy

### Section 40.1 — Permitted Advocacy

Members may organize and advocate concerning CMA matters.

### Section 40.2 — Prohibited Conduct

Advocacy shall not involve:

- bribery;
- threats;
- knowing material falsehood;
- harassment;
- misuse of confidential records;
- vote buying.

### Section 40.3 — Factions

Organized groups acquire no additional constitutional votes.

---

# ARTICLE XLI
## Use of Institutional Resources

### Section 41.1 — Official Communication

Institutional resources may be used to:

- explain proposals;
- distribute official information;
- facilitate discussion;
- present leadership recommendations.

### Section 41.2 — Fairness

For a genuinely contested Class D or E decision, official resources should not be used in a manner that makes meaningful contrary communication practically impossible.

### Section 41.3 — Leadership Advocacy

Leadership may advocate for a position while clearly distinguishing:

- institutional fact;
- governing authority;
- personal or collective recommendation.

---

# ARTICLE XLII
## Voting Principles

Formal CMA voting shall be:

- one eligible member, one vote;
- independently cast;
- accurately counted;
- verifiable as to eligibility;
- confidential where required;
- documented sufficiently to establish validity.

---

# ARTICLE XLIII
## Voting Methods

Voting may occur through:

- in-person ballot;
- secure electronic ballot;
- absentee ballot;
- another authenticated method approved by procedure.

### Section 43.1 — Reliability

The method shall provide reasonable assurance concerning:

- voter identity;
- ballot integrity;
- duplicate-vote prevention;
- confidentiality where required;
- accurate counting.

---

# ARTICLE XLIV
## No Proxy Voting

### Section 44.1 — General Rule

Proxy voting is prohibited unless a future constitutional amendment expressly authorizes it.

### Section 44.2 — Personal Exercise

Each member shall cast their own vote.

### Section 44.3 — Accessibility Distinguished

Assistance provided to a member with a disability does not constitute proxy voting where the member independently determines the vote.

---

# ARTICLE XLV
## Secret Ballot

Secret or confidential ballot shall ordinarily be used for:

1. PSL confirmation;
2. PSL removal;
3. contested leadership affirmations;
4. sensitive individual-office matters;
5. another matter where fear of retaliation could materially impair independent voting.

### Section 45.1 — Ballot Secrecy

The system should prevent ordinary leadership from learning how an individual voted.

### Section 45.2 — Verification

Ballot secrecy does not eliminate verification that only eligible persons voted.

---

# ARTICLE XLVI
## Open Voting

Open voting may be used for:

- procedural matters;
- routine CMA resolutions;
- noncontroversial affirmations;
- other matters where transparency outweighs secrecy concerns.

---

# ARTICLE XLVII
## Voting Period

### Section 47.1 — In-Person Vote

Voting may occur during the Assembly.

### Section 47.2 — Extended Ballot

Electronic or absentee voting may remain open for a defined period.

### Section 47.3 — Ordinary Range

An extended ballot should ordinarily remain open between three and fourteen days depending upon the matter.

### Section 47.4 — Equal Opportunity

All eligible voters shall receive substantially equal opportunity to vote.

---

# ARTICLE XLVIII
## Participation Threshold

### Section 48.1 — Purpose

Extraordinary decisions should not be determined by a small fraction of an otherwise active electorate.

### Section 48.2 — Ordinary Reserved Powers

Unless another rule applies, a reserved-power vote requires participation by at least **forty percent of the Governance-Eligible Roll**.

### Section 48.3 — PSL Confirmation

PSL confirmation requires participation by at least **forty percent** of eligible voters.

### Section 48.4 — PSL Removal

PSL removal requires participation by at least **fifty percent** of eligible voters.

### Section 48.5 — Class D Constitutional Matter

A Class D reserved-power vote requires participation by at least **fifty percent** of eligible voters.

### Section 48.6 — Class E Foundational Matter

A Class E vote requires participation by at least **sixty percent** of eligible voters unless a higher constitutional requirement applies.

### Section 48.7 — Early-Stage Exception

Where the Religious Body has fewer than twenty Governance-Eligible Covenant Members, the applicable procedure may use a lower numerical participation minimum if:

- every eligible member receives actual reasonable notice;
- the adjustment is adopted before the outcome is known;
- the adjustment does not reduce the substantive approval threshold.

---

# ARTICLE XLIX
## Effect of Failure to Meet Participation Threshold

If the participation threshold is not met:

- the proposal fails for lack of sufficient communal participation;
- the result shall not be characterized as substantive rejection unless the vote itself demonstrates rejection;
- the matter may be resubmitted according to applicable procedure.

### Section 49.1 — No Endless Immediate Revotes

Repeated immediate ballots shall not be used merely to wear down nonparticipating members.

---

# ARTICLE L
## Valid Votes Cast

For purposes of approval thresholds:

**Valid Votes Cast** means ballots validly recorded as:

- Yes / Approve;
- No / Reject.

### Section 50.1 — Abstentions

A formally recorded abstention counts toward participation where procedure permits but is not an affirmative vote.

### Section 50.2 — Blank Ballots

A blank or invalid ballot does not count as an affirmative vote.

### Section 50.3 — Participation Calculation

CMA-PROC-001 shall define consistently whether formal abstentions count toward participation thresholds.

---

# ARTICLE LI
## Ordinary CMA Decisions

Where this Charter authorizes an ordinary internal CMA decision and no higher rule applies, approval requires:

> **more affirmative than negative valid votes cast**

provided the applicable participation requirement is met.

---

# ARTICLE LII
## Reserved-Power Threshold

Unless a higher constitutional threshold applies, a CMA reserved-power decision requires:

> **at least two-thirds of Valid Votes Cast**

together with the required participation threshold.

---

# ARTICLE LIII
## Foundational Threshold

Where ECC-CON-001 requires foundational CMA concurrence, approval requires:

> **at least three-fourths of Valid Votes Cast**

together with the required Class E participation threshold and all applicable study and notice requirements.

---

# ARTICLE LIV
## Reserved Powers

The CMA possesses reserved-power concurrence concerning:

1. selection of a permanent PSL;
2. removal of the PSL;
3. amendment of REL-CON-001;
4. amendment of Entrenched Constitutional Commitments where authorized;
5. constitutional Refounding;
6. dissolution of the Religious Body;
7. merger fundamentally affecting religious identity;
8. formal affiliation surrendering superior ecclesiastical authority;
9. voluntary disaffiliation from such superior authority;
10. disposition of designated Principal Sacred Property;
11. other matters expressly assigned by higher constitutional instrument.

---

# ARTICLE LV
## PSL Confirmation

### Section 55.1 — Prior Selection

The CMA does not ordinarily select the PSL from an open field.

The EC first selects a candidate through the constitutional succession process.

### Section 55.2 — CMA Question

The CMA determines whether to confirm the selected candidate.

### Section 55.3 — Threshold

Confirmation requires:

- at least two-thirds of Valid Votes Cast;
- required participation under Article XLVIII.

### Section 55.4 — Failure

If confirmation fails:

- the candidate is not installed;
- the selection process returns to the competent succession body;
- the CMA does not automatically choose an alternative candidate.

### Section 55.5 — No Indefinite Resubmission

The same candidate should not be resubmitted repeatedly without substantial intervening reason.

---

# ARTICLE LVI
## PSL Removal

### Section 56.1 — Adjudication Before Community Vote

The CMA shall not ordinarily investigate the underlying allegations.

### Section 56.2 — Required Basis

A removal vote shall occur only after the constitutional prerequisites in ECC-CON-001 and ACCOUNT-001 are satisfied.

### Section 56.3 — Member Information

Members shall receive enough reliable information to exercise the reserved power responsibly while protecting:

- victims;
- witnesses;
- confidential evidence;
- legal requirements.

### Section 56.4 — Threshold

Removal requires:

- at least two-thirds of Valid Votes Cast;
- at least fifty percent electorate participation;
- all other constitutional approvals.

### Section 56.5 — No Trial by Assembly

The Assembly shall not become a public cross-examination forum for complainants or witnesses.

---

# ARTICLE LVII
## Amendment of ECC-CON-001

Where ECC-CON-001 requires CMA concurrence, the Assembly shall vote upon the amendment as presented after:

- required notice;
- constitutional study;
- EC approval;
- appropriate explanatory materials.

### Section 57.1 — Threshold

Required concurrence shall be at least two-thirds unless a higher constitutional provision applies.

---

# ARTICLE LVIII
## Amendment of REL-CON-001

### Section 58.1 — Extraordinary Nature

An amendment to REL-CON-001 is a Class E matter.

### Section 58.2 — Study

The minimum study and discernment requirements of ECC-CON-001 apply.

### Section 58.3 — Threshold

CMA approval requires:

> **three-fourths of Valid Votes Cast**

together with the Class E participation threshold.

### Section 58.4 — CCG Certification

The vote is not constitutionally complete until required CCG certification occurs.

---

# ARTICLE LIX
## Refounding

### Section 59.1 — Nature

A Refounding is not an ordinary constitutional amendment.

### Section 59.2 — Community Role

The CMA shall receive:

- Refounding Declaration;
- explanation of principles changing;
- continuity analysis;
- competing views;
- institutional implications.

### Section 59.3 — Extended Discernment

The constitutional one-year process shall be observed where required.

### Section 59.4 — Approval

CMA approval requires the threshold prescribed by ECC-CON-001.

---

# ARTICLE LX
## Dissolution

### Section 60.1 — Foundational Decision

Voluntary dissolution is a foundational decision.

### Section 60.2 — Required Information

Before CMA voting, members should receive reasonable information concerning:

- reason for dissolution;
- alternatives;
- institutional liabilities;
- continuation possibilities;
- disposition of charitable assets;
- effect on ministries and communities.

### Section 60.3 — No Member Distribution

No CMA decision may distribute charitable assets to members merely because dissolution occurs.

### Section 60.4 — Corporate Action

Dissolution requires separate lawful Civil Board action.

---

# ARTICLE LXI
## Merger Fundamentally Affecting Religious Identity

The CMA shall exercise reserved concurrence where a merger would materially alter:

- religious identity;
- constitutional independence;
- foundational governance;
- superior ecclesiastical affiliation.

### Section 61.1 — Transaction Detail

Members should receive enough information to understand the religious consequences rather than merely the transaction's financial terms.

---

# ARTICLE LXII
## External Ecclesiastical Affiliation

Where affiliation would subject the Religious Body to a superior external ecclesiastical authority, CMA approval is required.

### Section 62.1 — Required Disclosure

Members shall receive:

- authority being surrendered;
- authority being retained;
- doctrine or standards implicated;
- property consequences;
- withdrawal rules;
- disciplinary consequences;
- succession implications.

---

# ARTICLE LXIII
## Disaffiliation

Where the Religious Body proposes withdrawal from an external superior ecclesiastical authority, CMA concurrence is required.

The process should address:

- doctrinal implications;
- community implications;
- property;
- clergy credentials;
- pending discipline;
- affiliated institutions.

---

# ARTICLE LXIV
## Principal Sacred Property

### Section 64.1 — Reserved Power

Disposition of property formally designated as Principal Sacred Property requires CMA concurrence.

### Section 64.2 — Nature of CMA Review

The CMA evaluates the matter's religious and communal significance.

### Section 64.3 — Board Authority

The Civil Board separately evaluates:

- title;
- fiduciary duty;
- financing;
- restrictions;
- legal consequences.

### Section 64.4 — Failure of Concurrence

Without required concurrence, the proposed voluntary disposition shall not proceed.

---

# ARTICLE LXV
## Nominations

The CMA may possess nomination roles for:

- EC candidates;
- CCG candidates;
- EAC candidates;
- PSL Selection Commission representatives;
- other offices authorized by governing instrument.

### Section 65.1 — Nomination Is Not Election

Unless expressly provided otherwise, CMA nomination does not itself appoint a person to office.

### Section 65.2 — Qualification

All nominees remain subject to applicable eligibility review.

---

# ARTICLE LXVI
## EC Member Affirmation

Where ECC-CON-001 requires CMA affirmation of an EC candidate:

- the EC or Nominating Committee first completes qualification and selection steps;
- the CMA then affirms or declines the candidate.

### Section 66.1 — Threshold

Affirmation requires a majority of Valid Votes Cast unless a higher rule applies.

### Section 66.2 — Failure

Failure of affirmation returns the vacancy to the nominating process.

---

# ARTICLE LXVII
## CCG Member Affirmation

CMA affirmation of a Guardian requires majority approval after appropriate candidate information and conflict disclosure.

The CMA shall not require a candidate to promise particular constitutional outcomes.

---

# ARTICLE LXVIII
## EAC Member Affirmation

CMA affirmation of an EAC nominee requires majority approval after appropriate information concerning:

- independence;
- qualifications;
- conflicts;
- safeguarding competence.

The CMA shall not demand that a candidate promise to protect either leadership or complainants irrespective of evidence.

---

# ARTICLE LXIX
## Selection Commission Representatives

Where the CMA selects representatives to a PSL Selection Commission, the vote should seek persons capable of:

- confidentiality;
- independent judgment;
- discernment;
- candidate evaluation;
- resisting factional instruction.

Representatives are not bound delegates required to vote according to factional directives.

---

# ARTICLE LXX
## Leadership Recall Petitions

### Section 70.1 — No Direct Recall by Simple Petition

A petition alone does not remove a leader.

### Section 70.2 — Triggering Review

A sufficiently supported petition raising serious concerns may require:

- EC review;
- EAC referral;
- constitutional review;
- leadership evaluation.

### Section 70.3 — Due Process

The petition mechanism shall not bypass required investigation or adjudication.

---

# ARTICLE LXXI
## Member Questions to Leadership

Members may submit questions concerning:

- mission;
- governance;
- doctrine;
- institutional health;
- finances at an appropriate level;
- safeguarding systems;
- leadership succession;
- major decisions.

### Section 71.1 — Response

Leadership should ordinarily answer material good-faith questions unless:

- confidentiality;
- privilege;
- privacy;
- safeguarding;
- pending investigation

requires limitation.

### Section 71.2 — Honest Limitation

Where a question cannot be answered fully, leadership should ordinarily identify the reason rather than falsely claiming that no concern exists.

---

# ARTICLE LXXII
## Community Resolutions

The CMA may adopt nonbinding community resolutions expressing:

- concern;
- encouragement;
- recommendation;
- gratitude;
- request for study;
- communal position.

### Section 72.1 — No Jurisdiction Expansion

A community resolution does not become binding doctrine or corporate law merely through majority approval.

### Section 72.2 — Formal Response

A materially significant resolution directed to a constitutional body should receive appropriate acknowledgment.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIII
## Grievances and Accountability Reports

The Assembly may receive aggregate or institutional reports concerning:

- grievances;
- safeguarding systems;
- accountability;
- constitutional concerns.

### Section 73.1 — Individual Cases

The Assembly shall not ordinarily receive confidential case files.

### Section 73.2 — Systemic Concerns

Aggregate reporting should permit members to assess whether accountability systems function.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIV
## Assembly and Discipline

### Section 74.1 — No Trial by Popular Vote

Individual discipline shall not ordinarily be determined by general Assembly vote.

### Section 74.2 — Reserved Removal Exception

Where CMA concurrence is constitutionally required for removal of the PSL, the CMA acts upon an established disciplinary record rather than becoming the original tribunal.

### Section 74.3 — Confidentiality

Members shall respect legitimate confidentiality concerning disciplinary matters.

---

# ARTICLE LXXV
## Assembly and Doctrine

### Section 75.1 — Teaching Participation

The CMA may participate in:

- study;
- questions;
- consultation;
- testimony;
- communal discernment.

### Section 75.2 — Formal Doctrine

Formal Doctrine remains principally within EC jurisdiction.

### Section 75.3 — Foundational Doctrine

Where Foundational Principles are constitutionally amended, CMA reserved concurrence applies.

### Section 75.4 — Popularity Distinguished

A doctrine's truth is not determined merely by the number of members favoring it.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVI
## Accessibility

The CMA shall seek meaningful participation by members affected by:

- disability;
- geographic distance;
- caregiving;
- illness;
- work obligations;
- military service;
- comparable barriers.

### Section 76.1 — Reasonable Accommodation

Reasonable accommodations may include:

- remote participation;
- accessible formats;
- interpretation;
- assistive technology;
- extended ballot periods.

### Section 76.2 — Ballot Independence

Assistance shall preserve the member's independent vote.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVII
## Remote Participation

### Section 77.1 — Authorized

Assemblies may be:

- physical;
- hybrid;
- fully remote.

### Section 77.2 — Reliability

Technology should permit members reasonably to:

- hear or receive proceedings;
- participate where authorized;
- vote securely.

### Section 77.3 — Technology Failure

Material technical failures affecting substantial participation may justify:

- extension;
- recess;
- reopening voting;
- rescheduling.

---

# ARTICLE LXXVIII
## Absentee Voting

Absentee voting may be used where a formal vote does not reasonably require simultaneous deliberative presence.

### Section 78.1 — Materials

Absentee voters shall receive materially comparable voting information.

### Section 78.2 — Deadline

Ballots must be received by the established deadline.

---

# ARTICLE LXXIX
## Ballot Integrity

The voting system shall reasonably protect against:

- duplicate ballots;
- ineligible voting;
- alteration;
- deletion;
- unauthorized disclosure;
- fabricated ballots.

### Section 79.1 — Auditability

The system should permit verification of aggregate integrity without unnecessarily exposing individual votes.

---

# ARTICLE LXXX
## Vote Counting

### Section 80.1 — Neutral Count

Formal votes shall be counted by:

- Election Steward;
- neutral tellers;
- secure validated electronic system;
- combination thereof.

### Section 80.2 — Observers

For major contested votes, designated observers representing materially different positions may observe counting without compromising ballot secrecy.

### Section 80.3 — Certification

The Election Steward shall certify:

- electorate size;
- ballots cast;
- invalid ballots;
- abstentions where applicable;
- Yes votes;
- No votes;
- participation percentage;
- required threshold;
- result.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXI
## Result Publication

After certification, the CMA shall receive the result.

For major votes, the report should state:

- eligible electorate;
- participation;
- affirmative votes;
- negative votes;
- abstentions where recorded;
- whether threshold was satisfied.

Individual secret ballots shall not be published.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXII
## Tie Votes

Where an ordinary majority matter results in a tie:

> the proposal fails.

A tie does not authorize the Convener, PSL, Founder, or other leader to cast an additional vote unless a higher governing rule expressly provides otherwise.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIII
## Recount

### Section 83.1 — Automatic Recount

An automatic recount should ordinarily occur where the margin is sufficiently small that ordinary counting error could affect the result.

### Section 83.2 — Requested Recount

A recount may be requested by:

- a qualifying number of eligible members;
- an affected candidate;
- Election Steward;
- CCG.

### Section 83.3 — Electronic Audit

Electronic ballots may be reverified through appropriate audit methods.

### Section 83.4 — Final Certification

The recount result supersedes the original count where differences are found.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIV
## Election or Vote Challenges

A formal challenge may allege:

- incorrect voter roll;
- ineligible voting;
- excluded eligible voters;
- ballot tampering;
- coercion;
- material notice failure;
- improper counting;
- improper institutional interference;
- failure to meet participation threshold;
- failure to meet approval threshold.

### Section 84.1 — Deadline

Challenges should ordinarily be submitted within fourteen days after certification unless the violation could not reasonably have been discovered.

### Section 84.2 — Preliminary Review

The Election Steward may resolve ordinary administrative issues.

### Section 84.3 — Constitutional Challenge

Material validity disputes may be referred to the CCG.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXV
## Remedies for Voting Irregularities

Possible remedies include:

- correction without changing outcome;
- revised certification;
- recount;
- inclusion of wrongly excluded ballots where possible;
- exclusion of invalid ballots;
- reopening a ballot;
- repeating a vote;
- invalidating the result.

### Section 85.1 — Materiality

A defect should invalidate the result where it:

- materially compromised fairness;
- could reasonably have affected outcome;
- violated a structural constitutional requirement.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVI
## Member Privacy

### Section 86.1 — Participation Privacy

Attendance and voting information shall be handled responsibly.

### Section 86.2 — Secret Ballot

Individual ballot choices shall not be disclosed where secret voting applies.

### Section 86.3 — Advocacy Lists

The Membership Registry shall not be released wholesale for factional campaigning without valid policy authorization.

### Section 86.4 — Contact

The Religious Body may provide neutral mechanisms through which members can communicate without disclosing unnecessary personal data.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVII
## Confidential Sessions

The CMA may enter a restricted session where discussion involves:

- sensitive leadership matters;
- legal matters;
- safeguarding;
- confidential institutional negotiations;
- protected personal information.

### Section 87.1 — No Secrecy Abuse

Restricted sessions shall not be used merely to avoid embarrassment or legitimate accountability.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXVIII
## Minutes and Assembly Record

The Clerk shall maintain records sufficient to establish:

1. date;
2. notice;
3. attendance or participation;
4. presiding officer;
5. agenda;
6. petitions;
7. reports;
8. resolutions;
9. formal votes;
10. certified results;
11. challenges;
12. significant procedural rulings.

### Section 88.1 — No Verbatim Requirement

Minutes need not be a full transcript.

### Section 88.2 — Permanent Reserved-Power Record

Class D and E decisions and PSL selection/removal votes shall receive permanent retention.

---

# ARTICLE LXXXIX
## Assembly Decision Register

The CMA shall maintain a permanent **Assembly Decision Register**.

> **CMA-DEC-YYYY-###**

Each entry should identify:

- participation;

- effective status;
- required concurrent approvals.

Material petitions shall be tracked in a **CMA Petition Register**.

- petition identifier;

- disposition;
- date of response.

# ARTICLE XCI
## Record Integrity

No officer shall:

- destroy valid ballots prematurely;
- alter vote totals;
- rewrite minutes deceptively;
- manipulate eligibility records;
- conceal a failed threshold.

Material falsification may constitute serious misconduct.

---

# ARTICLE XCII
## Ballot Retention

Ballots and election audit records shall be retained for a period established by CMA-PROC-001.

Class D and E ballot evidence should ordinarily be retained long enough to permit:

- recount;
- challenge;
- constitutional review;
- historical verification.

Where secret ballots are used, retention shall preserve secrecy appropriately.

# ARTICLE XCIII
## Relationship With the Ecclesiastical Council

### Section 93.1 — Complementary Authority

The EC possesses principal conciliar ecclesiastical governance.

The CMA possesses communal participatory and reserved powers.

### Section 93.2 — No Council Supremacy Over Reserved Powers

The EC may not waive CMA concurrence where constitutionally required.

### Section 93.3 — No Assembly Supremacy Over Council Jurisdiction

The CMA may not seize ordinary EC jurisdiction merely because a majority disagrees with an EC decision.

### Section 93.4 — Consultation

The EC may seek CMA consultation on matters not requiring formal concurrence.

---

# ARTICLE XCIV
## Relationship With the PSL

### Section 94.1 — Leadership

The PSL may address and teach the Assembly.

### Section 94.2 — Accountability

The PSL shall participate in annual accountability as required.

### Section 94.3 — Reserved Powers

The PSL cannot waive or veto the CMA's valid reserved powers.

### Section 94.4 — No Personal Electorate

The PSL shall not control membership admission or suspension for the purpose of manufacturing CMA outcomes.

---

# ARTICLE XCV
## Relationship With the CCG

The CCG may review:

- electorate integrity;
- reserved-power jurisdiction;
- participation thresholds;
- notice;
- constitutional vote validity;
- petition rights;
- anti-packing;
- anti-purging;
- procedural constitutional questions.

### Section 95.1 — No Political Appeal

A member may not use the CCG merely to obtain a second vote because the member dislikes a valid CMA result.

---

# ARTICLE XCVI
## Relationship With the EAC

### Section 96.1 — Accountability Reporting

The EAC shall provide appropriate institutional reports to the CMA.

### Section 96.2 — PSL Removal

The EAC may provide findings necessary for responsible CMA consideration.

### Section 96.3 — Independence

The CMA shall not pressure the EAC to reach findings according to popular sentiment.

---

# ARTICLE XCVII
## Relationship With the Civil Board

### Section 97.1 — Distinct Institutions

The CMA is not the Civil Board.

### Section 97.2 — Civil Reporting

The Civil Board should provide appropriate nonconfidential information concerning:

- institutional finances;
- major risks;
- property;
- compliance;
- governance

to the extent useful for members' constitutional responsibilities.

### Section 97.3 — No Director Election by Implication

Consultation, nominations, or recommendations concerning Civil Board composition shall not become recurring CMA election or appointment of directors unless the Charter is intentionally amended after legal review.

### Section 97.4 — Concurrent Matters

Where a reserved CMA decision also requires Board action, both processes remain necessary.

---

# ARTICLE XCVIII
## Relationship With Local Communities

### Section 98.1 — General Assembly

The CMA initially functions as the general community assembly of the Religious Body.

### Section 98.2 — Local Assemblies

Future local communities may establish Local Covenant Assemblies.

### Section 98.3 — Representation

If organizational scale eventually makes one general direct-member assembly impracticable, a future constitutional amendment may establish:

- delegates;
- representatives;
- regional assemblies;
- mixed direct and representative systems.

### Section 98.4 — No Automatic Transition

A representative system shall not arise informally merely because direct participation becomes inconvenient.

---

# ARTICLE XCIX
## Delegate System If Future Growth Requires

Any future delegate system should protect:

1. proportional fairness;
2. local-community voice;
3. minority participation;
4. reasonable size;
5. recall or replacement procedure;
6. independent judgment;
7. transparent credentials.

### Section 99.1 — Delegates Distinguished From Statutory Members

Any future delegation system shall be legally reviewed to preserve the intended corporate membership structure.

---

# ARTICLE C
## Assembly Culture

The Assembly shall cultivate a culture in which:

- members may ask difficult questions;
- leaders may give difficult answers;
- disagreement does not automatically become hostility;
- voting does not make opponents enemies;
- minority positions remain part of institutional memory;
- constitutional losses are accepted peacefully;
- constitutional victories are not treated as permission for domination.

---

# ARTICLE CI
## Minority Statements

Where at least ten percent of participating eligible members oppose a Class D or E decision, they may submit a concise **Community Minority Statement** for the permanent constitutional record.

### Section 101.1 — Effect

The statement:

- does not suspend a valid decision;
- does not become doctrine;
- preserves reasoning for future generations.

### Section 101.2 — Good-Faith Protection

Participation in a minority statement shall not itself constitute disloyalty.

---

# ARTICLE CII
## Institutional Communication After Major Votes

After a major contested vote, leadership should communicate:

- what was decided;
- what was not decided;
- when the decision becomes effective;
- what concurrent action remains;
- how minority members remain part of the community;
- what implementation follows.

### Section 102.1 — No Triumph Narrative

Institutional communication should avoid unnecessarily treating constitutional disagreement as victory over enemies within the community.

---

# ARTICLE CIII
## Failed Proposals

A rejected proposal may ordinarily be raised again later.

### Section 103.1 — Cooling Period

CMA-PROC-001 may establish reasonable cooling periods for substantially identical extraordinary proposals.

### Section 103.2 — Changed Circumstances

Earlier reconsideration may occur where:

- material new evidence;
- changed circumstances;
- constitutional remand;
- emergency

justifies renewed consideration.

---

# ARTICLE CIV
## Member Conflict of Interest

Members exercising reserved powers should disclose material personal conflicts where relevant.

### Section 104.1 — Ordinary Interests

Holding a religious opinion about a governance matter does not itself constitute a conflict.

### Section 104.2 — Material Personal Benefit

A member should consider recusal where the vote directly concerns:

- a substantial personal financial benefit;
- personal appointment;
- close family benefit;
- another unusually direct private interest.

### Section 104.3 — General Membership Vote

Because the CMA is a broad participatory body, ordinary relational or ideological interests shall not trigger the same recusal standard applicable to adjudicators.

---

# ARTICLE CV
## No Vote Buying

No person may offer or receive:

- money;
- employment;
- religious office;
- preferential pastoral treatment;
- gifts of substantial value;
- other improper personal benefit

in exchange for a CMA vote.

Vote buying may constitute serious governance misconduct.

---

# ARTICLE CVI
## Threats and Intimidation

No person shall threaten:

- employment retaliation;
- ministry retaliation;
- membership retaliation;
- physical harm;
- financial harm;
- public humiliation;
- improper spiritual consequences

for the purpose of controlling a lawful CMA vote.

---

# ARTICLE CVII
## Misrepresentation

Knowingly fabricating material facts concerning a CMA matter may constitute governance misconduct.

### Section 107.1 — Opinion Distinguished

Strongly disputed interpretation is not necessarily knowing falsehood.

### Section 107.2 — Correction

Material official misinformation should be corrected promptly.

---

# ARTICLE CVIII
## Institutional Neutrality in Ballot Administration

Persons responsible for:

- eligibility;
- ballot systems;
- counting;
- certification

shall administer those functions neutrally regardless of personal preference concerning the outcome.

---

# ARTICLE CIX
## Assembly Safety

### Section 109.1 — Safe Participation

Assemblies shall be conducted in a manner reasonably designed to protect participants from:

- violence;
- credible threats;
- stalking;
- harassment.

### Section 109.2 — Removal From Session

A person may be removed from a session for serious disruptive or dangerous conduct.

### Section 109.3 — Voting Rights

Removal from a physical meeting does not automatically terminate voting eligibility unless valid disciplinary or safety rules require it.

---

# ARTICLE CX
## Children and Nonmember Community Participants

The Religious Body may create forums through which:

- youth;
- Adherents;
- Participants;
- local communities

may provide perspectives to the CMA.

Such consultation does not confer reserved-power voting rights.

---

# ARTICLE CXI
## Community Listening Sessions

The CMA or EC may convene nonvoting listening sessions concerning:

- doctrine;
- community health;
- institutional change;
- worship;
- ministry;
- governance.

### Section 111.1 — No False Vote

A listening session shall not be represented as formal approval unless formal constitutional voting occurred.

---

# ARTICLE CXII
## Assembly Education

Covenant Members should receive periodic education concerning:

- constitutional structure;
- civil–ecclesiastical distinction;
- reserved powers;
- petitions;
- voting thresholds;
- accountability;
- doctrinal classifications;
- member rights;
- member responsibilities.

An electorate unable to understand its powers is vulnerable to manipulation.

---

# ARTICLE CXIII
## Preparation for Extraordinary Votes

Before a Class D or E vote, the Religious Body should provide educational opportunities explaining:

1. existing rule;
2. proposed change;
3. reasons;
4. objections;
5. consequences;
6. vote threshold;
7. participation threshold;
8. legal or institutional implications.

---

# ARTICLE CXIV
## No Manufactured Emergency

A governing institution shall not artificially characterize a matter as urgent in order to:

- shorten required member notice;
- bypass deliberation;
- exclude absentee participation;
- prevent organization of opposition.

### Section 114.1 — Genuine Emergencies

Actual emergencies remain subject to the emergency provisions of the governing corpus.

---

# ARTICLE CXV
## Emergency Assembly Procedure

Where a genuine emergency requires CMA action sooner than ordinary notice permits:

1. the emergency shall be documented;
2. the CCG shall review constitutional validity where practicable;
3. the narrowest feasible action shall be presented;
4. every reasonable effort shall be made to notify eligible members;
5. emergency action shall not permanently alter protected constitutional rights unless a higher instrument expressly permits.

---

# ARTICLE CXVI
## Assembly Continuity

If the ordinary CMA administration becomes unable to function:

- the CCG may identify a temporary constitutional restoration procedure;
- the EC may provide logistical support;
- the Civil Board may provide lawful corporate resources;
- none of these bodies thereby acquires the CMA's reserved powers.

---

# ARTICLE CXVII
## Failure of Assembly Participation

Repeated inability to meet participation thresholds shall trigger institutional review.

The review should consider:

- member engagement;
- communication;
- electorate accuracy;
- excessive thresholds;
- inaccessible procedures;
- institutional distrust;
- overuse of reserved-power voting.

### Section 117.1 — No Automatic Threshold Reduction

Leadership may not simply lower constitutional participation requirements after observing that a proposal would otherwise fail.

---

# ARTICLE CXVIII
## CMA Self-Evaluation

At least every three years, the CMA should evaluate whether:

1. members understand the Assembly's role;
2. notice is adequate;
3. participation is meaningful;
4. voting systems are trustworthy;
5. questions reach leadership;
6. petitions receive serious consideration;

# ARTICLE CXIX
## Failure Modes

The CMA shall guard against becoming:

### 1. Ceremonial Audience

Members receive speeches but possess no meaningful role.

### 2. Permanent Referendum Government

Every ordinary decision becomes a popular vote.

### 3. Personality Electorate

Members merely ratify the wishes of a charismatic leader.

### 4. Factional Parliament

Every question becomes competition between permanent blocs.

### 5. Donor Democracy

Wealth acquires disproportionate influence.

### 6. Mob Tribunal

Serious allegations are adjudicated by public popularity.

### 7. Manufactured Electorate

Membership rolls are manipulated to produce outcomes.

### 8. Participation Theater

Leadership stages consultation after decisions have already become irrevocable.

### 9. Low-Turnout Constitutional Capture

A tiny minority determines foundational matters.

### 10. Majority Domination

Winning a vote is treated as authority to degrade dissenters.

### 11. Statutory Drift

Ecclesiastical participation quietly evolves into recurring corporate director elections contrary to the Charter.

# ARTICLE CXX
## Assembly Decision Quality Standard

Before exercising a major reserved power, the CMA should be able to answer:

1. What are we deciding?
2. Do we possess authority over it?
3. What other body has already acted?
4. What other body must still act?
5. What is the Record Date?
6. Is the voter roll accurate?
7. Was membership manipulated?
8. Was adequate notice given?
9. What threshold applies?
10. What participation threshold applies?
11. Have serious competing views been heard?
12. Is confidential information being protected appropriately?
13. Are members being coerced?
14. Is institutional information accurate?
15. What happens if the proposal passes?
16. What happens if it fails?
17. Is the action reversible?
18. Are charitable assets implicated?
19. Are constitutional rights implicated?
20. Would we consider the process legitimate if the outcome were opposite to our preference?

# ARTICLE CXXV
## Institutional Objective

The CMA must be powerful enough that the religious community cannot be ignored when the Constitution says its concurrence matters.

It must be restrained enough that ordinary governance does not become a permanent election campaign.

It must permit members to question leaders without requiring leaders to surrender every legitimate executive responsibility.

It must permit leaders to advocate without allowing them to manufacture consent.

It must permit religious conviction without allowing spiritual authority to become electoral coercion.

It must permit majority decisions without treating minorities as enemies.

It must allow deeply consequential decisions to be made by the community without pretending that truth itself is created by counting ballots.

It must distinguish:

> consultation from consent,

> petition from enactment,

> nomination from appointment,

> affirmation from selection,

> deliberation from adjudication,

> ecclesiastical voting from statutory corporate membership,

> participation from ownership,

> community authority from unlimited popular sovereignty.

Its ultimate constitutional purpose is therefore:

> **to make the religious community a genuine participant in its own constitutional life while preserving the differentiated authority, institutional competence, deliberative depth, and anti-capture protections upon which the Rule-Governed Conciliar Polity depends.**

We receive participation in this Assembly as a responsibility rather than an ownership right.

We shall exercise our votes freely.

We shall respect leadership without assuming that office makes leadership infallible.

We shall not use membership discipline to control elections.

We shall not use elections to replace due process.

We shall not confuse popularity with truth.

We shall not confuse religious membership with ownership of charitable property.

We shall exercise only those powers entrusted to this Assembly.
