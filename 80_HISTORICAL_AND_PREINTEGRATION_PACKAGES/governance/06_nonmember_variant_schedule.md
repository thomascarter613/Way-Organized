# Schedule N - No-Statutory-Member Model

Use only if the charter states that the corporation will not have statutory members.

## 1. Participants are not corporate members

Terms such as congregant, participant, affiliate, covenant participant, minister, donor, volunteer, or community member describe religious/community relationships only and do not confer statutory membership rights.

## 2. Participant Assembly

At least annually, the board shall convene an open Participant Assembly for governance reporting, questions, nominations, policy feedback, and presentation of the integrity dashboard. The assembly is advisory unless the charter is amended to create statutory members.

## 3. Board nomination safeguards

The Governance and Nominating Committee solicits candidate suggestions from participants, former directors, qualified outside advisers, and community stakeholders. At least one-third of each regular board-election slate should originate from sources other than the founder and sitting executive staff when feasible.

## 4. Board election

New directors are elected by the sitting board from the screened slate. A founder or chief executive may not vote if doing so would be decisive in creating or preserving a non-independent majority. No person may appoint a director unilaterally unless the charter expressly creates that appointment right and the appointment cannot defeat the two-thirds independence requirement.

## 5. Entrenched amendment substitute for member vote

Because there are no statutory members, any proposal to materially diminish an entrenched protection requires the enhanced board process in the charter plus an external adverse-rights review. The proposed amendment and review must be published to participants at least 30 days before the second board vote, with a channel for written comments.

## 6. Participant petitions

A petition signed by the greater of 25 regular adult participants or 10% of the average adult attendance over the prior six months may require the board to place a governance concern on the agenda of its next regular meeting, unless counsel determines the request is unlawful, privileged, duplicative, or threatens a person's safety/privacy. The board shall publish a response or explain why confidentiality prevents publication.
