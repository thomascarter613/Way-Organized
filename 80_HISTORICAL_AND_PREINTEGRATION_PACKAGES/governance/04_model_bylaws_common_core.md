# Model Bylaws - Common Core

**Organization:** [[ORGANIZATION_NAME]]  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Status:** Founding draft. Adopt only after selecting the member or nonmember schedule and counsel review.

## Article I. Governing hierarchy and mission

1. These bylaws are subordinate to applicable law and the charter.
2. The Institutional Integrity & Anti-Coercion Framework, Participant Bill of Rights, Founder Covenant, and Board Reserved Powers Schedule are incorporated as board-governance schedules to the extent consistent with the charter.
3. No officer, minister, committee, employee, volunteer, or doctrinal body may override a charter or bylaw protection by invoking spiritual authority, emergency, custom, revelation, founder intent, or institutional necessity.
4. The Corporation shall pursue its religious, charitable, and educational mission while preserving freedom of conscience and voluntary participation.

## Article II. Participation and notice of rights

1. Every participant shall have reasonable access to the Participant Bill of Rights, complaint channels, safeguarding reporting information, and current governance documents.
2. No participant is required to sign a waiver of non-retaliation, civil reporting rights, bodily autonomy, or access to independent legal/medical advice as a condition of ordinary participation.
3. Religious offices may have disclosed belief or conduct qualifications reasonably related to the role, but those qualifications shall not be enforced through coercive control.
4. The selected corporate-member schedule controls whether any participant also possesses statutory corporate membership rights.

## Article III. Board of Directors

### Section 3.1 Composition
After the formation transition, the board shall have 7-11 voting directors. At least two-thirds shall be independent. The board shall determine its exact size by resolution within that range, subject to the charter.

### Section 3.2 Independence
A director is independent only if the Governance and Nominating Committee determines annually that the director: (a) is not an employee or subordinate of an employee; (b) is not an immediate family member of a senior employee, founder with executive authority, or another director; (c) has no material compensated vendor or business relationship with the Corporation; (d) does not receive material economic benefit from the Corporation other than properly approved director compensation, if any, and expense reimbursement; and (e) has no other relationship reasonably likely to impair objective judgment.

### Section 3.3 Terms
Directors serve three-year staggered terms. No director may serve more than two consecutive full terms without at least a one-year break, except that a two-thirds vote of all independent directors may extend one director for up to one year solely to preserve continuity during an extraordinary transition. Partial initial terms used to establish staggering do not count as full terms.

### Section 3.4 Election / appointment
The Governance and Nominating Committee shall publish or circulate candidate qualifications, conduct independence/conflict screening, document nominations, and prevent the founder or chief executive from controlling a board majority. The selected member/nonmember schedule specifies the final election method.

### Section 3.5 Chair
The chair shall be independent and shall not simultaneously serve as chief executive, chief financial officer, lead clergy executive, or founder exercising executive authority.

### Section 3.6 Removal
A director may be removed according to applicable Tennessee law, the charter, and the selected schedule. The bylaws intentionally require a two-thirds vote of directors then in office for removal of a board-elected director without cause, unless a greater threshold applies. Removal for a conflict, safeguarding failure, retaliation, dishonesty, persistent nonattendance, incapacity, or serious breach of fiduciary duty may be treated as for-cause removal under a documented process.

### Section 3.7 Vacancies
Vacancies shall be filled using the same independence and nomination safeguards used for regular selection. A vacancy shall not be used to create founder or staff control.

### Section 3.8 Meetings
The board shall meet at least quarterly. Independent directors shall meet in executive session at least twice per year without management, the founder, or non-independent directors present, and additionally whenever senior-leadership complaints, compensation, succession, or related-party matters require it.

### Section 3.9 Access to advisers
The board and its independent committees may retain outside counsel, accountants, investigators, safeguarding professionals, compensation consultants, and other advisers at corporate expense when reasonably necessary for oversight.

## Article IV. Officers and executive leadership

1. Required officers shall include a president or chief executive, secretary, and treasurer or chief financial officer, with duties assigned by board resolution.
2. No person may simultaneously serve as board chair and chief executive.
3. The board appoints, evaluates, compensates, suspends, and removes the chief executive.
4. Senior spiritual titles do not create corporate authority unless the bylaws or board expressly assign it.
5. The founder, if serving as an officer, serves under the same fixed appointment, evaluation, conflict, suspension, and removal processes as another officer.
6. A person who is subject to a credible allegation presenting a safeguarding, retaliation, financial-integrity, or evidence-interference risk may be placed on non-disciplinary administrative leave pending investigation.

## Article V. Standing committees

### 5.1 Governance and Nominating Committee
Composed only of independent directors. Oversees nominations, board evaluation, independence determinations, succession process, and governance training.

### 5.2 Finance, Audit and Compensation Committee
Composed of a majority of independent directors and entirely disinterested directors for each transaction considered. Oversees budget, financial statements, external accounting review, executive compensation, restricted funds, and related-party transactions. No person participates in discussion or vote on their own economic benefit.

### 5.3 Safeguarding and Institutional Integrity Committee
Composed only of independent directors. Oversees complaint systems, non-retaliation, safeguarding, senior-leadership investigations, policy exceptions affecting participant rights, and the external integrity review. It may retain investigators without executive approval.

### 5.4 Doctrine and Research Council
An advisory religious/academic body may research and formulate teaching proposals. It does not control corporate investigations, compensation, safeguarding, elections, personal medical decisions, or individual conscience. Its procedures shall preserve sources, competing interpretations, confidence levels, and dissenting views for material doctrinal changes.

## Article VI. Reserved powers

The board may delegate administration but not final responsibility for the matters in the Board Reserved Powers Schedule. No executive, founder, clergy council, or staff committee may exercise a reserved power except pursuant to a board-approved temporary delegation that is lawful, written, narrow, time-limited, and does not concern that person's own compensation, discipline, or conflict.

## Article VII. Founder limitations and succession

1. Founder is an historical designation, not a corporate office unless separately elected or appointed.
2. The founder has no veto, ownership share, unilateral board-appointment power, right to select a successor, immunity, or automatic compensation.
3. Succession to executive or spiritual leadership shall be conducted by the Governance and Nominating Committee with conflict screening and an open candidate-identification process reasonably suited to the role.
4. A spouse, descendant, relative, business partner, close associate, or designated disciple of a founder receives no preference and must satisfy the same independent process.
5. The founder shall recuse from compensation, family employment, related-party transactions, complaints against the founder or close associates, investigator selection for such complaints, and succession in which the founder or relative is a candidate.
6. A serving founder signs the Founder Covenant annually.

## Article VIII. Conflicts, compensation, and related-party transactions

1. Directors and senior leaders disclose actual, potential, and apparent conflicts at appointment and at least annually, and update them promptly.
2. A conflicted person leaves deliberation and vote except to provide requested factual information before recusal.
3. Executive compensation shall be approved in advance by disinterested directors using appropriate comparability data and contemporaneous documentation.
4. Related-party transactions require documented alternatives, fairness analysis, recusal, and approval by disinterested directors. Material transactions should be reviewed by outside counsel or an independent adviser.
5. Loans to insiders, use of corporate assets for private purposes, and undocumented reimbursements are prohibited unless specifically lawful and approved under heightened controls.

## Article IX. Financial integrity

1. The board approves the annual budget and material deviations.
2. No single person shall control initiation, approval, payment, reconciliation, and reporting of a material transaction.
3. Bank access and payment authority shall follow a written matrix with dual approval above board-set thresholds.
4. Restricted gifts shall be tracked and used according to lawful donor restrictions.
5. The Corporation shall prepare quarterly financial reports for the board and an annual financial package suitable for independent review.
6. Leadership compensation, major related-party transactions, and significant use of charitable assets shall be transparently disclosed internally and publicly to the extent stated in policy and lawful.

## Article X. Safeguarding

1. The Corporation shall maintain a written safeguarding policy for minors and vulnerable adults.
2. Civil mandatory-reporting duties are never replaced by prayer, pastoral counseling, reconciliation, or internal investigation.
3. Programs involving minors shall use screening, role-appropriate background checks where lawful, two-adult or observable-environment practices, transportation/lodging controls, and documented training.
4. The Safeguarding and Integrity Committee may suspend access or duties immediately when reasonably necessary to protect a person or preserve an investigation, without prejudging final findings.

## Article XI. Complaints, investigations, and non-retaliation

1. At least two reporting channels shall exist, including one that bypasses the ordinary chain of command.
2. Complaints about a chief executive, founder, director, lead clergy executive, safeguarding officer, or person able to influence intake shall go directly to the independent Safeguarding and Integrity Committee or its external designee.
3. Retaliation is independent misconduct and may support interim protection or discipline even if the underlying allegation is not substantiated.
4. Investigators shall be screened for conflicts and given written terms of reference.
5. Criminal, child-protection, emergency, or other legally reportable matters shall be referred externally as required; internal fact-finding must not obstruct external authorities.
6. Findings affecting a person's office or membership shall use notice, impartial decision maker, opportunity to respond, and appeal consistent with the Discipline and Appeals SOP.

## Article XII. Pastoral ethics, conscience, and personal decisions

1. Pastoral/spiritual care shall not be represented as licensed medical, psychiatric, psychological, legal, or financial care unless the provider is actually qualified and acting within scope.
2. Leaders may offer counsel but shall not dictate competent adults' marriage, divorce, reproduction, healthcare, residence, education, career, personal finances, or voting choices as a condition of spiritual standing.
3. Confidential or intimate information shall not be used as leverage.
4. Sexual or romantic relationships are prohibited where a current pastoral, counseling, disciplinary, evaluative, or substantial supervisory power imbalance exists unless the power relationship is first ended and any additional ethics requirements are satisfied.

## Article XIII. Doctrine, revelation, and epistemic integrity

1. Claimed revelation is not self-authenticating and cannot bypass the charter, bylaws, civil law, safeguarding, financial controls, or individual conscience.
2. Material corporate teaching changes should identify evidence, inference, interpretation, theological judgment, confidence, and known substantial disagreement.
3. Research functions may publish evidence that complicates preferred conclusions without retaliation.
4. No spiritual claim may adjudicate the claimant's own compensation, discipline, authority, succession, or investigation.

## Article XIV. Exit and former-participant relations

1. Resignation from participation or office requires no spiritual release, payment, confession, or nondisparagement agreement.
2. Ordinary family and social relationships shall not be institutionally severed merely because of departure or criticism.
3. Corporate property must be returned and lawful debts or contractual obligations remain enforceable, but resignation does not forfeit personal property, earned wages, or civil rights.
4. Records concerning former participants remain subject to privacy, retention, litigation-hold, and safeguarding rules.

## Article XV. Records and transparency

1. The Corporation shall keep permanent records of board/member actions as required by law and appropriate accounting records.
2. The public governance page should include current directors, principal officers, core rights, complaint channels, safeguarding contact, bylaws or a governance summary, annual financial transparency report, and external integrity review findings/remediation summaries when available.
3. Confidentiality may protect victims, complainants, personnel, privileged advice, donors, security, and legally protected information; it shall not be used to conceal institutional misconduct.

## Article XVI. Meetings, action, and minutes

1. Notices, quorum, electronic participation, and written consents shall comply with Tennessee law and the selected member/nonmember schedule.
2. Board minutes shall document material decisions, conflicts and recusals, adviser reliance, comparability data for insider compensation, vote totals, and follow-up ownership without recording unnecessary confidential details.
3. The secretary maintains the corporate record book and amendment history.

## Article XVII. Amendment and entrenchment

1. Ordinary bylaws may be amended by two-thirds of all voting directors then in office after at least seven days' written notice containing the proposed text, unless the charter or selected schedule requires more.
2. An amendment that would materially diminish freedom of conscience, non-retaliation, safe exit, no-shunning protections, founder limitations, board independence, complaint independence, safeguarding, financial conflict controls, or entrenchment follows the charter's heightened amendment process.
3. No emergency power may suspend an entrenched protection.
4. Every proposed amendment shall include a written rights-impact statement and identify whether any affected person has a conflict.

## Article XVIII. External integrity review and annual certification

1. The Corporation shall commission an external institutional integrity review within 18 months after public launch and at least once every three years thereafter.
2. The reviewer shall have reasonable access to policies, board minutes relevant to scope, complaint/safeguarding statistics, financial controls, leadership certifications, and remediation records, subject to privilege and lawful privacy limits.
3. The board shall adopt a written remediation response and track actions to closure.
4. Each director, senior officer, senior spiritual leader, safeguarding lead, and finance lead shall complete an annual integrity certification.

## Article XIX. Indemnification and insurance

The Corporation may indemnify and insure directors, officers, employees, and agents to the maximum extent lawful, but no internal indemnification provision excuses intentional wrongdoing, personal financial benefit to which a person is not entitled, unlawful distributions, intentional criminal violations, retaliation, or misconduct that law makes non-indemnifiable.

## Article XX. Transition and severability

1. The initial board shall adopt a transition plan to reach full board independence and committee structure within 180 days of incorporation.
2. If any provision is held invalid, remaining provisions continue to the maximum lawful extent, and the board shall adopt the closest lawful substitute preserving the original integrity purpose.
