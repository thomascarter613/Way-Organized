# Article XV. Transparency and Public Accountability

**Purpose:** Make key controls visible enough that members and outsiders can verify how power is constrained.

## 15.1 Public governance information

The organization should publish its governing principles, board roster, leadership-selection process, safeguarding commitments, complaint pathway, conflict policy, and a plain-language description of how senior leaders can be removed.

## 15.2 Financial disclosure

The organization should publish or make readily available annual financial summaries, the independent accountant’s report where applicable, executive compensation-setting methodology, and material related-party disclosures consistent with privacy and law.

## 15.3 Material incidents

The board shall adopt a crisis-disclosure policy addressing when serious substantiated misconduct, safeguarding failures, material fraud, or governance breakdowns require disclosure to members, donors, insurers, regulators, or the public.

## 15.4 Corrections log

Material corrections to public historical, scientific, financial, or organizational claims should be documented in a durable form rather than silently rewritten where doing so would obscure accountability.

## 15.5 Policy access

Current policies governing rights, complaints, safeguarding, discipline, and financial conflicts shall be accessible to persons subject to them.
