# Article VI. Epistemic Integrity, Revelation, and Doctrine

**Purpose:** Prevent extraordinary spiritual claims from bypassing evidence, deliberation, or individual conscience.

## 6.1 Claim classification

The organization shall distinguish at minimum: observed or documentary evidence; historical inference; philosophical interpretation; theological hypothesis; communal teaching or tradition; and personal conviction or claimed revelation.

## 6.2 Revelation is not self-authenticating

No claimed revelation gains corporate authority solely from intensity, charisma, office, spiritual gifts, predictive claims, dreams, visions, altered states, or private certainty.

## 6.3 Public doctrine process

Material doctrinal changes shall follow a documented process that identifies evidence, competing interpretations, rationale, dissent, and confidence level. Emergency executive decree is not an acceptable ordinary path for doctrine.

## 6.4 Dissenting record

Where a governing or teaching body reaches a materially contested conclusion, good-faith minority reasoning may be preserved in the institutional record.

## 6.5 No manufactured certainty

Public teaching shall not represent disputed inference as established fact merely to increase commitment or obedience.

## 6.6 Correction duty

Material factual errors in public teaching, historical claims, fundraising narratives, or organizational representations shall be corrected promptly and visibly.

## 6.7 Research independence

Historical and phenomenological research functions shall be free to report evidence that complicates, contradicts, or weakens preferred theological conclusions.
