# Article I. Constitutional Principles

**Purpose:** Establish non-negotiable principles that control interpretation of every lower-level policy, office, and practice.

## 1.1 Freedom of conscience

No human being, office, council, text, claimed revelation, tradition, or institution may compel another person's conscience. The organization may teach, persuade, invite, recommend, and set neutral conditions for holding an office, but it shall not use coercion, threats, manipulation, material dependency, or abuse of authority to force belief or religious conformity.

## 1.2 Human dignity and agency

Every person retains moral agency and the right to make personal decisions about belief, association, family, marriage, medicine, education, vocation, residence, finances, and civic participation, subject only to generally applicable law and freely assumed contractual obligations.

## 1.3 No infallible living authority

No living founder, teacher, prophet, elder, director, officer, clergy member, council, or successor is infallible, immune from criticism, or beyond investigation, correction, suspension, or removal.

## 1.4 Claims are distinguishable from authority

A claimed revelation, vision, prophecy, mystical experience, discernment, or message is a claim to be evaluated. It does not become universally binding merely because it is sincerely reported, spiritually significant, historically influential, or endorsed by leadership.

## 1.5 Institutional humility

The organization shall distinguish evidence, interpretation, theological inference, communal tradition, and personal conviction. It shall permit the explicit conclusion “we do not know” where evidence does not justify confidence.

## 1.6 Non-retaliation

No person shall suffer retaliation for good-faith questions, disagreement, criticism, complaints, reports of misconduct, participation in an investigation, refusal of a religious practice, or departure from the organization.

## 1.7 Safe exit

Any participant may leave the organization without forfeiting personal property, wages, earned benefits, access to independent services, ordinary family relationships, or civil dignity. Departure shall not create a duty of social shunning.

## 1.8 Civil law and external accountability

The organization shall not claim that internal religious processes displace lawful civil reporting, criminal investigation, court orders, tax obligations, employment law, child-protection duties, or other applicable civil requirements.

## 1.9 Truth over institutional preservation

Protecting the organization’s reputation, founder, doctrine, finances, or continuity shall never justify concealment of abuse, destruction of evidence, retaliation, deception, or interference with lawful reporting.

## 1.10 Subsidiarity of authority

Authority shall be exercised at the lowest competent level. No office shall control a matter merely because it possesses spiritual status or organizational rank.
