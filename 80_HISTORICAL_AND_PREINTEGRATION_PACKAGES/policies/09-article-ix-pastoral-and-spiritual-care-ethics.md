# Article IX. Pastoral and Spiritual-Care Ethics

**Purpose:** Define boundaries for relationships involving spiritual trust and asymmetrical authority.

## 9.1 Scope honesty

Pastoral or spiritual caregivers shall clearly distinguish spiritual support from licensed psychotherapy, medical care, legal advice, financial advice, and other regulated professional services.

## 9.2 Sexual boundaries

A leader shall not exploit a pastoral, supervisory, counseling, mentoring, or disciplinary relationship for sexual or romantic access. Any potential relationship presenting a power imbalance must be handled under a written conflict and boundary process before romantic pursuit.

## 9.3 Confidentiality

The organization shall publish the limits of pastoral confidentiality, including safeguarding, threats, legal process, supervision, and other lawful exceptions.

## 9.4 No dependency engineering

Caregivers shall not deliberately make a person dependent on a particular leader for identity, decision-making, salvation assurance, ordinary social connection, or access to God or transcendent reality.

## 9.5 Referrals

Leaders shall refer matters beyond their competence to qualified independent professionals and shall not punish a participant for seeking such help.

## 9.6 Gifts

Material gifts to individual leaders from persons under their pastoral authority shall be restricted by policy, disclosed above stated thresholds, and rejected where acceptance would exploit vulnerability or create a significant conflict.
