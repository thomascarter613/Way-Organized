# HTE-001 — Prophetic Communication at Mari

## Identification
- **Experiencers:** multiple named and unnamed prophetic intermediaries, male and female
- **Setting:** Kingdom of Mari and neighboring kingdoms, especially reigns of Yasmah-Addu and Zimri-Lim
- **Approximate date:** chiefly 18th century BCE
- **Language of surviving documentation:** Akkadian cuneiform
- **Encounter modes:** MSG, AUD, VIS, DRM; possible ecstatic POS-like states in some reports

## What is directly established
The Mari archives preserve contemporary administrative and epistolary evidence that persons identified with titles such as **apilum/apiltum** (“answerer/interpreter/spokesperson”) and **muhhum/muhhutum** (often translated “ecstatic”) delivered messages attributed to named deities. The archive includes both male and female intermediaries. Messages could arise in temples, during sacrifice, through dreams, or in states described with language of frenzy/ecstasy. Officials, priests, the queen, or other witnesses often transmitted the message onward to the king.

Named deities represented in the wider Mari prophetic corpus include Dagan, Adad, Shamash, Marduk, Ninhursag, Diritum and others. The surviving letters are not merely later literary stories about a legendary age; they belong to the bureaucratic world in which the events were reported.

## Source grade
- **Existence of prophetic institution/practice:** P2, very strong
- **Specific oral oracle as reported by a near-contemporary witness:** P2
- **First-person subjective phenomenology:** usually weak/indirect
- **Objective existence of the claimed deity:** not established by the documents

## Phenomenology
The archive is frustratingly asymmetric. It tells us much more about **what society did with prophetic messages** than about what the prophet internally experienced. In some reports the prophet “arises” and speaks in a temple; other terminology suggests frenzy or ecstasy; dreams are separately attested. We should therefore resist imagining a single Mari prophetic state.

## Social function
- communication of divine approval/warning to kings
- military and diplomatic counsel
- cultic obligations and offerings
- legitimacy and criticism of royal conduct
- information about enemies and political risks

## Comparison value
Mari is an essential control for biblical prophecy because it demonstrates that ancient Near Eastern prophecy was already a recognizable institution before the classical Hebrew literary prophets. Similarities cannot automatically be explained as proof that the biblical prophets were uniquely unprecedented; differences must instead be identified at the level of message, theology, ethics, social role, and literary transmission.

## Hypothesis notes
- **H0 constructed:** possible for individual messages; bureaucracy could transmit political interests through divine speech.
- **H1 cognitive:** ecstatic/dream phenomena are compatible, but direct phenomenological data are too sparse for diagnosis.
- **H2 cultural:** strongly supported as a shaping factor; messages employ the deity/political categories of Mari society.
- **H3/H4 transcendent:** compatible with the claims but not independently demonstrated.
- **H5 mixed:** best working stance.

## Key research problem
Can any Mari oracle be shown to contain specific information unavailable by ordinary means, documented before verification? This will be tested in the primary-letter phase.

# HTE-006 — Shang Oracle-Bone Divination

## Identification
- **Experiencers/practitioners:** Shang kings and court diviners
- **Setting:** late Shang China
- **Approximate date of key surviving bones:** c. 1200–1050 BCE
- **Encounter mode:** DIV
- **Medium:** inscribed turtle plastrons and animal scapulae

## What is directly established
Archaeological oracle bones directly document an elaborate royal divination system. Questions and prognostications concern warfare, weather, harvest, childbirth, ritual, ancestors, illness and state decisions. The evidence is material and contemporary to the practice.

## Source grade
- **Practice of divination:** effectively P1/P2 material documentation
- **Questions/prognostications:** direct inscriptional evidence
- **Subjective experience of diviners:** poorly documented
- **Reality of ancestors/deities contacted:** not established by the bones themselves

## Phenomenology
Unlike prophetic narratives, oracle bones rarely provide introspection. The central phenomenon is **procedural access to hidden information**, not necessarily ecstatic vision or voice. This makes Shang divination a useful warning against treating all claimed transcendence as altered-state experience.

## Social function
Divination was integrated with governance. Major decisions could be framed through oracle consultation, embedding the unseen ancestral/divine realm within political administration.

## Comparison value
Because the Shang system developed far from the West-Semitic world, broad structural similarities with Mesopotamian divination are less likely to be direct literary borrowing. But the shared structure is still quite general: rulers seek uncertain information through sacred procedures. It therefore supports recurrence of divinatory behavior, not yet a common supernatural source.

## Hypothesis notes
- H0/H2 readily explain much of the institutional structure.
- H1 may be less central than in visionary cases because the process can operate without unusual subjective states.
- H3/H4 remain metaphysical possibilities, but the inscriptions do not independently discriminate them.

## Key research problem
Prospective accuracy. We need samples where question, prediction, and later result are all preserved clearly enough to evaluate whether the divinatory procedure performed beyond chance or ordinary political inference.

# HTE-007 — Vedic Rishis and the Rigveda

## Identification
- **Setting:** Indo-Aryan-speaking communities of northwest South Asia
- **Approximate composition:** latter half of the second millennium BCE
- **Language:** Vedic Sanskrit
- **Encounter modes:** INS, ILL, ritualized divine address; not reducible to one category

## What is directly established
The Rigveda is the oldest surviving Sanskrit text and contains more than a thousand highly sophisticated hymns addressed to various deities. The poetry belongs to identifiable bardic/poetic lineages. Modern scholarship emphasizes that Rigvedic poetry is not merely descriptive: sacred speech and ritual praise are understood as participating in or penetrating cosmic order.

Later Vedic tradition classifies the Veda as **shruti**, “heard” revelation, but HTERP must distinguish later canonical theory from what individual Rigvedic poets themselves claim.

## Source grade
- **Ancient hymnic compositions:** very high antiquity
- **Autobiographical reports by individual seers:** variable
- **Later theory that the whole text is timeless revelation:** historically later interpretive layer
- **Transmission:** extraordinarily controlled oral preservation, but still oral rather than autograph manuscript transmission

## Phenomenology
Some hymns speak with striking immediacy about inspiration, insight, divine encounter, sacred speech, hidden names, and cosmic mysteries. Yet the poet is also a ritual professional operating within patronage and sacrificial systems. “Seer” therefore cannot automatically be equated with “prophet who heard a divine voice.”

## Comparison value
The Rigveda forces HTERP to distinguish:
- revelation as **message from an agent**
from
- revelation as **inspired sacred speech**
from
- revelation as **perception of cosmic structure**.

It also provides an important Indo-Iranian comparison with the Gathas of Zarathustra; Vedic and Old Iranian religious-poetic traditions share inherited linguistic and ritual material, so similarities between them require dependency analysis rather than being counted as independent convergence.

## Hypothesis notes
H2 cultural/ritual shaping is clearly strong. H1 may explain altered or inspired states but cannot be assumed without descriptions. H3 is compatible with cosmic-order insight; H4 is compatible with hymnic addresses to deities. No single hypothesis is forced by the texts.

## Key research problem
Separate hymn-by-hymn claims of inspiration/encounter from later doctrines of Vedic revelation.

# HTE-008 — Zarathustra and the Gathas

## Identification
- **Experiencer:** Zarathustra/Zoroaster, assuming historical authorship of the core Gathic corpus
- **Setting:** ancient Iranian pastoral society; exact region/date disputed
- **Language:** Old Avestan
- **Encounter modes:** MSG, VIS, INS, dialogical prayer

## What is directly established
The Gathas are the oldest Zoroastrian texts. A major scholarly position attributes them to Zarathustra himself, though his date, homeland, historicity, and the precise extent of his authorship remain debated. The overwhelming majority of stanzas in the first four Gathas address **Ahura Mazda**. Iranica characterizes Zarathustra as maintaining a close revelatory relationship with Ahura Mazda and describes his vision as transforming inherited Iranian religious categories around truth, falsehood, moral choice, and divine supremacy.

## Source grade
- **If Zarathustra authored the Gathas:** P1
- **Because authorship/date remain debated:** P1? with explicit uncertainty flag
- **Later Avestan biography:** P4/P5 relative to Zarathustra
- **Later Pahlavi theology:** much later interpretive layer

## Phenomenology
The Gathas are not simple diary entries. They are liturgical-poetic compositions, prayers, questions, exhortations, and theological reflection. Zarathustra addresses Ahura Mazda, speaks of knowledge shared with the divine, and presents a moral cosmology structured around **asha** (truth/order) versus falsehood.

## Theological profile
Compared with many contemporaneous polytheistic systems, the Gathic material places extraordinary emphasis on Ahura Mazda. How close this is to later philosophical monotheism remains debated, especially given other divine entities and the complex status of the twin spirits.

## Comparison value
Zarathustra may be the strongest ancient non-biblical analogue for an **ethical revelatory reformer**. He therefore becomes crucial for testing whether moralized prophetic religion arose only within Israel or emerged independently/partly independently in Iranian religion.

However, Vedic and Iranian traditions share Indo-Iranian ancestry, so some structural and lexical parallels are inherited rather than independent.

## Hypothesis notes
- H0 cannot be excluded at the metaphysical level but is less useful if the Gathas are genuinely first-person founder compositions.
- H1 could mediate visionary/revelatory experience but lacks diagnostic data.
- H2 is demonstrably relevant because inherited Indo-Iranian ritual-poetic structures remain visible.
- H3/H4 are directly compatible with the experiencer’s theological interpretation but unverified externally.

## Key research problem
Build a Gatha-by-Gatha table distinguishing:
1. direct address to Ahura Mazda,
2. claimed answers or revelations,
3. poetic-theological inference,
4. ritual language inherited from Indo-Iranian tradition,
5. later Zoroastrian interpretation.
