# HTERP Baseline Source Ledger

This is a starting bibliography, not the final literature review.

## Ancient Near Eastern prophecy
- Martti Nissinen, *Ancient Prophecy: Near Eastern, Biblical, and Greek Perspectives* (Oxford University Press, 2017), especially “Ancient Near Eastern Sources.”
- Carolyn J. Sharp, ed., *The Oxford Handbook of the Prophets* (Oxford University Press, 2016).

## Greek oracles
- Martti Nissinen, “Greek Sources,” in *Ancient Prophecy* (OUP, 2017).
- Esther Eidinow, *Oracles, Curses, and Risk Among the Ancient Greeks* (OUP, 2007).

## Rigveda
- Stephanie W. Jamison and Joel P. Brereton, *The Rigveda* (OUP, 2020), especially chapters on historical context, structure, and poetry/poetics.

## Zarathustra
- Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Gathas i. Texts.”
- Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Zoroaster iii. Zoroaster in the Avesta.”
- Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Ahura Mazda.”
- Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Zoroastrianism i. Historical Review up to the Arab Conquest.”

## Hebrew prophecy
- *The Oxford Handbook of the Prophets* (2016).
- *The Oxford Handbook of Jeremiah* (2021), especially work on the historical Jeremiah and textual criticism.

## Jesus and early Christianity
- Richard Bauckham, *Jesus: A Very Short Introduction* (OUP, 2011), chapters on sources and the kingdom of God.
- Chris Keith, *The Gospel as Manuscript: An Early History of the Jesus Tradition as Material Artifact* (OUP, 2020).
- Matthew T. Sharp, *Divination and Philosophy in the Letters of Paul* (Edinburgh University Press, 2023), chapter “Visions.”

## Early Islam
- Andreas Görke and Gregor Schoeler, *The Earliest Writings on the Life of Muhammad: The 'Urwa Corpus and the Non-Muslim Sources* (2024; Cambridge online 2025).
- Fred Leemhuis, “From palm leaves to the Internet,” in *The Cambridge Companion to the Qur'an* (2007).
- Aziz Al-Azmeh, “Preliminaries to the Use of the Qur'an as an Historical Source,” in *The Arabs and Islam in Late Antiquity* (Cambridge online 2025).

## Sikhism
- Pashaura Singh, *The Guru Granth Sahib: Canon, Meaning and Authority* (OUP, 2003).
- Gurinder Singh Mann, *The Making of Sikh Scripture* (OUP, 2001).

## Shang divination
- British Museum oracle-bone collection records, late Shang examples dated approximately 1200–1050 BCE.

## Indigenous Australian traditions
- Australian Museum, “Indigenous Australians: Australia’s First Peoples,” especially its treatment of the Dreaming, ancestral beings, law, ritual, and restricted sacred knowledge.

## Yoruba Ifa
- Metropolitan Museum of Art collection documentation for Ifa divination vessels and trays, including descriptions of communication with Orunmila.

## Maya
- Cynthia Robin, “Time as Ordinary Practice: A Divination Building at Chan,” in *The Materialization of Time in the Ancient Maya World* (University Press of Florida, 2024).
- Metropolitan Museum of Art Maya ritual-object documentation concerning communication with divine powers.

## Experimental mystical experience
- Griffiths et al. (2006), “Psilocybin can occasion mystical-type experiences having substantial and sustained personal meaning and spiritual significance.”
- Barrett et al. (2015), “Validation of the revised Mystical Experience Questionnaire in experimental sessions with psilocybin.”
- MacLean et al. (2012), “Factor Analysis of the Mystical Experience Questionnaire.”

## Neurological controls
- Contemporary clinical/review literature on focal/temporal-lobe epilepsy and religious or mystical experiences. Historical figures are not to be retrospectively diagnosed without adequate evidence.

## Fraud controls
- Modern Spiritualist mediumship investigations, including well-documented exposures of fraudulent techniques; Harry Houdini’s 1920s anti-fraud work is one useful historical control set.
