# Human–Transcendent Encounter Research Program (HTERP)
## Working Baseline v0.1

### Governing question
What, if anything, can the global historical record of claimed human contact with transcendent reality tell us about reality, consciousness, revelation, religion, and the development of religious institutions?

### Governing methodological rule
HTERP does not begin by assuming that any religion is true, false, complete, corrupt, or uniquely authoritative. The unit of analysis is the **reported encounter**, not the later religion.

The working transmission model is:

possible event/reality  
→ human experience  
→ experiencer interpretation  
→ first report  
→ first hearers  
→ oral transmission  
→ written record  
→ editing/redaction  
→ canon  
→ commentary  
→ institution  
→ doctrine

Each layer must be analyzed separately.

### Three questions that must remain separate
1. **Report question:** Is there good evidence that a person or community actually reported an encounter?
2. **Reconstruction question:** Can we recover what was experienced or communicated with reasonable confidence?
3. **Explanation question:** What hypothesis best explains the experience/report?

Strong evidence for (1) does not automatically establish (3).

### Encounter ontology
- MSG — message/revelation
- VIS — vision
- AUD — auditory encounter
- DRM — revelatory dream
- POS — possession/mediumship
- DIV — divination/oracle
- JRN — otherworld journey
- UNI — unitive mystical experience
- PRS — sensed presence
- ILL — illumination/awakening
- INS — inspired composition
- APP — apparition
- CHA — channeling

Cases may receive multiple codes.

### Source-proximity ontology
- P1 — first-person material plausibly originating with the experiencer
- P2 — contemporary/nearly contemporary record from direct associates or officials
- P3 — early community tradition within roughly a generation or two
- P4 — substantially later transmitted tradition
- P5 — retrospective hagiography, legend, or theological reconstruction
- PX — provenance/date too uncertain for confident placement

A source grade is not a truth grade.

### Evidentiary dimensions
Do not collapse these into one numerical “credibility” score:
- source proximity
- textual stability
- independence
- historical context
- transmission transparency
- phenomenological detail
- external corroboration
- cultural dependency
- predictive specificity, when applicable
- susceptibility to fraud or ordinary information leakage

### Competing hypotheses
- H0 CONSTRUCTED — invention, legend, social formation, literary creation, fraud
- H1 COGNITIVE — neuropsychological processes generate the experience
- H2 CULTURAL — inherited concepts substantially shape/generate the experience
- H3 TRANSCENDENT-IMPERSONAL — humans sometimes encounter an impersonal transcendent reality
- H4 TRANSCENDENT-PERSONAL — humans sometimes encounter one or more noncorporeal intelligences
- H5 MIXED — different cases arise from different combinations of the above

HTERP begins with H5 as the safest working stance because it imposes the fewest premature universal claims.

### Dependency classes for apparent convergence
- I — plausibly independent
- C — historically connected/possible influence
- D — demonstrably or very probably inherited
- U — relationship unknown

Only convergence surviving dependency analysis counts strongly toward cross-cultural recurrence.

### Constructive-theology firewall
No theological doctrine is adopted merely because:
- it is old,
- it belongs to a large religion,
- a scripture calls itself revelation,
- a founder is revered,
- multiple dependent traditions repeat it,
- an experience felt certain or ineffable.

Constructive theology begins only after historical, phenomenological, dependency, and control analyses.

### Work waves
**Wave 1 — Comparative anchors:** Mari, Shang, Rigveda, Zarathustra, Hebrew literary prophets, Greek oracles, Buddha, Jesus, Paul, Muhammad, Guru Nanak, Ifa, Aboriginal traditions, Maya traditions, experimental/neurological controls.

**Wave 2 — Ancestral and ancient background:** Mesopotamian dreams/divination, Egypt, Abraham, Melchizedek, Moses, early Israelite prophecy, Upanishadic/Jain, early Chinese, Daoist.

**Wave 3 — Mystical and intermediary traditions:** Christian, Jewish, Islamic, Hindu, Buddhist, Sikh, African, Indigenous American, Siberian/Inner Asian, Oceanic.

**Wave 4 — Modern high-documentation cases and controls:** Swedenborg, Spiritualism, Joseph Smith, Bahá’u’lláh, apparitions, Pentecostal prophecy, channeling, laboratory mystical experience, psychedelics, dreams, neurological states, documented fraud.

**Wave 5 — Synthesis:** dependency-controlled convergence, contradiction analysis, hypothesis comparison, and only then possible theology/religion.
