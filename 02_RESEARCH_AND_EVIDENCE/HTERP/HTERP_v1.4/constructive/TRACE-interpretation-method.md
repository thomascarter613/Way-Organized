# Interpretation and Conflict-Resolution Method — TRACE

When texts, teachers, or traditions disagree, use TRACE.

## T — Text and provenance
What is the earliest form?
Who wrote/transmitted it?
How close is it to the claimed event?

## R — Reconstruct the historical setting
What did the words/actions mean in their original linguistic, social, political, and religious context?

## A — Analyze alternatives
What competing readings exist?
What later assumptions are being imported backward?

## C — Compare
Compare:
- parallel sources;
- independent traditions;
- archaeology/history;
- science;
- ethical consequences;
- HTERP controls.

## E — Epistemic status
Assign:
- established;
- strongly supported;
- plausible;
- possible;
- open;
- unsupported.

## Conflict rule
A later authority may reinterpret an earlier source, but it may not silently pretend the earlier source said what it did not say.

## Moral-conflict rule
Where a text appears to command serious harm, historical context and evidentiary scrutiny increase rather than decrease.

## No forced harmonization
Contradictions may remain contradictions until better evidence appears.
