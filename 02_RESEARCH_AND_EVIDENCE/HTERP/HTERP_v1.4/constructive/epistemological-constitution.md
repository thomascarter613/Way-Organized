# Provisional Epistemological Constitution

## Article 1 — Reality outranks doctrine
No scripture, prophet, institution, teacher, or HTERP model makes reality true by declaring it.

## Article 2 — Truth may appear outside the chosen religious spine
A teaching is not rejected merely because it is Jewish, Christian, Muslim, Zoroastrian, Buddhist, Hindu, Sikh, Indigenous, philosophical, scientific, or secular.

## Article 3 — Experience does not authenticate itself
A vision, voice, mystical state, prophecy, dream, or sense of certainty may be meaningful without proving its interpretation.

## Article 4 — Revelation claims are received critically
Every revelation claim is evaluated through:
- provenance;
- source proximity;
- historical context;
- dependency;
- coherence;
- ethical consequences;
- external evidence where available.

## Article 5 — Human reception is potentially fallible
Even if genuine transcendent revelation occurs, human perception, memory, language, transmission, redaction, and interpretation may distort it.

## Article 6 — Faith is labeled as faith
A devotional commitment may be sincerely held without being mislabeled as empirical proof.

## Article 7 — Unknown is sacred intellectual territory
Where knowledge is unavailable, the framework permits “we do not know.”

## Article 8 — Science is not a rival revelation
Reliable empirical findings inform interpretation of religious experience and scripture.

## Article 9 — No authority is beyond moral scrutiny
A claimed divine command that would authorize abuse, exploitation, coercion, or cruelty receives the highest evidentiary scrutiny and no automatic obedience.

## Article 10 — Revision is a virtue
Changing a belief because better evidence emerges is fidelity to truth, not betrayal of faith.
