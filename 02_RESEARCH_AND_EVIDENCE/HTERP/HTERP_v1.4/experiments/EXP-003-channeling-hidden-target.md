# EXP-003 — Channeling Hidden-Target Trial

## Research question
Can a person claiming communication with a noncorporeal entity obtain information about hidden randomized targets beyond control expectation?

## Why channeling is experimentally useful
The purported agent can often be questioned repeatedly.
That allows identity and information claims to be tested prospectively.

## Phase 1 — Forced-choice targets
Independent server selects one target from a large bank:
- image;
- word;
- geometric object;
- short factual description.

Channeler receives only session ID.

Channeler records:
- entity identity claimed;
- free description;
- forced-choice guess if protocol includes choices;
- confidence.

Response locked before target reveal.

## Phase 2 — Post-selected targets
Optional exploratory test:
response is locked **before** random target is generated.

This differentiates ordinary clairvoyance-style models from precognition-like claims, though a positive result would still not identify source.

## Phase 3 — Entity consistency
Across sessions, test:
- self-identification;
- factual biography claimed by entity;
- vocabulary/style;
- knowledge continuity;
- contradictions;
- response to hidden challenge questions.

## Phase 4 — Cross-channeler identity
If two independent channelers claim the same entity:
- no contact;
- no shared briefing;
- blinded parallel questions;
- compare specific answers.

Generic spiritual language is low-weight.
Specific uncommon convergence is higher-weight.

## Controls
- imaginative role-play participants;
- trained actors;
- automatic-writing controls where appropriate;
- target-bank permutation.

## Primary outcome
Forced-choice or preregistered semantic similarity measured blind.

Do not rely on subjective “this feels like my entity” ratings.

## Ethics
Participants remain free to interpret experiences spiritually.
Researchers do not reinforce dangerous command content.
Acute distress/paranoia/function loss triggers safety referral, not experimental escalation.
