# EXP-001 — Multisite Experimental Mediumship Replication

## Research question
Can selected mediums produce target-specific information about deceased persons at rates exceeding preregistered chance/control expectations under strong blinding?

## What this experiment does not test directly
It does not directly distinguish:
- deceased-person communication;
- telepathy from living people;
- clairvoyance;
- precognition;
- unknown information leakage.

First detect whether a replicable anomaly exists.

## Governance
Three sites:
1. proponent-friendly lab;
2. skeptical/adversarial lab;
3. neutral replication lab.

One shared preregistration and randomization service.

## Participants
### Mediums
- adults;
- self-identified practicing mediums;
- predefined experience criteria;
- no payment contingent on accuracy.

### Target sitters
Adults representing one deceased person they knew well.

Exclude where:
- participation risks acute grief destabilization;
- target information is heavily publicized online and cannot be controlled;
- investigator has prior relationship creating leakage.

## Target preparation
Each target receives an opaque random ID.

Sitter creates a sealed factual target file containing preregistered categories:
- relationship;
- sex;
- approximate age at death;
- distinctive occupation/role;
- major life events;
- cause/circumstances of death where ethically acceptable;
- distinctive hobbies;
- personality traits;
- 3–5 uncommon verifiable facts.

The medium does not receive this file.

## Reading phase
Medium receives only:
- target ID;
- standardized instruction.

No sitter contact.
No social-media access.
No investigator who knows target details is present in the reading room.

Medium provides:
- free-response reading;
- structured forced categories where appropriate;
- confidence per claim.

All statements are time-stamped and locked before scoring.

## Matching design
Use batches of at least five target-reading pairs.

Each sitter receives:
- the intended reading;
- at least four decoy readings from the same batch;
in randomized order.

Primary outcome:
**rank/order identification of the intended reading.**

Secondary:
- standardized factual-accuracy score;
- unusual-specific-detail score;
- false-positive count;
- confidence calibration.

## Independent coding
Blind coders classify each statement:
- exact;
- partially correct;
- incorrect;
- unverifiable;
- generic.

Coders do not know intended target.

## Controls
- non-medium control writers producing “readings” from target codes;
- optional cold-reading-trained control;
- public-information audit;
- leakage audit.

## Statistics
Primary analysis fixed before data collection.

Use:
- permutation/randomization test for matching;
- effect sizes with uncertainty;
- correction for secondary outcomes.

No post-hoc “best medium only” result becomes the primary finding.

## Replication rule
A positive first site does not advance HTERP beyond AI-3 unless:
- direction replicates independently;
- pooled preregistered analysis is positive;
- heterogeneity is reported;
- all failed sessions remain in dataset.

## Source-identification phase
Only if anomaly replicates:
run separate experiments testing competing source models.

## Safety
No participant receives:
- medical diagnosis;
- suicide/death prediction;
- financial instruction;
- guilt claims from the deceased;
as part of experimental feedback.

Medium readings remain blinded from sitters until debrief unless protocol explicitly permits safe disclosure.
