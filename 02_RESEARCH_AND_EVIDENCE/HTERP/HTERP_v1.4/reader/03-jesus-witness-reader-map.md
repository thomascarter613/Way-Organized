# Reader III — Jesus Witness Corpus

## Constructive role
Level 1 spine.

## Critical source rule
Jesus wrote no surviving text known to scholarship.

The reader therefore studies **witnesses to Jesus**, not an imagined transcript.

## Source architecture

### Paul
Earlier than the written Gospels, but mainly witnesses:
- crucifixion;
- resurrection belief/appearances;
- some teachings/traditions;
not a detailed ministry biography.

### Mark
Generally treated as the earliest surviving canonical Gospel.
Primary first-pass narrative source.

### Matthew and Luke
Use Mark plus additional traditions/material.
Shared non-Markan material is analytically important, but hypothetical source models such as “Q” remain scholarly reconstructions, not discovered manuscripts.

### John
Highly developed theological/literary witness with distinctive traditions.
Do not simply merge John's long discourses with Synoptic sayings as if source form were identical.

## Historical-attribution workflow
For any saying:
1. earliest attestation;
2. multiple/independent attestation where applicable;
3. Jewish first-century plausibility;
4. literary context;
5. redaction;
6. theological development.

No criterion mechanically proves authenticity.
