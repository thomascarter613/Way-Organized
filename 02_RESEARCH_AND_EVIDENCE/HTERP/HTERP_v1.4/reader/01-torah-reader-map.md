# Reader I — Torah

## Constructive role
Level 1 Abrahamic Prophetic Spine.

## Historical caution
The Torah/Pentateuch is a composite literary corpus with long formation history. HTERP does not treat narratives about Abraham or Moses as contemporary biographies merely because the canonical text presents them as ancient.

## Core reading map

### Genesis 1–11 — Primeval world
Topics:
- creation;
- image/vocation of humanity;
- moral knowledge;
- violence;
- flood;
- nations/Babel.

Comparative links:
Mesopotamian creation/flood traditions.

Difficult-text flags:
- flood destruction;
- etiological readings;
- historicity questions.

### Genesis 12–25 — Abraham cycle
Topics:
- calling/migration;
- El/YHWH name traditions;
- covenant;
- Melchizedek/El Elyon;
- hospitality;
- Sodom;
- Hagar;
- Akedah.

HTERP links:
HTE-010 Abraham; HTE-011 Melchizedek.

Key terms:
- **El**
- **El Elyon**
- **El Shaddai**
- **berit** — covenant
- **tsedaqah** — righteousness

Difficult-text flags:
- treatment of Hagar;
- conquest/promise implications;
- sacrifice of Isaac.

### Genesis 26–50 — Isaac/Jacob/Joseph
Topics:
- ancestral deity-language;
- Bethel/Peniel encounters;
- Israel name;
- providence and family conflict.

### Exodus 1–18 — Liberation and Moses
Topics:
- oppression;
- burning bush;
- divine name;
- signs;
- Passover;
- exodus;
- wilderness.

Key terms:
- **YHWH**
- **ehyeh**
- **Elohim**

Difficult-text flags:
- plagues;
- firstborn;
- historical/archaeological questions.

### Exodus 19–24 — Sinai covenant
Topics:

- Decalogue;
- covenant law.

### Exodus 25–40 — sanctuary
Read for:

### Numbers
Focus:
- wilderness authority;
- prophecy (Numbers 11–12);
- rebellion;
- Balaam/divination;
- violence and land.

### Deuteronomy
Focus:
- covenant centralization;
- Shema (6);
- ethical memory of slavery;
- prophet test (13, 18);
- social law;
- blessing/curse;
- Deuteronomy 32 divine-council textual problem.

Key terms:
- **shema** — hear/heed
- **ahavah** — love
- **mishpat** — justice/judgment

## TRACE questions
- Which divine name appears?
- Does the passage reflect later Israelite theology projected into patriarchal time?
- Is law moral, ritual, civil, identity-boundary, or mixed?
- What later Jewish/Christian/Islamic readings changed its meaning?
- What ethical principle, if any, survives the historical setting?
