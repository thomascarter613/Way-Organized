# Reader Method

## Reading modes

### H — Historical
What can be said about date, authorship, source layer, redaction, audience, and original context?

### D — Devotional
What does the community hear as a call to practice?

### C — Comparative
How does this material agree/conflict with other streams?

### E — Evidentiary
What, if anything, does the passage contribute to HTERP claims about encounter/revelation?

A reading session should explicitly state its mode.

## Source-proximity tags
Reuse HTERP:
- **P1** first-person/founder-associated material;
- **P2** contemporary/near-contemporary witness;
- **P3** early community tradition;
- **P4** substantially later transmitted tradition;
- **P5** retrospective hagiography/systematization;
- **PX** uncertain.

A whole biblical/Qur'anic/Buddhist book does not necessarily receive one tag; units may differ.

## TRACE annotation
For every unit:
- **T** Text/provenance
- **R** Reconstruct context
- **A** Alternatives
- **C** Compare
- **E** Epistemic status

## Devotional restraint
Do not turn:
> “this text says X”
into
> “therefore reality is X”
without identifying the inferential step.

## Difficult-text rule
If a passage contains:
- violence;
- slavery;
- coercion;
- exclusion;
- gender hierarchy;
- apparent contradiction;
- historically failed prediction;
- morally troubling divine command;
it receives a flag rather than being omitted.

## Comparative respect
A source from another tradition is not extracted from its context merely because one sentence sounds compatible with the Provisional Way.
