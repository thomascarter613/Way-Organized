# Reader VI — Guru Nanak / Early Sikh Witness

## Constructive role
Level 2 parallel high-value witness.

## Source focus
Prioritize compositions with strong early manuscript/canonical association rather than later Janamsakhi biography.

## Primary reading anchors
- **Japji Sahib** / opening composition traditionally attributed to Guru Nanak;
- selected Guru Nanak hymns in the Guru Granth Sahib;
- selected Asa di Var material where attribution/source context is clear.

## Key terms
- **Ik Oankar** — One Reality / One Creator formulation, translation nuanced
- **Naam** — Name/divine presence/remembrance
- **hukam** — divine order/command
- **seva** — service
- **simran** — remembrance
- **haumai** — ego-centeredness

## Comparative questions
- What does divine unity mean here?
- How does remembrance transform conduct?
- How are empty ritual and social hierarchy criticized?
- Which ideas reflect the Punjab Hindu/Islamic environment?
- What is distinctively Sikh rather than reducible to a blend?

## Respect rule
Sikh material is not mined merely as confirmation of Abrahamic theology.
Read it as Sikh scripture first, comparative witness second.
