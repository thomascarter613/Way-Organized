# Deuteronomy 6 — Shema and Allegiance

## T
Deuteronomy belongs to a Deuteronomic literary/theological tradition usually situated within first-millennium BCE Judah/Israel, with significant compositional history.

## R
The chapter commands:
- hearing/obeying;
- exclusive covenant loyalty;
- love of YHWH;
- teaching children;
- embodied remembrance.

## A
The Hebrew wording of Deut 6:4 permits nuances such as:
“YHWH is our God, YHWH alone”
or
“YHWH our God, YHWH is one.”

The theological emphasis includes both identity and exclusive allegiance.

## C
Compare:
- Decalogue;
- later Jewish Shema practice;
- Jesus' use of the command;
- Qur'anic divine unity.

## E
Historical:
A key text in Israelite/Jewish movement toward exclusive YHWH allegiance.

Metaphysical:
It does not by itself prove philosophical monotheism as an external fact.

## Constructive status
**Retain as core devotional text**, interpreted under the provisional Most-High faith postulate.

## Key term
`shema` = hear/heed/obey, not merely auditory reception.
