# Genesis 1:1–2:4a — Creation

## T — Text / provenance
Genesis 1 is commonly associated in historical-critical scholarship with Priestly literary/theological material, though contemporary Pentateuchal models differ on how that material was composed and combined.

The chapter is a highly structured literary account, not an eyewitness report.

## R — Reconstruct context
It belongs to an ancient Near Eastern world where creation was commonly narrated through divine ordering of primordial reality. Comparisons with Mesopotamian cosmologies are relevant, but Genesis 1 is not simply a copy: it radically organizes the cosmos under one sovereign creator and demotes sun/moon from divine status to created lights.

## A — Alternatives
- literal modern scientific chronology;
- ancient cosmological theology;
- liturgical/temple reading;
- anti-idolatry polemic;
- composite of several functions.

HTERP does not use Genesis 1 as a scientific source for physical cosmology.

## C — Compare
Compare:
- Mesopotamian creation traditions;
- later Isaiah creator theology;
- Qur'anic creation/sign language.

## E — Epistemic status
Historically strong:
Genesis 1 witnesses an Israelite/Judahite theological cosmology.

Not established:
the mechanism, chronology, or physics of cosmic origins.

## Constructive status
**Retain as foundational theological poetry/narrative.**
Core practice themes:
- reality is ordered rather than divine chaos;
- creation has value;
- human beings possess vocation/responsibility;
- celestial bodies are not gods.

## Difficult-text flag
Interpretive conflict with modern science if read as modern scientific prose.
