# Genesis 12:1–9 — Abram's Call

## T
The story is preserved in a Pentateuchal text written/redacted long after the era in which a historical Abraham would conventionally be placed. No contemporary Abrahamic inscription identifies this event.

## R
The narrative presents migration, divine promise, land, descendants, blessing, and altar-building as the beginning of Abram's special vocation.

## A
Possible readings:
- preserved ancestral memory;
- theological origin story of Israel;
- combination of memory and later national theology;
- literary construction.

HTERP cannot currently distinguish these with high confidence.

## C
Compare:
- wider West-Semitic migration/pastoral contexts;
- Genesis 14 El Elyon;
- later prophetic universal-blessing interpretations;
- Qur'anic Abraham traditions.

## E
Strong conclusion:
The tradition remembers Abraham as responding to a divine call.

Weak conclusion:
That we possess a transcript of a historical noncorporeal communication to Abram.

## Constructive status
**Retain as foundational vocation narrative.**
Use for:
trust, departure from inherited certainty, blessing-oriented vocation.

## Difficult-text flag
Land promise and later political appropriation must not be converted automatically into modern territorial entitlement.
