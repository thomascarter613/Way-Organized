# Qur'an 112 — al-Ikhlāṣ

## T
A compact Qur'anic formulation of divine unity. Exact relative chronology is less important here than its clear place within the Qur'anic monotheistic discourse.

## R
Core claims:
- God is one;
- God is self-sufficient/eternal depending translation of `al-ṣamad`;
- neither begetting nor begotten;
- none comparable.

## A
`al-ṣamad` is lexically/theologically difficult and should not be reduced to one English gloss without study.

## C
Compare:
- Deuteronomy 6;
- later Jewish/Christian debates about divine sonship;
- late-antique monotheistic polemic.

## E
Strong:
Qur'anic uncompromising divine singularity/transcendence.

Not established by text alone:
that every competing theological model is externally false.

## Constructive status
**Major witness to the provisional Most-High theology**, while Christological conflict remains openly labeled.
