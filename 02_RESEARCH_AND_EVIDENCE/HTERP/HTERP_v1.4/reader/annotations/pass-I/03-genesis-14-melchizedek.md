# Genesis 14:18–24 — Melchizedek / El Elyon

## T
Genesis 14 is unusually difficult to date and analyze. Scholarship has proposed early and late compositions, composite models, interpolation theories, and major redaction hypotheses.

Robert Cargill (2019) argues, controversially, that the Melchizedek narrative underwent substantial scribal alteration and that YHWH in v.22 is a later gloss. This is an important scholarly model, not consensus established fact.

## R
In the received Masoretic text:
- Melchizedek is king of Salem;
- priest of El Elyon;
- blesses Abram;
- El Elyon is called creator/possessor of heaven and earth;
- Abram's oath uses YHWH together with El Elyon.

## A
Major questions:
- Was Melchizedek originally a distinct priest-king?
- Is El Elyon an epithet of El, a distinct deity/title, or already identified with YHWH?
- Is v.22's YHWH secondary?
- Is Salem Jerusalem, another Shalem, or literarily relocated?

## C
Compare:
- Northwest Semitic El traditions;
- Psalm 110;
- later Hebrews;
- Deuteronomy 32;
- West-Semitic priest-kings.

## E
Established:
The received Israelite text integrates El Elyon language with Abram/YHWH theology.

Open:
What the earliest form of the passage said and what historical cult lies behind it.

## Constructive status
**Retain as key evidence for pre-/extra-Israelite Most-High priesthood traditions, with major uncertainty label.**

## Difficult-text flag
Do not use Melchizedek as proof of a historically continuous hidden monotheistic priesthood without independent evidence.
