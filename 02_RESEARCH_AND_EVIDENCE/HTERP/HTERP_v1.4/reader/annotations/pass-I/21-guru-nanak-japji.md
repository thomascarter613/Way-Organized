# Guru Nanak — Japji / Opening Sikh Witness

## T
Early Sikh manuscript scholarship suggests written transmission of bani may have begun during Guru Nanak's lifetime and that early pothis preserve material of the first Gurus.

Pashaura Singh specifically analyzes the Mul Mantar and Japji within early manuscript/textual history.

## R
Themes include:
- divine unity;
- Name/remembrance;
- hukam/order;
- truthful living;
- ego/haumai;
- disciplined recitation/contemplation.

## A
Later Janamsakhi biography must not be treated as equally source-proximate to founder-associated bani.

## C
Compare:
- Islamic remembrance;
- Hindu devotional/contemplative environment;
- Abrahamic divine unity;
without reducing Sikhism to a hybrid of other religions.

## E
Strong:
An unusually founder-proximate sacred composition tradition relative to many ancient cases.

## Constructive status
**Level 2 parallel witness**, especially for remembrance, ego-critique, service, and truthful living.
