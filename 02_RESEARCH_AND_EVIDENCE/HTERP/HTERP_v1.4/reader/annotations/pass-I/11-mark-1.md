# Mark 1:1–15 — Baptism, Vision, Kingdom

## T
Mark is generally treated as the earliest surviving canonical Gospel. It is still decades after Jesus' ministry and is a literary-theological work.

## R
Sequence:
- John the Baptist;
- Jesus' baptism;
- heavens opened;
- Spirit;
- heavenly voice;
- wilderness testing;
- proclamation of God's kingdom and repentance.

Jesus' baptism by John is widely regarded as historically strong. The exact visionary details are less independently recoverable.

## A
Possible models:
- Jesus had a commissioning vision later transmitted;
- Mark shaped a remembered baptism theologically;
- both.

## C
Compare:
- Jewish prophetic commission narratives;
- Spirit traditions;
- later Gospel modifications;
- baptismal practices.

## E
Strong:
baptism by John; kingdom-centered public movement.

Plausible but unverified:
specific private vision/voice.

## Constructive status
**Core Jesus-witness unit.**
It supports reading Jesus primarily within Jewish prophetic/eschatological context before later Christological systems are imposed.
