# Early Buddhist Sample — First-Teaching / No-Self Complex

## T
No autograph of the Buddha survives.
Early discourses were orally transmitted, formulaically composed, and preserved in multiple school canons. Parallel comparison is therefore more important than treating one late manuscript as a verbatim transcript.

## R
Early Buddhist teaching strongly centers on:
- dukkha/unsatisfactoriness;
- causal conditions;
- cessation;
- path;
- impermanence;
- non-identification with a permanent self;
- disciplined ethical/meditative practice.

## A
The traditional “first sermon” is a powerful canonical framing, but exact speech wording and sequence cannot be historically recovered with transcript-level confidence.

## C
Compare:
- Jain/śramaṇa ascetic environment;
- Upanishadic self theories;
- later Buddhist school developments;
- modern meditation controls.

## E
Strong:
A historically early awakening/liberation tradition not dependent on revelation from a creator deity.

## Constructive status
**Level 3 comparative wisdom witness.**
Its no-self framework is not silently converted into Abrahamic soul language.

## Key methodological function
This case prevents the proto-canon from defining all genuine religious knowledge as “a personal God spoke.”
