# Exodus 3 — Burning Bush and Divine Name

## T
Exodus 3 is one of the Pentateuch's central divine-name/theophany texts. Historical-critical scholarship has long compared it with Exodus 6, which presents another name-disclosure formulation.

## R
Narrative sequence:
- Moses in Midian;
- mountain/wilderness setting;
- flame/bush theophany;
- divine identification with ancestral God;
- commission to confront Egypt;
- `ehyeh asher ehyeh`;
- YHWH named as the God of the ancestors.

## A
`Ehyeh asher ehyeh` has multiple defensible renderings:
“I am who I am,”
“I will be what I will be,”
and related formulations.

Its relation to the etymology of YHWH remains debated.

## C
Compare:
- Exodus 6:2–3;
- southern YHWH geography traditions;
- Midian traditions;
- older El/Shaddai ancestral language.

## E
Strong:
The text consciously theologizes the divine name and continuity between ancestral deity-language and YHWH.

Open:
Whether it preserves the historical first introduction of YHWH worship to Moses/Israel.

## Constructive status
**Retain as foundational name/commission text.**
Do not claim that a proposed etymology exhausts the divine nature.

## Difficult-text flag
Commission leads into plague/violence narratives requiring separate analysis.
