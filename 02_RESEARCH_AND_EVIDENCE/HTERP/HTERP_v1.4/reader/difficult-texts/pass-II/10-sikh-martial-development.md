# Sikh Martial / Khalsa Development

## Historical finding
Guru Nanak's founder-associated bani and the later Khalsa warrior tradition belong to the same Sikh historical tradition but not the same historical layer.

Modern scholarship emphasizes that Khalsa identity and martial institutions developed dynamically through:
- later Gurus;
- Mughal conflict;
- Guru Gobind Singh;
- eighteenth-century political/military conditions;
- peasant, scholarly, and elite actors.

Purnima Dhavan argues that key Khalsa practices did not simply appear fully formed at one instant in 1699.

## Constructive risk
Later militarization should not be projected backward onto Guru Nanak as though his original teaching were a warrior code.

Nor should Sikh martial tradition be caricatured as mere corruption; it emerged in concrete persecution/political contexts and became integral to Sikh history.

## Decision
Guru Nanak's bani remains Level 2 founder-associated witness.

Later Khalsa martial theology enters:
**historical/interpretive tradition**, evaluated separately.

The Provisional Way may learn:
- courage;
- defense of the vulnerable;
- resistance to oppression;
without importing a separate communal martial identity wholesale.
