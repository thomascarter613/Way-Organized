# Qur'anic Slavery and Concubinage

## Historical finding
Slavery was a social fact in seventh-century Arabia and early Islamic societies.

The Qur'an:
- recognizes enslaved persons;
- encourages manumission in several contexts;
- regulates relations involving “those whom the right hand possesses”;
- condemns forcing slave women into prostitution;
but does not simply abolish slavery as an institution.

Classical Islamic exegesis/jurisprudence normalized slavery and concubinage. Historical legal traditions generally did not require an enslaved woman's consent for sexual access by an owner in the way modern ethics requires meaningful consent.

## Strongest reform trajectory
Modern Muslim abolitionist readings emphasize:
- manumission;
- spiritual equality;
- gradual restriction;
- changed moral/legal context.

## Ethical objection
Ownership makes meaningful sexual consent structurally compromised.

## Decision
**REJECT NORMATIVE FORCE of slavery and concubinage.**

No person may be:
- owned;
- bought/sold;
- sexually accessed by ownership;
- forced into marriage/sex.

Manumission texts are retained as liberation trajectories, not as permission to recreate slavery.
