# Torah Sexuality and Purity

## Source cluster
Leviticus 12–20; Deuteronomic sexual/family law; related purity texts.

## Historical finding
Purity/pollution categories were central to ancient Israelite/Judahite religious identity. Scholarship shows that sexual-pollution language is culturally and rhetorically constructed, sometimes encoding patriarchal assumptions about bodies, property, community boundary, and holiness.

Leviticus 18 applies pollution language to prohibited male sexual actions within a Holiness framework and polemically marks Egyptians/Canaanites as negative foils.

## Constructive issue
The proto-canon cannot simply translate:
**ritually/culturally impure in an ancient holiness system**
into
**intrinsically immoral for all humans in all contexts**
without further argument.

## Decision
**SUSPEND blanket transfer of ancient purity law into universal civil/moral law.**

Retain:
- consent;
- fidelity;
- protection against exploitation/incest/abuse;
- seriousness of sexuality and family obligation.

Require separate ethical analysis for:
- same-sex relations;
- menstrual/sexual purity;
- mixed marriages;
- gendered property structures.

No person is treated as contaminating by bodily sex characteristics, menstruation, or involuntary bodily processes.

## Source
Eve Levavi Feinstein, *Sexual Pollution in the Hebrew Bible*.
