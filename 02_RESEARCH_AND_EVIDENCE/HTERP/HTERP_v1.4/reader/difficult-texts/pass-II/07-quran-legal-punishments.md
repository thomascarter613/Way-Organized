# Qur'anic and Later Islamic Legal Punishments

## Scope
The Qur'an contains legal/punitive material concerning theft, sexual wrongdoing, retaliation, false accusation, warfare, and community order. Later fiqh develops much more extensive criminal-law systems.

## Historical rule
Never collapse:
Qur'an → hadith → juristic doctrine
into one source layer.

Recent historical work on stoning illustrates why: Syed Atif Rizwan (OUP, 2026) argues from hadith transmission history that stoning was not an Islamic punishment during Muhammad's lifetime and became normalized later.

That thesis is important and recent, not automatically final consensus.

## Constructive decision
**No religious penal code is adopted from scripture by direct transfer.**

Community religious discipline may include:
- mediation;
- restitution;
- temporary role suspension;
- membership consequences;
but never corporal or capital punishment.

Civil crime is handled by legitimate civil institutions and due process.

## Principle
The more irreversible the penalty, the higher the evidentiary and procedural burden.

## Source
Syed Atif Rizwan, *Stoning as Punishment in Early Islam* (OUP, 2026), plus Qur'anic-law scholarship.
