# Early Christian Anti-Judaism and Supersessionism

## Problem
Some Christian texts portray “the Jews,” Jewish leaders, synagogue communities, or Torah practice polemically. Later readers often detached intra-Jewish first-century disputes from context and used them against Jews as a people.

## Source-critical point
Jesus, his earliest followers, Paul, and the Gospel traditions emerge from Jewish contexts. Matthew can be read as participating in intra-Jewish polemic; John's `Ioudaioi` language is rhetorically complex and has a dangerous reception history.

Early Christian apocrypha also show varying degrees of anti-Judaism and supersessionism.

## Constructive decision
**REJECT collective Jewish guilt and replacement theology that denies Jewish dignity or living legitimacy.**

Read polemic with:
- historical context;
- speaker/audience specificity;
- awareness of later antisemitic reception.

The Abrahamic spine explicitly preserves Judaism as a living tradition rather than merely a failed preface to Christianity.
