# Paul, Slavery, and Household Relations

## Source cluster
Philemon; Pauline/Deutero-Pauline household material; later Pastorals and 1 Peter.

## Historical finding
Early Christianity arose within a slaveholding Roman world.

Laura Nasrallah argues that Paul's letters, including Philemon, are deeply embedded in Roman slave-trading social realities. Onesimus remains socially legible as both person and property within that environment.

Later household codes tell enslaved people to obey masters and helped Christian communities adapt to slaveholding household structures.

## Strongest constructive trajectory
Paul can rhetorically destabilize status distinctions (“brother,” shared identity in Christ), and Philemon has been read toward manumission.

## Ethical objection
The New Testament does not issue a clear universal abolition command and was later used to legitimate slavery.

## Decision
**REJECT NORMATIVE FORCE of slave obedience/master authority.**

Retain:
- equal dignity;
- brother/sister language;
- social-status relativization;
- liberationist trajectories.

No apostolic text can restore human ownership to the Provisional Way.

## Sources
