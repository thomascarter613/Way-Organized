# Prophetic Nationalism and the Nations

## Problem
Prophetic books contain both:
- universal moral visions and peace;
- national election language;
- violent judgment against foreign nations;
- restoration and sometimes domination imagery.

## Historical context
Pre-exilic and post-exilic prophetic texts speak from communities experiencing imperial domination, internal violence, conquest, exile, and return. Scholarship on the Minor Prophets notes recurring cycles in which violence is answered by imagined divine violence.

## Constructive risk
Ancient national-theological categories can be recruited into modern ethnonationalism:
> our nation = God's uniquely righteous people;
> opponents = divinely condemned nations.

## Decision
**REINTERPRET election as responsibility, not ethnic superiority.**

Retain:
- accountability of rulers/nations;
- concern for oppressed peoples;
- peace and restoration visions.

Reject:
- racial/ethnic supremacy;
- automatic sacralization of any modern state;
- claims that contemporary geopolitical enemies are prophetically designated for destruction.

## Source
Oxford Handbook of the Minor Prophets, “Violence in the Minor Prophets.”
