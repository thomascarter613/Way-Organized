# Biblical Capital Punishment

## Source cluster
Pentateuchal laws and narrative/legal traditions prescribing death for murder and various religious, sexual, filial, or communal offenses.

## Historical finding
Capital penalties are genuinely present in biblical law. The relationship between written legal collections and actual court enforcement is historically complex.

Modern scholarship on biblical ethics emphasizes that the Hebrew Bible itself contains multiple perspectives on killing, punishment, witness, premeditation, sanctuary, and refuge.

## Constructive objection
Irreversible punishment requires extraordinary certainty. Many biblical capital categories concern conduct that the Provisional Way does not regard as a legitimate basis for execution, including religious offenses.

## Decision
**REJECT NORMATIVE FORCE of scriptural capital punishment as community religious law.**

The religious community has no authority to execute:
- apostates;
- blasphemers;
- Sabbath violators;
- sexual offenders merely because an ancient text assigns death.

Serious violent crime belongs to legitimate civil law with due process, not clerical punishment.

## Source
Susan Niditch, “On Killing and Dying: The Case of Capital Punishment,” OUP.
