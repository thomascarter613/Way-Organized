# Exodus Plagues and Death of the Firstborn

## Problem
Exodus frames liberation from oppression through escalating plagues culminating in the death of Egyptian firstborns.

## Historical/memory context
The plague cycle is a crafted theological narrative. Scholars have explored memories of epidemic, conflict, ritual, and literary development; no single historical reconstruction is established.

## Strongest theological defense
- judgment on an oppressive imperial order;
- liberation of enslaved people;
- Pharaoh repeatedly refuses release;
- narrative demonstrates YHWH's supremacy.

## Ethical objection
The final plague imposes death on children/households beyond the individual culpability of Pharaoh.
Collective punishment remains morally troubling even within a liberation narrative.

## Constructive decision
**SUSPEND metaphysical justification; reject human imitation.**

Retain:
- liberation from oppression;
- resistance to imperial dehumanization;
- Passover remembrance of deliverance.

Do not teach:
- that killing opponents' children is a legitimate instrument of justice;
- that collective suffering proves divine guilt.

## Epistemic humility
The framework does not claim to possess a complete moral defense of every act attributed to God in ancient narrative.
