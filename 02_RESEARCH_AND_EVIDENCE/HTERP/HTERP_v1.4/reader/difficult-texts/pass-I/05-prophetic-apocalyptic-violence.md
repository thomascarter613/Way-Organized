# Prophetic and Apocalyptic Divine Violence

## Source cluster
Judgment oracles in the Prophets, Ezekiel, later apocalyptic literature, Revelation.

## Literary setting
Ancient prophetic/apocalyptic texts frequently dramatize moral conflict through:
- war;
- plague;
- cosmic collapse;
- divine armies;
- destruction of enemies.

Revelation in particular intentionally reuses Exodus/holy-war imagery.

## Strongest constructive reading
Violence can symbolize:
- defeat of oppression;
- judgment;
- reversal of unjust power;
- hope that evil does not rule forever.

## Ethical objection
Symbolic or imagined violence can:
- normalize enemy dehumanization;
- license later literal violence;
- depict justice through acts that appear themselves unjust.

Modern scholarship explicitly recognizes this ethical paradox in Revelation.

## Constructive decision
**REINTERPRET and constrain.**

Retain:
- moral accountability;
- hope that oppression ends;
- refusal to declare evil permanent.

Reject:
- use of apocalyptic enemies as templates for harming real-world outgroups;
- claims that contemporary war is automatically divine judgment.
