# Qur'an 4:34 — Marital Hierarchy / Discipline

## Problem
Q 4:34 has historically been read to grant husbands authority within marriage and, in cases of a wife's `nushūz`, to permit a sequence culminating in `wa-ḍribūhunna`, classically often understood as physical striking.

## Historical reception
Ayesha Chaudhry's extensive study shows that major premodern Sunni legal traditions generally accepted a husband's right to physically discipline a wife within limits, even while restricting severity.

Modern Muslim interpreters have developed:
- nonviolent lexical readings;
- procedural/legal reinterpretations;
- egalitarian theological frameworks;
- contextual-historical limitation.

The history is genuinely plural, but it is inaccurate to pretend the dominant premodern legal tradition simply prohibited all physical discipline.

## Ethical objection
Intimate partner violence and coerced marital hierarchy conflict with:
- equal human dignity;
- bodily safety;
- meaningful consent;
- safeguarding.

## Constructive decision
**REJECT NORMATIVE FORCE for spouse-beating or unilateral coercive marital authority.**

Whatever historical meaning is assigned to the verse, it cannot authorize violence in the Provisional Way.

Marriage requires:
- mutual consent;
- equal dignity;
- safety;
- reciprocal responsibility;
- legal protection.

## Source
Ayesha Chaudhry, *Domestic Violence and the Islamic Tradition*; Hadia Mubarak; Oxford Handbooks on Islam and women.
