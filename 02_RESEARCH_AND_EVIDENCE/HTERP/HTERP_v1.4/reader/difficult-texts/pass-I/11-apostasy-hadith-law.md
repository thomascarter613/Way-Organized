# Apostasy — Qur'an, Hadith, and Later Law

## Source distinction

### Qur'an
The Qur'an condemns apostasy/rejection religiously in several passages but does not clearly prescribe a worldly death penalty simply for changing belief.

### Hadith / classical law
Virtually all classical jurists developed some form of capital apostasy rule, drawing on hadith, political concepts of community/rebellion, and later jurisprudence.

Modern scholars and jurists debate whether:
- early apostasy was inseparable from treason/rebellion;
- relevant hadith justify a universal death penalty;
- the classical rule remains valid.

## Historical importance
The classical rule is real; it should not be erased by pretending it never existed.

But “classical consensus” is not identical with Qur'anic proclamation or automatically final morality.

## Constructive decision
**REJECT NORMATIVE FORCE of punishment for mere change of belief.**

A person may:
- join;
- leave;
- change beliefs;
- criticize the framework;
without religiously authorized violence or civil punishment from the community.

Actual crimes—violence, espionage, treason—are judged as those crimes, not as beliefs.
