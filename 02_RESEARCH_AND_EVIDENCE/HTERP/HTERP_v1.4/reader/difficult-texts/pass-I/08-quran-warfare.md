# Qur'anic Warfare Passages

## Source cluster
Especially Q 2:190–193, Q 8, Q 9, Q 22:39–40, and related passages.

## Historical context
Many warfare passages arise within the Medinan community's concrete conflicts, treaty relations, expulsion, and state formation.

Classical and modern interpreters differ over:
- defensive versus offensive jihad;
- abrogation;
- scope of Q 9:5 and 9:29;
- relationship between particular conflicts and universal law.

Modern contextual scholarship has argued that Q 9:5/9:29 need not abrogate restrictions on fighting and should be read within treaty/conflict contexts.

## Strongest traditional expansive reading
Some classical frameworks developed continuing offensive-jihad rules and political subordination categories.

## Strongest contextual reading
Commands to fight are conditioned by concrete hostility, treaty violation, expulsion, or political conflict rather than universal orders to kill unbelievers.

## Constructive decision
**REJECT NORMATIVE FORCE for forced conversion, genocide, or war merely because another community has different beliefs.**

Potential force is limited by:
- defense/protection;
- necessity;
- discrimination between combatant/noncombatant;
- proportionality;
- pursuit of peace when possible.

## Freedom of conscience
Belief itself is not a legitimate target of violence.
