# Ḥerem / Conquest / Holy-War Texts

## Source cluster
Deuteronomy 7, 20; Joshua; related conquest traditions.

## Historical/literary issue
The texts depict or command destruction of populations under the category often called **ḥerem** (“devoted to destruction” in many translations).

Whether the narratives correspond closely to historical conquest events is separate from their literary-theological function. Archaeology and historical reconstruction complicate a simple literal conquest model, but that does not remove the ethical problem from the text.

## Strongest charitable readings
Traditional and modern interpreters have proposed:
- unique unrepeatable divine judgment;
- hyperbolic ancient war rhetoric;
- spiritual/allegorical interpretation;
- gradual moral pedagogy;
- literary rather than historical description.

## Strongest ethical objection
At face value, some passages depict divinely authorized destruction of entire populations.

Such texts have also had a reception history in which conquest categories were applied to later human enemies.

## Constructive decision
**REJECT NORMATIVE FORCE for genocide, ethnic cleansing, forced conversion, or holy war against populations.**

No modern leader, community, or alleged prophet may invoke these passages to authorize extermination.

Historical/literary study remains mandatory.

## Principle
Even a claimed revelation commanding group extermination would require evidence far beyond private certainty; under the current constitution it cannot become actionable community law.

## Source
Christian Hofreiter, *Making Sense of Old Testament Genocide* (OUP), including its reception-history analysis.
