# Genesis 22 — The Binding of Isaac

## Problem
The narrative portrays God testing Abraham by commanding the offering of his son, then stopping the killing.

## Historical context
Child sacrifice and firstborn sacrifice belong to the wider ancient Near Eastern religious world. The text can be read against that background, though the precise relationship remains debated.

## Strongest constructive readings
- radical trust;
- exposure and rejection of child sacrifice;
- divine provision rather than human sacrifice;
- a narrative about testing rather than a standing moral law.

## Ethical objection
If read as a timeless model of obedience, the story can imply:
> a claimed divine command may suspend ordinary moral protection of one's child.

That principle is unacceptable under HTERP safeguards.

## Constructive decision
**REINTERPRET; explicitly reject imitation.**

Retain:
- willingness to surrender ego/possessiveness;
- recognition that children are not property;
- divine provision/mercy themes.

Reject:
- harming a child because of a private revelation;
- treating unquestioning obedience as superior to safeguarding.

## Rule
A parent who believes a voice commands harm to a child must not obey the voice and should seek immediate safety/clinical/support intervention where necessary.
