# Reader VII — Early Buddhist Witness

## Constructive role
Level 3 comparative awakening/wisdom.

## Source caution
No surviving text is an autograph of the Buddha.
Early Buddhist discourses were orally transmitted and developed across schools.

## Method
Prioritize teachings appearing in early Nikāya/Āgama strata and parallel traditions where possible.

## Thematic map

### Four Noble Truths / path
Study the early discourse traditions surrounding the first sermon.

### Impermanence / no-self
Study early discourse parallels on:
- conditioned phenomena;
- aggregates;
- self-identification.

### Dependent arising
Read multiple early formulations; do not assume every later scholastic elaboration was present in identical form.

### Ethics
- non-harming;
- right speech;
- right livelihood;
- generosity;
- compassion;
- mental discipline.

### Meditation
Separate:
- calm/absorption;
- insight;
- later school-specific techniques.

## Key terms
- **dukkha**
- **anicca**
- **anattā/anātman**
- **dhamma/dharma**
- **nibbāna/nirvāṇa**
- **sati**
- **samādhi**
- **mettā**
- **karuṇā**

## Comparative question
Does the framework describe an ultimate reality, a method of liberation, a phenomenology of cognition, or all three?

## Constructive limit
Buddhist teachings may be adopted as wisdom/practice where compatible, but are not silently translated into theism.
