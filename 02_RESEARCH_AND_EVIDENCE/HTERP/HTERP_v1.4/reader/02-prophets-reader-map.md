# Reader II — Hebrew Prophets

## Constructive role
Level 1 spine; especially important for justice, idolatry critique, repentance, and prophetic encounter.

## Amos
Read:
1–2 judgment frame
3 prophetic vocation
5 justice/cult critique
7–9 visions

Key tension:
ritual religion versus justice.

## Hosea
Read:
1–3 symbolic marriage carefully
6 covenant/knowledge
11 divine compassion
14 restoration

Difficult-text:
gendered/metaphorical violence and family symbolism.

## Isaiah
### Isaiah 1–12
- justice;
- temple vision (6);
- Assyrian crisis;
- Immanuel material.

### Isaiah 40–55
- exile/restoration;
- creator language;
- strong monotheistic formulations;
- servant texts.

### Isaiah 56–66
- post-exilic community;
- inclusion/exclusion;
- eschatological hope.

Reader rule:
Do not treat all 66 chapters as one eighth-century transcript.

## Jeremiah
Read:
1 call
7 temple sermon
20 prophetic burden
31 new-covenant material
36 scroll/transmission narrative

Special source note:
Masoretic and Septuagint Jeremiah differ significantly.

## Ezekiel
Read:
1–3 throne/commission
8–11 temple vision
18 responsibility
34 failed shepherds
36 transformation
37 dry bones
40–48 ideal temple

Control links:
vision, journey, symbolic action, literary shaping.

## Micah
Read:
3 leadership corruption
4 peace vision
6 justice/mercy/humility

## Additional prophetic corpus
Zephaniah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi, Joel, Obadiah, Jonah are included in the wider reader and must not be excluded merely because their emphases fit the framework less comfortably.

## Prophetic comparison questions
- Does the prophet claim a voice, vision, dream, symbolic action, or poetic insight?
- How is the message authenticated?
- Is prediction central or secondary?
- Does the prophet oppose cult, or corrupt cult?
- What is the relationship between national deity-language and universal sovereignty?
- How much of the surviving book is later tradent/redactor material?
