# HTE-050 — Buddhist Cultivated Meditation and Absorption

## Why separated from HTE-019
HTE-019 concerns the historical Buddha/awakening tradition. HTE-050 treats the **replicable practice systems** developed in Buddhist communities.

## Practice diversity
Sarah Shaw emphasizes that “Buddhist meditation” is not one technique. Practices include:
- seated and walking cultivation;
- samatha/śamatha calming;
- vipassanā/vipaśyanā investigation;
- absorptions/jhānas;
- chanting/devotion;
- recollections;
- visualization in later traditions;
- ethical and doctrinal preparation.

## Encounter classification
Primarily **cultivated alteration of cognition/attention**, not communication from a creator deity.

## Phenomenology
Tradition-dependent reports include:
- concentration;
- tranquility;
- altered sensory salience;
- rapture/bliss;
- equanimity;
- insight into impermanence/no-self;
- cessation-like states;
- visionary imagery in some forms.

## HTERP relevance
Buddhist systems provide a long-term training model in which extraordinary states can be intentionally conditioned. This strongly supports investigation of learning, expectation, attentional control, and neuroplasticity.

## Common-core caution
Equating Buddhist cessation/no-self with Christian union, Sufi nearness, or Vedāntic Brahman realization can erase substantive doctrinal differences.

## Baseline source
Sarah Shaw, “Buddhist Meditation and Contemplation,” Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Religion.
