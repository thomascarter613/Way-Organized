# HTE-013 — Early Israelite Prophetic Tradition

## Scope
Samuel, Nathan, Elijah/Elisha, seer traditions, prophetic bands, and early monarchy narratives.

## Comparative position
These traditions emerge within the same broad ancient Near Eastern world in which Mari and Neo-Assyrian prophecy, dream revelation, omen science, and oracles already existed. Israelite prophecy is therefore not historically sui generis at the level of “a deity communicates through a human intermediary.”

## Distinctive trajectory
Over time, Israelite/Judahite prophetic literature increasingly foregrounds:
- YHWH's exclusive authority;
- covenantal fidelity;
- critique of kings;
- justice and exploitation;
- rejection of rival cults;
- national catastrophe interpreted morally/theologically.

## HTERP research task
Separate the historical institution of prophecy from later literary portraits of heroic prophets. Messenger formulas and divine speech should be compared directly with Mari/Assyrian corpora before claims of uniqueness are made.
