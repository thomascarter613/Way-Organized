# HTE-006 — Shang Oracle-Bone Divination

- **Setting:** late Shang royal court, China
- **Evidence:** directly inscribed animal bones/turtle plastrons; British Museum examples date c. 1200–1050 BCE
- **Mode:** DIV

The bones are exceptionally strong evidence that royal divination actually occurred. Questions/prognostications concern war, harvest, weather, childbirth, illness, hunting, ritual, and ancestors. They are weak evidence for subjective phenomenology: the inscriptions document a procedure and record, not an introspective account of what a diviner experienced.

HTERP therefore classifies Shang material as **procedural access to hidden information**, not messenger prophecy. Its distance from Mari makes direct literary borrowing unlikely, but both systems address universal political and environmental uncertainty. The high-value test is whether securely ordered inscriptions preserve prediction plus independently known outcome sufficiently well for accuracy analysis.

**Baseline:** British Museum OA+.180 and 1911,1111.2.
