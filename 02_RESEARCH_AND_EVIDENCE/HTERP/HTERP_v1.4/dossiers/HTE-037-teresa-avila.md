# HTE-037 — Teresa of Ávila

## Identification
- **Period:** sixteenth-century Spain
- **Source advantage:** extensive first-person writings composed within Teresa's lifetime
- **Mode:** contemplative prayer, PRS, VIS, ecstatic/unitive states

## Source profile
Teresa describes graded prayer, recollection, rapture, locutions, visions, perceived divine presence, and “spiritual marriage.” Her writings are unusually valuable because the experiencer herself records and analyzes the phenomena while also submitting them to theological discernment.

## Important theological distinction
Teresa's “union” is relational and Christian-theistic. It does not straightforwardly assert that the human self becomes numerically identical with God.

## Phenomenology
Her corpus includes:
- inward recollection;
- altered bodily control during rapture;
- locutions;
- intellectual/imaginative visions;
- overwhelming love;
- pain/ecstasy;
- transformation in conduct;
- difficulty expressing the experience.

## Epistemology
Modern philosophical discussion often uses Teresa in debates over whether mystical experience can justify belief. Steven Payne cautions against cherry-picking isolated experiences: Teresa embeds them in long-term virtues, discernment, theological study, and transformed capacities.

## Controls
Potential comparisons:
- absorption;
- dissociation;
- ecstatic seizure phenomenology;
- expectation shaped by Christian devotional culture;
- social/institutional pressures of sixteenth-century Spain.

Retrospective diagnosis is prohibited.

## HTERP result
Teresa is a premier case for **high-documentation, first-person, cultivated theistic mysticism**, not external verification of God.

## Baseline sources
- Steven Payne, “Teresa of Ávila,” *Oxford Handbook of the Epistemology of Theology*.
- Edward Howells, “Mystical Theology and Human Experience,” *Oxford Handbook of Mystical Theology*.
