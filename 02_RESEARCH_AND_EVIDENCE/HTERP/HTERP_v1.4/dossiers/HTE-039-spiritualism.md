# HTE-039 — Nineteenth-Century Spiritualism

## Historical starting point
Modern Anglo-American Spiritualism is conventionally associated with the Fox sisters' 1848 rapping claims at Hydesville, New York. The movement rapidly developed séances, table phenomena, trance mediums, spirit writing, photography, and physical mediumship.

## Evidentiary importance
Spiritualism creates both:
1. a huge corpus of apparently information-bearing spirit claims; and
2. a huge fraud-control corpus.

Exposures by magicians and investigators demonstrated that some seemingly supernatural phenomena could be produced through concealed physical techniques, cueing, prepared environments, and performance.

## HTERP rule
No inference from:
> “some mediums were fraudulent”
to:
> “all mediumship is fraudulent.”

And no inference from:
> “some sitters received accurate information”
to:
> “the deceased communicated.”

Each case must survive leakage and deception controls.

## Historical transition
The Society for Psychical Research and later experimental programs arose partly because séance-room evidence was too uncontrolled. That methodological transition is central to HTERP.

## Fox sisters caution
One of the Fox sisters publicly confessed to fraud decades later and later repudiated that confession. The conflicting later testimony makes the origin story itself a poor decisive case.

## Result
Historical Spiritualism is most valuable as the source of **adversarial methodology**: it teaches us exactly why blinding, full recording, decoys, and fraud expertise are mandatory.
