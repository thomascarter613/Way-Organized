# HTE-026 — The Apocalypse / Revelation of John

## Identification
- **Source:** Book of Revelation
- **Claimed seer:** John on Patmos
- **Mode:** VIS, AUD, MSG, JRN-like heavenly vision
- **Genre:** apocalypse / prophecy / letter

## Phenomenology in the text
John repeatedly reports:
- being "in the Spirit";
- auditory commands;
- luminous/humanlike heavenly figures;
- throne-room visions;
- angelic interpreters;
- symbolic beasts and cosmic scenes;
- transport into visionary settings;
- written transmission of what is seen/heard.

Christopher Rowland emphasizes that Revelation's repeated "I saw" language places significant visionary experience within first-century Christian prophecy and connects it to Ezekiel and Zechariah.

## Source problem
Revelation is itself the earliest surviving literary artifact of the experience claim. It is not an external observer's transcript. Its highly structured symbolism, scriptural allusion, rhetoric, and political theology mean phenomenology and literary artistry cannot be naively separated.

## Dependency
The work is deeply dependent on:
- Ezekiel;
- Daniel;
- Zechariah;
- Exodus/plague imagery;
- early Christian Christology.

Therefore similarity with those texts is literary-theological dependence, not independent convergence.

## HTERP value
A strong case for **visionary literature whose author presents himself as experiencer**. It gives better access to a visionary self-presentation than to the external cause of the experience.

## Control questions
- dreamlike symbolic recombination;
- fasting/prayer context (where inferable);
- literary composition after the experience;
- community expectations of apocalypse;
- possibility that "vision" functions partly as a literary convention.

## Baseline source
Christopher Rowland, “The Book of Revelation: The Apocalypse of Jesus Christ,” Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Religion.
