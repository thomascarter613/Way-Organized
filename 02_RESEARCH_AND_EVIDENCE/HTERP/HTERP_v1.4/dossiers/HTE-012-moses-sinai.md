# HTE-012 — Moses / Sinai Traditions

## Canonical role
The Pentateuch portrays Moses as:
- liberator from Egypt;
- recipient/mediator of YHWH's revelation;
- wilderness leader;
- law mediator;
- founder figure for Yahwistic covenantal religion.

Oxford Bibliographies summarizes these as the central literary events of Moses' life, including Midian, encounter with Yahweh at the divine mountain, Exodus, and lawgiving.

## Historical status
There is no contemporary Moses archive comparable to Mari, Shang, or Paul. Historical reconstruction of a single Moses biography is therefore far weaker than the canonical narrative's detail.

## Southern-Yahweh relevance
The narrative's Midian and wilderness geography intersects with independent scholarly arguments that early Yahweh traditions have southern associations. This makes the cluster historically interesting but does not prove the canonical sequence happened exactly as narrated.

## HTERP result
Treat Moses as:
**foundational Yahwistic memory + possible historical core(s) + extensive literary/theological formation**.
