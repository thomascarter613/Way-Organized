# HTE-041 — Bahá'u'lláh

## Identification
- **Mirza Husayn-'Ali Nuri / Bahá'u'lláh:** 1817–1892
- **Context:** nineteenth-century Iranian Shi'i/Babi messianism
- **Modes:** MSG, INS, revelation-writing

## Historical genealogy
Encyclopaedia Iranica places the Bahá'í Faith historically within:
Shi'i Shaykhism → Bábism → Bahá'u'lláh's claim → Bahá'í religion.

Bahá'u'lláh emerged from the Babi movement and eventually proclaimed himself the promised figure anticipated by the Báb. His followers gradually became a distinct Bahá'í community during the 1860s–70s.

## Source advantage
Bahá'u'lláh produced a large founder-era written corpus. This gives HTERP direct access to claimed revelatory products without centuries of oral transmission.

## Methodological distinction
The existence, date, and content of Bahá'u'lláh's writings are historical facts.

The claim that the writings were divinely revealed is a separate metaphysical proposition.

## Progressive revelation
The movement explicitly develops a theory of religious evolution/progressive revelation. This makes it unusually relevant to HTERP because it offers an internal theological explanation for cross-religious similarity and difference.

## Dependency
The system is historically dependent on:
- Shi'i Islam;
- Shaykhism;
- Bábism;
- Abrahamic prophetic categories.

Therefore its cross-religious synthesis cannot count as independent evidence that all earlier prophets shared one source.

## Result
Bahá'u'lláh is exceptionally useful for studying **a modern founder consciously reframing prior revelation into a universal historical theology**.
