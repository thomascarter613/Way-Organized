# HTE-005 — Egyptian Dream and Oracle Traditions

**Modes:** DRM, DIV, oracle consultation.

## Direct material evidence
British Museum Papyrus Chester Beatty III, from Deir el-Medina and dated around 1220 BCE, preserves a hieratic manual for interpreting dreams. The manual uses recurring “if a man sees himself in a dream…” structures and assigns good/bad prognoses and interpretations.

This provides exceptionally concrete evidence that dream interpretation was formalized in New Kingdom Egypt.

Later Egyptian evidence also contains temple oracles and dreams as important forms of interaction with gods; in later periods festivals and processional oracles became major settings for consultation.

## HTERP significance
Egypt provides another ancient system where dream content is **decoded through a culturally standardized interpretive matrix**. This supports H2 cultural shaping strongly: the dream occurs, but meaning is supplied through a learned system.
