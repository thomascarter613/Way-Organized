# HTE-042 — Marian and Related Apparition Traditions

## Why apparitions need internal separation
“Apparition” can refer to:
- one visionary seeing a figure;
- several visionaries sharing a claim;
- a crowd observing secondary physical phenomena;
- later healings or signs;
- institutional recognition of devotional value.

These are different evidence types.

## Lourdes, 1858
Bernadette Soubirous reported eighteen visions of a female figure later identified as Mary. Reference scholarship notes that her companions did not see the apparition itself.

### Evidentiary form
single principal visionary  
+ public observation of visionary behavior  
+ later investigation  
+ subsequent healing/pilgrimage tradition.

## Fátima, 1917
Three shepherd children reported a sequence of Marian appearances. For the final October event, a very large crowd gathered after a public prediction of a sign. Many witnesses reported an unusual solar phenomenon, though descriptions varied.

### Crucial distinction
The crowd is evidence concerning **reported solar phenomena**, not tens of thousands independently seeing Mary.

## Institutional discernment
The Catholic Church's 2024 norms explicitly separate:
- possible signs/spiritual fruits;
- doctrinal/moral problems;
- pastoral permission for devotion;
from
- a definitive supernatural declaration.

The norms generally avoid requiring an affirmative judgment that an event is supernatural.

## HTERP result
Apparitions are one of the best domains for a **witness-topology model**:
visionary witness ≠ behavioral witness ≠ secondary-phenomenon witness ≠ later testimony.
