# HTE-040 — Joseph Smith

## Identification
- **Life:** 1805–1844
- **Movement:** Latter Day Saint tradition
- **Modes:** VIS, APP, MSG, revelatory dictation/translation claims

## Documentary advantage
The Joseph Smith Papers provide multiple firsthand and contemporary accounts of Smith's claimed First Vision written during his lifetime.

The project identifies four firsthand/Smith-directed account groups:
- ca. 1832 history;
- 1835 journal;
- 1838 history;
- 1842 published “Church History” account.

It also preserves accounts written by contemporaries who heard Smith narrate the experience.

## HTERP significance
This permits a direct **version-history analysis** impossible for many ancient founders.

## What variation can mean
Differences among accounts may reflect:
- audience;
- purpose;
- memory;
- theological development;
- compression/expansion;
- scribal mediation;
- genuine inconsistency.

Variation is neither automatically proof of fabrication nor irrelevant.

## Earliest-document problem
The claimed event is conventionally dated to around 1820, while the earliest known firsthand written account is ca. 1832. That gap must remain visible.

## Wider revelation corpus
Smith's career includes:
- angelic visitation claims;
- plates/translation claims;
- dictated revelations;
- visionary temple experiences.

These require separate dossiers rather than being treated as one evidentiary event.

## HTERP result
Joseph Smith is a premier modern case for studying:
**encounter claim → repeated retelling → canonization → institutional theology**.

## Baseline source
Joseph Smith Papers, “Accounts of Joseph Smith's First Vision.”
