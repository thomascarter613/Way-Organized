# HTE-045/046 — Controlled and Psychoactive-Occasioned Mystical Experience

## Why this is a calibration case

Unlike ancient prophecy, modern experiments can manipulate a causal variable, observe participants prospectively, standardize questionnaires, and compare conditions.

This makes psychedelic mystical experience one of HTERP's strongest **mechanism controls**.

## Core experimental evidence

In the Griffiths research program, healthy volunteers received psilocybin under controlled supportive conditions. Double-blind designs compared psilocybin with methylphenidate or varied dose. The work demonstrated that psilocybin can occasion experiences participants rate as mystical, personally meaningful, and spiritually significant, with some reported effects persisting months later.

The revised Mystical Experience Questionnaire (MEQ30) has four empirically supported dimensions:
1. mystical factor — unity, sacredness, noetic quality;
2. positive mood;
3. transcendence of time and space;
4. ineffability.

## What the experiment proves

It strongly supports:
- a pharmacological intervention can increase the probability/intensity of mystical-type phenomenology;
- the phenomenology can be measured prospectively;
- at least some experiences described with religious/mystical vocabulary do not require spontaneous external events to occur;
- intense “noetic” certainty can be generated in a controlled context.

## What it does **not** prove

It does not establish:
- that all historical mystical experience was drug-induced;
- that no transcendent reality exists;
- that pharmacological induction makes an experience metaphysically false;
- that the participant's interpretation is correct.

The logical point is narrower: **mystical phenomenology alone is not sufficient evidence for external transcendence**, because similar phenomenology is causally inducible.

## Expectancy and context

The original Griffiths studies used substantial preparation and a supportive environment. Many volunteers had some religious/spiritual involvement. Set, setting, expectation, interpersonal support, and questionnaire framing are therefore potential cultural/psychological moderators.

This is not a flaw; it is evidence that phenomenology is produced by interactions among pharmacology, person, and context.

## Source grade
P1 experimental self-report with controlled causal manipulation and investigator observation.

## Hypothesis analysis

### H0
Not useful as a global explanation: participants are not generally fabricating the fact that they experienced something.

### H1
Strongly supported. Neuropharmacological manipulation demonstrably changes the probability of mystical-type states.

### H2
Strongly supported as moderator/interpretation layer.

### H3/H4
Not ruled out logically, but the standard study design does not test external transcendence. An H3/H4 claim would require independent information or effects not predicted by the pharmacological/cognitive model.

### H5
Working stance.

## Discriminating next tests
- neutral versus explicitly spiritual framing;
- cross-cultural samples;
- blinded analysis of descriptions;
- prospective anomalous-information tasks embedded within altered states;
- comparisons with meditation, spontaneous mystical experience, and neurological ecstasy.
