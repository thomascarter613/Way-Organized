# HTE-030 — Guru Nanak and Early Sikh Sacred Composition

**Guru Nanak:** 1469–1539, Punjab.  
**Modes:** INS, devotional/noetic encounter; MSG/ILL evaluated text by text.

Sikh manuscript scholarship suggests written transmission of *bani* may have begun during Nanak's lifetime. Early sixteenth- and later sixteenth-century pothi traditions make Sikhism unusually valuable for studying the relation between founder-associated composition and early scripture formation.

HTERP separates: compositions plausibly attributable to Nanak; early manuscript witnesses; later Janamsakhi biography; later doctrinal systematization. Founder-associated teaching strongly emphasizes one divine reality, remembrance, moral integrity, devotion, and critique of empty external religiosity.

**Baseline:** Pashaura Singh, *The Guru Granth Sahib*; Gurinder Singh Mann, *The Making of Sikh Scripture*.
