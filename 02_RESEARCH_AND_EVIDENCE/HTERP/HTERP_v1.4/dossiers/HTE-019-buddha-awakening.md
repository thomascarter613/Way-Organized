# HTE-019 — Gautama Buddha and Awakening

- **Historical figure:** generally placed in northern India in the fifth century BCE
- **Mode:** ILL / disciplined insight; later narratives also contain visionary/supernatural material

The Buddha's historicity is broadly accepted, but exact biography and wording are difficult to reconstruct. Early Buddhist teaching was orally transmitted; the Pali canon was written down centuries later, while Gandharan manuscripts illuminate the shift from oral to written Buddhist literature from about the first century BCE onward. Recent scholarship emphasizes the creative, formulaic character of early discourse composition.

The foundational epistemic claim is not principally “a creator spoke to me,” but **disciplined awakening to the structure of suffering, causation, impermanence, no-self, and liberation**. This makes Buddhism an essential category-control case.

**Baseline:** Alexander Wynne, “Gotama, the Historical Buddha”; Oxford Research Encyclopedia on earliest Buddhist manuscripts; Eviatar Shulman, *Visions of the Buddha*; SEP “Buddha.”
