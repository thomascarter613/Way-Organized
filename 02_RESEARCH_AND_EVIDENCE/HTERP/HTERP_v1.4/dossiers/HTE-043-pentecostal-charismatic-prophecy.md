# HTE-043 — Modern Pentecostal / Charismatic Prophecy

## Scope
Contemporary Christian communities in which members report:
- words from God;
- impressions;
- visions;
- dreams;
- predictive messages;
- “words of knowledge”;
- healing information.

## Evidentiary advantage
These claims occur in the age of:
- video;
- social media;
- timestamps;
- electronic archives;
- prospective preregistration.

There is therefore no methodological reason to rely primarily on retrospective anecdotes.

## Current scholarly picture
Empirical and theological scholarship documents prophecy as a real and socially consequential Pentecostal/charismatic practice. Some regional movements include “forensic prophecy,” prophetic consultation, and predictive claims.

Documentation of the practice is **not** evidence that predictions exceed chance or normal inference.

## HTERP-PRED application
A participating prophet could submit:
- exact prediction;
- target;
- probability;
- deadline;
- success/failure criterion;
before outcome.

Repeated performance could then be compared with:
- base rates;
- informed forecasters;
- chance;
- calibration.

## HTERP-AI application
“Word of knowledge” cases can be tested with:
- blinded recipients;
- no prior contact;
- decoys;
- full transcript;
- independent scoring.

## Result
This is one of HTERP's best **future prospective laboratories**. Retrospective miracle collections should receive lower weight than tests that can be performed now.

## Baseline sources
- Samuel W. Muindi, empirical-biblical study of Pentecostal-charismatic prophecy, University of Birmingham.
- Mookgo Kgatle, critical study of Pentecostal prophecy practices in Southern Africa.
