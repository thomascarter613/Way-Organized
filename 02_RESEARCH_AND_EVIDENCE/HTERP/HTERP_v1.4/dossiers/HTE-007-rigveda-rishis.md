# HTE-007 — Rigvedic Poets / Rishis

- **Corpus:** Rigveda
- **Language:** Vedic Sanskrit
- **Date:** generally latter half of the second millennium BCE
- **Mode:** INS; divine address; ritual-poetic insight

Jamison and Brereton describe the Rigveda as the oldest attested Sanskrit text, over a thousand hymns composed by numerous poets belonging to named lineages. Dating is difficult because composition and preservation were oral for millennia.

Later Hindu doctrine calls Veda *shruti* (“heard”), but HTERP will not project that later canonical theory uniformly onto every hymn. The hymns include direct divine address, inspiration, ritual efficacy, hidden/cosmic knowledge, and reflection on how humans approach divine/cosmic realms.

This is not one prophet's diary. It broadens the taxonomy from “message from agent” toward **inspired sacred composition and ritual-poetic insight**. Vedic/Gathic parallels require strong dependency controls because of shared Indo-Iranian ancestry.

**Baseline:** Jamison & Brereton, *The Rigveda* (OUP, 2020), Introduction; Dating and Authorship.
