# HTE-036 — Lakota Vision-Seeking Traditions

## Scope and cultural protocol
This dossier uses publicly documented Lakota material and does not generalize it to all Native North American nations.

## Hanblečeya / vision seeking
Public Lakota accounts describe disciplined solitary prayer/vision-seeking, often translated “crying for a vision.” Historical documentation is largely nineteenth- and twentieth-century and therefore must be analyzed against colonial disruption, translation, ethnographic mediation, and Indigenous-authored/collaborative sources.

## Method
Reported practices may involve:
- preparation under an authorized elder/holy person;
- isolation;
- fasting;
- prayer;
- exposure to landscape;
- waiting for dream/vision/voice;
- subsequent interpretation by knowledgeable community members.

## Phenomenology
Possible experiences include:
- animals/persons as visionary figures;
- voices;
- symbolic scenes;
- intense dreams;
- perceived commission or life role.

## Control relevance
Isolation, fasting, sleep disruption, expectation, and sensory deprivation can alter perception. That provides an H1/H2 mechanism without deciding the metaphysical interpretation.

## Historical prediction subcase
Public Smithsonian material recounts Sitting Bull's June 1876 Sun Dance vision of soldiers falling into camp before the Little Bighorn battle. HTERP-PRED should treat this as a historical prediction claim requiring:
- pre-battle source dating;
- exact wording;
- independence of surviving account;
- post-event editing analysis.

It is a promising historical case, not yet a calibrated prediction.

## Baseline sources
- Lakota-authored/collaborative materials collected in *Lakota Belief and Ritual*.
- National Museum of the American Indian/Smithsonian public historical material on Sitting Bull's 1876 vision.
