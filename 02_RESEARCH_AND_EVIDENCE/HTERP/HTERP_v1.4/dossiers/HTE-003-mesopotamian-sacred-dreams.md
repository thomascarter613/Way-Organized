# HTE-003 — Mesopotamian Sacred Dreams

**Mode:** DRM, sometimes MSG/VIS.  
**Setting:** Mesopotamian cultures across multiple periods.

Dreams occupied an unstable but important place in ancient divination: they could be dismissed, suspected, or treated as reliable divine communication depending on context. Ancient dream interpretation therefore cannot be reduced to one belief that “every dream is revelation.”

Mesopotamian culture also developed dream omen traditions and specialists who interpreted signs. Dreams belong to the same wider epistemic ecology as extispicy, astrology, spontaneous prophecy, and other omen systems.

## HTERP significance
Dreams are a crucial bridge category because they naturally generate vivid agents, dialogue, travel, prediction, symbolic imagery, and noetic certainty. A dream report must therefore be analyzed separately from its divine interpretation.

## Baseline
Jason P. Davies, “Whose Dream Is It Anyway? Navigating the Significance of Dreams in the Ancient World,” in *Ancient Divination and Experience* (OUP, 2019).
