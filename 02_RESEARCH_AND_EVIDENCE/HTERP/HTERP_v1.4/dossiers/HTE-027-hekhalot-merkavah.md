# HTE-027 — Hekhalot / Merkavah Traditions

## Scope
Late-antique Jewish texts concerning heavenly palaces, angelic gatekeepers, divine-throne vision, and ascent/descent traditions.

## Historical caution
Recent scholarship warns against imposing a universal category of "mysticism" on ancient Jewish sources. Hekhalot material is a contextualized late-antique corpus that develops earlier Ezekiel/Daniel throne imagery.

## Core motifs
- seven heavenly palaces/heikhalot;
- throne-chariot/Merkavah;
- angelic gatekeepers;
- divine names/seals;
- ritual preparation;
- ascent/descent language;
- danger and qualification;
- transformation/exaltation in some texts.

## Source profile
Major texts include Hekhalot Rabbati, Hekhalot Zutarti, 3 Enoch, and Ma'aseh Merkavah. Their dating, compositional layers, and relationship to actual practitioner experience are debated.

## HTERP distinction
Three layers must remain separate:
1. **literary heavenly-ascent narrative**;
2. **ritual/practical instruction**;
3. **actual altered-state practice by historical practitioners**.

A text that instructs ascent is not automatically proof that a practitioner experienced literal ascent.

## Dependency
Directly develops biblical throne traditions, especially Ezekiel 1/10 and Daniel 7. It therefore demonstrates **reperformance and elaboration of an inherited visionary template**.

## Esoteric structure
Restrictions may reflect competence, ritual danger, lineage authority, or literary elite formation. HTERP does not translate restriction automatically as deception or "secret doctrine."

## Baseline source
- Ra'anan Boustan, “Early Jewish Mysticism,” *Cambridge Handbook of Western Mysticism and Esotericism*.
- Hekhalot/Merkavah corpus descriptions used as orientation only; individual text dating requires critical editions.
