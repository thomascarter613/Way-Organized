# HTERP-WP-004 — Mystical and Intermediary Traditions

**Status:** completed baseline comparative pass  
**Version:** 0.5  
**Scope:** HTE-025, HTE-026, HTE-027, HTE-029, HTE-032, HTE-035, HTE-036, HTE-037, plus new comparative cases HTE-049 and HTE-050.

## Objective

Extend HTERP beyond prophecy/divination into traditions in which purported contact is cultivated through prayer, contemplation, asceticism, initiation, heavenly ascent, possession, trance, meditation, or visionary discipline.

WP-004 asks whether these practices reveal a common phenomenology or whether "mysticism" is itself another category that combines importantly different phenomena.

## Major results

### W4-01 — Mysticism is not one event class
The material separates into at least:
- visionary revelation,
- heavenly ascent,
- unitive/contemplative prayer,
- possession/identity displacement,
- shamanic spirit mediation,
- vision-seeking through isolation/fasting,
- yogic concentration/cessation,
- Buddhist cultivation/absorption/insight.

Treating all as "mystical experience" obscures causal and doctrinal differences.

### W4-02 — Voluntary cultivation is a major axis
Some experiences are unsolicited (vision, voice, apparition), while others are deliberately cultivated through fasting, prayer, breath, concentration, recitation, music, isolation, or ritual. This affects causal inference because trained practices can systematically alter attention, expectation, interoception, and self-modeling.

### W4-03 — Initiation/restriction is not the same as secrecy
Hekhalot, some Indigenous ceremonial knowledge, esoteric contemplative lineages, and specialist possession/shamanic traditions often restrict access because competence, lineage, ritual status, or community authority is required. HTERP distinguishes:
- secrecy for power;
- restriction for safety;
- restriction for lineage/community sovereignty;
- prerequisite-based advanced training.

### W4-04 — Agentive and non-agentive experiences coexist
Some cases involve explicit agents (angel, spirit, Christ, deity, ancestor). Classical Yoga and Buddhist meditation can culminate in states framed without a communicating external agent. A one-source theory must explain this divergence rather than erase it.

### W4-05 — "Union" language is tradition-sensitive
Teresa's union remains relational and theistic; it is not straightforward identity with God. Buddhist no-self/cessation and Yoga's discriminative/liberative states should not be translated into the same metaphysical category merely because all involve altered self-experience.

### W4-06 — Possession requires a separate identity/agency model
Cross-cultural scholarship distinguishes possession in which another agent is believed to take executive control from possession understood as illness/misfortune. These are not identical to visions or contemplative union.

### W4-07 — Shamanism is a historically loaded comparative category
The narrow term derives from Siberian/Tungus contexts. Its twentieth-century globalization can create false similarities by retroactively labeling many unrelated Indigenous specialists "shamans."

### W4-08 — The common-core hypothesis is weakened at the doctrinal level but remains testable phenomenologically
Some recurrent features—presence, luminosity, self-boundary change, altered time, certainty, auditory/visual imagery—appear across traditions and controls. Yet their combinations, cultivation methods, agent models, and interpretations vary enough that a single timeless mystical essence is not established.
