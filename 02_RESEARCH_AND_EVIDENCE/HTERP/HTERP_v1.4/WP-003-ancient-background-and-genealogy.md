# HTERP-WP-003 — Ancient Background and Genealogy of Concepts

**Status:** completed baseline pass  
**Version:** 0.4 working tree

## Objective
Trace the historical background beneath later Abrahamic prophetic religion and compare it with parallel ancient Indian and Chinese developments.

## Cases
- HTE-003 Mesopotamian sacred dreams

## Main results

### W3-01 — Divination predates biblical prophecy as a mature information system
Mesopotamian omen sciences and dream interpretation were highly developed before classical Israelite prophecy. Prophecy therefore emerges inside a broader ancient Near Eastern ecology of divine-human information exchange.

### W3-02 — Abraham is historically a memory-complex, not an accessible biography
Current evidence does not establish the original Abraham as a recoverable individual. Some features of patriarchal tradition may preserve genuinely old memories, including pre-Israelite El worship and an Amorite/ancestral background.

### W3-03 — El and Yahweh have distinguishable historical profiles
El is deeply rooted in West Semitic religion and ancestral/family traditions. Yahweh has an extra-biblical trail and biblical archaic-poetry associations pointing southward toward wilderness regions. Their eventual identification is historically more defensible than assuming every early use began as one unchanged name-set.

### W3-04 — El Elyon cannot simply be treated as a transparent synonym from the beginning
Genesis 14's El Elyon tradition is textually and historically contested. Some scholarship treats YHWH in Gen 14:22 as secondary and sees an older El Elyon layer. HTERP records this as a live scholarly model, not a settled fact.

### W3-05 — Moses functions as the canonical bridge to Yahwism, but historical reconstruction remains difficult
The literary Moses is the founder/mediator of Yahwistic religion. Historical evidence does not allow a confident biography. The Midian/Sinai/Seir/Paran nexus is therefore analyzed as a tradition cluster and possible geography of early Yahwism, not proof of one historical sequence.

### W3-06 — Revelation and divination overlap historically more than later religious categories imply
Ancient systems range from solicited signs, omen catalogs, dreams, and oracles to spontaneous messenger prophecy. The hard boundary later traditions draw between “revelation” and “divination” is partly theological classification.

### W3-07 — Indian and Chinese developments independently complicate a single Abrahamic model
Upanishadic knowledge is framed around insight into atman/brahman and liberation; Jain teaching centers liberated knowers and ethical-ascetic knowledge; early Chinese religion moves from ancestor-oracle governance toward a moralized Heaven framework; organized Daoism later develops explicit revelation lineages.
