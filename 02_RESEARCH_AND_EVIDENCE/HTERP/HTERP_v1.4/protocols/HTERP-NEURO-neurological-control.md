# HTERP-NEURO — Neurological Control Protocol

## Purpose
Determine which features of claimed transcendent encounters are known to occur through identifiable neurocognitive mechanisms.

## Control families
- focal epilepsy/ecstatic seizures
- auditory verbal hallucinations
- sleep paralysis/hypnagogia/hypnopompia
- migraine aura
- dissociation
- psychotic-spectrum states
- bereavement hallucinations
- meditation-induced altered states
- psychedelic states
- sensory deprivation
- extreme fasting/sleep deprivation where ethically studied

## Feature vector
For every control case:
- presence sensation
- voice
- visual figure/light
- agency attribution
- self-identification of entity
- command content
- noetic certainty
- unity
- ego dissolution
- time distortion
- bliss/terror
- motor/autonomic effects
- memory continuity
- recurrence
- cultural interpretation
- response to treatment/manipulation

## Comparison rule
A historical religious case receives no evidentiary credit for a feature merely because it is dramatic if that feature is common in established controls.

Potentially discriminating observations include:
- veridical information with no ordinary access;
- independently observed external physical effect;
- repeatable intersubjective content beyond expectation;
- prospective prediction beyond base rates.

## Historical-diagnosis prohibition
Do not diagnose named historical figures from narrative resemblance alone.
Permitted language:
> “Feature X has a known analogue in condition Y.”

Prohibited language without clinical evidence:
> “Person X had condition Y.”

## Interpretation
Neural mediation is expected under both naturalistic and transcendent hypotheses. The discriminating issue is whether the model predicts **only phenomenology** or also independently verifiable external information/effects.
