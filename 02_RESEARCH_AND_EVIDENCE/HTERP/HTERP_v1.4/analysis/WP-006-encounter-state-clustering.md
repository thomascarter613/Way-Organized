# WP-006 — Encounter-State Clustering

These clusters are analytical families, not metaphysical categories.

## C1 — Procedural divination
Examples:
Shang, Ifá, Greek oracles, Mesopotamian divination, Maya divination.

Structure:
uncertainty → ritual/sign procedure → interpretation → decision.

## C2 — Agentive messenger prophecy
Examples:
Mari, Israelite prophecy, Qur'anic prophetic model, modern charismatic prophecy.

Structure:
apparent agent/source → message → human intermediary → audience.

## C3 — Visionary commission/apocalypse
Examples:
Ezekiel, Jesus baptism tradition, Paul, Revelation, Joseph Smith.

Structure:
vision/presence → identity/authority claim → commission/cosmology.

## C4 — Possession/channeling/mediumship
Examples:
possession traditions, Spiritualism, modern channeling, experimental mediumship.

Structure:
alternate apparent agent/persona → speech/information through human medium.

## C5 — Relational contemplative mysticism
Examples:
Teresa, Sufi practice.

Structure:
disciplined prayer/remembrance → presence/love/nearness/union-language.

## C6 — Non-agentive or weakly agentive contemplative insight
Examples:
Buddhist meditation, classical Yoga, some Upanishadic trajectories.

Structure:
training → altered attention/self-processing → insight/liberation claim.

## C7 — Inspired sacred composition
Examples:
Rigvedic poetry, Gathas, Guru Nanak's bani.

Structure:
poetic/religious insight → authoritative sacred speech/text.

## C8 — Sacred-land / ancestral-law systems
Examples:
publicly documented Aboriginal traditions.

Structure:
Country/place + ancestors + law + ritual + authorized transmission.

## C9 — Spontaneous natural controls
Examples:
dreams, sleep paralysis, bereavement presence.

## C10 — Induced physiological controls
Examples:
psychedelic mystical states, neurological/ecstatic events.

## Result

No single cluster can function as the template for all religious experience.

Future anomaly testing must compare each religious case to its **nearest causal control**, not to a generic category called “mysticism.”
