# FRC Phase II — Fulfillment Claim Validation

## Jesus as Messiah

Jewish restoration and messianic expectations clearly predate Jesus, but no single pre-Christian checklist corresponds exactly to the later Christian biography. Crucifixion created apparent disconfirmation pressure; resurrection/exaltation and Parousia created a two-stage fulfillment structure.

Result: genuine preexisting expectation + very early identification; specific identification is interpretive rather than uniquely compelled.

## Twelfth Imam as Mahdi

Generic Mahdi/Qa'im and occultation traditions predate 874. The complete mature sequence of twelve Imams, hidden son of the eleventh, deputies, and two occultations becomes clearer after the succession crisis.

Result: old expectation + crisis + specific identification + retrospective consolidation.

## Maitreya

The future Maitreya expectation genuinely predates modern claimants and has a distinctive role as next Buddha, but poor near-term identifying data and a traditionally remote-future setting.

## The Bab as Qa'im/Mahdi

The Bab arose within a Twelver/Shaykhi expectation environment.

Sequence:
1844 Gate/representative → increasing identification → 1848 Qa'im/Mahdi claim → new manifestation/new dispensation.

Literal fit to the inherited hidden ninth-century son biography is weak; fit requires typological/spiritual redefinition of return.

## Baha'u'llah as the Bab's promised figure

Prediction priority is strong because the Bab's writings predate Baha'u'llah's public claim. But personal specificity is low, multiple rival claimants existed, claimant independence is low, and chronology remains interpretively flexible.

## Counterfactual Uniqueness

Ask how many other people could satisfy the prophecy under the same interpretive freedom.

- CU-VH: one plausible candidate
- CU-H: very small class
- CU-M: multiple candidates
- CU-L: large class
- CU-VL: almost any sufficiently charismatic reformer could fit

## Four thresholds

1. Prior existence.
2. Identification power.
3. Independence from reinterpretation.
4. Counterfactual uniqueness.
