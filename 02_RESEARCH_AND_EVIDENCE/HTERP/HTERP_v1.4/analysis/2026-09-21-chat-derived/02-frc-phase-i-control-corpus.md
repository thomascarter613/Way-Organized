# FRC Phase I — Fulfillment / Restoration Claim Corpus

## Purpose

Study historical people who claimed, or were claimed, to fulfill inherited prophetic or eschatological roles.

prediction → identification → outcome → reinterpretation.

## Initial cohort

| ID | Claimant | Role | Mechanism | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FRC-001 | Jesus of Nazareth | Jewish Messiah | self-understanding disputed; followers explicitly identify him | execution, resurrection faith, expected return |
| FRC-002 | Bar Kokhba | Davidic/Messianic restorer | follower/rabbinic identification | temporary Jewish state; revolt crushed |
| FRC-003 | Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya | Mahdi/Qa'im | follower identification | death reinterpreted by some as occultation |

## Mechanisms

- Self-Identification
- Follower Identification
- Community/System Identification
- Successor-Fulfillment Identification
- Post-event Reinterpretation

## Major patterns

- Bar Kokhba: claimant need not personally claim the role.
- Ibn al-Hanafiyya: early Mahdi occultation pattern predates mature Twelver doctrine.
- Ibn Tumart: successors can complete a claimant's alleged fulfillment.
- The Bab: restoration claim can escalate into new-disposition revelation.
- Baha'i interpretation: multiple previously distinct expectations can be compressed into one universal fulfillment framework.
- Sudanese Mahdism: prophecy–mobilization feedback can generate apparent confirming events.
- Sabbatai Zevi: disconfirmation can collapse a claim or be converted into hidden fulfillment.

## FRC-VP-001 dimensions

P1 prediction priority; P2 specificity; P3 text stability; C1 claimant independence; C2 self-claim; F1 pre-event fit; F2 non-self-fulfillable fit; F3 exclusivity; D1 disconfirmation; D2 interpretive flexibility; G1 genealogical continuity; S1 source proximity; I1 independent witnesses; R1 revelation novelty; O1 outcome.

No composite probability of messiahship is assigned.
