# WP-004 — Common-Core Mysticism Stress Test

## Hypothesis
Different religious traditions may contain a common phenomenological core beneath divergent interpretations.

## Features with significant recurrence
- altered sense of self;
- altered time/space;
- intense salience/noetic certainty;
- ineffability;
- presence;
- luminosity/vision;
- auditory phenomena;
- bliss/love/terror;
- perceived transformation.

## Why this is insufficient
The same or overlapping features also occur in:
- psychedelics;
- sleep transitions;
- epilepsy/neurology;
- bereavement;
- absorption;
- sensory deprivation;
- ritual trance.

And religious traditions combine the features differently.

## Strong doctrinal divergences
- personal God vs no creator required;
- relational union vs identity/monism vs no-self;
- revelation from agent vs insight through disciplined cognition;
- one transcendent agent vs plural spirits;
- possession vs contemplation;
- salvation by divine relation vs liberation by insight/practice.

## Current judgment
**Phenomenological-family hypothesis: supported.**  
Humans possess recurring capacities for nonordinary experience.

**Single universal mystical experience: not established.**

**Single transcendent source behind all such experiences: open, not demonstrated.**

## Best next test
Create blinded feature vectors from first-person or earliest reports and cluster them without tradition labels. Then reveal traditions and compare:
1. phenomenological clustering;
2. practice/induction clustering;
3. theology clustering.

If phenomenology clusters independently of theology, a cross-cultural human-state model gains support.
If specific high-dimensional patterns recur only in historically independent traditions and resist natural controls, H3/H4 gain interest.
