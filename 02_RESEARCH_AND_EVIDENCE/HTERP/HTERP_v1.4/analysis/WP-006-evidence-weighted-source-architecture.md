# WP-006 — Evidence-Weighted Source Architecture

## Do not rank religions by one score

A tradition may be excellent for one question and weak for another.

## Source strengths by question type

### Contemporary material evidence for practice
Especially strong:
- Mari archives;
- Shang oracle bones;
- some Greek oracle inscriptions;
- Maya archaeology;
- modern experimental/clinical cases.

Best question:
> Did this practice/institution actually exist?

Weak question:
> What did the practitioner subjectively experience?

### First-person experience claims
Especially strong:
- Paul;
- Teresa of Ávila;
- Swedenborg;
- Joseph Smith's later firsthand accounts;
- Bahá'u'lláh's founder-era writings;
- modern experimental subjects.

Best question:
> Did this person report/interpret an experience this way?

Weak question:
> Was the apparent external source objectively present?

### Highly conservative oral/liturgical transmission
Important:
- Rigveda;
- some living Indigenous knowledge systems;
- specialist oral corpora.

Best question:
> Can a community preserve structured material without early manuscripts?

Caution:
Manuscript age alone does not measure age of composition.

### Founder/teacher reconstructed through later community texts
Examples:
- Buddha;
- Jesus;
- Abraham/Moses at much greater distance;
- some prophetic/personality traditions.

Best question:
> What teaching/profile is recoverable by source-critical comparison?

Caution:
Do not turn plausible reconstruction into verbatim transcript.

## Preservation rule

Every proposition entering constructive theology must carry:
- source type;
- source proximity;
- dependency status;
- preservation vector;
- confidence statement;
- earliest layer in which it is securely attested.
