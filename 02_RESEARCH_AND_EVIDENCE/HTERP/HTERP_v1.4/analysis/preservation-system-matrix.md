# Preservation-System Matrix

| Tradition | Initial medium | Earliest surviving evidence | Main preservation risk | Proper proximity concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shang | bone inscription | contemporary inscriptions | selection/archive bias | physical contemporaneity |
| Rigveda | oral poetry | much later manuscripts; ancient linguistic corpus | oral recomposition vs conservative memorization | formula/lineage antiquity |
| Hebrew prophets | oral + written oracle collections | later manuscript witnesses | rolling redaction, actualization, textual variants | unit-level stratification |
| Greek oracle | oral consultation + inscriptions | tablets/inscriptions at some sites | literary retrojection | sanctuary-specific evidence |
| Early Buddhism | oral formulaic discourses | manuscripts from c. 1st BCE onward | creative recomposition, school divergence | cross-version/formula comparison |
| Paul | letters | early copies of first-century letters | copying/compilation/interpolation debates | authorial self-report proximity |
| Sikh bani | composition + early writing | 16th-century manuscript tradition | compilation/recension development | founder/early-Guru manuscript relation |
| Ifá | oral interpretive corpus + ritual apparatus | material objects + living tradition | dating individual verses, interpretive flexibility | specialist-chain continuity |
| Aboriginal songline traditions | oral/performance/place | living knowledge + recordings | colonial disruption, language loss, restricted access | authorized knowledge-chain continuity |
| Maya | inscription/iconography/material ritual | archaeological/epigraphic evidence | incomplete corpus, elite bias, colonial loss | object/context-specific |

## New model: Preservation Vector
Replace one scalar “distance from founder” with: temporal interval; medium; redundancy; specialist control; variation tolerance; editorial evidence; external anchoring; continuity evidence; access restrictions; language continuity.
