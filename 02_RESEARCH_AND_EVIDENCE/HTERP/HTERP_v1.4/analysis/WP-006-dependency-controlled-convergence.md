# WP-006 — Dependency-Controlled Convergence

## Evidence classes

### DC-A — Robust independent recurrence
Recurs across historically separated settings and is also known from modern controls.

1. **Dreams as sacred/information-bearing events**
2. **Visions/visual presences**
3. **Voices or message-like cognition**
4. **Sensed agency/presence**
5. **Ritual specialists mediating uncertainty**
6. **Deliberate induction through fasting, isolation, prayer, repetition, concentration, music, or ritual**
7. **Intense noetic certainty**
8. **Long-term behavioral transformation after salient experiences**

These are robust as human phenomena.

### DC-B — Broad cross-cultural recurrence, but difficult to code as one phenomenon
1. sacred law/order embedded in cosmos or land;
2. altered self-boundaries;
3. ascent/journey imagery;
4. luminous or elevated beings;
5. special knowledge assigned to trained specialists;
6. initiation/prerequisite structures.

These require tradition-specific analysis before metaphysical comparison.

### DC-C — Recurring ethical attractors
Common but not universal:
- truth/integrity;
- generosity/compassion;
- justice/non-exploitation;
- restraint;
- humility;
- disciplined practice;
- accountability/transformation.

These may reflect:
- transcendent moral reality;
- convergent social evolution;
- shared human psychology;
- or combinations thereof.

### DC-D — Historically connected convergence
Not usable as independent evidence without stronger analysis:
- Abrahamic monotheism;
- Abraham/Moses/prophet genealogies;
- resurrection/judgment motifs within connected late-antique worlds;
- angelic hierarchies;
- messianic patterns;
- Indo-Iranian ritual/cosmic-order parallels.

## Core finding

The strongest independent convergence is **phenomenological and functional**, not doctrinal.

That finding favors a model in which:
- humans share capacities for nonordinary experience;
- cultures construct different ontologies around those capacities;
while leaving open whether some experiences also respond to an external reality.
