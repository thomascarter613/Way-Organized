# WP-006 — Candidate Ethical Convergence

This is **not** a revealed moral code. It is a comparative candidate set.

## Recurring ethical attractors

### Truth / integrity
Strong in Zarathustrian truth-vs-lie framing, Israelite prophetic critique, Jesus traditions, Qur'anic ethics, Sikh teaching, and many philosophical/ascetic systems.

### Justice / non-exploitation
Especially strong in Israelite prophecy and later Abrahamic ethical traditions; analogues occur elsewhere.

### Compassion / generosity
Repeatedly central in Buddhist, Christian, Islamic, Sikh, and other ethical systems.

### Restraint / mastery of destructive appetite
Extremely widespread in ascetic, contemplative, prophetic, and philosophical streams.

### Humility / ego-decentering
Appears in submission-to-God, contemplative, monastic, Sufi, Buddhist, and other forms, though metaphysical interpretations differ.

### Disciplined attention
Prayer, remembrance, meditation, self-examination, ritual discipline, and study recur as technologies of transformation.

### Accountability / transformation
Many traditions judge a path by what kind of person it forms, even where the theory of salvation/liberation differs.

## Naturalistic caution
Convergent ethics can arise because human societies face similar cooperation problems. Ethical convergence is therefore not proof of revelation.

## Constructive use
These attractors are suitable for **provisional practice** when:
- they have strong human-welfare reasons;
- they do not require disputed metaphysics;
- they remain revisable;
- they respect consent and pluralism.
