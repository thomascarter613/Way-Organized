# WP-006 — Contradiction Localization by Historical Layer

## Purpose
Determine whether incompatible religious claims arise mainly:
1. in the earliest recoverable layer,
2. during interpretation/transmission,
3. or only in later institutions.

## Early/founder-proximate contradictions

### Ontology
- Rigvedic plurality versus later exclusive monotheism.
- Zarathustrian Mazda-centered moral cosmology versus polytheistic systems.
- Early Buddhist liberation without a creator-revelation framework versus creator-centered prophecy.
- Ifá/Yoruba divine ontology versus exclusive Abrahamic worship.

### Selfhood
- Buddhist no-permanent-self analysis versus traditions preserving enduring personal identity.

### Epistemic mechanism
- prophetic communication from an agent versus awakening/insight through disciplined cognition.

### Worship
- plural divine cult versus exclusive worship of one God.

These differences cannot all be assigned to late institutional corruption.

## Contradictions intensified by later development

Examples:
- detailed Christological metaphysics;
- ecclesial/sacramental authority systems;
- mature rabbinic legal structures;
- classical Islamic legal/theological schools;
- mature Sufi metaphysics;
- elaborated Buddhist cosmologies/schools;
- later hagiographies of founders.

These may magnify earlier differences or introduce new ones.

## Interpretive consequences

### One-source model
Still possible only if the source is heavily filtered through:
- culture;
- cognition;
- language;
- historical need;
- incomplete understanding.

The stronger the early contradiction, the more work the filtering hypothesis must do.

### Multiple-intelligence model
Can accommodate contradictory agent communications, but raises:
- identity/authentication problems;
- deception/error problems;
- hierarchy/ultimate-source problems.

### Naturalistic/mixed model
Explains divergence through human cognitive/cultural diversity and known mechanisms, but does not by itself explain any future robust anomalous information.

## Result
A responsible constructive theology must **permit genuine uncertainty and plural models** rather than pretending all early streams transparently teach the same doctrine.
