# Dependency Analysis — Calibration Corpus

## Rule

Similarity counts as evidence for independent convergence only after plausible historical/cultural transmission is assessed.

Dependency codes:
- **I:** plausibly independent
- **C:** historically connected or influence plausible
- **D:** direct/strong inheritance
- **U:** unresolved

## Pairwise structure

### Mari ↔ Israelite/Jewish prophecy — C
Both belong to the wider ancient Near Eastern prophetic/divinatory world. Nissinen argues that enough commonalities exist among Near Eastern, biblical, and Greek evidence to speak of a common Eastern Mediterranean prophetic phenomenon. Similar messenger formulas, temple relations, kingship interactions, and divine speech therefore cannot be treated as independent supernatural confirmations.

### Zarathustra ↔ Vedic material — D/C
Old Iranian and Vedic traditions share Indo-Iranian ancestry. Lexical, ritual, poetic, and cosmic-order parallels require inherited-background controls.

### Zarathustra ↔ later Judaism/Christianity — U/C
Possible Iranian influence on Jewish eschatology, angelology, resurrection concepts, or dualistic patterns has long been debated. HTERP will not score such parallels as independent until specific chronology and transmission are demonstrated.

### Jesus ↔ Hebrew/Second Temple tradition — D
Jesus is a Jewish teacher operating inside Jewish scripture, prophetic tradition, eschatology, and ritual culture. His references to God, Spirit, kingdom, Torah, prophets, and repentance are not independent of that tradition.

### Muhammad/Qur'an ↔ Jewish/Christian/late antique traditions — C/D by motif
The Qur'an explicitly reuses and reinterprets figures such as Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Mary, and Jesus. Monotheism, prophecy, resurrection, judgment, and scripture are therefore historically connected categories. The question becomes what is distinctive in the Qur'anic recombination and proclamation, not whether those motifs arose independently.

### Experimental mystical experience ↔ religious traditions — I for pharmacological mechanism; C for interpretation
The drug effect is independently manipulable, but participants bring cultural expectations and spiritual vocabularies. The phenomenology can therefore be causally independent of a religious tradition while its interpretation is culturally dependent.

### Neurological states ↔ religious traditions — I for mechanism; C for interpretation
A seizure can occur independently of theology; what it is called may depend heavily on theology.

### Fraud cases ↔ target religion — D by design
Fraud often deliberately imitates culturally expected signs. Similarity is therefore evidence of mimicry, not independent convergence.

## Consequence

The calibration corpus currently yields strong evidence for **cross-cultural recurrence of encounter structures**, but much weaker evidence for independent recurrence of specific theology.

The global search should therefore prioritize:
1. geographically/culturally distant cases;
2. specific phenomenological structures;
3. source-proximate documentation;
4. cases with minimal contact;
5. content that could not easily arise from shared human concerns or known cultural transmission.
