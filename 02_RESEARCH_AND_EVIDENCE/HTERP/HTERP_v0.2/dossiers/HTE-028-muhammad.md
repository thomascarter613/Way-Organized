# HTE-028 — Muhammad and Qur'anic Revelation

## Scope

This dossier separates two source corpora that are often improperly collapsed:

1. **The Qur'anic proclamation** — early text close to the founding community.
2. **Biographical encounter narratives** — detailed accounts of Muhammad's first and subsequent revelatory experiences transmitted through early Islamic historical tradition.

## Qur'anic source layer

The Qur'an presents itself as revealed communication. Relevant passages include:
- Q 96:1ff — an imperative to recite/read in the name of the Lord;
- Q 2:97 — Gabriel is said to have brought the revelation down upon Muhammad's heart by God's permission;
- Q 42:51 — a general theory of divine-human communication: revelation/inspiration, from behind a veil, or by a sent messenger;
- Q 53:1–18 — a difficult visionary passage describing reception/vision and “great signs”;
- Q 81:19ff — reference to a noble messenger in the revelatory process.

Interpretation of Q 53 in particular is disputed; scholars differ over whether the visionary object is Gabriel, God, or another referent. HTERP records the ambiguity rather than harmonizing it.

## Material history

Very early Qur'anic manuscripts place substantial Qur'anic text close to the first Islamic century. The Birmingham parchment has a radiocarbon range of 568–645 CE at 95.4% probability; parchment age does not by itself date the ink, but the manuscript is paleographically early. Corpus Coranicum catalogs a much wider body of early manuscript evidence.

Recent scholarship by Görke and Schoeler argues that the historical value assigned to early Islamic traditions has increased as early manuscript and transmission research has advanced.

## First-revelation narrative

Görke and Schoeler reconstruct multiple recensions of an early report attributed to ʿUrwa b. al-Zubayr. The best-attested al-Zuhri recension begins the revelatory period with true dreams and proceeds to the first revelatory encounter. Versions differ in wording, including Muhammad's response to the command to recite/read.

This is important:
- the **existence of a relatively early narrative complex** can be studied historically;
- the exact wording and phenomenology cannot simply be read from a single later hadith version and called contemporary transcript.

## Source assessment
- **Qur'anic proclamation:** very early, founder-era/first-community source; not a biography
- **detailed first-revelation narrative:** early tradition reconstructed through later witnesses; source grade roughly P3 with some elements potentially earlier
- **later hadith elaboration:** graded individually
- **later theology of prophethood:** downstream interpretive layer

## Phenomenology
Across the Qur'an and early biographical tradition, the following modes appear:
- communication “to the heart”;
- messenger/angel mediation;
- visionary experience;
- true dreams in the biographical tradition;
- imperative recitation;
- recurrent rather than one-time revelation.

The corpus does not justify reducing all revelation to a single sensory modality.

## Message profile
Early Qur'anic proclamation emphasizes:
- one sovereign creator God;
- judgment and resurrection;
- moral accountability;
- charity and care for the vulnerable;
- rejection of idolatry;
- continuity with earlier messengers;
- Muhammad's role as messenger/warnerm.

## Cultural/dependency position
The Qur'an emerges in a late antique environment containing Jewish, Christian, Arabian, and wider Near Eastern religious traditions. Its Adamic, Abrahamic, Mosaic, and Jesus traditions are therefore **not independent data points** relative to Judaism and Christianity.

Its specific recombination, Arabic proclamation, polemics, and self-understanding remain historically important; similarity alone cannot establish either borrowing or common revelation without further analysis.

## Hypothesis analysis

### H0
Literary composition is obviously involved in the production of text, but the early dating of the Qur'anic corpus weighs against theories requiring a very late invention of the proclamation. Fraud remains logically possible but requires positive evidence rather than assertion.

### H1
Auditory/visionary/revelatory experiences have plausible neurocognitive analogues. No legitimate retrospective diagnosis can be made from the evidence.

### H2
Strong cultural shaping is certain, especially through late antique monotheistic discourse and Arabic religious vocabulary.

### H3
Some “revelation to the heart” language could be modeled nonpersonally, but the Qur'an itself consistently attributes agency to God and messengers.

### H4
Directly compatible with the Qur'an's own interpretation: a personal transcendent God communicates, sometimes through a messenger.

### H5
Working stance.

## Development map

reported revelatory experiences  
→ oral proclamation/recitation  
→ early written witnesses and codices  
→ Qur'anic canonical text  
+ parallel biographical memory  
→ ʿUrwa/early sira transmission  
→ Ibn Ishaq / hadith corpora / tafsir  
→ systematic theology and law.

These branches must not be collapsed.
