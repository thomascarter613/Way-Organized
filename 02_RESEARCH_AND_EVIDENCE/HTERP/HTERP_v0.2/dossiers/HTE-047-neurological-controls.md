# HTE-047 — Neurological Comparison Cases

## Scope

HTERP uses neurological cases as **mechanistic controls**, not as retrospective diagnoses of prophets.

## Relevant phenomena

Clinical literature on focal epilepsy, especially temporal/insular networks, documents rare experiences that may include:
- intense bliss or peace;
- extraordinary mental clarity/certainty;
- sensed divine presence;
- voices;
- luminous or sacred imagery;
- conversion-like interpretation;
- altered self/world boundary.

Recent work on ecstatic epilepsy has increasingly implicated the dorsal anterior insula in the generation of certainty/bliss features, rather than treating “temporal lobe” as a complete explanation.

Auditory verbal hallucinations can also occur in healthy and clinical populations and may be interpreted religiously.

## What this establishes

The human nervous system is demonstrably capable of producing:
- agent-like experiences,
- revelatory certainty,
- mystical affect,
- voices,
- sensed presence,
- radical meaning attribution.

Therefore those features are not unique signatures of external noncorporeal agency.

## What it does not establish

It does not establish:
- that every prophet had epilepsy;
- that a neurological event is necessarily “unreal” in metaphysical terms;
- that similar verbal descriptions imply identical mechanisms.

## Anti-retrospective-diagnosis rule

HTERP prohibits assigning epilepsy, psychosis, dissociation, migraine, or another diagnosis to historical figures without contemporaneous clinical evidence sufficient for diagnosis.

Statements such as “Paul/Jesus/Muhammad/Teresa had temporal-lobe epilepsy” are hypotheses at best and usually underdetermined.

## Comparison dimensions
For each neurological case:
- event onset/offset;
- EEG/imaging localization if available;
- awareness;
- auditory/visual phenomena;
- sensed presence;
- noetic certainty;
- affect;
- memory;
- recurrence;
- medication response;
- cultural interpretation.

## Hypothesis effect
H1 is strongly established as a cause of **some** encounter-like experiences. This raises the evidentiary threshold for H3/H4: externally transcendent hypotheses require observations that are not already expected under known neurocognitive mechanisms.

## Baseline sources
- Picard and colleagues, work on ecstatic epilepsy and the anterior insula.
- Review literature on hyperreligiosity and focal epilepsy.
- Review literature on auditory verbal hallucinations in temporal-lobe disturbance and nonclinical populations.
