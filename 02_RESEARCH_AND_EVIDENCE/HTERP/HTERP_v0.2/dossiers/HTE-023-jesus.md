# HTE-023 — Jesus of Nazareth: Encounter, Commission, and Proclamation

## Scope

This dossier does **not** attempt to decide later Christian claims about incarnation, Trinity, resurrection metaphysics, or ecclesial authority. It asks a narrower HTERP question:

> What can the earliest recoverable sources tell us about Jesus' own reported relationship to God and any initiating/commissioning experience?

## Source structure

Jesus left no known writings. The principal narrative sources are the canonical Gospels, written within early Christian communities after his death. Paul is earlier than the written Gospels but gives relatively little biographical information about Jesus' pre-crucifixion life.

The Gospel tradition therefore provides **early but not first-person** evidence.

## Baptism by John

The baptism is one of the strongest historically accepted events in Jesus' early public life. Joel Marcus argues that Jesus' baptism by John is a “bedrock historical datum,” in part because later Christian traditions had reasons to soften the implication that Jesus underwent John's repentance baptism.

Mark 1:9–11 presents the event as a personal visionary/auditory experience:
- Jesus emerges from the water;
- he sees the heavens opened;
- the Spirit descends;
- a heavenly voice addresses him.

The wording in Mark depicts the vision as experienced by Jesus; later Gospel versions reshape the scene in different ways.

### Historical distinction
**Historically strong:** Jesus was baptized by John.  
**Historically possible but not independently verifiable:** Jesus underwent a visionary/commissioning experience at that baptism.  
**Theological interpretation:** the voice's identity and ontological meaning.

## Immediate proclamation

Mark links baptism/wilderness experience to Jesus' public activity: proclamation that God's kingdom has drawn near, with a call to repentance. Historical-Jesus scholarship widely treats the **kingdom/reign of God** as central to Jesus' proclamation.

This gives the calibration case a plausible sequence:

baptism by prophetic reformer  
→ reported vision/voice/Spirit experience  
→ wilderness/testing tradition  
→ public proclamation of God's reign  
→ ethical/communal teaching and healing/exorcistic activity.

It is not possible to prove from the source that the vision caused the ministry, but early Christian narrative explicitly connects them.

## Source grade
- **Jesus was baptized by John:** high-confidence historical event; source tradition P3
- **Exact baptismal phenomenology:** P3 narrative, not first-person
- **Kingdom-centered proclamation:** strong early tradition
- **Later metaphysical Christology:** separate downstream layer, not used as evidence for the initiating encounter

## Phenomenology
Reported:
- visual opening of heaven;
- Spirit descending in symbolic form;
- auditory communication;
- perceived personal divine approval/commission;
- subsequent testing/wilderness motif.

Unknown:
- whether the auditory component was externally audible;
- whether Jesus reported it directly to disciples;
- exact wording of any original report;
- physiological/altered-state features.

## Cultural context
The experience is embedded in Second Temple Jewish categories:
- Israel's God;
- Spirit;
- prophetic commission;
- scripture;
- repentance;
- eschatological kingdom.

Therefore the content is **not culturally independent** of Israelite/Jewish prophetic tradition.

## Hypothesis analysis

### H0
Later literary shaping is certain at some level; total fabrication of the baptism itself is historically less persuasive. The vision scene may preserve experience, literary theologization, or both.

### H1
Vision/voice experiences are neurocognitively possible. No clinical evidence exists for Jesus; retrospective diagnosis is prohibited.

### H2
Very strong as interpretive shaping. The imagery is saturated with Jewish scriptural categories.

### H3
Less directly aligned because the reported source is personal and agentive.

### H4
Compatible with Jesus' reported interpretation: a personal God communicates approval/commission. Historical sources cannot independently verify external agency.

### H5
Working stance.

## Development map

historical baptism / possible experience  
→ Jesus' proclamation  
→ disciples' memory  
→ oral tradition  
→ Markan narrative  
→ Matthew/Luke/John reinterpretation  
→ post-resurrection Christology  
→ patristic/creedal theology.

Each transition must be independently studied.
