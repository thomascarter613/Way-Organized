# The Assembly of the Way
## Founding Gathering Circle Pilot Operations Manual v1.4

**Date:** September 21, 2026  
**Status:** Pre-incorporation pilot operations manual  
**Scope:** Adult-only founding pilot; not a recognized House of Prayer, legal entity, or tax-exempt organization.

### 1. Purpose
This manual converts the 90-day pilot framework into an executable field curriculum. Its purpose is to test whether the Way can be practiced in ordinary community life while preserving conscience, safe disagreement, truthful teaching, outside relationships, independent complaint access, and founder-replaceability.

The pilot does not optimize for conversion, retention, donations, rapid growth, or founder loyalty. A participant deciding not to continue can be a healthy result.

### 2. Non-negotiable launch gates
- Adult-only pilot by default. No children's ministry.
- Publicly disclose pre-incorporation / experimental status.
- Distribute participant rights before any belonging decision.
- Establish one normal concern contact and one genuinely independent concern route before recurring public sessions.
- Maintain a venue/emergency plan.
- Do not solicit organizational donations or route funds through a founder's personal account.
- Do not represent the Gathering Circle as a recognized House of Prayer, incorporated nonprofit, or IRS-recognized charity.

### 3. Facilitation constitution
Every facilitator shall: (1) identify whether a claim is source, historical reconstruction, interpretation, current teaching, or open question; (2) state when participation is optional; (3) permit a person to pass without explanation; (4) avoid public pressure for disclosure; (5) protect disagreement; (6) name conflicts of interest; and (7) correct material errors openly.

A facilitator shall not use fear, spiritual status, supernatural threat, social exclusion, exhaustion, forced confession, or urgency to obtain assent.

### 4. Standard 75-minute gathering order
1. **Arrival and welcome — 10 min.** Status reminder, logistics, voluntary participation, how to step out.
2. **Attention / opening prayer — 8 min.** Silence or simple prayer; participation may be observational.
3. **Witness / teaching frame — 15 min.** Source or topic presented with confidence status.
4. **Discussion / practice — 25 min.** Questions, cases, paired reflection, practice, or source comparison.
5. **Rights and dissent check — 5 min.** Explicit invitation to disagree, identify pressure, or request correction.
6. **Closing reflection — 5 min.** Prayer, gratitude, silence, or clear non-liturgical closing.
7. **Anonymous pulse / logistics — 7 min.** Brief feedback; no donation appeal.

### 5. Thirteen-session sequence

#### Week 1: Orientation I: What the Way Is
**Core content:** Status, rights, vocabulary, pilot purpose.  
**Practice:** Attention + questions.  
**Integrity test:** Can participants explain that this is voluntary and pre-incorporation?

#### Week 2: Orientation II: How We Know
**Core content:** Encounter, testimony, interpretation, teaching, doctrine; confidence labels.  
**Practice:** Source/claim sorting exercise.  
**Integrity test:** Can participants distinguish evidence from interpretation?

#### Week 3: Prayer I: Attention and Honest Address
**Core content:** Prayer grammar; silence; gratitude; lament; petition; listening.  
**Practice:** 10-minute prayer experiment.  
**Integrity test:** Can participants decline forms without awkwardness or pressure?

#### Week 4: Study I: Reading Witnesses Responsibly
**Core content:** Primary sources, context, translation, competing interpretations.  
**Practice:** Compare two source accounts.  
**Integrity test:** Did facilitator distinguish source from conclusion?

#### Week 5: Fellowship: The Common Table
**Core content:** Hospitality, companionship, equality, voluntary belonging.  
**Practice:** Shared meal / fellowship.  
**Integrity test:** Were status differences or insider privilege visible?

#### Week 6: Service: Good Without Conversion
**Core content:** Service, charitable integrity, non-conditional aid.  
**Practice:** One practical service action.  
**Integrity test:** Was any aid tied to attendance, belief, or loyalty?

#### Week 7: Discernment I: Disagreement Without Defeat
**Core content:** How to frame a question, disclose conflicts, hear minority views.  
**Practice:** Structured discernment case.  
**Integrity test:** Was dissent preserved rather than overcome?

#### Week 8: Ethics, Power, and Boundaries
**Core content:** Consent, spiritual authority limits, money, family, outside advice.  
**Practice:** Boundary case studies.  
**Integrity test:** Could participants identify inappropriate spiritual control?

#### Week 9: Open Theology: What We Do Not Yet Know
**Core content:** God, Jesus, Muhammad, Buddha, afterlife, scripture, marriage, ordination.  
**Practice:** Open-question map.  
**Integrity test:** Did anyone feel pressure to adopt a provisional answer?

#### Week 10: Companion Pathway
**Core content:** Companion identity, commitment, Rule of Life, free exit.  
**Practice:** Optional personal Rule of Life draft.  
**Integrity test:** Was there a clear no-decision / no-joining option?

#### Week 11: Founder-Exit Exercise
**Core content:** Non-founder facilitation; written systems; authority handoff test.  
**Practice:** Founder absent from facilitation.  
**Integrity test:** Could the gathering function without founder interpretation?

#### Week 12: Repair, Complaints, and Leaving Well
**Core content:** Complaint routes, correction, retaliation, departure, re-entry.  
**Practice:** Complaint/exit tabletop.  
**Integrity test:** Can participants identify an independent concern route?

#### Week 13: Day-90 Review and Closure Decision
**Core content:** Review evidence; publish lessons; continue/revise/pause/close.  
**Practice:** Formal review circle.  
**Integrity test:** Did evidence rather than loyalty drive the decision?

### 6. Session preparation checklist
Before each gathering, the facilitator records the session purpose, source materials, claim-status labels, any known conflicts, accessibility needs, emergency logistics, independent complaint contact, and the single most important pressure/coercion risk for that session.

### 7. Standard participant pulse
After each session, ask anonymously where feasible:
- I understood that attendance and participation were voluntary.
- I felt free to disagree or pass.
- I could distinguish what was evidence/source from what was interpretation or open theology.
- I know how to raise a concern outside the facilitator/founder.
- I experienced pressure concerning belief, joining, money, disclosure, relationships, or loyalty.
- One thing that was meaningful.
- One thing that should change.

### 8. 30-day review
Review weeks 1-4 for rights comprehension, epistemic clarity, facilitator behavior, accessibility, complaints, correction log, attendance patterns, and any emerging founder dependency. Publish a short change note to participants.

### 9. 60-day review
Review weeks 5-8 for service integrity, social dynamics, dissent quality, money/cost handling, boundaries, and ability of another facilitator to lead parts of the gathering. Decide whether to continue Companion orientation in weeks 9-10.

### 10. Founder-exit exercise — Week 11
The founder prepares materials but does not facilitate, interpret disputed questions, resolve logistics, or act as fallback authority during the gathering except for a genuine emergency. A non-founder facilitator runs the session from the written packet. Afterward, participants anonymously record where the system depended on founder presence. Any failure becomes a design issue, not a loyalty issue.

### 11. Complaint / exit tabletop — Week 12
Run at least three scenarios: (a) participant says facilitator pressured them to agree; (b) participant reports serious misconduct by founder; (c) Companion says they are leaving and publicly criticizing the group. Participants must be able to identify the protected response, independent route, non-retaliation rule, and what records should be preserved.

### 12. Day-90 decision standard
The pilot may **continue**, **revise and repeat**, **pause**, or **close**. After incorporation it may later seek House-in-Formation status, but only if the evidence supports it. The decision must consider complaints, departure experiences, anonymous feedback, founder-exit results, correction history, operating burden, service integrity, and whether participants can explain their rights.

### 13. Stop conditions
Suspend the affected activity immediately for credible allegations of violence, stalking, sexual misconduct, child/vulnerable-person safeguarding issues, serious fraud, retaliation, coercive isolation, or use of spiritual authority to control housing, employment, healthcare, legal matters, finances, education, or intimate relationships. Safety and lawful reporting outrank continuity of the pilot.

### 14. Records created by the pilot
Keep only what is needed: session log, aggregate/anonymous feedback, correction log, incident/complaint metadata, decision log, cost/time log, facilitator roster, and Day-90 review. Avoid collecting trauma histories, private confessions, medical details, or other sensitive data merely for formation.

### 15. What happens after Day 90
If the pilot continues, produce a public lessons-learned summary and a private operational review. The next version of the Way's governing/practice documents should cite observed failures, corrections, and participant feedback rather than treating the founding corpus as self-validating.
