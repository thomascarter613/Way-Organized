# 1. The Whole: What the Way Is and Is Not
The Way is a living religious path concerned with humanity's relationship to ultimate reality, truth, and the manner in which human life ought to be lived in response to that reality. The Way is not identical with any corporation, board, building, leader, document, doctrine, or generation.
The deepest ordering principle is that reality precedes the institution. The institution exists to help people seek, understand, practice, transmit, and safeguard the Way. The Way does not exist to preserve the institution.
The governing distinction is therefore: truth is discovered and discerned; authority is entrusted and limited; participation is voluntary; conscience remains personal; and institutional power is always accountable.

## The ten-layer model
- 1. Ultimate Reality / God / the Transcendent - whatever is ultimately real exists independently of the movement and its descriptions.
- 2. Encounter and revelation - human beings report experiences interpreted as contact with or disclosure from transcendent reality.
- 3. Testimony and record - encounters become stories, sayings, texts, traditions, rituals, artifacts, memories, and historical records.
- 4. Discernment and knowledge - testimony is investigated through reason, scholarship, history, philosophy, science, lived experience, prayer, and communal discernment.
- 5. The Way - understanding becomes practice: prayer, ethics, contemplation, study, service, justice, charity, hospitality, fellowship, and other disciplines.
- 6. The Companion - an individual freely chooses to walk the Way with others without surrendering conscience.
- 7. The Assembly - Companions and welcome participants gather for prayer, study, service, fellowship, discernment, and mutual care.
- 8. The House of Prayer - the Assembly becomes local and stable in a particular place or network.
- 9. Ministry and governance - Teachers, Ministers, Stewards, Elders, councils, and the Board exercise defined, limited, reviewable functions.
- 10. Transmission - publications, education, ritual, archives, institutions, Houses of Prayer, and successive generations carry the Way forward.

## The cycle of the Way
- Reality -> Encounter -> Testimony -> Discernment -> Understanding -> The Way -> Practice -> Companion -> Assembly -> House of Prayer -> Service and Transmission -> New Encounter / New Generation -> renewed discernment.
- This is a cycle rather than a closed chain. New evidence can correct older interpretation; new generations can deepen or revise inherited teaching without treating correction as betrayal.

## What constitutes the beginning
- The ultimate beginning is not the founding corporation. The movement begins from the claim that reality and truth precede us.
- The historical beginning of this movement is the decision to examine humanity's inherited records of religious encounter, seek what is true in them, live according to what can responsibly be discerned, and remain open to correction and further truth.
- The personal beginning is seeking: a person asks what is real, how one should live, and whether there is a Way worth walking with others.
- The communal beginning occurs when people freely gather; a stable local gathering may become a House of Prayer. The legal beginning is only a civil formation event for a nonprofit corporation.

## What constitutes the end
- The ultimate spiritual end remains an open theological question. The movement should not manufacture certainty about salvation, awakening, resurrection, liberation, judgment, union with God, or other final states before its theology supports such teaching.
- The institutional end state is clearer: the Way can reproduce itself faithfully without dependence on the founder. A person can discover the Way, participate safely, become a Companion voluntarily, mature, serve, teach, help form another House of Prayer, and pass the tradition forward without requiring direct access to the founder.
- A healthy end state also includes the protected exit path: a person can leave, criticize, retain ordinary relationships, property, work, family, dignity, and privacy, and experience no institutional retaliation.

# 2. Human Lifecycle of the Way
The lifecycle is not a rank ladder. It describes relationships and possible forms of participation. No person is required to progress beyond Companion, and no office makes a person spiritually superior.

## Lifecycle principles
- Belonging and office are separate. Becoming a Companion never creates an obligation to become a Minister, Teacher, Steward, Elder, donor, employee, or resident.
- Office is functional, not ontological. Commissioning gives defined authorization; it does not create a superior class of human being.
- Maturity is shown by honesty, compassion, judgment, service, and teachability - not proximity to the founder, wealth, charisma, mystical claims, or organizational loyalty.
- The path of development is learn -> practice -> discern -> mature -> serve -> teach or mentor when appropriate -> remain teachable.

| Stage | Meaning | Typical activities | Rights / safeguards | Possible next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seeker | A person exploring ultimate questions or the Way without belonging obligations. | Public gatherings, reading, conversations, service, prayer if desired. | No pressure to join; access to public information; freedom to question and consult outside sources. | Participant or continued independent seeking. |
| Participant / Guest | A person who regularly joins gatherings without identifying as a Companion. | Prayer, study, fellowship, service, open classes. | No lesser human dignity; no pressure to disclose private information or donate. | Companion, continued participation, or departure. |
| Companion | A person who freely undertakes to walk the Way with others. | Rule of Life, Assembly participation, service, study, mutual care. | Freedom of conscience, exit, criticism, outside relationships and advice, due process, non-retaliation. | Maturity, service, or ministry if separately called and qualified. |
| Maturing Companion | A Companion with demonstrated stability, service, and understanding. | Mentoring, deeper study, facilitating gatherings, service leadership. | No automatic office; no secret teachings required; reviewable responsibilities. | Teacher, Steward, Minister, Elder candidate, or simply continued companionship. |
| Commissioned Servant | A person entrusted with a defined ministry function. | Teaching, pastoral care, administration, ritual/service leadership, safeguarding or stewardship. | Written scope, supervision, review, complaint accountability, removal with due process. | Renewal, change of ministry, return to ordinary Companion role. |
| Elder / Mentor (if adopted) | A mature Companion recognized for wisdom and service, not supremacy. | Mentoring, counsel, memory, formation support. | No inherent executive, financial, or revelatory control. | Continued service, emeritus status, return to ordinary Companion role. |
| Former Companion | A person who no longer identifies as a Companion. | May attend public events, maintain friendships, or have no further relationship. | No shunning, retaliation, coercive exit process, loss of ordinary rights, or misuse of confidential information. | None required. Re-entry remains possible if freely desired. |

# 3. The Companion Pathway and Companion Commitment
Companion is a religious identity and relationship, not statutory corporate membership. The Companion pathway is designed to be meaningful without becoming coercive, secretive, or irreversible.

## Becoming a Companion
- A person should ordinarily have enough exposure to the Way to understand its core practices, rights, governance safeguards, and current teachings.
- No minimum donation, property transfer, confession, severing of outside relationships, housing arrangement, employment relationship, or loyalty oath may be required.
- A Companion Commitment may be made publicly, privately, or in writing. It is not a civil contract and should explicitly remain revocable.

## Working Companion Commitment
- I choose freely to walk the Way in companionship with others.
- I will seek truth with humility, practice prayer and study according to conscience, serve where I am able, and treat others with dignity.
- I will distinguish testimony from interpretation, authority from truth, and spiritual conviction from coercive command.
- I will not surrender my conscience to a leader, office, council, or institution, nor demand that another person surrender conscience to me.
- I will respect the freedom of Companions and non-Companions alike to question, disagree, seek outside counsel, participate, or depart.
- I will support safeguarding, financial integrity, honest inquiry, and accountability.
- I understand that I may cease identifying as a Companion at any time without permission, penalty, shunning, or loss of ordinary human relationships.

## Exit and return
- A clear statement that a person no longer identifies as a Companion is sufficient. No approval, debrief, payment, confession, doctrinal recantation, or spiritual release is required.
- A former Companion may later return through an ordinary relational process. Return is not conditioned on withdrawing legitimate criticism or denying past experience.

# 4. The Assembly: Religious Community Architecture
The Assembly is the religious community created when Companions and welcome participants gather around the Way. It is broader than the corporation and distinct from the Board of Directors.

## Functions of the Assembly
- Prayer and worship; study and inquiry; fellowship and mutual care; service and charitable action; discernment; teaching; formation; remembrance; rites or sacred practices as they develop; and transmission to future generations.

## Assembly and corporate governance
- The Assembly may deliberate religious questions, express communal consensus, and form councils with defined religious responsibilities.
- The nonprofit Board retains the civil fiduciary responsibilities assigned to it by law and governing documents. Spiritual language cannot transfer fiduciary authority implicitly.
- The Assembly cannot suspend participant rights, safeguarding, financial controls, due process, or founder limitations by acclamation, prophecy, or informal consensus.

## Possible future councils
- Council of the Assembly - a deliberative body for doctrine, practice, formation, and communal discernment once the community is large enough to justify it.
- Council of Houses - a representative forum linking multiple Houses of Prayer without creating unnecessary central hierarchy.
- Regional Assembly - a geographic coordination layer only when multiple Houses in a region require shared infrastructure.

# 5. House of Prayer Operating Model
A House of Prayer is a stable local expression of the Assembly. It is a community before it is a building. A House may gather in a home, rented room, public facility, dedicated property, or online/hybrid environment where appropriate.

## Recognition stages
- 1. Gathering Circle - a recurring group exploring the Way; no claim to be an official House.
- 2. House-in-Formation - meets stated minimum practices, completes safeguarding and governance training, and has an accountable local Steward.
- 3. Recognized House of Prayer - formally recognized through a written instrument defining scope, responsibilities, financial arrangements, complaint routes, and review date.
- 4. Mature House - demonstrates stable practice, local formation, healthy leadership rotation, transparent finances, service, and ability to reproduce without dependence on one charismatic person.
- 5. Dormant / Closed House - recognition may end without shame when the community no longer functions. Property and records follow written legal arrangements; people remain free.

## Local leadership
- House Steward - coordinates logistics, scheduling, records, budget interfaces, and facility matters.
- Teacher - teaches within a defined area of competence and is subject to review and correction.
- Minister - provides defined religious or pastoral functions when commissioned.
- Safeguarding Contact - maintains reporting information and independent escalation access; should not be the sole local executive if avoidable.
- No local role may independently create debt, employ staff, open accounts, sign property agreements, issue credentials, or represent itself as a separate legal entity without written corporate authority.

## House review
- Each recognized House should complete at least an annual health review covering participation, safeguarding, finances, leadership concentration, complaint accessibility, teaching integrity, service, departures, and whether people can disagree or leave safely.

| Minimum function | Required at recognition | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular gathering | Yes | Creates an actual community rather than a nominal chapter. |
| Prayer / worship | Yes | Maintains the devotional center of the House. |
| Study / inquiry | Yes | Keeps teaching connected to evidence, sources, and ongoing learning. |
| Service / mutual care | Yes | Turns the Way outward into practical care. |
| Safeguarding contact and escalation path | Yes | Ensures protection does not depend on the local leader. |
| Local Steward | Yes | Coordinates operations without becoming local sovereign. |
| Teacher / Minister | No at first | May be shared, visiting, remote, or developed later. |
| Owned building | No | House means community/function, not property ownership. |
| Separate bank account or legal entity | No | Requires explicit written authorization and legal structure. |

# 6. Gatherings and Religious Practice Framework
The first generation should establish recognizable patterns without freezing a complete liturgy before lived practice develops. Each gathering type therefore has a purpose, minimum elements, optional elements, and prohibited control techniques.

## Practice safeguards
- No gathering may use sleep deprivation, food deprivation, prolonged exhaustion, forced confession, humiliation, intimidation, deceptive recruitment, restricted exit, confiscation of property or devices, or social isolation to produce commitment.
- Prayer and testimony should not be used to announce binding commands over another adult's marriage, sexuality, healthcare, finances, residence, employment, education, or family relationships.
- Children and vulnerable persons participate under safeguarding rules appropriate to the activity.

## The Common Table
- The Common Table remains a candidate practice rather than a fixed sacrament. It may become a shared meal expressing hospitality, equality, gratitude, and companionship. No one should be excluded from ordinary food because of belief, status, donation level, criticism, or departure.

| Gathering | Core purpose | Minimum elements | Optional elements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gathering for Prayer | Devotion, reverence, silence, petition, gratitude, lament, praise, remembrance. | Opening welcome; prayer/silence; reading or reflection; communal intercession; closing. | Music, chant, spontaneous prayer, structured prayers, candles, sacred art, periods of silence. |
| Gathering for Study | Serious learning and disciplined inquiry. | Primary source; historical/contextual framing; competing interpretations; discussion; uncertainty made explicit. | Language study, guest scholar, comparative texts, workshops, reading groups. |
| Gathering for Service | Practical care for people, communities, creatures, or environment. | Real service need; voluntary participation; no conversion requirement; safety plan when needed. | Food relief, mutual aid, environmental work, visitation, community projects. |
| Gathering for Fellowship / Common Table | Relationship, hospitality, equality, companionship. | Shared welcome and meal or fellowship; accessibility; no status seating. | Blessing, remembrance, testimony, celebration. |
| Gathering for Discernment | Communal testing of a question or proposed action. | Question stated; conflicts disclosed; evidence/arguments heard; silence/reflection; recorded outcome and uncertainty. | Consensus methods, structured dialogue, expert consultation. |

# 7. Rule of Life Framework
A Rule of Life is a voluntary pattern that helps a Companion embody the Way. It should guide rather than surveil. It is not an enforceable loyalty code and should allow adaptation to health, disability, family obligations, work, culture, and conscience.

| Domain | Working practice | Freedom / safeguard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prayer | Maintain a regular practice of prayer, silence, contemplation, or devotional attention. | Form and frequency may vary; pastoral guidance does not override conscience. |
| Study | Engage sacred sources, history, scholarship, and competing interpretations. | Outside books, traditions, critics, and scholarship are welcomed. |
| Ethics | Seek honesty, justice, mercy, non-exploitation, sexual integrity, and responsible use of power. | Ethics are taught transparently; discipline requires due process. |
| Service | Offer time, skill, resources, or care according to capacity. | No compulsory labor or giving; service never purchases spiritual rank. |
| Fellowship | Participate in mutual care and community life as reasonably able. | Outside friendships and family relationships are never prohibited. |
| Discernment | Test beliefs, motives, experiences, and major decisions. | Leaders may advise but cannot seize personal decision-making authority. |
| Rest / retreat | Make room for rest, reflection, and voluntary retreat. | No isolation or deprivation used to create dependence. |

# 8. Ministry Roles, Commissioning, and Accountability
Ministry is service under defined trust. Commissioning is the present working process for authorizing a person to perform specified religious functions. Ordination remains reserved until theology and practice justify a distinct enduring office.

## Commissioning sequence
- 1. Need and role definition.
- 2. Candidate self-nomination or nomination without pressure.
- 3. Conflict, safeguarding, and background review appropriate to the role.
- 4. Competence and formation assessment.
- 5. Opportunity for relevant community feedback.
- 6. Written scope, supervision, term, boundaries, and complaint route.
- 7. Public commissioning or written authorization.
- 8. Periodic renewal, review, and continuing formation.
- 9. Suspension or removal through defined due process when necessary.
- 10. Return to ordinary Companion status is normal and carries no stigma.

## Founder rule
- The founder may be commissioned for specific functions like any other person, but founder status by itself grants no ministry credential, doctrinal veto, succession right, compensation authority, or exemption from discipline.

| Role | Primary function | Required controls | Not conferred |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teacher | Teach defined subjects or practices. | Competence review, source integrity, feedback, periodic renewal. | Infallibility, financial authority, pastoral control. |
| Minister | Prayer, pastoral care, worship leadership, rites or other commissioned functions. | Training, ethics, safeguarding, supervision, scope statement, complaint route. | Automatic executive authority, immunity, sexual access, binding revelation. |
| Steward | Resources, operations, property, administration, records, or local coordination. | Segregation of duties, financial controls, documentation, review. | Spiritual rank or ownership of entrusted assets. |
| Elder (provisional) | Wisdom, mentoring, continuity, counsel. | Transparent criteria, term/review, conflict limits. | Automatic board seat, executive authority, doctrinal veto. |

# 9. Epistemic Integrity: Encounter to Doctrine
The Way requires a disciplined grammar for religious knowledge. The purpose is neither to dismiss spiritual experience nor to let intense experience become unreviewable institutional command.

## Confidence levels
- Established - strong and convergent reasons with no serious unresolved contradiction known to the Assembly.
- Well-supported - substantial evidence and reasoning, while meaningful questions remain.
- Provisional - useful current judgment held openly to revision.
- Permitted interpretation - compatible with the Way but not taught as a communal conclusion.
- Open question - the Assembly deliberately does not claim a conclusion.
- Rejected as harmful or unsupported - may describe claims judged incompatible with evidence, ethics, participant rights, or core commitments; reasons must be stated rather than enforced through stigma.

## Doctrine adoption record
- Every material doctrinal proposal should identify: the proposition; sources; evidence; historical context; competing interpretations; strongest arguments for and against; confidence level; practical consequences; rights impact; dissenting views; decision body; conflicts; vote/consensus record; effective date; and review date.
- No revelation claim may override safeguarding, mandatory reporting, ordinary informed consent, financial controls, governing documents, or another adult's healthcare/legal decisions.

## Dissent
- A Companion may disagree with a teaching without being treated as disloyal. Certain offices may require ability to represent particular teachings accurately, but office qualification must not be converted into control over private conscience or ordinary community relationships.

| Level | Definition | Typical evidence | Institutional effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Encounter | An experience or event interpreted as contact with transcendent reality. | First-person report, contemporaneous notes, witnesses, context. | None by itself. |
| Testimony | An account of an encounter, event, conviction, or practice. | Oral/written report, text, tradition, record. | Evidence for consideration. |
| Interpretation | Human explanation of what the testimony means. | Reasoning, theology, comparison, historical context. | Open to competing interpretations. |
| Teaching | A proposition or practice the Assembly currently teaches. | Reviewed sources, reasons, communal discernment. | Guides formation; confidence level stated. |
| Doctrine | A teaching formally adopted with defined status and implications. | Formal record, evidence dossier, objections, decision process. | Greater stability but remains reviewable. |

# 10. Transmission, Succession, and the Founder-Exit Test
A religion becomes transgenerational when knowledge, practice, community, and accountability can survive the departure of its founder and first generation. Succession is therefore a system property rather than the selection of a single heir.

## What must be transmitted
- The native lexicon; primary source archive; adopted teachings and confidence levels; practices; gathering patterns; safeguarding and integrity rules; governance boundaries; formation curriculum; historical record; financial stewardship norms; complaint and investigation mechanisms; translation guidance; and the documented history of doctrinal revision.

## Founder-exit test
- The founder should be able to die, resign, retire, become incapacitated, or disagree with the institution without causing constitutional collapse.
- No person should inherit founder status. Executive, teaching, spiritual, corporate, financial, and safeguarding responsibilities should pass through separate lawful processes.
- The founder's writings remain sources to be studied and criticized, not commands merely because of authorship.
- The institution should periodically run a tabletop scenario in which the founder is unavailable for twelve months. Any essential function that cannot continue becomes a remediation item.

## Closure as fidelity
- A House of Prayer, program, council, or even corporation may close when it no longer serves its purpose. Institutional survival is not the highest religious good. Dissolution should protect people, honor donor restrictions and law, preserve appropriate archives, and transfer charitable assets according to governing documents.

# 11. The Human Lifecycle in One View

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| How does it begin? | A person seeks; encounters the Way; participates without obligation; and may freely become a Companion. |
| What is belonging? | Walking the Way with others, not submission to a person or corporation. |
| What is maturity? | Growing in truthfulness, compassion, discernment, service, judgment, and teachability. |
| What is ministry? | Defined service entrusted through commissioning, training, supervision, and accountability. |
| What is community? | Companions and participants gathered as the Assembly, locally embodied in Houses of Prayer. |
| What is doctrine? | A formally adopted teaching supported by recorded reasons, evidence, dissent, and review. |
| What is succession? | Transmission of a system, not enthronement of an heir. |
| What is leaving? | A protected, ordinary exercise of conscience that requires no permission. |
| What is the institution for? | To serve, protect, coordinate, and transmit the Way. |
| What is the institution not for? | To replace conscience, own people, manufacture truth, or preserve itself at any cost. |

# 12. v0.6 Implementation Sequence
This architecture should be piloted before being constitutionalized in every detail. The first generation should learn from actual gatherings while preserving the non-negotiable anti-coercion safeguards.

## Stage A - Formation before public Companion enrollment
- Approve the Companion rights statement and Commitment text as provisional.
- Define public participant versus Companion terminology.
- Prepare a basic Gathering for Prayer and Gathering for Study pilot pattern.
- Train founding leaders in complaint, safeguarding, boundaries, and epistemic distinctions.

## Stage B - First recurring community
- Operate as a Gathering Circle before claiming House of Prayer status.
- Pilot prayer, study, fellowship, service, and discernment formats.
- Collect voluntary feedback, including anonymous feedback.
- Track whether people understand that participation and Companion identity are voluntary.

## Stage C - First House-in-Formation
- Appoint a House Steward and independent safeguarding contact.
- Adopt the House recognition instrument.
- Establish a simple budget/financial interface and records process.
- Commission only those roles actually needed.
- Run founder-complaint and leader-misconduct tabletop exercises.

## Stage D - Recognized House of Prayer
- Recognize only after sustained community life demonstrates the minimum functions.
- Publish local leadership, complaint routes, safeguarding information, gathering schedule, and the rights of participants.
- Complete annual health review and renewal.

## Stage E - Multi-House network
- Only after more than one mature House exists should the Assembly consider a Council of Houses, regional coordination, inter-House credential portability, or common property structures.

# v0.6 Decision Register

## Adopt now
- Human lifecycle is non-hierarchical.
- Companion identity is voluntary and revocable.
- Assembly is distinct from Board/corporation.
- House of Prayer is a community/function and not automatically a separate legal entity.
- Gathering categories: Prayer, Study, Service, Fellowship/Common Table, Discernment.
- Commissioning is the working authorization process for ministry.
- Encounter -> Testimony -> Interpretation -> Teaching -> Doctrine is the epistemic sequence.
- Succession is distributed system continuity, not appointment of a founder-heir.
- Founder-exit continuity testing is a recurring control.

## Provisional / pilot before constitutionalizing
- Exact Companion formation period.
- Exact wording and ceremonial form of the Companion Commitment.
- Common Table as a named recurring practice.
- Elder as a formal recognized role.
- Council of the Assembly / Council of Houses.
- House recognition thresholds such as frequency and minimum number of Companions.
- Exact Rule of Life frequencies.

## Reserved for later theological work
- Formal canon.
- Ordination as distinct from commissioning.
- Sacramental theology.
- Final doctrine of God / divine names.
- Soteriology / afterlife / ultimate human end.
- Comprehensive holy-day calendar.
- Binding universal liturgy.
