# EPC-003 — Canon and Authority Protocol

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Working Constitutional Baseline  
**Parents:** EPC-001 — Epistemological Constitution; EPC-002 — Revelation Discernment Protocol  
**Domain:** Canon, scripture, foundational witness, textual authority, admission, interpretation, revision, and removal

# Preamble

A canon is a community's formally received body of authoritative witness.

Canonical status shall not be confused with truth itself.

Reality precedes canon.

A text does not become true because a community canonizes it, and a true proposition does not become false because it stands outside the canon.

Canon exists to preserve, order, transmit, interpret, and publicly identify the materials that a community judges most important to its continuing search for and response to transcendent truth.

Canon shall serve truth; truth shall never be subordinated to canon.

The canon shall possess real authority without becoming immune to historical investigation, textual criticism, moral scrutiny, reason, or new evidence.

# Article I — Canon Is a Status of Reception

Canonical status means the community has formally received material as an enduring and weight-bearing witness within its religious life.

Canonical status does not by itself establish divine authorship, verbal dictation, inerrancy, historical accuracy in every detail, uniform authorship, freedom from redaction, absence of variants, scientific accuracy, moral perfection of every depicted actor, correctness of every later interpretation, or equal authority of every sentence.

# Article II — Historical Humility Concerning Canon

Religious history demonstrates that scriptural authority and canon formation often develop gradually.

This community shall not imagine that canon formation becomes illegitimate merely because it possesses a history.

The proper question is whether that history was transparent, disciplined, accountable, and truth-seeking.

# Article III — The Source Archive Precedes the Canon

The community shall maintain a permanent Universal Source Archive — USA.

It shall be broader than the canon and may contain canonical, rejected, disputed, variant, historical, archaeological, liturgical, mystical, philosophical, comparative, critical, adverse, and formerly canonical material.

Material shall not be destroyed merely because its authority changes.

# Article IV — Canonical Layers

## Layer A — Foundational Witness Corpus — FWC-A

Highest received textual layer, containing materials judged indispensable to foundational understanding of transcendent reality, revelation, ethics, humanity, transformation, and foundational encounter.

Layer A need not be internally uniform.

## Layer B — Early Witness Corpus — EWC-B

Historically close to foundational persons, communities, events, or teachings but below A in constitutional authority.

## Layer C — Received Wisdom Corpus — RWC-C

Material judged spiritually, morally, philosophically, or contemplatively valuable without being foundational revelation.

May arise from any culture or religion.

## Layer D — Interpretive Tradition — IT-D

Theology, philosophy, jurisprudence, commentary, exegesis, mysticism, councils, scholarship, sermons, and institutional reflection.

Influential but corrigible.

## Layer E — Exploratory Witness and Wisdom — EW-E

Potentially important but insufficiently established material.

## Layer U — Unresolved

Judgment deliberately suspended.

# Article V — Received Prophetic Witness

RPW-1 under EPC-002 does not automatically equal FWC-A.

Credible revelation does not automatically equal scripture; scripture does not automatically equal foundational canon; foundational canon does not equal infallibility.

# Article VI — Scripture and Canon Distinguished

Scripture means material formally received for sustained communal religious use and formation.

Canon means the constitutionally defined collection and ordering of authoritative materials.

A text may be scriptural and foundational, scriptural but secondary, liturgically authoritative but not foundational doctrine, historically important without being scripture, or spiritually valuable without being canonical.

# Article VII — Authority Is Multidimensional

Each work may carry separate authority dimensions:

- HA — Historical Authority.
- FA — Founder Attribution.
- TA — Theological Authority.
- EA — Ethical Authority.
- LA — Liturgical Authority.
- SA — Spiritual/Formative Authority.
- CA — Constitutional Authority.

These shall not be collapsed into a single numerical score.

# Article VIII — Genre Controls Interpretation

Every canonical unit shall be read according to genre: narrative, law, poetry, hymn, parable, prophecy, apocalypse, wisdom, genealogy, prayer, correspondence, philosophical discourse, mythic narrative, symbolic vision, liturgy, biography, historical chronicle, or other.

Poetry is not automatically scientific prose.
Apocalypse is not automatically journalistic prediction.
Parable is not automatically historical event.
Narrative description is not automatically moral command.

# Article IX — Claim-Level Authority

Canonical status belongs to a work; epistemic evaluation belongs ultimately to claims within it.

No doctrine shall be defended merely by saying “it is canonical.”

Ask what the passage claims, genre, textual history, likely producer, historical context, development, and whether stronger evidence challenges an interpretation.

# Article X — Canon Shall Preserve Internal Development

A canon shall not be artificially homogenized.

Development, disagreement, variants, and distinct community traditions shall remain visible.

Canon may preserve conversation across generations rather than simulate unanimous dictation.

# Article XI — Original Languages

Where original or earliest recoverable languages survive, they remain interpretively primary.

Translations are necessary and authoritative for ordinary communal use but inherently interpretive.

No doctrine shall depend entirely on a disputed translation without disclosure.

# Article XII — Textual Variants

Meaningful variants shall be preserved in critical apparatus or associated commentary.

Uncertainty about earliest wording shall be stated.

# Article XIII — Attribution Labels

Where possible, works receive:

- AT-A — highly secure attribution.
- AT-B — probable.
- AT-C — plausible.
- AT-D — uncertain.
- AT-E — traditional attribution historically doubtful.
- AT-U — unknown.

Traditional titles may remain even where historical authorship is uncertain.

# Article XIV — Dating Labels

Record where feasible composition range, earliest manuscript evidence, earliest quotation/attestation, major redaction stages, and confidence.

Traditional and critical dates may both be displayed.

# Article XV — Provenance Is Not Devotion

A text may be profound despite false traditional attribution.

Ancient provenance does not establish theological truth.

Historical provenance and religious value are separate questions.

# Article XVI — Foundational Admission Criteria

FWC-A candidates should show exceptional strength across multiple domains:

1. proximity to foundational encounters/teachers;
2. historical recoverability;
3. transmission quality;
4. textual stability;
5. importance in earliest community;
6. theological depth;
7. ethical integrity;
8. coherence with stronger evidence;
9. sustained spiritual fruit;
10. resilience under criticism;
11. independence from one living claimant's charisma;
12. relevance beyond transient conflict;
13. comparative consideration of contrary evidence;
14. mature communal reception.

No single criterion decides admission.

# Article XVII — Foundational Exclusions

Material shall not ordinarily enter FWC-A primarily because it is old, popular, founder-authored, leader-commanded, predictive of the leader's authority, emotionally powerful, miraculous, widely followed, called scripture elsewhere, or included for interfaith symmetry.

# Article XVIII — Living Claimant Restriction

No living claimant shall ordinarily have their own claimed revelation permanently elevated to FWC-A while their charisma, office, wealth, authority, or personal presence materially affects judgment.

Lower/provisional classifications remain available.

# Article XIX — No Founder Exception

A founder shall not declare whatever they write foundational merely because they are founder.

Foundational authority must be independently received.

# Article XX — Canon Admission Dossier

Every Layer A/B candidate receives a CAD — Canon Admission Dossier including identity, title, traditional/critical attribution, language, date, geography, textual history, witnesses, variants, recensions, redaction evidence, context, claim inventory, genre, founder proximity, ethical and contradiction reviews, alternatives, parallels, reception history, arguments for/against, minority report, recommended layer, and confidence.

# Article XXI — Adversarial Canon Review

Every Layer-A candidate receives a formal case for and against inclusion.

Negative reviewers must be protected from retaliation.

# Article XXII — Charisma-Blind Canon Review

Where feasible, propositions and passages shall be evaluated without emphasizing author prestige.

Would the teaching be judged equally strong if the author's name were unknown? Would it be accepted if it came from another religion?

# Article XXIII — Canon Admission Procedure

1. Nomination.
2. Source review.
3. Claim review.
4. Comparative review.
5. Adversarial review.
6. Public examination.
7. Discernment decision.
8. Enhanced-threshold ratification.
9. Publication of reasons, objections, confidence.

# Article XXIV — Truth Is Not Determined by Vote

A supermajority can determine formal reception, not manufacture reality.

# Article XXV — Foundational Stability

FWC-A shall be deliberately difficult to change.

Stability protects intergenerational continuity, shared vocabulary, liturgy, historical identity, and resistance to charismatic takeover or ideological fashion.

# Article XXVI — Stable but Not Metaphysically Closed

FWC shall normally be stable but not declared incapable in principle of further foundational revelation.

Presumptively stable, exceptionally expandable, never casually mutable.

# Article XXVII — Addition Threshold

A new Layer-A addition requires substantially greater evidence than admission to lower layers.

Ordinarily it must show extraordinary provenance, sustained scrutiny, durable reception, exceptional relevance, ethical integrity, and inability to perform its legitimate role adequately at a lower layer.

# Article XXVIII — Demotion and Removal

Status may be reconsidered for deliberate forgery, catastrophic provenance failure, major textual corruption, fundamental ethical incompatibility, materially authority-creating false attribution, manipulation in canonization, or another foundational defect.

Removal from authority never means removal from history.

# Article XXIX — Canonical Demotion

Possible reclassification:

FWC-A → EWC-B → RWC-C → IT-D, or any layer → U.

Demotion changes judgment of authority, not historical significance.

# Article XXX — Amendment Procedure

An amendment proposal states:
1. proposed status change;
2. new evidence/reason;
3. why prior reasoning is inadequate;
4. consequences;
5. remaining objections.

“We no longer like this teaching” is insufficient.
“Our ancestors accepted it” is also insufficient to block justified review.

# Article XXXI — No Secret Canon

No binding canonical material may be restricted to a leadership elite.

Advanced study may require preparation, but foundational obligations may not depend on secret texts.

# Article XXXII — Public Accessibility

Canonical materials shall ordinarily be publicly accessible in original/earliest language, accurate working translation, relevant critical notes, provenance information, and authority status.

Financial inability shall not prevent ordinary access.

# Article XXXIII — Commentary Shall Not Masquerade as Text

Canonical text and commentary shall be visibly distinguishable.

Editorial headings, punctuation, paragraphing, or supplied words affecting meaning should be disclosed where practicable.

# Article XXXIV — No Authorized Translation Monopoly

Standard translations may exist, but responsible independent translation and linguistic analysis shall not be prohibited for doctrinal control.

# Article XXXV — Interpretation Hierarchy

Ordinary sequence:

    text
      ↓
    language
      ↓
    literary context
      ↓
    historical context
      ↓
    genre
      ↓
    source/redaction history
      ↓
    canonical context
      ↓
    comparative interpretation
      ↓
    constructive theology
      ↓
    contemporary application

The purpose is to prevent contemporary doctrine from being projected backward and mistaken for original meaning.

# Article XXXVI — Original Meaning and Received Meaning

Distinguish:
- OM — Original/Historical Meaning.
- RH — Reception-Historical Meaning.
- CT — Constructive Theological Meaning.

These may overlap but shall not be conflated.

# Article XXXVII — Prophecy in Canon

Canonical prophecy remains subject to EPC-002 prediction rules.

Distinguish prediction before event, post-event interpretation, editorial framing, typological reuse, and later fulfillment claims.

Canonical status does not retroactively increase predictive specificity.

# Article XXXVIII — Ethical Interpretation Rule

For ethically difficult material ask:
1. commanded, permitted, narrated, or criticized?
2. universal or contextual?
3. does the corpus itself develop?
4. does stronger foundational ethical reasoning challenge literal modern application?
5. evidence of cultural conditioning?

The community shall not defend cruelty merely because it appears in ancient sacred literature.

# Article XXXIX — Difficult Texts Shall Not Be Hidden

Texts involving violence, slavery, sexuality, gender, outsiders, warfare, judgment, ethnic hostility, coercion, or similar difficulties remain available for study.

# Article XL — Canonical Contradiction Register

Classify:
- CR-1 apparent linguistic tension;
- CR-2 perspective difference;
- CR-3 developmental difference;
- CR-4 historical discrepancy;
- CR-5 genuine unresolved contradiction.

Unresolved contradiction may remain in the canon.

# Article XLI — Intercanonical Comparison

External evidence may preserve earlier readings, stronger history, better ethical reasoning, or unnoticed problems.

External status does not disqualify evidence.

# Article XLII — External Wisdom

Material need not become foundational scripture to influence the community.

RWC-C exists to receive justified wisdom from Buddhist, Stoic, Hindu, Islamic, Jewish, Christian, Sikh, Indigenous, scientific, secular, or other sources without fictional claims of one historical canon.

# Article XLIII — No Retroactive Founder Capture

Later communities shall not casually attribute mature doctrine to earlier founders.

Evidence is required before saying an earlier founder taught later systematic metaphysics or jurisprudence.

# Article XLIV — No Authority by Antiquity Alone

Older material receives historical interest, not automatic theological superiority.

Early material gets special weight in founder reconstruction because of proximity, not magical sanctity.

# Article XLV — No Authority by Novelty Alone

New does not equal improved.

Novel interpretations must establish their case.

Tradition receives neither automatic obedience nor automatic suspicion.

# Article XLVI — Liturgical Canon

A narrower Liturgical Canon may draw from multiple authority layers.

Appropriateness for worship does not imply FWC-A historical or doctrinal status.

# Article XLVII — Study Canon

A broader Study Canon may include foundational, disputed, comparative, critical, and contrary texts for serious inquiry.

# Article XLVIII — Canonical Metadata

Each work should eventually record:

canonical_id, title, authority_layer, authority_dimensions, language, date_range, traditional_attribution, critical_attribution, genre, earliest_witness, textual_status, major_variants, historical_context, reception_status, review_date, open_questions.

# Article XLIX — Versioned Canon

Canonical decisions shall be versioned.

Earlier versions remain documented.

# Article L — Canonical Audit Trail

For every admission, amendment, demotion, or removal preserve proposal, evidence, review, debate, minority opinions, decision record, rationale, effective date, and later reassessment.

# Article LI — Scheduled Review

Layer A is not reopened casually merely on a calendar.

The canon system itself may be periodically reviewed for new manuscripts, changed historical conclusions, translation needs, metadata, open contradictions, or newly available evidence.

Procedural review does not presume canonical change.

# Article LII — Emergency Canon Change Prohibited

War, charismatic crisis, schism, leadership transition, alleged prophecy, panic, or political emergency shall not permit immediate permanent FWC-A alteration.

Temporary guidance may be issued; permanent change follows ordinary safeguards.

# Article LIII — Schism Safeguard

Competing groups shall not rewrite history to claim their preferred canon was always uncontested.

Preserve competing lists, recensions, dissent, and chronology.

Institutional victory does not erase alternatives.

# Article LIV — Canon and Governance

No canonical passage automatically grants unchecked institutional power.

Governance applications remain subject to EPC-001, EPC-002, governance constitution, ethics, transparency, and accountability.

# Article LV — Canon and Conscience

Members may question canonical interpretation and may in good faith question the status of particular material through review procedures.

Inability to affirm a disputed historical or interpretive proposition shall not automatically sever belonging.

# Article LVI — Foundational Witness Does Not Mean Equal Weight

Within FWC-A, different materials may function as revelation, ethics, prayer, history, wisdom, community memory, or other genres.

Every verse need not carry identical authority for every question.

# Article LVII — The Canon Shall Not Become a Paper Pope

Canonical scripture shall not transfer absolute authority from human leader to text interpreted without accountability.

A text always requires interpretation; “text alone” can conceal human interpretive power.

# Article LVIII — Nor Shall the Interpreter Become Supreme

No council, scholar, priest, prophet, founder, teacher, or governing body may claim that text means whatever they declare.

Interpretation is constrained by language, evidence, context, reason, and transparent argument.

# Article LIX — Canonical Presumption

Once material enters FWC-A through full process, it receives a rebuttable presumption of continued foundational importance.

Those proposing demotion/removal bear the burden.

# Article LX — External-Truth Rule

If an external proposition is better supported than a conflicting interpretation inside the canon, external evidence shall not be dismissed for being external.

Interpretation, genre, translation, historical claim, or in serious cases canonical status may need reconsideration.

# Article LXI — Open Question Register

Every canonical corpus maintains an OQR — Open Question Register for uncertain authorship, dating, variants, ethical difficulty, contradictory parallels, uncertain events, disputed theological implications.

Inclusion does not require pretending questions are closed.

# Article LXII — Canon Formation and Humility

Future generations may possess evidence unavailable to the present.

Canonization shall be serious without assuming present knowledge exhausts truth.

# Article LXIII — Canonical Reception Formula

“We receive this material as a foundational witness for this community because, after disciplined historical, textual, ethical, theological, and communal examination, we judge it sufficiently trustworthy and enduring to shape our common search for and response to transcendent truth. We do not thereby declare every proposition infallible, every interpretation final, or future examination forbidden.”

# Article LXIV — Canonical Demotion Formula

“We preserve this work as part of our religious history while concluding that the evidence no longer warrants its previous degree of authority. We record the reasons openly and leave the evidence available to future generations.”

# Article LXV — Constitutional Status of Canon

Canon is subordinate to the epistemological constitution.

No FWC-A interpretation may abolish freedom of inquiry, ethical scrutiny, discernment safeguards, accountability, evidence, or the distinction between revelation and interpretation.

# Article LXVI — Final Canonical Architecture

    REALITY / TRUTH
            ↓
    EPC — Epistemological Constitution
            ↓
    FWC-A — Foundational Witness Corpus
            ↓
    EWC-B — Early Witness Corpus
            ↓
    RWC-C — Received Wisdom Corpus
            ↓
    IT-D — Interpretive Tradition
            ↓
    EW-E — Exploratory Witness/Wisdom

Alongside:
U — Unresolved Material.

Underlying the evidentiary record:
USA — Universal Source Archive.

# Article LXVII — Governing Canonical Maxims

Preserve before selecting.
Recover before interpreting.
Distinguish text from attribution.
Distinguish authority from infallibility.
Distinguish original meaning from later reception.
Distinguish liturgical value from historical reliability.
Admit slowly.
Never canonize living charisma.
Never erase adverse evidence.
Never let a majority vote manufacture truth.
Never let canon silence reality.
Preserve uncertainty visibly.
Permit revision only through evidence and disciplined reasoning.
Let foundational witness guide the search for truth without becoming a substitute for truth itself.
