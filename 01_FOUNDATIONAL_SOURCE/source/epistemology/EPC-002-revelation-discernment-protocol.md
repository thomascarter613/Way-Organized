# EPC-002 — Revelation Discernment Protocol

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Working Constitutional Baseline  
**Parent:** EPC-001 — Epistemological Constitution  
**Domain:** Contemporary revelation, prophecy, visions, voices, dreams, mystical experiences, illumination, extraordinary spiritual experiences, and claimed divine commands

# Preamble

This protocol governs reception and evaluation of contemporary claims of revelation or extraordinary spiritual experience.

Its purpose is neither to suppress revelation nor authenticate it automatically.

It begins from four propositions:

1. genuine revelation remains possible in principle;
2. unusual spiritual experiences may be meaningful without proving a supernatural cause;
3. human perception, interpretation, memory, and transmission are fallible;
4. no claimed revelation automatically creates authority over another person.

Every new claim begins as testimony, not doctrine.

# Article I — Governing Maxim

Record first. Stabilize second. Interpret cautiously. Test alternatives. Examine ethics. Seek corroboration. Compare with evidence. Permit uncertainty. Grant authority only after substantially stronger justification than is required for private belief.

No person shall be punished, rewarded, promoted, or condemned merely for reporting an unusual spiritual experience.

# Article II — Scope

This protocol applies to claims including:
- divine/spiritual voices;
- words believed to originate from God or another nonhuman intelligence;
- visions;
- prophetic dreams;
- extraordinary intuitions;
- automatic/inspired writing;
- mystical union or presence;
- perceived angelic/spiritual encounters;
- revelations during prayer, contemplation, fasting, retreat, wilderness isolation, or ritual;
- predictions attributed to revelation;
- claimed divine commands;
- claims of prophetic identity or appointment;
- claims concerning another person's destiny or role;
- claimed new scripture;
- extraordinary illumination or awakening.

It applies regardless of claimant status.

# Article III — Default Classification

Every claimed revelation begins as:

## TS-0 — Testimonial Source

Meaning only:
A person reports an experience and assigns, or is considering assigning, spiritual significance to it.

It does not mean God spoke, the claimant is a prophet, the message is false, the claimant is mentally ill, or the community must obey.

# Article IV — Four-Part Record

Every claim shall be decomposed into:

A. Experience — what was actually experienced.

B. Interpretation — what the experiencer thinks caused it.

C. Proposition — what information is asserted.

D. Authority claim — who, if anyone, is allegedly obligated.

These shall never be collapsed.

# Article V — Gate Zero: Immediate Safety and Authority Freeze

A claimed revelation shall not be acted upon solely because it feels urgent if it calls for violence, self-harm, harm to others, abandonment of dependents, dangerous exposure, severe food/water deprivation, prolonged intentional sleep deprivation, abrupt discontinuation of necessary medication, reckless travel, illegal entry, surrender of substantial assets, sexual activity, marriage/divorce, irreversible financial commitments, property destruction, or similarly serious irreversible consequences.

If the experiencer is at immediate risk of harming self/others, severely confused/disoriented, unable to maintain basic safety, extremely agitated, unable to sleep for a prolonged period, or unable to distinguish commands from consensual reality sufficiently to remain safe, theological discernment is temporarily subordinated to safety and appropriate professional assessment.

Such assessment is not proof that the experience was spiritually false.

# Article VI — Physiological Stabilization Rule

No major doctrinal or irreversible decision shall ordinarily be made while the claimant is substantially affected by severe sleep deprivation, dehydration, starvation/dangerous fasting, intoxication, withdrawal, high fever, acute neurological symptoms, extreme exhaustion, or another condition reasonably capable of altering perception/cognition.

Such conditions constitute evidence relevant to causal interpretation without proving spiritual meaning absent.

# Article VII — Stage One: Contemporaneous Record

Use EPC-RDP-01 — Initial Experience Record.

Record:
- date/time;
- location type;
- people present;
- preceding activity;
- sleep, food, water, fasting, illness, medications, medication changes, substances, exertion;
- modality: auditory, visual, tactile, dream, inner speech, intuition, sense of presence, automatic writing, altered state, other;
- exact content if remembered;
- uncertainty in wording;
- emotional state;
- initial interpretation;
- degree of conviction;
- witnesses;
- material evidence.

# Article VIII — Memory Preservation Rule

The initial record shall be preserved unchanged.

Later reflections are appended, never substituted.

This permits observation of whether the claimed message becomes more specific, dramatic, theological, or materially different over time.

Retroactive improvement of revelation records is prohibited.

# Article IX — Cooling Interval

No ordinary revelatory claim should immediately become communal doctrine.

The appropriate interval depends on seriousness.

Claims affecting doctrine, governance, property, communal law, leadership, sexual ethics, family relationships, institutional authority, or scripture require substantially longer review.

Urgency asserted by the revelation itself does not waive this protection.

# Article X — Stage Two: Phenomenological Reconstruction

Investigators ask what happened experientially before deciding why.

Prefer neutral descriptions.

Example:
Instead of “An angel appeared and commanded me,” record “The claimant reports seeing a humanlike luminous figure and experiencing words understood as a command.”

# Article XI — Stage Three: Alternative-Explanation Review

Every serious claim receives an Alternative Explanation Matrix.

Possible classes:
- ordinary cognition;
- physiological causes;
- psychological factors;
- environmental causes;
- social-cultural causes;
- deliberate human causes;
- transcendent hypotheses.

Ordinary cognition includes inner speech, memory association, dreams, intuition, coincidence, pattern recognition.

Physiological alternatives include sleep loss, dehydration, fasting, illness, migraine, neurological conditions, medication or substance effects.

Psychological alternatives include grief, trauma, severe stress, expectation, suggestibility, dissociation, intense emotional arousal.

Environmental alternatives include actual voices, radio/audio sources, light/reflection, animals, weather, environmental sounds.

Social-cultural alternatives include recent scripture reading, sermons, prophecy literature, group expectations, suggestion by leaders, online content, prior conversation.

Deliberate human causes include deception, manipulation, practical jokes, fabrication, coaching.

Transcendent hypotheses may include personal God, spiritual intelligence, mystical apprehension, extraordinary cognition, or another presently unknown cause.

# Article XII — Alternatives Are Defeaters, Not Automatic Refutations

An ordinary causal explanation can reduce confidence in a transcendent inference without logically proving transcendence impossible.

Likewise absence of an obvious ordinary cause does not establish supernatural origin.

# Article XIII — Stage Four: Cultural Contamination Analysis

Investigators shall identify concepts available to the claimant before the experience.

Ask whether the claimant recently read about the subject, actively sought a specific revelation, was primed by another person, immersed in apocalyptic material, expected a particular figure, intentionally imitated a prophet, knew the prophecy later claimed fulfilled, or could have acquired the imagery normally.

Cultural availability affects claims of inaccessible knowledge but does not automatically falsify an experience.

# Article XIV — Stage Five: Message Extraction

Decompose a complex message into individual propositions.

Example:
“The community is corrupt; a transcendent intelligence communicated this; the communicator was God; I am appointed prophet; I therefore possess authority; everyone must relocate; the destination is correct.”

Each proposition is evaluated separately.

# Article XV — Stage Six: Ethical Admissibility Gate

Major adverse presumptions arise when a claimed revelation grants:
- violence privilege;
- sexual privilege;
- property privilege;
- authority privilege;
- deception privilege;
- family severance;
- dependency creation;
- dehumanization;
- immunity from accountability.

The more a message benefits the claimant in power, wealth, sex, obedience, prestige, or immunity, the stronger the required independent evidence.

# Article XVI — Anti-Charisma Rule

Charisma is evidentially neutral.

Eloquence, magnetism, confidence, unusual dress, ascetic appearance, apparent holiness, large following, wealth, poverty, miracle reputation, social reach, suffering, persecution, or willingness to die do not authenticate revelation.

Sincerity may establish that a claimant believes a claim. It does not establish truth.

# Article XVII — Stage Seven: Empirical and Historical Corroboration

Potentially useful corroboration includes:
- independent contemporary witnesses;
- records created before the event;
- information demonstrably unknown to claimant;
- externally verifiable facts;
- predictions with frozen criteria;
- independently recorded experiences before communication;
- archaeological/documentary confirmation;
- repeated observations under controls.

Evidence loses weight under prior knowledge, cueing, selective reporting, vague wording, retrospective reinterpretation, or claimant-controlled fulfillment.

# Article XVIII — Independent Witness Protocol

Obtain witness accounts before witnesses discuss details where possible.

Each witness separately records perception, exact words, sequence, timing, sensory modality, interpretation, confidence.

Agreement after extensive discussion lacks the independence of prior separate records.

# Article XIX — Prediction Protocol

Use EPC-RDP-02 — Prophecy Registration Record.

Record exact text, timestamp, intended meaning, date window, location if applicable, persons involved, success criteria, failure criteria, permitted ambiguity, and disconfirming events.

After registration, the text may not be changed. Interpretive revisions are separately timestamped.

# Article XX — Stage Eight: Contradiction Analysis

Compare the message against:
A. strong external evidence;
B. internal coherence;
C. earlier foundational witness;
D. moral foundations;
E. claimant's prior revelations.

Contradiction creates a problem requiring resolution; it does not automatically identify which side is false.

# Article XXI — Novel Revelation Rule

A claim shall not be rejected merely because it conflicts with tradition.

Otherwise genuine corrective revelation would be impossible.

Contradiction with inherited doctrine triggers heightened scrutiny, not automatic rejection.

However, “God told me the old rule is obsolete” does not establish authority to abolish the old rule.

# Article XXII — Stage Nine: Fruit and Consequence Analysis

Relevant positive tendencies may include truthfulness, humility, compassion, justice, courage, reconciliation, intellectual honesty, freedom of conscience, responsibility, service, moral seriousness.

Relevant adverse tendencies may include coercion, paranoia, deception, hatred, dependency, exploitation, grandiosity, recklessness, isolation, unquestioning obedience, institutional immunity.

Consequences are evidence, not proof.

# Article XXIII — Stage Ten: Discernment Council

Claims seeking communal authority shall be reviewed by a plural body rather than a sole leader.

Competencies should ordinarily include historical/textual analysis, ethics, theology/comparative religion, critical reasoning, governance, and where relevant health or psychological assessment.

Clinical expertise evaluates health-related alternatives but has no unilateral authority over metaphysical theology.

Clergy likewise have no unilateral authority to dismiss medical concerns as purely spiritual.

# Article XXIV — Conflict-of-Interest Rule

The claimant shall not control the body judging the claimant's revelation.

The claimant may testify, provide evidence, answer questions, challenge inaccuracies, and submit reasoning.

The claimant shall not cast the decisive vote concerning their own prophetic status, expanded authority, personal financial benefit, sexual privileges, succession rights, or canonization of their own revelation.

A founder is not exempt.

# Article XXV — Anonymous Review

Where practicable, reviewers should first evaluate content without knowing claimant identity, rank, wealth, popularity, gender, social status, or office.

This is Charisma-Blind Review.

# Article XXVI — Adversarial Review

For significant claims, one competent reviewer shall construct the strongest reasonable case against divine provenance and another may construct the strongest case for it.

Criticism is not hostility.

A revelation that cannot survive questioning should not govern other people's lives.

# Article XXVII — Stage Eleven: Classification

Possible statuses:

- TS-0 — Testimonial Source.
- PW-1 — Personal Witness.
- SI-1 — Spiritual Insight.
- AE-1 — Anomalous Experience.
- PPW-1 — Provisional Prophetic Witness.
- RPW-1 — Received Prophetic Witness.
- FWC-1 — Foundational Witness Candidate.
- FWC-2 — Foundational Witness.

Most experiences need never progress beyond PW-1 or SI-1.

# Article XXVIII — Non-Received Classifications

- NR-U — Unresolved.
- NR-N — Natural explanation presently stronger.
- NR-C — Contradicted.
- NR-E — Ethically inadmissible.
- NR-F — Fabricated.
- NR-X — Unclassifiable.

Not received does not necessarily mean dishonest.

# Article XXIX — Authority Ladder

    TS-0 Testimonial Source
          ↓
    PW-1 Personal Witness
          ↓
    SI-1 / AE-1
          ↓
    PPW-1 Provisional Prophetic Witness
          ↓
    RPW-1 Received Prophetic Witness
          ↓
    FWC-1 Foundational Witness Candidate
          ↓
    FWC-2 Foundational Witness

No stage is automatic.

# Article XXX — Living-Claimant Canon Rule

A living claimant's revelation should ordinarily not be granted irreversible foundational-canonical status while the claimant's charisma, leadership, financial influence, or institutional power materially shapes evaluation.

Full foundational status ordinarily requires historical distance.

# Article XXXI — No Constitutional Office of Infallible Prophet

Recognition of prophetic witness does not create unlimited authority.

A person may produce one credible revelatory insight and later be mistaken.

Prophetic authority attaches primarily to the evaluated witness/message, not permanently to the claimant's person.

# Article XXXII — Commands Concerning Third Parties

Claims such as “God told me you must marry me,” “give me your property,” “join this community,” “your child belongs to this mission,” or equivalent statements create no binding authority over the third party merely because reported.

The third party retains freedom of conscience and ordinary rights.

# Article XXXIII — Revelations Concerning Leadership

A revelation whose content is substantially “I should receive more power” receives heightened scrutiny.

Examples include supreme leadership, indefinite tenure, exemption from removal, money control, exclusive interpretation rights, hereditary succession, or immunity from discipline.

No governance system shall be altered solely because its beneficiary claims divine authorization.

# Article XXXIV — Revelations Concerning Money

No revelation alone authorizes compelled donations, transfers of homes/inheritances/wages, unusual gifts to leaders, or personal control of communal property.

Transparent financial governance remains binding.

# Article XXXV — Revelations Concerning Sexuality and Marriage

No private revelation can create another person's consent.

No divine-command claim can substitute for voluntary, informed consent.

Leaders may not use claimed revelation to obtain sexual or marital access.

# Article XXXVI — Revelations Concerning Medicine

No revelatory claim alone shall require another person to stop prescribed treatment, refuse emergency care, discontinue psychiatric medication, substitute prayer for necessary medical treatment, or conceal significant medical symptoms.

Spiritual practice and medical care may coexist.

# Article XXXVII — Revelations During Fasting, Isolation, or Retreat

Experiences during wilderness retreat, fasting, sensory restriction, prolonged prayer, or intense contemplation shall be recorded carefully as historically common contexts of religious experience.

They shall also be examined for caloric deprivation, dehydration, sleep loss, heat/cold exposure, exhaustion, isolation effects, expectation effects, and altered states.

No one shall deliberately intensify danger to make revelation more likely.

A revelation does not become more credible because the claimant nearly died obtaining it.

# Article XXXVIII — Apparent Repetition

Repeated experiences may increase evidentiary interest but do not automatically authenticate causation.

Ask whether content is new, triggering conditions repeat, prediction improves, interpretation grows more elaborate, repetition is expectation-driven, and independent observers report the same phenomenon.

# Article XXXIX — External Confirmation

The strongest potential confirmations are those least controlled by the claimant:
- independent matching records before communication;
- accurate inaccessible information with documented exclusion of ordinary acquisition;
- specific frozen predictions;
- independently reproducible phenomena;
- later evidence confirming prior records.

Even these require audits for coincidence, leakage, selection effects, and alternative explanations.

# Article XL — Failed Prophecy

A registered prediction that fails according to frozen criteria shall be recorded as failed.

The original record shall not be deleted or rewritten.

A revised interpretation may be proposed but must be labeled post-outcome reinterpretation.

Repeated failed predictions materially reduce confidence absent compelling independent reasons.

# Article XLI — Anti-Retrofitting Rule

After an event occurs, interpreters shall not change dates, redefine names, broaden geography, convert literal wording to symbolism, or introduce unstated criteria without explicitly marking the change.

Symbolic reinterpretation may have theological value but shall not be misrepresented as exact literal fulfillment.

# Article XLII — Time Test

Some questions cannot responsibly be settled immediately.

Received status should consider whether predictions survive, hidden contradictions emerge, claimant conduct remains credible, institutional incentives distort interpretation, followers exaggerate stories, and the message remains coherent outside emotional intensity.

Time is part of discernment.

# Article XLIII — Reception Review

Every RPW or FWC classification remains reviewable.

Review may produce affirmation, reclassification, qualification, suspension, or withdrawal.

The historical record remains preserved.

# Article XLIV — Minority Reports

Reasoned minority opinions shall be preserved.

Institutional unity shall not be purchased by falsifying actual disagreement.

# Article XLV — Claimant Rights

The claimant retains the right to be heard respectfully, obtain records, correct factual misrepresentation, respond to objections, retain personal spiritual interpretation, decline public identification, and appeal procedural irregularities.

The claimant does not have a right to communal acceptance of metaphysical conclusions.

# Article XLVI — Community Rights

The community retains the right to question, request evidence, decline obedience, suspend judgment, seek independent expertise, reject coercion, and reconsider prior conclusions.

Disbelief in a contemporary revelation shall not itself establish moral or spiritual deficiency.

# Article XLVII — Discernment Decision Form

Every major review shall state separately:
1. phenomenological judgment;
2. historical judgment;
3. causal judgment;
4. content judgment;
5. ethical judgment;
6. authority judgment;
7. confidence;
8. minority view.

# Article XLVIII — Discernment Gate Sequence

    CLAIMED EXPERIENCE
            ↓
    GATE 0 — SAFETY
            ↓
    CONTEMPORANEOUS RECORD
            ↓
    PHYSIOLOGICAL STABILIZATION
            ↓
    PHENOMENOLOGICAL RECONSTRUCTION
            ↓
    ALTERNATIVE-EXPLANATION MATRIX
            ↓
    CULTURAL-CONTAMINATION REVIEW
            ↓
    MESSAGE EXTRACTION
            ↓
    ETHICAL ADMISSIBILITY
            ↓
    CORROBORATION
            ↓
    CONTRADICTION TESTING
            ↓
    FRUIT / CONSEQUENCE REVIEW
            ↓
    CHARISMA-BLIND REVIEW
            ↓
    ADVERSARIAL REVIEW
            ↓
    DISCERNMENT COUNCIL
            ↓
    PROVISIONAL CLASSIFICATION
            ↓
    TIME TEST
            ↓
    REVIEW / RECLASSIFICATION

No single gate establishes divine provenance.

# Article XLIX — Hard Stops

Regardless of claimed supernatural provenance, no revelation shall receive binding communal authority if implementation entails nonconsensual sex, sexual exploitation, child abuse, deliberate unjustified violence, enslavement, compelled marriage, deliberate deprivation of necessary care, systematic deception, total immunity of leadership from accountability, coerced transfer of personal property, or suppression of all questioning.

# Article L — Principle of Dual Humility

Avoid skeptical arrogance: “I have an ordinary explanation, therefore transcendence is impossible.”

Avoid revelatory arrogance: “I had an extraordinary experience, therefore my interpretation must be divine truth.”

An extraordinary experience deserves neither ridicule nor automatic obedience. It deserves careful description, humane treatment, critical examination, and confidence proportionate to total evidence.

# Article LI — Final Reception Rule

A contemporary claimed revelation begins as Testimonial Source.

It may remain private spiritual testimony, received insight, unresolved anomalous experience, or provisional prophetic witness.

Only unusually strong cases should become Received Prophetic Witness.

Only exceptionally, and ordinarily after mature historical reception independent of living charisma or office, should material be considered for the Foundational Witness Corpus.

# Article LII — Final Maxim

Preserve the experience without exaggeration. Separate experience from interpretation. Test ordinary explanations without presuming them. Remain open to transcendence without surrendering judgment. Never allow a private revelation to erase another person's conscience, rights, consent, or safety. Demand stronger evidence as authority increases. Permit uncertainty. Let time test what excitement cannot.
