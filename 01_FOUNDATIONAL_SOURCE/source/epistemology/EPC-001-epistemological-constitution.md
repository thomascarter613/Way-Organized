# EPC-001 — Epistemological Constitution

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Working Constitutional Baseline  
**Domain:** Revelation, knowledge, evidence, scripture, interpretation, authority, and uncertainty

## Preamble

Truth is not created by authority, tradition, popularity, antiquity, charisma, institutional power, or personal conviction.

If transcendent reality exists, it remains what it is regardless of human belief about it.

Human beings may encounter truth, misunderstand truth, partially apprehend truth, transmit truth accurately, distort truth unintentionally, corrupt truth deliberately, or remain uncertain about truth.

Accordingly, this tradition shall remain simultaneously open to revelation, resistant to credulity, respectful of inherited wisdom, critical of inherited error, receptive to reason and evidence, and willing to leave unresolved what cannot responsibly be resolved.

No claim shall become true merely because it is declared sacred.

No claim shall become false merely because it comes from outside the tradition.

# Article I — First Epistemic Principles

## §1. Reality precedes doctrine

Reality possesses priority over every description of reality.

Where doctrine and reality demonstrably conflict, doctrine must be reconsidered rather than reality denied.

## §2. Truth is not owned

No people, lineage, prophet, scripture, institution, clergy, nation, philosophical school, or religious community possesses truth merely by institutional inheritance.

Truth may be encountered wherever it occurs.

## §3. Human reception is fallible

Even if an encounter with transcendent reality is genuine, it does not automatically follow that the recipient understood it perfectly, described it perfectly, listeners remembered it perfectly, scribes transmitted it perfectly, institutions interpreted it perfectly, or later theology systematized it perfectly.

Therefore revelation and interpretation of revelation shall never be treated as automatically identical.

## §4. Extraordinary claims remain admissible

Claims involving revelation, vision, prophecy, mystical encounter, unusual cognition, miracles, or nonhuman intelligence shall not be rejected merely because they are extraordinary.

Neither shall they be accepted merely because they are extraordinary.

The proper response is investigation.

## §5. Confidence shall be proportional

Beliefs shall be held with confidence appropriate to the quality of their evidence.

Certainty shall not be manufactured where evidence yields only probability or possibility.

# Article II — Fundamental Distinctions

The following shall remain permanently distinct:

- Event — what actually occurred.
- Experience — what a participant consciously experienced.
- Interpretation — what the participant believed the experience meant.
- Testimony — what the participant subsequently reported.
- Transmission — how that testimony reached others.
- Text — the surviving written representation.
- Interpretation of text — what later readers understand the surviving text to mean.
- Doctrine — a community's systematic conclusion.
- Institution — the human structure that teaches, preserves, regulates, or enforces doctrine.

Conceptual sequence:

    REALITY / EVENT
          ↓
    EXPERIENCE
          ↓
    INTERPRETATION
          ↓
    EXPRESSION
          ↓
    TESTIMONY
          ↓
    TRANSMISSION
          ↓
    TEXT
          ↓
    EXEGESIS
          ↓
    DOCTRINE
          ↓
    INSTITUTION

Epistemic confidence may increase or decrease at every transition.

# Article III — Definitions

## §1. Revelation

Revelation is a claimed disclosure of information, truth, command, or reality whose ultimate source is understood to transcend the ordinary cognitive resources of the recipient.

A claim of revelation is initially a claim about provenance, not proof of provenance.

## §2. Prophecy

Prophecy is claimed revelatory communication intended, at least substantially, for persons beyond the immediate recipient.

Prophecy may include warning, moral instruction, prediction, interpretation, exhortation, or disclosure.

Prediction is not necessary to prophecy.

## §3. Inspiration

Inspiration is heightened apprehension, expression, creativity, understanding, or moral insight attributed wholly or partly to a transcendent source without necessarily involving verbal revelation.

## §4. Illumination

Illumination is claimed direct apprehension of some aspect of reality through transformed consciousness, contemplation, insight, or awakening.

Illumination need not presuppose a personal communicating deity.

## §5. Mystical experience

A mystical experience is an experience interpreted as unusual immediacy, union, presence, apprehension, participation, or contact with ultimate or transcendent reality.

Its phenomenological reality and metaphysical interpretation shall be evaluated separately.

## §6. Scripture

Scripture is a text or textual corpus received by a community as possessing exceptional religious authority.

Scriptural status describes community reception. It does not, by itself, establish authorship, historical accuracy, divine origin, textual inerrancy, or correct interpretation.

## §7. Tradition

Tradition is accumulated transmitted teaching, interpretation, practice, memory, custom, and institutional reasoning.

Tradition is evidence of what communities believed. It is not automatically evidence that those beliefs were correct.

## §8. Reason

Reason includes valid inference, logical consistency, conceptual analysis, comparison, explanatory reasoning, and critical reflection.

No interpretation may demand acceptance of a formal contradiction without acknowledging the contradiction.

## §9. Evidence

Evidence is information that rationally changes the credibility of a proposition.

Evidence may include direct observation, material or archaeological evidence, documentary evidence, independent testimony, textual evidence, historical context, reproducible empirical evidence, logical implication, experiential evidence, negative evidence, and evidence concerning transmission.

## §10. Faith

Faith may include trust, commitment, hope, loyalty, and action extending beyond what evidence alone compels.

Faith may exceed demonstration.

It shall not be represented falsely as demonstration.

# Article IV — Classes of Claims

Every significant religious proposition shall, when feasible, be identified by claim type:

- HC — Historical Claim.
- AC — Attribution Claim.
- PC — Phenomenological Claim.
- MC — Metaphysical Claim.
- EC — Ethical Claim.
- CC — Cosmological Claim.
- ESC — Eschatological Claim.
- IC — Interpretive Claim.
- DC — Doctrinal Claim.
- PR — Practice Claim.

Different claim types require different methods of evaluation.

Strong evidence for an HC does not automatically establish an MC.

# Article V — Evidence Classes

Evidence shall be recorded rather than collapsed into a single religious-authority score.

Relevant dimensions include:

- H — Historical Attestation.
- P — Source Proximity.
- A — Attribution.
- T — Textual Stability.
- E — Encounter Directness.
- X — External Corroboration.
- I — Independence.
- C — Conceptual Coherence.
- D — Defeater Status.

No composite numerical truth score shall be required.

False precision is itself an epistemic defect.

# Article VI — Degrees of Confidence

Claims may be provisionally classified:

Established; Highly probable; Probable; Plausible; Possible; Indeterminate; Improbable; Highly improbable; Refuted within present evidence.

These labels describe present warranted confidence, not declarations of eternal certainty.

Every classification remains revisable upon materially new evidence.

# Article VII — The Rule of Testimony

Human civilization necessarily depends upon testimony.

Therefore testimony shall not be presumed false merely because it is testimony.

But testimony is defeasible.

Relevant considerations include proximity to the event, opportunity to observe, competence, consistency, independence, motive, known reliability, corroboration, memory effects, social pressure, institutional incentives, transmission history, and contrary evidence.

A revered witness receives the same basic scrutiny as an ordinary witness.

Charisma shall never function as an epistemic credential.

# Article VIII — Revelation Reception Rule

Every contemporary claimed revelation shall begin at the level of Testimonial Source.

It shall not automatically become doctrine, command, scripture, communal obligation, prophetic office, or proof of supernatural status.

Its first legitimate formulation is:

“Person X reports experiencing Y and interprets it as Z.”

The following shall initially remain separate:

    CLAIM
    “I received a command from God.”

    REPORTABLE FACT
    “X reports experiencing what X understood as a divine command.”

    INTERPRETATION
    “The experience was caused by God.”

    AUTHORITY CLAIM
    “Others must obey it.”

The first may be factually reportable.

The second-order interpretations require additional justification.

# Article IX — Private Revelation and Public Obligation

No private revelation, vision, dream, voice, intuition, omen, synchronicity, or mystical experience shall by itself impose an obligation upon another person.

The burden of evidence increases with the magnitude of the demanded consequence.

A private revelation alone shall never be sufficient grounds for requiring another person to surrender property, sever family relationships, submit sexually, enter marriage, abandon necessary medical care, commit violence, break civil law, relinquish ordinary freedom, transfer irrevocable authority, or accept another human being as infallible.

A purported divine command does not remove the obligation of moral scrutiny.

# Article X — The Non-Self-Authentication Principle

No person may authenticate their own supreme religious authority merely by claiming revelation.

The reasoning “I speak for God because God told me that I speak for God” has no independent evidentiary force.

Likewise, “This scripture is infallible because the scripture says it is infallible” does not establish infallibility independently.

Circular claims may describe a tradition's self-understanding but cannot alone establish their own truth.

# Article XI — Scripture Evaluation

Every scriptural corpus may be examined using textual criticism, manuscript evidence, linguistic analysis, archaeology, source criticism, literary criticism, form criticism, redaction criticism, oral-tradition studies, comparative literature, historical context, reception history, and other relevant methods.

No method is itself beyond criticism.

Competing scholarly models shall be recorded when materially significant.

Where authorship, dating, redaction, or original wording is disputed, doctrine shall not pretend that scholarly uncertainty does not exist.

# Article XII — Founder versus Tradition

The teachings of a founder or revelatory figure shall not automatically be equated with every doctrine later attributed to that figure.

The hierarchy of historical reconstruction shall ordinarily be:

    earliest recoverable encounter
        ↓
    earliest recoverable teaching
        ↓
    earliest followers
        ↓
    early textual witness
        ↓
    later interpretation
        ↓
    systematic doctrine
        ↓
    institutional formulation

Later material may contain genuine insight.

Earlier material nevertheless receives priority when the question is what the founder most probably taught.

# Article XIII — Multiple Attestation and Independence

Agreement among multiple sources increases evidentiary significance only to the extent that those sources are genuinely independent.

Five descendants of one earlier tradition do not constitute five independent witnesses.

Common ancestry, copying, shared oral tradition, and cultural contact must be investigated.

Apparent cross-cultural convergence shall be classified as inherited, contact-entangled, relatively independent, structurally analogous, or presently unresolved.

# Article XIV — Contradiction Rule

When two authoritative traditions make mutually incompatible claims, the contradiction shall be acknowledged explicitly.

Permissible responses include:
1. one proposition may be false;
2. both may contain partial truth;
3. one may be metaphorical;
4. they may concern different levels of analysis;
5. one may represent later development;
6. human interpretation or transmission may have introduced error;
7. the traditions may derive from different sources;
8. judgment may remain suspended.

The following response is prohibited: pretending that two contradictory propositions are identical merely because reconciliation is religiously desirable.

# Article XV — Progressive Revelation

Progressive revelation shall remain an admissible hypothesis, not a presupposition.

Differences in ritual, law, political organization, dietary requirements, communal practice, and culturally contingent regulation may plausibly reflect different historical circumstances.

Direct contradictions concerning ontology require more explanation.

Progressive revelation shall not function as a universal escape clause.

# Article XVI — Religious Experience

A religious or mystical experience may provide evidence to the experiencer.

Its evidentiary force for other people is ordinarily weaker.

The following questions must be separated:

- Did the experience occur?
- What was its phenomenology?
- What caused it?
- Was the interpretation correct?
- Does it yield knowledge?
- Does it create authority over others?

A strong answer to the first question does not automatically answer the remaining questions.

# Article XVII — Miracles

Miracle claims are admissible.

They shall be evaluated according to ordinary principles of evidence plus the extraordinary nature of the causal inference.

Relevant questions include what is claimed to have happened, whether independently attested, source proximity, ordinary explanations, development of reports over time, and whether the supernatural cause uniquely explains the event.

“Unexplained” shall never automatically mean “therefore divine.”

Nor shall “unusual” mean “therefore impossible.”

# Article XVIII — Prophecy and Prediction

A predictive prophecy shall be evaluated using a frozen pre-fulfillment form wherever possible.

The analysis must establish:
1. that the prediction predates the event;
2. earliest wording;
3. original contextual meaning;
4. specificity;
5. whether the claimant knew it;
6. which correspondences were under claimant control;
7. mismatches as well as matches;
8. competing plausible claimants;
9. retrospective reinterpretations;
10. counterfactual uniqueness.

Prophecy shall not be judged by counting vague retrospective similarities.

# Article XIX — Science and Empirical Knowledge

Reliable empirical investigation has legitimate authority regarding empirically testable questions.

Religious doctrine shall not receive automatic exemption from evidence where it makes ordinary empirical claims.

A theological interpretation that contradicts overwhelmingly supported empirical evidence carries the burden of reconsideration.

Science does not thereby decide every metaphysical, ethical, theological, or existential question.

Domains must not be confused.

# Article XX — Reason and Logic

A contradiction does not become coherent by being called a mystery.

There may be truths humans cannot fully comprehend.

But “beyond complete understanding” is not equivalent to “formally contradictory.”

Where paradox is claimed, the precise propositions involved shall be stated before appeal is made to mystery.

# Article XXI — Conscience

Conscience is morally significant but fallible.

A person's powerful feeling that something is right, commanded, destined, or divinely approved may constitute evidence about that person's psychological or spiritual experience.

It is not conclusive evidence that the belief is objectively correct.

Conscience itself may require formation, criticism, and correction.

# Article XXII — Ethical Defeaters

A claimed revelation that commands cruelty, exploitation, domination, deception, or arbitrary harm creates a major defeater against its claimed divine authority.

Claims shall be tested against unnecessary suffering, coercion, exploitation, dishonesty, domination, dehumanization, arbitrary discrimination, and corruption of accountability.

No claimant receives exemption because they claim divine authorization.

# Article XXIII — Disagreement and Epistemic Humility

Serious disagreement among informed, sincere, competent investigators is evidence that a question may be more difficult than one party perceives.

Persistent high-quality disagreement shall ordinarily reduce unwarranted certainty.

It does not require permanent skepticism.

Uncertainty honestly acknowledged is epistemically superior to certainty falsely manufactured.

# Article XXIV — Authority

Religious authority shall be derivative rather than self-grounding.

Authority may arise from demonstrated knowledge, trusted character, relevant expertise, historical proximity, community recognition, sound reasoning, spiritual wisdom, faithful stewardship, or institutional responsibility.

None produces infallibility.

Office does not transform opinion into truth.

# Article XXV — No Living Infallible Interpreter

No living individual shall possess constitutional authority to declare their own interpretation beyond examination.

This applies regardless of title.

A person may be highly trusted without becoming epistemically absolute.

# Article XXVI — Canonical Authority

If a canon is eventually constituted, canonical status shall not mean every sentence has identical provenance, genre, historical reliability, interpretive clarity, or theological weight.

Canon may instead mean texts received by the community as foundational witnesses to its search for, encounter with, and understanding of transcendent truth.

Internal differentiation may remain permissible.

# Article XXVII — Authority Layers

Provisional hierarchy:

- Layer A — Foundational Witness.
- Layer B — Early Witness.
- Layer C — Received Wisdom.
- Layer D — Interpretive Tradition.
- Layer E — Exploratory Wisdom.
- Layer U — Unresolved.

Placement is revisable.

Age alone does not determine layer.

# Article XXVIII — Doctrine Formation

No major binding doctrine should ordinarily be established from one ambiguous passage, one private revelation, one late witness, one mistranslation, one unexplained experience, one disputed miracle, or one charismatic teacher's interpretation.

The stronger the proposed obligation, the stronger and broader the required evidence.

# Article XXIX — Status of Unresolved Questions

The category Unresolved is constitutionally legitimate.

The community shall not manufacture doctrine merely because members desire an answer.

Questions may remain unresolved across generations.

Permitted formulations include:
- We do not know.
- The evidence presently permits several interpretations.
- The tradition has not reached a warranted conclusion.
- This proposition is permitted but not binding.
- Further evidence could alter the present judgment.

# Article XXX — Revision

Every historical, scientific, interpretive, and secondary doctrinal conclusion remains corrigible.

Legitimate grounds include newly discovered evidence, improved textual reconstruction, stronger historical argument, conceptual contradiction, empirical discovery, demonstrated ethical failure, better explanatory models, or correction of earlier methodological error.

# Article XXXI — Asymmetry Between Personal Belief and Binding Doctrine

Individuals may hold beliefs beyond what the community can responsibly establish as doctrine.

    PERMITTED PERSONAL BELIEF
        may rest partly on personal experience or faith

    COMMUNAL TEACHING
        requires stronger shared justification

    BINDING DOCTRINE
        requires still stronger justification

    IRREVERSIBLE OR COERCIVE ACTION
        requires the strongest independent justification

This protects both spiritual freedom and communal integrity.

# Article XXXII — Epistemic Freedom

Members shall remain free to ask questions, doubt, investigate, compare traditions, examine historical evidence, study critical scholarship, disagree with interpretations, propose revisions, and suspend judgment.

Good-faith questioning shall not constitute apostasy, disloyalty, or moral failure merely because it challenges inherited belief.

Truth has nothing to fear from honest investigation.

# Article XXXIII — Foundational Maxim

Seek what is true. Preserve what is well grounded. Recover what is original where possible. Test what is inherited. Distinguish encounter from interpretation. Distinguish testimony from proof. Accept insight wherever found. Reject no claim merely because it is foreign. Accept no claim merely because it is sacred. Proportion confidence to evidence. Permit uncertainty where knowledge is unavailable. Remain open to correction.

# Article XXXIV — Provisional Epistemic Hierarchy

When claims conflict, the following order shall guide investigation as a constitutional presumption:

    REALITY / TRUTH
          ↓
    strong reproducible or independently corroborated evidence
          ↓
    sound reason and logical coherence
          ↓
    well-established historical evidence
          ↓
    earliest recoverable revelatory/prophetic witness
          ↓
    multiple independent convergent witnesses
          ↓
    credible individual testimony
          ↓
    received scriptural interpretation
          ↓
    later tradition and theology
          ↓
    institutional opinion
          ↓
    individual speculation

No human level is infallible.

Reality remains above the hierarchy.

# Article XXXV — Constitutional Position on Revelation

This tradition shall neither affirm that revelation has ceased and no further divine communication is possible nor presume that every person claiming revelation has received it.

Its constitutional position shall be:

Further revelation, prophecy, illumination, or extraordinary spiritual experience remains possible in principle. Every such claim begins as testimony and is subject to disciplined discernment. No contemporary revelation becomes self-authenticating merely by being experienced with certainty or proclaimed with authority.

# Article XXXVI — Constitutional Position on Existing Religions

No existing religion shall initially be classified as wholly true or wholly false.

Each shall be decomposed into constituent claims, sources, historical strata, practices, interpretations, and experiences.

A tradition may simultaneously preserve historical truth, revelatory insight, profound ethical wisdom, later doctrinal development, cultural assumptions, institutional innovation, legendary material, and error.

The proper unit of investigation is ultimately the claim and its evidence, not merely the religious label attached to it.

# Closing Principle

The purpose of this epistemology is not to reduce religion to perpetual skepticism.

Its purpose is to make serious faith possible without requiring intellectual surrender.

If transcendence is real, disciplined investigation cannot threaten it.

If a doctrine is true, examination can refine our understanding of it.

If a doctrine is false, loyalty to truth requires correction.

Where evidence does not permit a responsible conclusion, reverent uncertainty is preferable to manufactured certainty.
