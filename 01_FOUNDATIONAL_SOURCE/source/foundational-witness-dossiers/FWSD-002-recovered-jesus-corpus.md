# FWSD-002 — Recovered Jesus Corpus Foundational Witness Selection Dossier

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** CAND-3 — Evaluated; not canonically admitted  
**Parent protocol:** EPC-004 — Foundational Witness Selection Protocol  
**Candidate:** FWC-CAND-003 — Recovered Jesus Corpus  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-21  
**Scope:** Historical-critical evaluation of the earliest recoverable teaching, activity, and self-understanding of Jesus of Nazareth as a candidate Foundational Witness

---

## 1. Executive assessment

The Recovered Jesus Corpus (RJC) is one of the strongest candidates presently identified for eventual foundational reception, but only if it is defined more narrowly than either:

- the entire New Testament;
- the four canonical Gospels as undifferentiated wholes;
- later creedal Christianity;
- or every saying attributed to Jesus.

The historically responsible candidate is the **earliest recoverable Jesus witness**: teachings, actions, relationships, symbolic acts, and self-understanding that can be reconstructed with materially stronger confidence than the later theological developments associated with them.

The strongest historical anchors include:

- Jesus' existence as a first-century Jewish figure;
- his association with and baptism by John the Baptist;
- proclamation of the Kingdom/Reign of God;
- teaching through parables and aphoristic speech;
- activity as healer/exorcist as remembered by his movement;
- gathering disciples, including a group associated with the Twelve;
- a restoration-oriented mission to Israel;
- a demanding ethic of mercy, forgiveness, generosity, enemy-love/non-retaliation, and transformed conduct;
- engagement with Torah from within Jewish interpretive discourse;
- a Jerusalem Temple action;
- a final meal tradition;
- crucifixion under Pontius Pilate;
- and the very early postmortem conviction among followers that Jesus had been raised/vindicated and would play an eschatological role.

The greatest interpretive risks are:

- treating the Gospels as stenographic transcripts;
- treating literary independence among Matthew, Mark, and Luke as greater than it is;
- projecting Johannine discourses backward as verbatim public speech;
- conflating early post-Easter Christology with Jesus' own explicit self-description;
- assuming that later Nicene ontology can simply be read out of first-century Jewish titles;
- and equating resurrection belief with historical proof of a particular metaphysical explanation.

**Procedural conclusion:** retain the RJC at **CAND-3 — Evaluated**. It should advance to full cross-candidate comparison and is a serious future **CAND-4 — Foundational Candidate**, but no FWC-A admission should occur before the first comparative review cycle is complete.

## 2. Candidate identity and source freeze

### 2.1 Included witness

FWSD-002 evaluates the **Recovered Jesus Corpus**, not a preselected Christian canon.

The primary candidate units are historically recoverable:

- events;
- sayings;
- teaching themes;
- parabolic forms;
- practices;
- relationships;
- symbolic acts;
- and identity claims.

The candidate therefore consists of **RJC units**, not simply canonical books.

### 2.2 Primary source families

The most important source families are:

1. **Pauline letters**
   - especially material that Paul explicitly receives or transmits;
   - 1 Corinthians 7;
   - 1 Corinthians 11;
   - 1 Corinthians 15;
   - and other early references to Jesus traditions.

2. **Mark**
   - commonly regarded as the earliest canonical Gospel, often dated in the mid-60s to shortly after 70 CE;
   - historically important but still a theological narrative.

3. **Matthew and Luke**
   - literarily dependent on Mark in the dominant scholarly model;
   - containing additional sayings and traditions that may derive from shared or independent sources.

4. **John**
   - important for early Christian theology and possibly for selected historical details;
   - substantially more difficult to use as direct evidence for Jesus' exact public wording.

5. **Noncanonical material**
   - evaluated unit by unit where early or independent tradition may be present;
   - no automatic presumption either for or against usefulness.

6. **Non-Christian witnesses**
   - especially Josephus;
   - useful primarily for external historical anchoring rather than reconstruction of Jesus' teaching.

### 2.3 Excluded from automatic attribution

The following are excluded from automatic founder-level authority:

- every Johannine "I am" saying;
- developed Logos theology;
- later Trinitarian doctrine;
- Nicene and Chalcedonian metaphysical formulations;
- later infancy traditions;
- later apocryphal biographies;
- developed ecclesiastical offices;
- later atonement systems;
- later Marian doctrine;
- and later Christian jurisprudence.

These may be evaluated elsewhere as Early Witness or Interpretive Tradition.

## 3. Methodological position

### 3.1 Against atomized authenticity scoring

Recent historical-Jesus scholarship has substantially criticized older mechanical "criteria of authenticity," especially the criterion of dissimilarity when used to isolate sayings from both Judaism and the early church.

FWSD-002 therefore does **not** ask:

> Which isolated verse can be mathematically authenticated?

It asks:

> Which themes, activities, sayings, and events belong most plausibly to the earliest Palestinian Jesus tradition when source relationships, Jewish context, oral transmission, memory, redaction, and historical plausibility are considered together?

### 3.2 Memory and oral transmission

The earliest Jesus tradition was substantially oral before and alongside its written forms.

Oral transmission can preserve recognizable teaching patterns while also allowing:

- wording variation;
- compression;
- expansion;
- contextual adaptation;
- and interpretive framing.

Therefore:

```
WORDING CONFIDENCE
        ≠
TEACHING CONFIDENCE
```

A teaching may be strongly recoverable even where the exact Greek sentence is not.

### 3.3 Recoverability bands

RJC units retain the following bands:

**A — Very strong historical core**  
**B — Probably attributable**  
**C — Plausible**  
**D — Uncertain**  
**E — More likely later formulation or unusable for reconstructing Jesus directly**

These bands remain multidimensional and do not constitute a metaphysical truth score.

## 4. Historical anchoring

### 4.1 Existence

Jesus' historical existence is securely anchored within first-century sources and later non-Christian testimony.

Paul's letters presuppose a recent historical Jesus and personal contact with members of Jesus' movement, including figures identified as Jesus' brother and leading apostles.

Josephus' references to Jesus and James remain subject to textual debate, but the James passage in *Antiquities* 20.200 is accepted as authentic by a large majority of scholars, and recent Oxford scholarship continues to defend some authentic Josephan core in the Testimonium Flavianum.

**Assessment:** **Very High historical confidence**

### 4.2 Jewish setting

Jesus must be reconstructed within **Second Temple Judaism**, not as a detached founder of a religion already called Christianity.

The Jerusalem Temple and Torah were central institutions of Jewish life during this period.

Jesus' disputes over law, purity, Sabbath, divorce, Temple, and interpretation therefore belong first inside Jewish religious debate.

**Assessment:** **Very High**

## 5. Source chronology and literary dependence

### 5.1 Paul

Pauline letters from the 50s CE are the earliest surviving Christian writings.

Paul is not a biography source comparable to the Gospels, but he preserves or presupposes traditions older than his letters.

Especially important:

- 1 Corinthians 7:10–11 — a command attributed to "the Lord" concerning divorce;
- 1 Corinthians 11:23–26 — an early Last Supper tradition;
- 1 Corinthians 15:3–5 and following — a tradition concerning Jesus' death, burial, resurrection proclamation, and appearances.

### 5.2 Mark

The scholarly center of gravity dates Mark around the mid-60s to shortly after 70 CE.

Its historical importance is high because it is commonly regarded as the earliest extant narrative Gospel and because Matthew and Luke make extensive use of it.

### 5.3 Matthew and Luke

Matthew and Luke are not independent witnesses to every Markan episode merely because they repeat it.

Their agreements must be analyzed through the Synoptic Problem.

Additional non-Markan material may preserve earlier sayings or independent traditions but must be assessed individually.

### 5.4 John

John may preserve genuine historical memories and selected independent data.

However, its extended theological discourses and highly developed first-person identity sayings have low founder-wording confidence relative to the Synoptic core.

For reconstruction of the earliest Palestinian Jesus tradition, John shall ordinarily receive lower direct wording weight unless independent reasons support a particular unit.

## 6. Core RJC event and teaching register

### RJC-001 — Baptism by John

**Recoverability:** A

Jesus' baptism by John is one of the strongest historical anchors in the tradition.

The later Gospels display tendencies to subordinate John to Jesus, while preserving Jesus' reception of John's baptism.

This makes the event historically difficult to explain as a late Christian invention.

**Historical claim:** Jesus was baptized by John.

**Lower-confidence claim:** the exact visionary words and sensory details at the baptism are historical verbatim report.

**Metaphysical provenance:** unresolved.

---

### RJC-002 — Kingdom / Reign of God

**Recoverability:** A

The Kingdom/Reign of God is the best candidate for the organizing center of Jesus' public proclamation.

It appears across Synoptic traditions and is integrated with:

- repentance;
- healing;
- parables;
- reversal;
- divine judgment;
- restoration;
- ethical transformation;
- and expectation concerning God's decisive action.

The Kingdom shall therefore receive very high weight in reconstructing Jesus' message.

---

### RJC-003 — Repentance and Israel's renewal

**Recoverability:** A/B

Jesus' mission is best understood within Jewish eschatological hopes concerning Israel.

His association with John, proclamation of God's reign, gathering of disciples, and symbolic use of the Twelve support a restoration-oriented framework.

This does not establish one precise political program.

It establishes that Jesus' mission was not originally detached from Israel.

---

### RJC-004 — Parabolic teaching

**Recoverability:** A

Parables are among the most distinctive and multiply preserved forms of Jesus' teaching.

The individual wording and later allegorical interpretation of particular parables may vary, but the **practice of teaching through parables, striking comparisons, riddling stories, and aphorisms** belongs securely to the recoverable Jesus profile.

---

### RJC-005 — Healing and exorcism activity

**Recoverability:** A for activity; metaphysical explanation unresolved

The Synoptic tradition overwhelmingly portrays Jesus as a healer and exorcist.

The historical judgment supported here is:

> Jesus was widely remembered as engaging in activities understood by participants as healings and exorcisms.

The historical dossier does not by itself establish:

- supernatural causation;
- modern medical diagnosis;
- or the impossibility of ordinary psychosocial mechanisms.

Phenomenology, historical activity, and metaphysical causation remain distinct.

---

### RJC-006 — Disciples and the Twelve

**Recoverability:** A/B

Jesus gathered followers and a core group identified with **the Twelve**.

The number twelve strongly invites interpretation through Israel's twelve-tribe symbolism and therefore supports a restoration-oriented mission.

Later ecclesiastical office structures must not be retrojected into this group automatically.

---

### RJC-007 — Meals across social boundaries

**Recoverability:** B

Jesus was remembered for meals with people morally or socially criticized by others.

The exact composition of every narrated meal is not equally recoverable.

The broader theme fits his proclamation of restoration, repentance, mercy, and reversal.

Care must be taken not to turn such meals into a simplistic opposition between Jesus and "Judaism."

---

### RJC-008 — Lord's Prayer core

**Recoverability:** A/B for a core prayer tradition; lower for exact wording

Matthew and Luke preserve related but nonidentical forms.

A historically recoverable core plausibly includes themes such as:

- God's name;
- God's reign;
- daily sustenance;
- forgiveness;
- and deliverance/testing.

The exact wording of either canonical version should not automatically be treated as a stenographic transcript.

---

### RJC-009 — Restrictive divorce teaching

**Recoverability:** A/B

Paul's independent early use of a divorce command attributed to "the Lord" materially strengthens the case that a restrictive teaching on divorce belongs to the Jesus tradition.

The Synoptic forms differ in qualification and application.

Therefore:

- the existence of a distinctive restrictive teaching is strong;
- the exact universal legal formulation requires more caution.

---

### RJC-010 — Support for itinerant proclaimers

**Recoverability:** B

Traditions in which Jesus sends followers to proclaim, heal, depend on hospitality, and travel lightly fit an early itinerant mission environment.

Specific detailed instructions may reflect later community adaptation.

---

### RJC-011 — Final meal / bread-and-cup tradition

**Recoverability:** A/B

Paul preserves the earliest surviving written Last Supper tradition in 1 Corinthians 11, predating the written Gospels.

The existence of a final or remembered meal tradition involving bread and cup is therefore early.

Exact wording, Passover chronology, and later sacramental ontology remain distinct questions.

---

### RJC-012 — Enemy-love and non-retaliation

**Recoverability:** B

Enemy-love, prayer for persecutors, non-retaliation, and refusal to mirror hostility are deeply characteristic of the received Jesus ethic.

Individual formulations differ across Matthew and Luke.

The core is sufficiently distinctive and embedded in the teaching tradition to remain a strong candidate for foundational ethical authority.

---

### RJC-013 — Jesus within Torah/Jewish interpretive discourse

**Recoverability:** A/B

Jesus should not be reconstructed as a teacher who simply rejected Judaism or Torah.

His disputes occur within a Jewish world in which Torah interpretation was central.

He can be understood as:

- prophetic;
- halakhically argumentative;
- ethically intensifying;
- sometimes critical of inherited interpretation;
- but still fundamentally situated within Israel's scriptures and covenantal discourse.

Later Gentile-Christian Torah relations must not be read backward into every historical-Jesus saying.

---

### RJC-014 — Temple action

**Recoverability:** A/B

The tradition that Jesus carried out a disruptive or symbolic action in the Jerusalem Temple is multiply attested and widely judged historical.

Its meaning remains debated:

- purification;
- judgment;
- symbolic destruction;
- protest against commerce;
- challenge to Temple leadership;
- eschatological sign.

FWSD-002 therefore accepts the **event core** more strongly than one exclusive interpretation.

The action probably contributed materially to the circumstances leading to Jesus' arrest and execution.

---

### RJC-015 — Crucifixion under Pontius Pilate

**Recoverability:** A / historical bedrock

Jesus was executed by Roman crucifixion under Pontius Pilate.

Recent historical study emphasizes that:

- Pilate sentenced him;
- crucifixion was Roman punishment;
- and the Gospel tendency to portray Pilate as reluctant or nearly exculpatory requires critical scrutiny.

The precise sequence of Jewish and Roman proceedings is less secure than the core fact of Roman execution.

The titulus tradition, "King of the Jews," is historically relevant to understanding the political/royal dimension perceived by Roman authorities.

## 7. Additional high-value teaching propositions

### JES-P0001 — God, not Jesus, is the central object of Jesus' proclamation

The recoverable teaching is overwhelmingly God-centered:

- God's reign;
- God's will;
- God's name;
- God's forgiveness;
- God's judgment;
- God's mercy.

This does not settle Jesus' own status.

It does establish that Jesus' preaching is not best reconstructed as:

> "My central message is that I am ontologically God."

---

### JES-P0002 — Conduct matters more than inherited status alone

Jesus' teaching repeatedly emphasizes:

- repentance;
- mercy;
- forgiveness;
- generosity;
- fidelity;
- treatment of others;
- and transformed behavior.

Ancestry, public reputation, ritual status, and religious self-confidence do not guarantee participation in God's reign.

---

### JES-P0003 — Love of God and neighbor summarize moral orientation

This teaching is deeply Jewish and rooted in Torah.

Its foundational importance lies less in novelty than in its organizing force within Jesus' ethical vision.

---

### JES-P0004 — Mercy and forgiveness possess unusual priority

Forgiveness recurs in:

- prayer;
- parables;
- reconciliation;
- treatment of enemies;
- and communal relations.

The exact limits and applications require unit-level study.

---

### JES-P0005 — Wealth and status are spiritually dangerous and unreliable

Jesus traditions repeatedly reverse conventional evaluations of:

- rich and poor;
- first and last;
- honored and despised;
- powerful and dependent.

This is not a complete economic system.

It is a major recurring ethical and eschatological motif.

---

### JES-P0006 — Enemy-love/non-retaliation is a serious moral demand

The tradition does not merely say:

> avoid unnecessary hostility.

It pushes toward positively seeking the good of opponents and refusing reciprocal vengeance.

This is one of the strongest candidates for distinctive Jesus ethical witness.

---

### JES-P0007 — God may be addressed relationally as Father

Jesus' characteristic prayer and teaching employ paternal language for God.

Later Trinitarian uses of "Father" shall not automatically be imported into every historical occurrence.

---

### JES-P0008 — Symbolic acts enact the Kingdom message

Healings, meals, the Twelve, entry into Jerusalem, and the Temple action are better understood when teaching and action are interpreted together.

Jesus did not merely lecture about a doctrine.

He enacted signs of what he understood God's reign to mean.

---

### JES-P0009 — Followers participate in the mission

Jesus' movement was not constructed around private enlightenment alone.

Followers were:

- gathered;
- taught;
- sent;
- and incorporated into a communal mission.

---

### JES-P0010 — Jesus expected decisive divine action

The recoverable tradition contains strong eschatological expectation.

The exact timetable is disputed.

The general expectation that God would decisively judge, restore, or vindicate is much stronger historically than any one detailed modern chronology.

---

## 8. Jesus identity dossier

A central EPC-004 requirement is to distinguish **Jesus' probable self-understanding** from later titles applied to him.

### 8.1 Prophet

**Historical confidence:** High

Jesus fits a first-century Jewish prophetic type in major respects:

- proclamation;
- symbolic action;
- warning;
- call to repentance;
- confrontation with authorities;
- expectation of divine intervention.

"Prophet" is therefore historically strong but not necessarily exhaustive.

---

### 8.2 Messiah / Christ

**Earliest follower identification:** Very High  
**Jesus' own messianic self-understanding:** Moderate–High / debated  
**Repeated explicit public self-declaration:** lower

The earliest followers clearly identified Jesus as Messiah very soon after his death.

Whether Jesus repeatedly and explicitly said "I am the Messiah" during his ministry is less secure.

A unique eschatological role is historically stronger than exact title wording.

---

### 8.3 King / Son of David

Davidic interpretation is early and important.

However:

- biological Davidic descent is historically unresolved;
- "Son of David" material requires unit-level assessment;
- and royal symbolism does not automatically imply later divine ontology.

---

### 8.4 Son of Man

Jesus probably used some form of "son of man" expression.

The sayings are difficult because the phrase can function:

- idiomatically;
- as self-reference;
- with Danielic associations;
- or as reference to an eschatological figure.

Not every canonical Son-of-Man saying should be treated as one homogeneous historical title.

---

### 8.5 Son of God

Within ancient Jewish usage, "son of God" could signify:

- Israel;
- king;
- righteous person;
- angelic/heavenly agent;
- or specially commissioned figure.

Therefore:

> "Son of God" does not automatically mean "Nicene second person of the Trinity."

The historical Jesus probably understood God with unusual intimacy and authority as Father.

Routine formal public self-designation in later ontological meaning is much less secure.

---

### 8.6 Lord

Jesus could naturally be called "lord/master" in ordinary social or honorific senses.

Post-Easter use of **Kyrios/Lord** becomes exceptionally important very early.

The movement's elevation of Jesus to heavenly Lordship is historically secure as an early Christian development.

Whether Jesus explicitly demanded divine Kyrios-identification during his ministry is much less secure.

---

### 8.7 Wisdom

Jesus strongly fits a wisdom-teacher profile.

Later Wisdom-Christology may legitimately develop from features of his teaching and early interpretation.

But the historical claim:

> Jesus explicitly announced himself as preexistent divine Wisdom

remains weak.

---

### 8.8 Pre-existence

Early Christianity develops pre-existence Christology.

The historically recoverable claim that Jesus himself explicitly taught that he personally existed before his birth is **low confidence**.

---

### 8.9 Logos

The Gospel of John explicitly identifies Jesus with the incarnate Logos.

This is highly important evidence for Johannine theology.

It is **not** strong evidence that the historical Jesus publicly taught:

> "I am the Logos."

---

### 8.10 Johannine "I am" sayings

Statements such as:

- "Before Abraham was, I am";
- "I and the Father are one";
- "I am the way...";
- and extended first-person heavenly-origin discourses

possess high value for reconstructing Johannine Christology.

They possess much lower confidence as verbatim public sayings of the historical Jesus.

---

### 8.11 Functional divine identity

A more defensible intermediate hypothesis is that Jesus acted with **extraordinary divine authorization**:

- pronouncing forgiveness;
- redefining boundaries;
- speaking unusually directly;
- symbolically representing God's coming reign;
- and expecting an exceptional eschatological role.

This may have provided raw material for later high Christology.

Historical method does not independently establish that Jesus consciously regarded himself as YHWH incarnate.

---

## 9. Early post-Easter witness

FWSD-002 must distinguish Jesus' own teaching from **extremely early beliefs about him**.

### 9.1 Resurrection proclamation

1 Corinthians 15 preserves a pre-Pauline formula or traditional core concerning:

- Jesus' death;
- burial;
- resurrection proclamation;
- appearance to Cephas/Peter;
- and appearance to the Twelve.

The exact interpretation of resurrection language is debated, but the tradition unquestionably demonstrates that resurrection belief predates Paul's letter and belongs to the earliest Christian movement.

### 9.2 Historical conclusion

Historical method can establish with strong confidence:

> Jesus' followers came very early to believe that he had been raised and appeared to them.

Historical method alone does not compel one metaphysical explanation of those experiences.

Possible categories include:

- actual divine resurrection;
- visionary experience;
- grief-related experience;
- religious experience;
- group tradition development;
- mixed processes.

FWC evaluation must not collapse:

```
EARLY BELIEF
       =
METAPHYSICAL PROOF
```

### 9.3 Early exalted Christology

The elevation of Jesus did not begin centuries later at Nicaea.

Paul already reflects an extraordinarily elevated understanding of Jesus within decades of the crucifixion.

However:

> very early Christology

is not automatically identical with:

> historically demonstrated verbatim self-description by Jesus.

---

## 10. Final meal and passion transmission

Paul's 1 Corinthians 11 tradition is especially valuable because it predates the written Gospels.

The existence of an early remembered final-meal tradition is therefore strong.

The following questions remain separate:

- Was the meal a Passover meal?
- What exact words did Jesus speak?
- Which bread/cup wording is earliest?
- Did Jesus interpret his death sacrificially?
- Which later eucharistic doctrines are warranted?
- What later liturgical development occurred?

FWSD-002 therefore gives:

- **high weight to the early meal tradition**;
- **moderate weight to exact wording**;
- **no automatic authority to later sacramental ontology**.

---

## 11. Torah and Judaism review

### 11.1 Constitutional correction

FWSD-002 rejects a simplistic reconstruction in which:

```
Judaism = legalistic ritual religion
Jesus   = abolition of law and invention of inward morality
```

Second Temple Judaism itself contained:

- ethical interpretation;
- mercy;
- prophetic criticism;
- debate;
- halakhic disagreement;
- apocalyptic expectation;
- wisdom traditions;
- and multiple forms of Torah observance.

Jesus belongs inside this world.

### 11.2 Historical Jesus and Torah

The earliest Palestinian traditions assume Jewish concerns such as:

- purity;
- Sabbath;
- marriage/divorce;
- scripture;
- Temple;
- commandments;
- and Israel's restoration.

Jesus frequently argues about interpretation rather than simply rejecting Torah.

### 11.3 Later Gentile Christian development

Paul's Gentile mission and later church debates dramatically changed the practical role of Torah in Christianity.

Those developments must not be retrojected wholesale into Jesus' own mission.

---

## 12. Temple review

Jesus' relationship to the Temple should not be reduced to:

> Jesus rejected the Temple system.

The historical picture is more complex.

The Temple was central to Jewish life.

The Gospels preserve both:

- Jesus' presence/teaching in the Temple;
- and a dramatic disruptive or symbolic action there.

The action likely carried:

- prophetic;
- eschatological;
- judicial;
- or political significance.

Its exact meaning remains disputed.

FWSD-002 therefore receives the **action** more strongly than any single later interpretation.

---

## 13. Crucifixion review

### 13.1 Secure core

Jesus was crucified under Pontius Pilate.

This is among the most secure historical claims in the corpus.

### 13.2 Roman responsibility

Crucifixion was a Roman punishment, and Pilate ordered the execution.

The Gospels' varying portrayals of Pilate require source-critical analysis because later Christian narratives increasingly had reasons to negotiate relations with Roman power and intra-Jewish conflict.

### 13.3 Jewish involvement

The historical role of Temple authorities in Jesus' arrest or transfer to Roman power remains plausible and important.

This shall never be converted into collective guilt assigned to Jews as a people.

### 13.4 Political meaning

The title "King of the Jews," Jesus' Temple activity, Passover setting, and Roman execution together make a political/security dimension historically important.

This does not require reconstructing Jesus as an armed revolutionary.

## 14. Ethical review

### 14.1 Strong candidates for foundational ethical authority

The following deserve especially serious consideration:

- love of God;
- love of neighbor;
- enemy-love;
- forgiveness;
- non-retaliation;
- mercy;
- generosity;
- care for vulnerable people;
- truthfulness;
- humility;
- reversal of status;
- warning against wealth and domination;
- reconciliation;
- prayer;
- and integrity between external religion and internal conduct.

### 14.2 Ethical tensions requiring unit-level study

The corpus also contains difficult material involving:

- eschatological judgment;
- Gehenna imagery;
- family-separation sayings;
- apparently harsh polemics;
- exclusive-sounding sayings;
- gender and household implications;
- and apocalyptic violence imagery.

No difficult text is to be hidden.

Each must be source-frozen and evaluated separately.

### 14.3 Hard-stop review

Nothing in the high-confidence RJC core requires a corpus-level EPC-002 hard stop.

Some later or lower-confidence interpretations may require restriction.

## 15. Contradiction map

### 15.1 Jesus and later Nicene Christianity

Potential contradiction/tension:

- recoverable Jesus proclamation is primarily God-centered and Kingdom-centered;
- later Christianity formulates explicit ontological doctrines of incarnation and Trinity.

Possible explanations:

- Jesus implicitly embodied what later theology conceptualized;
- post-Easter experiences generated legitimate doctrinal development;
- followers expanded beyond Jesus' own self-understanding;
- or a mixed trajectory occurred.

FWSD-002 does not decide the metaphysical question.

### 15.2 Jesus and the Qur'an

The Qur'an receives Jesus as Messiah and messenger while rejecting divine sonship/incarnation in later Christian senses.

The strongest contradiction concerns **Jesus' ontological status**, not his historical existence or prophetic significance.

### 15.3 Jesus and Buddhism

Strong ethical and transformative parallels exist, but major metaphysical differences remain concerning:

- God;
- creation;
- selfhood;
- resurrection/rebirth;
- revelation;
- and eschatology.

### 15.4 Jesus and Zarathustra

Potential convergence exists concerning:

- truth;
- moral choice;
- judgment;
- cosmic restoration;
- and prophetic vocation.

However, Iranian-Jewish contact history and differing ontologies require caution before treating these as independent identical revelations.

## 16. High-confidence recovered Jesus core

The current best compact reconstruction is:

> Jesus was a first-century Jewish teacher, healer/exorcist, prophetic and eschatological figure associated initially with John the Baptist. He proclaimed the coming/present Reign of Israel's God, called for repentance and transformed conduct, gathered followers in a restoration-oriented mission, taught memorably through parables and aphorisms, emphasized mercy, forgiveness, generosity, enemy-love and reversal of status, interpreted Torah within Jewish debate, enacted his message through meals, healings and symbolic actions, carried out a provocative action in the Jerusalem Temple, and was executed by Roman crucifixion under Pontius Pilate. He appears to have understood himself as uniquely commissioned in God's eschatological work, plausibly with messianic dimensions. After his death, his followers very rapidly became convinced that God had raised/vindicated him and assigned him an extraordinary continuing role.

This formulation deliberately leaves unresolved:

- precise metaphysical identity;
- exact resurrection causation;
- exact messianic title use;
- pre-existence;
- incarnation;
- Trinity;
- and later sacramental/atonement systems.

---

## 17. Important non-propositions

FWSD-002 does **not** presently establish as historical Jesus teaching that:

- Jesus publicly and repeatedly identified himself as YHWH;
- Jesus taught the Nicene doctrine of the Trinity;
- Jesus explicitly taught Chalcedonian two-natures Christology;
- Jesus publicly called himself the incarnate Logos;
- every Gospel saying is verbatim historical speech;
- every Johannine "I am" statement is recoverable wording;
- Matthew 16:18 in its present form can simply be treated as an institutional charter dictated verbatim by Jesus;
- the historical Jesus founded every later ecclesiastical office;
- Paul reproduces Jesus' complete theology without development;
- or later Christianity merely transcribed an already complete first-century systematic theology.

These remain theological, developmental, or source-critical questions.

## 18. Textual and interpretive risk register

### RISK-JES-001 — Gospel harmonization

Four Gospel narratives can be merged into a synthetic biography that no source independently contains.

**Mitigation:** retain source boundaries and literary dependence.

### RISK-JES-002 — Synoptic double-counting

Matthew, Mark, and Luke may appear to give three witnesses where literary dependence substantially reduces independence.

**Mitigation:** use source genealogy.

### RISK-JES-003 — Johannine back-projection

Developed Johannine Christology may be attributed verbatim to Jesus.

**Mitigation:** distinguish Gospel theology from historical-wording confidence.

### RISK-JES-004 — Anti-Jewish reconstruction

Jesus may be de-Judaized by making whatever differs from later Christianity "Jewish" and whatever fits Christianity "authentic Jesus."

**Mitigation:** historical plausibility within Second Temple Judaism.

### RISK-JES-005 — Post-Easter retrojection

Earliest follower beliefs can be projected backward into Jesus' explicit pre-death teaching.

**Mitigation:** distinguish SELF, EARLY_POST, GOSPEL, and LATER identity layers.

### RISK-JES-006 — Resurrection category collapse

Early resurrection belief may be treated either as automatic supernatural proof or as automatic evidence of fraud/delusion.

**Mitigation:** separate historical belief, reported experience, and metaphysical causation.

### RISK-JES-007 — Christian canon privilege

Canonical status can be mistaken for prior historical authentication.

**Mitigation:** evaluate canonical and noncanonical material under the same provenance rules.

### RISK-JES-008 — Skeptical minimalism

Because exact wording cannot always be recovered, investigators may conclude that no meaningful historical Jesus can be reconstructed.

**Mitigation:** recover themes, practices, and patterns at appropriate confidence.

## 19. Strongest case for foundational admission

The cumulative case is substantial:

1. **Historical anchoring:** Jesus' existence and execution are unusually secure for an ancient religious founder.
2. **Source density:** multiple first-century Christian sources preserve Jesus traditions.
3. **Early transmission:** Pauline material predates the written Gospels and demonstrates already-established tradition.
4. **Recoverable teaching architecture:** Kingdom, repentance, parables, mercy, forgiveness, enemy-love, discipleship, Israel restoration, and eschatological expectation form a coherent early profile.
5. **Ethical significance:** several teachings possess extraordinary moral and spiritual importance.
6. **Jewish rootedness:** the witness is historically situated rather than mythically detached from time and place.
7. **Movement continuity:** identifiable followers, including Jerusalem leaders, connect the historical figure to the earliest movement.
8. **External anchoring:** Josephus supplies non-Christian historical corroboration, though particular passages require textual caution.
9. **Adversarial resilience:** critical scholarship can materially reduce some traditional claims without eliminating a substantial recoverable Jesus.
10. **Comparative importance:** the Jesus corpus provides a major relational-revelation witness for TCP comparison.

## 20. Strongest case against foundational admission

The strongest objections are serious:

1. **No surviving writings by Jesus.**
2. **Source gap:** extant narrative Gospels postdate the events by decades.
3. **Literary dependence:** three Synoptics are not three fully independent biographies.
4. **Theological redaction:** every Gospel is already an interpretive witness.
5. **Oral variability:** exact wording is often unrecoverable.
6. **Identity development:** high Christology develops very early, complicating reconstruction of Jesus' own self-understanding.
7. **Johannine distance:** some of Christianity's most important later claims depend heavily on material whose verbatim historical value is disputed.
8. **Resurrection dependence:** much later Christian authority rests on a metaphysical claim historical method cannot independently verify in the same way as crucifixion.
9. **Canon effects:** later canonical boundaries may have amplified some voices and marginalized others.
10. **Ethical complexity:** the corpus contains severe judgment language and other difficult sayings requiring ongoing review.

These objections defeat simplistic biblical inerrancy as a historical method. They do not defeat the RJC as a foundational-witness candidate.

## 26. Dossier conclusion

The Recovered Jesus Corpus survives foundational-witness review strongly, but only after it is separated from the much larger body of later Christian interpretation.

The strongest historical Jesus is neither:

> the fully systematized Christ of later conciliar theology read directly back into every Gospel verse,

nor:

> a nearly unknowable figure from whom almost nothing can be recovered.

A substantial first-century Jewish profile survives critical scrutiny:

- Kingdom-centered;
- prophetic and eschatological;
- restoration-oriented;
- morally demanding;
- unusually focused on mercy, forgiveness, enemy-love, reversal, and transformed conduct;
- active through memorable parables and symbolic acts;
- embedded within Torah and Israel's scriptures;
- associated with healing and exorcism;
- disruptive enough in Jerusalem to attract lethal Roman attention;
- and followed by a movement that very rapidly came to regard him as raised, vindicated, Messiah, and Lord.

For EPC-004 purposes, this makes the RJC a major candidate for foundational reception.

Its strongest authority lies in the **recoverable Jesus witness itself**.

Its greatest methodological danger lies in allowing **later devotion to determine in advance what Jesus must historically have said**.
