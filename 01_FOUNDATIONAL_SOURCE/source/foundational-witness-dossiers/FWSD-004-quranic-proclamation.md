# FWSD-004 — Qurʾanic Proclamation Foundational Witness Selection Dossier

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** CAND-3 — Evaluated; not canonically admitted  
**Parent protocol:** EPC-004 — Foundational Witness Selection Protocol  
**Candidate:** FWC-CAND-004 — Qurʾanic Proclamation  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-21  
**Scope:** Historical-critical evaluation of the Qurʾanic proclamation associated with Muḥammad's movement, source-frozen before later sīra, ḥadīth, tafsīr, kalām, and fiqh are allowed to define its content

---

## 1. Executive assessment

The Qurʾanic Proclamation is one of the strongest candidates presently identified for eventual foundational reception because its surviving textual tradition is unusually close to the founding movement that produced and transmitted it.

The candidate is **not**:

- the entire later Islamic tradition;
- every report in sīra or ḥadīth;
- later Sunni or Shiʿi theology;
- later jurisprudence;
- medieval tafsīr;
- a single modern printed reading treated as though no variants ever existed;
- or the traditional biography of Muḥammad silently projected into every verse.

The historically responsible candidate is the **Qurʾanic proclamation itself**, evaluated through:

- the canonical consonantal text / rasm;
- the earliest manuscript witnesses;
- early regional codex evidence;
- the qirāʾāt / reading traditions;
- the Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest and other evidence of early textual plurality;
- internal literary and chronological analysis;
- and the Qurʾan's Late Antique Arabian, Jewish, Christian, and broader Near Eastern environment.

The strongest recoverable features include:

- uncompromising orientation toward one sovereign creator God;
- rejection of idolatry and rival divine claims;
- divine creation, providence, mercy, judgment, and power;
- recurring proclamation of resurrection and a final accounting;
- human moral responsibility;
- prayer, remembrance, gratitude, and submission to God;
- repentance and divine forgiveness;
- charity and concern for vulnerable people;
- justice, truthful conduct, promise-keeping, and economic responsibility;
- continuity with Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and earlier prophetic figures;
- Muḥammad's role as messenger, warner, and proclaimer rather than divine figure;
- a community-forming process that develops from early warning proclamation into a regulated religious polity;
- and a self-understanding of the proclamation as revealed communication rather than merely private philosophical reflection.

The principal limitations are equally important:

- the Qurʾan is not a biography of Muḥammad;
- the received sura order is not a chronological transcript of proclamation;
- precise Meccan and Medinan sequencing remains model-dependent beyond broad stages;
- some suras show evidence of growth or later extension;
- the dominant text type was standardized very early, but evidence of pre- or non-ʿUthmānic textual plurality survives;
- the canonical reading traditions represent a later stage than the bare consonantal skeleton;
- the Qurʾan is deeply intertextual with Late Antique Jewish and Christian traditions, so many apparent Abrahamic "convergences" are not independent data points;
- and historical proximity does not by itself establish the Qurʾan's claimed divine provenance.

**Procedural conclusion:** retain the Qurʾanic Proclamation at **CAND-3 — Evaluated**. It should advance to full cross-candidate comparison and is a serious future **CAND-4 — Foundational Candidate**, but no FWC-A admission should occur before the first comparative review cycle is complete.

## 2. Candidate identity and source freeze

### 2.1 Included witness

FWSD-004 evaluates the Qurʾan first as a **founding-era Arabic proclamation corpus** associated with Muḥammad's movement.

The principal candidate is the text represented by the early Qurʾanic manuscript tradition and the received consonantal text, with significant early variants preserved rather than suppressed.

### 2.2 Primary source families

The principal source families are:

1. **The ʿUthmānic consonantal text type**
   - the dominant textual ancestry underlying nearly all known Qurʾanic manuscripts;
   - preserved through geographically dispersed early codices;
   - showing strong evidence of descent from a written archetype.

2. **Early Qurʾanic manuscripts**
   - especially Ḥijāzī and other seventh- and early eighth-century witnesses;
   - used for orthography, text order, wording, and textual history.

3. **Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest lower text**
   - evidence of a materially distinct early textual type;
   - important control against assuming that all early Qurʾanic textual transmission was uniform from the first moment.

4. **Regional codex variants**
   - manuscript and literary evidence for differences associated with early regional copies.

5. **Qirāʾāt / reading traditions**
   - important evidence for how the consonantal text was vocalized, parsed, and transmitted;
   - later canonicalization of readings must be distinguished from the earlier rasm.

6. **Internal chronological/literary strata**
   - broad Meccan and Medinan phases;
   - finer early-, middle-, and late-Meccan models where responsibly supported;
   - possible intra-sural growth and later extensions.

### 2.3 Excluded from automatic founder-level authority

The following are excluded from the source freeze unless independently required for a specific historical question:

- Ibn Isḥāq/Ibn Hishām biography;
- later maghāzī narratives;
- canonical ḥadīth collections;
- later tafsīr;
- Sunni and Shiʿi dogmatic systems;
- later kalām;
- madhhab jurisprudence;
- later Sufi metaphysics;
- medieval prophetology;
- and later claims about detailed occasions of revelation where the Qurʾanic text itself does not establish them.

These materials remain historically important but belong to later evidentiary layers.

## 3. Historical Muḥammad and source proximity

### 3.1 Historicity

The existence of Muḥammad and the broad fact that an Arabian proclamation movement formed around him in the early seventh century are historically very strong.

The Qurʾan itself is the earliest and most important source for that movement.

Later biography adds detail but must not be used circularly to define the Qurʾanic evidence.

### 3.2 What the Qurʾan can securely tell us

The Qurʾan itself supports a historical profile in which the proclaimer:

- receives or presents revelatory communication;
- addresses opponents and followers;
- warns of judgment;
- calls for worship of one God;
- engages earlier scriptural traditions;
- leads a developing community;
- encounters rejection and conflict;
- and participates in a Medinan phase involving communal law, war, alliances, family matters, and political-religious organization.

### 3.3 What the Qurʾan does not securely give us by itself

The Qurʾan does not provide:

- a continuous chronological biography;
- a full account of Muḥammad's childhood;
- a detailed narrative of the cave encounter;
- a complete list of military campaigns;
- a detailed succession history;
- or most of the later biographical chronology familiar from sīra.

**Historical founder existence:** **Very High**  
**Founder-era textual proximity:** **Very High**  
**Detailed biography from Qurʾan alone:** **Low–Moderate**

## 4. Textual history

### 4.1 Early manuscript evidence

Early Qurʾanic manuscripts place substantial portions of the text within the first Islamic century.

Corpus Coranicum's manuscript program combines:

- palaeography;
- codicology;
- radiocarbon dating;
- orthographic analysis;
- and textual comparison.

The accumulated evidence is incompatible with models requiring the Qurʾan to have been created only centuries after Muḥammad.

### 4.2 Written archetype

A major line of recent manuscript research argues that the ʿUthmānic text type descends from a **single written archetype**.

Shared orthographic irregularities occurring in the same places across geographically separated manuscripts are difficult to explain through independent oral transcription alone.

This materially supports a very early codification of the dominant consonantal text.

### 4.3 What this does not prove

An early written archetype does **not** by itself prove:

- that every word in that archetype was written down during Muḥammad's lifetime;
- that no earlier variants existed;
- that later reading traditions were already fixed;
- or that the text's divine-provenance claim is historically demonstrated.

Textual antiquity and theological provenance remain separate questions.

---

## 5. Early textual plurality

### 5.1 Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest

The lower writing of the Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest preserves a Qurʾanic text with variants sufficiently significant to be treated by major scholarship as a text type distinct from the dominant ʿUthmānic tradition.

This means:

> **very early Qurʾanic textual history included some plurality.**

### 5.2 Dominant outcome

The overwhelming majority of surviving early manuscripts belong to the ʿUthmānic text type.

Thus the historical picture is neither:

> complete chaos until a late canon,

nor:

> absolute textual uniformity from the first recitation onward.

A more defensible model is:

```
oral / written proclamation
        ↓
early textual plurality
        ↓
very early standardization of dominant consonantal text
        ↓
regional codex transmission
        ↓
reading traditions
        ↓
later canonical qirāʾāt
        ↓
modern printed editions
```

---

## 6. Rasm and reading traditions

The earliest consonantal Arabic script did not encode all features represented in later fully vocalized texts.

Therefore:

```
EARLY RASM
     ≠
EVERY LATER CANONICAL READING
```

The qirāʾāt tradition preserves legitimate historical information about how the consonantal text could be read.

It also reflects later scholarly selection, systematization, and canon formation.

The familiar modern Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim text is therefore an important received reading, not a neutral photographic reproduction of one fully vocalized seventh-century autograph.

---

## 7. Chronology of proclamation

### 7.1 Broad chronology

The broad distinction between:

- Meccan proclamation;
- Hijra;
- and Medinan proclamation

is historically useful and reflected in major changes of subject, style, community structure, law, and polemical setting.

### 7.2 Finer chronology

More detailed chronological systems remain reconstructions.

Corpus Coranicum and other scholars distinguish:

- early Meccan;
- middle Meccan;
- late Meccan;
- and Medinan phases.

Stylometric and literary work continues to refine such models.

Recent 2026 work by R. K. Farrin argues for an especially early Meccan Qurʾanic nucleus, approximately 609–614 CE, with a strong apocalyptic emphasis.

This model is important but shall be treated as a scholarly reconstruction, not as settled chronology.

### 7.3 Intra-sural development

Some suras may contain later expansions or supplements to earlier proclaimed units.

This is not automatically "corruption."

It may reflect the growth of proclamation during the community's lifetime.

## 8. Qurʾanic genre and self-presentation

The Qurʾan is not primarily:

- continuous biography;
- chronological national history;
- or systematic theology.

Its genres include:

- proclamation;
- warning;
- prayer;
- hymn-like praise;
- narrative recollection;
- disputation;
- legal instruction;
- parable;
- oath;
- eschatological vision;
- communal regulation;
- and scriptural reinterpretation.

The sura is therefore a major literary unit.

The corpus repeatedly presents itself as:

- recitation;
- reminder;
- guidance;
- revelation;
- scripture;
- and communication "sent down."

Under EPC-004 this makes the Qurʾan principally an:

## **R — Revelatory Witness**

## 9. Core Qurʾanic proposition register

### QUR-P0001 — Ultimate divine reality is one and without peer

**Recoverability:** A / corpus-wide

The Qurʾan is uncompromisingly theocentric.

Its central theological claim is that God is:

- one;
- sovereign;
- incomparable;
- and not one member of a divine plurality.

This is among the strongest corpus-wide propositions.

---

### QUR-P0002 — God is creator and sustainer

**Recoverability:** A

Creation is repeatedly invoked as evidence of divine sovereignty and human dependence.

God is presented as creator of:

- heavens and earth;
- human beings;
- animals;
- rain;
- vegetation;
- life and death;
- and the ordered world.

Creation language is not marginal background.

It grounds worship, gratitude, accountability, and resurrection.

---

### QUR-P0003 — Creation functions as a sign

**Recoverability:** A

The Qurʾan repeatedly treats natural phenomena and ordinary life as **āyāt / signs**.

Human beings are asked to:

- observe;
- reflect;
- remember;
- and infer divine power and generosity.

This creates a strong epistemic theme:

> reality itself contains signs that should provoke moral and theological recognition.

---

### QUR-P0004 — God communicates with human beings

**Recoverability:** A

The Qurʾan understands itself as divine communication.

It also reflects on modes of communication, including:

- revelation/inspiration;
- mediation by a messenger;
- and speech from behind a veil.

The text therefore presents revelation as a real relation between transcendent agency and human recipients.

Historical method can establish this self-understanding.

It cannot by itself prove the claimed divine cause.

---

### QUR-P0005 — Muḥammad functions as messenger, warner, and proclaimer

**Recoverability:** A

The Qurʾan does not present Muḥammad as divine.

His role is repeatedly framed as:

- messenger;
- warner;
- bearer of good news;
- reciter/proclaimer;
- community leader;
- and recipient/transmitter of revelation.

Later prophetological elaboration remains separate.

---

### QUR-P0006 — Judgment and resurrection are central

**Recoverability:** A / especially early proclamation

The coming Hour, resurrection, judgment, reward, and punishment are major organizing themes.

Early Meccan proclamation is especially saturated with imminent or urgent eschatological warning.

The corpus insists that human beings will not escape accountability through death.

---

### QUR-P0007 — Human beings are morally accountable

**Recoverability:** A

Humans are repeatedly addressed as responsible agents whose:

- belief;
- disbelief;
- generosity;
- injustice;
- gratitude;
- ingratitude;
- truthfulness;
- oppression;
- and conduct

have consequences.

The Qurʾan therefore combines divine sovereignty with persistent human accountability.

Later theological debates over predestination and free will must not be projected wholesale backward.

---

### QUR-P0008 — Repentance and divine mercy remain available

**Recoverability:** A

Judgment rhetoric is balanced by recurrent emphasis on:

- mercy;
- forgiveness;
- repentance;
- return to God;
- and the possibility of moral change.

The Qurʾanic moral world is therefore not simply punitive.

---

### QUR-P0009 — Ritual does not exhaust righteousness

**Recoverability:** A/B

Qurʾan 2:177 provides an especially clear example: righteousness is not reduced to ritual orientation but is associated with:

- faith;
- generosity;
- prayer;
- almsgiving;
- covenant fidelity;
- and perseverance.

The corpus does not reject ritual.

It embeds ritual inside a larger ethical and covenantal life.

---

### QUR-P0010 — Care for vulnerable people is a serious religious obligation

**Recoverability:** A

Repeated concern appears for:

- orphans;
- poor people;
- travelers;
- petitioners;
- captives/slaves;
- kin;
- debtors;
- and people lacking protection.

This is especially prominent in early warning texts linking wealth, arrogance, and neglect of the vulnerable with judgment.

---

### QUR-P0011 — Wealth is morally dangerous when treated as self-sufficiency

**Recoverability:** A/B

The Qurʾan repeatedly criticizes:

- hoarding;
- exploitation;
- refusal to give;
- arrogance generated by wealth;
- and the illusion of independence from God.

Wealth itself is not universally condemned.

Its moral use is heavily scrutinized.

---

### QUR-P0012 — Justice and truthful dealing matter religiously

**Recoverability:** A/B

The Qurʾan treats:

- justice;
- honest testimony;
- fair dealing;
- measured trade;
- fulfillment of agreements;
- and resistance to corruption

as religious rather than merely secular concerns.

Specific legal implementation varies by context and later jurisprudence.

The ethical themes are corpus-level.

---

### QUR-P0013 — Prayer, remembrance, gratitude, and worship orient human life toward God

**Recoverability:** A

Ritual prayer and remembrance are among the most persistent practical responses to revelation.

The Qurʾan repeatedly links them with:

- gratitude;
- humility;
- patience;
- and moral consciousness.

The exact later standardized ritual form of ṣalāt involves additional historical evidence beyond the Qurʾan alone.

---

### QUR-P0014 — Idolatry and divided divine allegiance are rejected

**Recoverability:** A

The Qurʾan's anti-idolatry polemic is foundational.

Its critique targets:

- worship of other deities;
- assigning partners to God;
- divine kinship claims;
- reliance on unauthorized intercessors;
- and cultic practices interpreted as compromising God's sovereignty.

Historical interpretation must distinguish the Qurʾan's actual opponents from later stereotypes of "paganism."

---

### QUR-P0015 — Earlier messengers belong to one broad revelatory history

**Recoverability:** A

The Qurʾan situates Muḥammad's proclamation in continuity with figures including:

- Noah;
- Abraham;
- Moses;
- David;
- Jesus;
- and others.

This generates a strong internal progressive/recurring-revelation model.

However:

> Qurʾanic retellings are evidence for seventh-century engagement with earlier traditions, not independent historical evidence for the earlier figures themselves.

---

### QUR-P0016 — The Qurʾan reinterprets earlier Jewish and Christian traditions

**Recoverability:** A

The corpus does not merely repeat biblical narratives.

It:

- selects;
- compresses;
- rearranges;
- disputes;
- corrects;
- and rhetorically repurposes

traditions circulating in Late Antiquity.

This is especially visible in accounts of:

- Abraham;
- Moses;
- Mary;
- Jesus;
- creation;
- judgment;
- and communities of scripture.

The precise mechanism of transmission must be investigated case by case.

---

### QUR-P0017 — Jesus is Messiah and messenger but not identical with God

**Recoverability:** A at corpus level

The Qurʾan gives Jesus extraordinary status:

- Messiah;
- messenger;
- word associated with God;
- miraculous birth;
- signs/miracles by God's permission.

At the same time it explicitly resists formulations that identify Jesus with God or place him inside a divine plurality.

This creates one of the strongest hard contradictions with Nicene Christianity.

It must not be harmonized away.

---

### QUR-P0018 — Mary receives exceptional honor without becoming divine

**Recoverability:** A/B

Mary is presented with unusual reverence and narrative prominence.

Her story participates in Qurʾanic debates about:

- divine power;
- miraculous birth;
- Jesus;
- purity;
- and correct theology.

Later Islamic Marian devotion must be distinguished from the Qurʾanic source layer.

---

### QUR-P0019 — Religious communities can misunderstand, conceal, forget, or misuse revelation

**Recoverability:** B / passage-dependent

The Qurʾan contains criticisms of earlier scriptural communities involving:

- concealment;
- distortion in speech or interpretation;
- forgetting;
- selective obedience;
- and disputation.

This should not be reduced to the later simplistic formula:

> "the entire Bible was textually corrupted."

Different passages accuse different groups of different failures.

The Qurʾanic corpus must be read at claim level.

---

### QUR-P0020 — Community formation becomes increasingly institutional in Medina

**Recoverability:** A

The Medinan corpus addresses:

- communal identity;
- prayer and fasting;
- almsgiving;
- marriage and divorce;
- inheritance;
- contracts;
- warfare;
- diplomacy;
- crime;
- pilgrimage;
- and relations with other groups.

This is a major change from the shorter early Meccan warning texts.

Thus the Qurʾan itself preserves evidence of a movement changing from proclamation under opposition into organized community and polity.

---

### QUR-P0021 — Law and ethical principle are interwoven

**Recoverability:** A/B

The Qurʾan contains both:

- broad moral principles;
- and concrete context-bound regulations.

Later fiqh systematizes these into much larger legal structures.

FWSD-004 shall therefore distinguish:

```
QURʾANIC COMMAND
      ≠
LATER JURISTIC SYSTEM
```

Specific legal rules require separate historical and ethical review before contemporary application.

---

### QUR-P0022 — Warfare is present but contextually embedded

**Recoverability:** A

The Qurʾan contains genuine commands and regulations concerning armed conflict.

It therefore cannot responsibly be reconstructed as a purely pacifist text.

Nor should individual warfare passages be detached automatically from:

- persecution;
- treaty;
- expulsion;
- aggression;
- community survival;
- and specific opponents.

Later doctrines of jihād require their own provenance analysis.

---

### QUR-P0023 — Coercion, religious difference, and communal boundaries are complex rather than reducible to one verse

**Recoverability:** A/B

The corpus contains:

- "no compulsion in religion";
- recognition of some righteous members of earlier communities;
- polemics against unbelief and theological error;
- communal boundary-making;
- warfare texts;
- treaty language;
- and eschatological judgment.

No one verse should be used to erase the others.

The Qurʾan's religious-pluralism profile requires a dedicated dossier.

---

### QUR-P0024 — Revelation is responsive to historical circumstance

**Recoverability:** A/B

The Qurʾanic proclamation frequently addresses:

- questions;
- disputes;
- crises;
- communal change;
- opponents;
- war;
- family matters;
- and evolving ritual practice.

This supports a model of revelation as historically situated rather than delivered as one timeless systematic book at a single moment.

## 10. Early Meccan core

Recent literary, philological, and stylometric research places strong weight on an early Meccan corpus characterized by:

- short, rhetorically intense suras;
- oath sequences;
- divine signs;
- resurrection;
- judgment;
- social inequality;
- individual accountability;
- and urgent expectation of the Hour.

R. K. Farrin's 2026 study proposes a nucleus in approximately **609–614 CE**.

This chronology is important but remains a reconstruction.

For FWSD-004 purposes, the stronger proposition is:

> **eschatological warning, divine signs, human dependence, and moral accountability belong to the earliest recoverable Qurʾanic proclamation.**

---

## 11. Q 96 and the "first revelation"

Islamic tradition famously identifies Q 96:1–5 with the first revelation.

The passage is certainly early in major chronological schemes.

However, the Qurʾan itself does not narrate the later full cave story.

Therefore FWSD-004 distinguishes:

### Strong

- Q 96 belongs to very early proclamation;
- it commands recitation/reading in the Lord's name;
- it invokes creation, knowledge, human dependence, transgression, prayer, and nearness to God.

### Less secure historically

- that Q 96:1–5 preserves a stenographic transcript of one datable cave encounter in exactly the form narrated by later sīra and ḥadīth.

The detailed encounter narrative belongs to a separate Muḥammad-biography dossier.

---

## 12. Revelation phenomenology

The Qurʾan presents multiple modes or descriptions of revelation, including:

- revelation "to the heart";
- messenger/angel mediation;
- visionary language;
- divine communication;
- and recitation/proclamation.

Q 42:51 supplies a general taxonomy of divine-human communication.

Q 2:97 explicitly names Gabriel in relation to bringing revelation to Muḥammad's heart.

Q 53 and Q 81 preserve difficult visionary/messenger material whose exact referents remain debated.

Therefore:

> **The Qurʾanic evidence does not justify reducing Muḥammad's claimed revelation to one single sensory mechanism.**

Historical evidence supports a strong phenomenological claim:

> Muḥammad's movement understood the Qurʾan as recurrent revelatory communication.

Its metaphysical cause remains subject to EPC-001.

---

## 13. Late Antique context and intertextuality

The Qurʾan belongs to Late Antiquity.

Its conceptual environment includes:

- Jewish scriptural and post-scriptural traditions;
- Christian canonical and noncanonical traditions;
- Syriac homiletic and liturgical materials;
- Arabian monotheism;
- Arabic poetic and rhetorical culture;
- and wider Near Eastern apocalyptic expectations.

This means:

> **Biblical-Qurʾanic similarity cannot be counted automatically as independent convergence.**

At the same time:

> **contact and shared tradition do not prove simple copying.**

The Qurʾan frequently transforms inherited material for new rhetorical and theological purposes.

## 14. Ethical review

### 14.1 Strong ethical features

The source-frozen corpus gives major weight to:

- care for poor and vulnerable people;
- protection of orphans;
- charity;
- justice;
- honest trade;
- truthful testimony;
- forgiveness;
- patience;
- gratitude;
- promise-keeping;
- repentance;
- restraint;
- and accountability before God.

These are strong candidates for foundational ethical authority.

### 14.2 Context-bound legal material

The corpus also contains detailed legislation concerning:

- inheritance;
- marriage;
- divorce;
- sexual conduct;
- punishment;
- slavery/captivity;
- warfare;
- and gender.

These require individual ethical and historical dossiers.

No ancient regulation becomes permanently normative merely because it is Qurʾanic.

### 14.3 Slavery and captive systems

The Qurʾan emerged in a slaveholding world.

It regulates, ameliorates, and encourages manumission in various contexts, but it does not abolish slavery institutionally.

Therefore modern ethical reception must distinguish:

- historically meaningful amelioration;
- from permanent endorsement of the institution.

### 14.4 Gender and family law

The corpus includes both:

- significant protections and property/family rights for women in its historical environment;
- and asymmetrical gender rules.

These require claim-level review rather than apologetic flattening in either direction.

### 14.5 Warfare and punishment

War and punishment texts require context-sensitive examination.

Neither:

> "the Qurʾan commands unrestricted violence"

nor:

> "the Qurʾan contains no coercive or violent commands"

is historically responsible.

## 15. Contradiction map

### 15.1 With Nicene Christianity

The strongest contradiction concerns Jesus.

The Qurʾan reveres Jesus but rejects divine-identification formulations accepted by mainstream Christianity.

**Status:** hard theological contradiction unless one or both systems is reinterpreted.

### 15.2 With early Buddhist teaching

Major tensions include:

- personal creator versus non-creator-centered liberation;
- resurrection versus rebirth;
- persistent moral personhood versus stronger no-self interpretations;
- divine command/judgment versus karmic conditionality.

At an abstract level, both traditions converge on disciplined transformation, moral responsibility, reduced attachment, and consequences of action.

### 15.3 With the Gāthās

Potential convergences include:

- supreme divine authority;
- truth versus falsehood;
- moral choice;
- judgment;
- and eschatological consequence.

But Indo-Iranian versus Semitic/Late Antique historical pathways must be examined before claims of independence are made.

### 15.4 With later Islam

The Qurʾan itself may stand in tension with later:

- ḥadīth-based law;
- sectarian dogma;
- political theology;
- and jurisprudential consensus.

Later Islam shall therefore not be allowed to define the source-frozen Qurʾan automatically.

---

## 16. Independence analysis

### 16.1 Relative independence

The Qurʾan is highly independent as an Arabic founding document of a seventh-century Arabian movement.

It is not merely a late Christian or Jewish manuscript.

### 16.2 Contact-entangled content

Its stories and theological arguments concerning:

- Adam;
- Abraham;
- Moses;
- Mary;
- Jesus;
- scripture;
- judgment;
- and prophecy

belong to a Late Antique environment already saturated with Jewish and Christian traditions.

These are therefore **contact-entangled**, not independent confirmations of the earlier traditions.

### 16.3 Distinctive synthesis

The Qurʾan's historical significance lies partly in how it:

- recombines;
- disputes;
- universalizes;
- Arabizes;
- and reorients

earlier monotheistic material into a new proclamation and community.

## 17. Textual and interpretive risk register

### RISK-QUR-001 — Later biography back-projection

Sīra and ḥadīth may be used to assign a precise narrative setting to every verse.

**Mitigation:** Qurʾan-first analysis; later reports graded separately.

### RISK-QUR-002 — Modern printed text treated as the original physical codex

The Cairo 1924 edition and Ḥafṣ reading may be mistaken for a seventh-century fully vocalized autograph.

**Mitigation:** distinguish rasm, manuscripts, readings, and modern editions.

### RISK-QUR-003 — Textual plurality denial

Evidence such as the Ṣanʿāʾ lower text may be ignored.

**Mitigation:** preserve non-ʿUthmānic evidence explicitly.

### RISK-QUR-004 — Textual chaos exaggeration

Early variants may be used to imply that no stable Qurʾan existed until centuries later.

**Mitigation:** account for the very early written archetype and overwhelming dominance of the ʿUthmānic text type.

### RISK-QUR-005 — Chronology overconfidence

A scholarly Meccan chronology may be presented as exact revelation dates.

**Mitigation:** label chronology by confidence and model.

### RISK-QUR-006 — Hadith/Qurʾan collapse

Later ḥadīth doctrine may be called "what the Qurʾan teaches."

**Mitigation:** source labels for every proposition.

### RISK-QUR-007 — Biblical dependence simplification

Shared narratives may be described either as direct plagiarism or as independent revelation without evidence.

**Mitigation:** map specific intertexts and transmission possibilities.

### RISK-QUR-008 — "Corruption" oversimplification

Qurʾanic criticisms of earlier communities may be converted into one total doctrine of textual falsification.

**Mitigation:** classify each accusation separately.

### RISK-QUR-009 — Qurʾanic ethics reduced to later fiqh

Later legal schools may define the entire ethical witness.

**Mitigation:** extract Qurʾanic ethical propositions before jurisprudential reception.

### RISK-QUR-010 — Pacifist or violent proof-texting

Isolated passages may erase the rest of the warfare/peace corpus.

**Mitigation:** build a dedicated conflict-and-coercion dossier.

### RISK-QUR-011 — Founder infallibility assumption

Because the Qurʾan claims divine source, every later interpretation of Muḥammad may receive automatic authority.

**Mitigation:** distinguish Qurʾanic claim, Muḥammad biography, later Sunnah, and theology.

### RISK-QUR-012 — Polemical translation

Translation choices may silently decide theological disputes.

**Mitigation:** preserve Arabic terms and compare major philological readings.

## 18. Strongest case for foundational admission

The strongest cumulative case is:

1. **Founder-era proximity:** the corpus is extraordinarily close to the founding Islamic movement.
2. **Early manuscript support:** substantial Qurʾanic text survives in first-century Islamic manuscript traditions.
3. **Very early standardization:** the dominant consonantal text shows strong evidence of descent from an early written archetype.
4. **Recoverable plurality:** surviving non-ʿUthmānic evidence enables rather than prevents critical textual history.
5. **Coherent theological center:** one creator God, revelation, judgment, mercy, and accountability are unmistakably central.
6. **Ethical seriousness:** justice, charity, protection of vulnerable people, truthful dealing, and repentance recur throughout the corpus.
7. **Historical development preserved internally:** Meccan-to-Medinan change gives direct evidence of community formation.
8. **Revelatory self-consciousness:** unlike later biography, the Qurʾan itself is the claimed revelatory artifact.
9. **Comparative importance:** it directly engages Jewish, Christian, Arabian, and broader Late Antique traditions.
10. **Corrective function:** it prevents the eventual foundational corpus from ending with Jesus or assuming that later Christian interpretation closes revelation automatically.
11. **Text-critical tractability:** manuscripts, readings, orthography, and chronology can be studied with unusually rich evidence.

---

## 19. Strongest case against foundational admission

The strongest objections are substantial:

1. **Divine provenance remains unverified:** textual proximity does not prove revelation.
2. **Founder authorship model is complex:** the Qurʾan presents itself as God's speech/proclamation through Muḥammad, not ordinary authored memoir.
3. **Early textual plurality exists:** the Ṣanʿāʾ lower text and companion-codex traditions prevent claims of absolute pristine uniformity.
4. **Chronological reconstruction is incomplete:** exact order and circumstances of many passages remain disputed.
5. **Intertextual dependence/contact:** major content participates in preexisting Jewish and Christian traditions.
6. **Hard theological contradiction:** its Jesus claims conflict with mainstream Christian incarnation doctrine.
7. **Ancient legal context:** some gender, slavery, punishment, and warfare materials require major ethical scrutiny.
8. **Later interpretive dominance:** centuries of Islamic theology and law can obscure the source-frozen text.
9. **Reading plurality:** the relationship between rasm and later readings complicates any claim to one uniquely original full vocalization.
10. **Historical biography is sparse:** the Qurʾan cannot independently answer many questions about Muḥammad's life.

These objections defeat simplistic claims of an unproblematic, context-free, fully interpreted scripture.

They do not defeat the Qurʾanic Proclamation as a foundational-witness candidate.

## 22. Provisional recommendation

### 22.1 Procedural status

**Remain CAND-3 — Evaluated.**

### 22.2 Recommendation after first-cycle comparison

The Qurʾanic Proclamation should be treated as a **serious candidate for CAND-4 — Foundational Candidate**.

If eventually admitted, the most defensible architecture would likely distinguish:

### FWC-A1 candidate material

- one-God proclamation;
- creation and signs;
- revelation;
- judgment and resurrection;
- mercy and repentance;
- ethical accountability;
- justice;
- charity and vulnerable-care;
- prayer/remembrance;
- major prophetic-history claims;
- and source-controlled community formation.

### FWC-A2 / EWC-B candidate material

- context-bound legal regulation;
- warfare legislation;
- disputed chronology;
- historically conditioned social structures;
- passages heavily dependent on uncertain occasion-of-revelation reconstruction;
- and detailed metaphysical propositions where translation or intertext is materially disputed.

This is only a provisional structural suggestion, not an admission decision.

### 22.3 What should not happen

Do not:

- canonize later ḥadīth or fiqh merely because the Qurʾan is foundational;
- treat the 1924 Cairo printing as a seventh-century autograph;
- deny the Ṣanʿāʾ lower-text evidence;
- exaggerate early variants into centuries of textual chaos;
- let sīra dictate the meaning of every verse;
- count biblical-Qurʾanic parallels as independent revelation evidence without contact analysis;
- flatten every Qurʾanic criticism of earlier communities into "the Bible was corrupted";
- erase difficult warfare, gender, slavery, or punishment texts;
- assume "historically early" equals "metaphysically true";
- or assume that criticism of later Islamic doctrine is equivalent to rejection of the Qurʾan itself.

## 25. Dossier conclusion

The Qurʾanic Proclamation survives foundational-witness review unusually strongly, but not because historical criticism proves its claim to divine origin.

It survives because:

- the text stands extremely close to the founding movement;
- early manuscript evidence is abundant relative to most ancient religious corpora;
- the dominant consonantal text descends from a very early written archetype;
- significant early plurality can still be documented rather than denied;
- the text preserves a recognizable development from urgent Meccan proclamation to Medinan community formation;
- and its theological and ethical architecture is sufficiently coherent to be evaluated at proposition level.

The strongest recoverable Qurʾanic witness is neither:

> an absolutely variant-free modern printed codex dropped into history fully vocalized,

nor:

> a late medieval compilation disconnected from Muḥammad's lifetime.

A more defensible historical model is:

> **a seventh-century revelatory proclamation associated closely with Muḥammad's movement, transmitted orally and in writing, standardized very early in a dominant written consonantal form, accompanied by some early textual plurality and later reading-system development.**

For EPC-004 purposes, that makes the Qurʾanic Proclamation a major candidate for foundational reception.

Its greatest strength is **source proximity**.

Its greatest methodological danger is **allowing later Islamic tradition to become indistinguishable from the Qurʾan itself**.

FWSD-004 shall preserve the difference.
