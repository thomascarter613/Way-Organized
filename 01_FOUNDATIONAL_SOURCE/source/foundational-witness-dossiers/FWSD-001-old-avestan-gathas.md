# FWSD-001 — Old Avestan Gāthās Foundational Witness Selection Dossier

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** CAND-3 — Evaluated; not canonically admitted  
**Parent protocol:** EPC-004 — Foundational Witness Selection Protocol  
**Candidate:** FWC-CAND-005 — Old Avestan Gāthās  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-21  
**Scope:** Source-critical evaluation of the five Old Avestan Gāthās as a possible Foundational Witness Corpus candidate

---

## 1. Executive assessment

The Old Avestan Gāthās are among the strongest non-Abrahamic candidates presently identified for foundational review because they are:

- linguistically ancient;
- sharply distinguishable from later Avestan strata;
- embedded in a living ritual transmission;
- historically close, at least in language and religious setting, to the earliest recoverable Mazdayasnian movement;
- centered on a coherent constellation of truth/order, moral choice, good and evil, right-mindedness, divine wisdom, judgment, and human responsibility;
- and sufficiently early to provide a major control against constructing a supposedly universal religious core solely from biblical, Christian, or Islamic materials.

At the same time, the corpus has serious limitations:

- Zarathustra's exact date and homeland remain unresolved;
- the attribution of the Gāthās to one historical Zarathustra, though traditional and supported by substantial scholarship, is disputed;
- the texts were transmitted orally for many centuries before the surviving written tradition;
- the Old Avestan text has undergone phonological, liturgical, redactional, and scribal change;
- interpretation is unusually difficult, and major passages admit materially different translations;
- later Zoroastrian doctrines cannot be projected backward into the Gāthās without source control.

**Procedural conclusion:** retain the Gāthās at **CAND-3 — Evaluated**. They warrant advancement to full cross-candidate comparison and are a serious future **CAND-4 — Foundational Candidate**, but no FWC-A admission should occur until the first comparative cycle is complete.

## 2. Candidate identity and source freeze

### 2.1 Included witness

For this dossier, **Gāthās** means the seventeen Old Avestan hymnic chapters conventionally grouped into five metrical collections within the Yasna:

1. **Ahunavaiti Gāthā** — Yasna 28–34  
2. **Uštavaiti Gāthā** — Yasna 43–46  
3. **Spənta Mainyu Gāthā** — Yasna 47–50  
4. **Vohu Xšaθra Gāthā** — Yasna 51  
5. **Vahištōišti Gāthā** — Yasna 53

The corpus is Old Avestan and is distinguished linguistically from the Younger Avestan materials that surround and later interpret it.

### 2.2 Excluded from this candidate

The following are not silently folded into FWSD-001:

- Yasna Haptaŋhāiti / Yasna Haptanghaiti (Y. 35–41);
- the Old Avestan prayers surrounding the Gāthās;
- Younger Avestan texts;
- later Yašts;
- Vidēvdād;
- Middle Persian/Pahlavi Zand and theological literature;
- later biographies of Zarathustra;
- later systematized doctrines of the Aməša Spəntas;
- mature Ahriman/Ohrmazd cosmology;
- developed Saošyant eschatology;
- later Zoroastrian ritual or legal systems.

These materials may receive independent dossiers.

### 2.3 Why the freeze matters

Later Zoroastrian tradition often explains the Gāthās through categories that became clearer only in later periods. This dossier therefore asks first what the Old Avestan witness itself can responsibly support.

## 3. Language, date, and geography

### 3.1 Language

The Gāthās are composed in **Old Avestan**, one of the oldest attested Iranian language strata and linguistically close to Vedic Sanskrit.

Old Avestan is distinct from Younger Avestan. The difference is substantial enough that scholarship treats the Old Avestan corpus as chronologically earlier by centuries.

### 3.2 Date

A precise date cannot be established because the Gāthās mention no securely datable historical event.

A defensible working statement is:

> The Gāthās most probably belong to the late second or early first millennium BCE, with a frequently cited scholarly center of gravity around **1000 BCE, give or take roughly a century**, while reputable proposals have ranged much earlier and later.

The wide range must remain visible. The dossier shall not convert a scholarly center of gravity into a false exact date.

### 3.3 Geography

The linguistic and cultural evidence points broadly toward an **eastern Iranian / Central Asian** setting rather than western Iran, but no exact homeland is securely established.

### 3.4 Historical implication

The candidate therefore has high antiquity but only moderate chronological precision.

**Assessment:**  
- Antiquity: **High**  
- Absolute dating precision: **Moderate/Low**  
- Broad regional provenance: **Moderate/High**  
- Exact geographic provenance: **Low/Moderate**

## 4. Zarathustra attribution and historicity

### 4.1 Traditional attribution

Zoroastrian tradition attributes the Gāthās to **Zarathustra (Zaraθuštra / Zoroaster)** himself.

Several Gathic passages use the name Zarathustra, and some passages are reasonably read as first-person self-reference. The overwhelming majority of many hymnic units are addressed directly to Ahura Mazdā.

### 4.2 Critical dispute

The attribution is not uncontested.

Some scholars have questioned whether:

- every Gathic hymn is by one individual;
- third-person and vocative references to Zarathustra are compatible with single authorship;
- the corpus represents an individual prophet or a priestly/religious group;
- or the traditional founder biography can be reconstructed from the Old Avestan texts at all.

The existence of later hagiography makes it especially important not to reconstruct Zarathustra's life from post-Gathic legends and then use those legends to prove Gathic authorship.

### 4.3 Working judgment

For this project, the historically responsible formulation is:

> **A historical Zarathustra associated closely with the Gāthic religious movement is probable, and substantial scholarship accepts Zarathustrian authorship of the Gāthās, but single-author attribution is not established beyond reasonable scholarly dispute.**

Therefore:

- the Gāthās receive strong early-movement attribution;
- individual passages should not automatically be treated as verbatim autobiographical statements;
- later biographical details receive no authority from this dossier merely because they concern Zarathustra.

**Founder attribution:** **Moderate–High, disputed**  
**External corroboration of Zarathustra as an individually datable historical person:** **Low**

## 5. Transmission history

### 5.1 Oral transmission

The early Avesta was transmitted orally by trained priests for many centuries.

The Gāthās were especially protected by metrical, liturgical, and memorized recitation. Their survival is therefore not comparable to uncontrolled folk transmission.

However, oral discipline does not imply perfect preservation.

### 5.2 Written fixation

The special Avestan script is generally placed in the Sasanian period. It appears to have been designed with unusually fine phonetic precision to record the inherited recitation.

The surviving written text therefore reflects at least:

1. original Old Avestan composition;
2. oral/liturgical transmission;
3. pronunciation changes associated with chanting;
4. Young Avestan influence on Old Avestan forms;
5. deliberate orthoepic/editorial intervention;
6. Sasanian written fixation;
7. post-Sasanian manuscript transmission;
8. later scribal errors.

### 5.3 Surviving manuscripts

The oldest surviving Avestan manuscripts are medieval, centuries later than the Sasanian archetype and much later than the Old Avestan compositions.

The manuscript tradition is nevertheless sufficiently rich for critical philology, and modern projects continue to collate Yasna witnesses and integrate the living recitational tradition with manuscript evidence.

### 5.4 Consequence for authority

The corpus has:

- **high stability as an identifiable ancient corpus**;
- **moderate confidence in exact original phonological wording at every point**;
- **significant philological recoverability**;
- but no surviving autograph or near-contemporary manuscript.

**Transmission assessment:** **Moderate–High with explicit qualifications**

## 6. Genre and ritual setting

The Gāthās should not be reduced to a modern collection of sermons.

They are **metrical ritual poetry** embedded in the Yasna liturgy. A major strand of current scholarship emphasizes sacrificial and liturgical context rather than reading the hymns simply as transcripted public preaching.

That does not make their theological or ethical content unreal. It means that:

- invocation;
- ritual performance;
- poetic form;
- praise;
- petition;
- dialogue;
- and religious teaching

are intertwined.

Genre therefore limits overly literal reconstructions of a single historical speech event behind every hymn.

## 7. Primary proposition inventory

The following propositions are source-frozen at the Gathic level. They are provisional analytical abstractions rather than imposed creedal wording.

### GAT-P0001 — Ahura Mazdā occupies the supreme theological center

The hymns overwhelmingly orient themselves toward **Mazdā Ahura / Ahura Mazdā**, the Wise Lord.

Ahura Mazdā is associated with wisdom, creation, truth/order, good mind, and the beneficent ordering of existence.

**Qualification:** the Gāthās should not simply be labeled later-style monotheism without nuance. Other divine/spiritual entities appear, and the relationship between Ahura Mazdā, Spənta Mainyu, the Ahuras, and personified religious realities is complex.

### GAT-P0002 — Aša is an objective normative order of truth/rightness

**Aša** is one of the central Gathic concepts.

It carries interrelated senses involving:

- truth;
- right order;
- correctness;
- reality as it should be;
- and righteous alignment.

The Gathic moral world is not presented as arbitrary divine preference.

### GAT-P0003 — Druj / falsehood is genuinely opposed to aša

The opposition between **aša** and **druj** is not merely intellectual disagreement.

Falsehood is morally and existentially destructive.

This creates one of the earliest recoverable Iranian formulations of an objective conflict between truthful/right order and deceitful/disordered life.

### GAT-P0004 — Human choice has serious moral significance

Yasna 30 and related passages frame human beings as participants in a consequential choice between better and worse ways of thinking, speaking, and acting.

Later Zoroastrianism systematizes this strongly, but the moral-choice structure is already Gathic.

### GAT-P0005 — Thought, speech, and action belong together ethically

The Gāthās connect moral differentiation with thinking, speaking, and acting.

**Qualification:** the famous fixed formula “good thoughts, good words, good deeds” is most explicitly attested in the Old Avestan Yasna Haptaŋhāiti and later tradition; it should not be carelessly quoted as a fixed Gathic slogan without source distinction.

### GAT-P0006 — Vohu Manah / Good Mind is integral to proper alignment

The hymns repeatedly connect **Vohu Manah** with the human-divine relationship and with right orientation toward Ahura Mazdā and aša.

Whether such entities should be translated as abstract qualities, divine beings, aspects of Ahura Mazdā, or categories that resist those modern distinctions must remain open passage by passage.

### GAT-P0007 — Beneficent and destructive spiritual orientations are real features of the Gathic worldview

Yasna 30.3–4 and 45.2 contain the famous opposition of two spirits or spiritual orientations.

The text is genuinely dualistic at some level.

However, the later equation:

> Ohrmazd versus Ahriman as two fully systematized cosmic opponents

must not simply be projected backward as if it were the unambiguous wording of the Gāthās.

### GAT-P0008 — Moral action has postmortem consequences

The Gāthās contain concepts associated with:

- the best and worst existences;
- the House of Song;
- the House of Lie;
- the Činvat bridge;
- and judgment.

The precise relationship between individual judgment and universal eschatology is not always clear in the Old Avestan material.

### GAT-P0009 — The religious life is participatory rather than merely assent-based

The hymns call for active alignment with aša through thought, judgment, worship, truthful speech, and action.

This aligns strongly with TCP-001's broader transcultural finding that transformation is more central than mere propositional assent.

### GAT-P0010 — Revelation/encounter is presented relationally and dialogically

The Gāthās repeatedly address Ahura Mazdā directly and ask questions concerning truth, creation, judgment, community, and right action.

This is valuable evidence for a **Type I relational-revelation model**, though historical evidence cannot by itself establish the metaphysical cause of the experience.

### GAT-P0011 — Religious and social disorder are linked

The Gathic world links false teaching, bad rule, destructive behavior, and disorder rather than isolating religion from social conduct.

This requires more unit-level review before any detailed political or social program is extracted.

### GAT-P0012 — “Saošyant” language is early but not yet the later single final-savior biography

The Gathic term later associated with the final Saošyant can function in the older sense of **benefactor/helper**, including plural or broader usage.

The full later savior system is excluded from the Gathic source freeze.

## 8. Important non-propositions

FWSD-001 does **not** presently establish that the Gāthās teach, in mature later form:

- two eternal coequal gods;
- the complete Amesha Spenta system;
- the complete Ahriman cosmology of Pahlavi literature;
- the full three-Saošyant scheme;
- all later Zoroastrian purity law;
- a fully developed bodily resurrection doctrine in its later form;
- or every later Zoroastrian teaching attributed back to Zarathustra.

These require later-source dossiers.

---

## 9. Relationship to Indo-Iranian inheritance

The Gāthās are not culturally isolated.

Old Avestan and Vedic Sanskrit descend from a common Indo-Iranian linguistic and cultural background.

The relationship between Avestan **aša** and Vedic **ṛta** is especially important.

Therefore:

> similarity between aša and ṛta is evidence of inherited Indo-Iranian conceptual ancestry before it is evidence of two independent revelations reaching the same proposition.

This materially affects the TCP independence analysis.

At the same time, the Gathic restructuring of inherited concepts around Ahura Mazdā, aša, moral choice, and opposition to falsehood may represent significant innovation within that inherited world.

---

## 10. Encounter provenance

If substantial Zarathustrian authorship is accepted, the Gāthās are unusually close to the reported religious experience because the poems themselves directly address Ahura Mazdā.

That would give them high **encounter directness** compared with a biography written centuries later about what a prophet allegedly experienced.

However, historical method can establish only:

> a poet/priestly voice addresses and interprets reality in revelatory relationship to Ahura Mazdā.

It cannot establish by textual analysis alone:

> Ahura Mazdā objectively caused the experience.

The metaphysical provenance therefore remains **unresolved** under EPC-001.

## 11. Ethical review

### 11.1 Strengths

The strongest ethically relevant Gathic features include:

- truth over deceit;
- consequential moral choice;
- responsible thought, speech, and action;
- opposition to destructive behavior;
- concern for good order;
- and rejection of alignment with the Lie.

These are significant candidates for Received or Foundational ethical authority.

### 11.2 Cautions

The Gāthās arise from an ancient pastoral, ritual, and conflict-laden society.

Terms denoting enemies, false teachers, ritual opponents, and destructive factions require historical study before they are universalized.

No ancient polemical classification may automatically become a warrant for treating modern religious outsiders as morally corrupt.

### 11.3 Hard-stop review

Nothing in the source-frozen Gathic core presently identified requires an EPC-002 Article XLIX hard stop for the corpus as a whole.

Individual passages still require claim-level ethical review.

## 12. Contradiction map

### 12.1 With strict Abrahamic monotheism

Potential tension exists over:

- the ontological status of the two spirits;
- the divine entities associated with Ahura Mazdā;
- and the extent to which the Gathic system is monotheistic, dualistic, or structurally different from both labels.

**Status:** unresolved framework tension, not yet a simple contradiction.

### 12.2 With Buddhism

Potential contradictions include:

- creator/supreme-personal-source language versus a liberation framework not requiring a creator;
- postmortem/judgment structures versus rebirth frameworks;
- personhood and moral accountability metaphysics.

At the same time, both traditions may converge abstractly on misalignment, disciplined transformation, and objective moral/experiential consequences.

### 12.3 With later Zoroastrianism

The Gāthās themselves can conflict with simplistic retrospective descriptions of later doctrine.

The later system must therefore be treated as development, interpretation, or additional revelation—not automatically as a transparent restatement of the Old Avestan hymns.

### 12.4 With the Vedic corpus

The relation is partly genealogical rather than contradictory. Shared Indo-Iranian concepts may be preserved, transformed, or rejected differently in Iranian and Indic branches.

---

## 13. Independence analysis

### 13.1 High-value independence

Relative to early Israelite prophetic traditions and early Buddhism, the Gāthās provide a major cross-cultural comparison point.

Direct literary borrowing from those later or geographically distinct corpora is not required to explain the existence of the Gathic text.

### 13.2 Reduced independence

Against Vedic evidence, common Indo-Iranian ancestry materially reduces independence.

### 13.3 Later contact contamination

Comparisons between developed Zoroastrianism and later Judaism, Christianity, Manichaeism, or Islam require separate contact and diffusion analysis.

FWSD-001 does not use later Iranian-Abrahamic similarities as independent evidence for the Gāthic core.

## 14. Textual and interpretive risk register

### RISK-GAT-001 — Translation instability

Old Avestan is difficult enough that materially different translations can arise from the same passage.

**Mitigation:** retain key terms in transliteration and compare multiple philological translations.

### RISK-GAT-002 — Later theological back-projection

Later Avestan and Pahlavi doctrine can make the Gāthās appear more systematic than the Old Avestan text itself.

**Mitigation:** source freeze and explicit layer labels.

### RISK-GAT-003 — Founder idealization

Later Zarathustra biography can be used circularly to interpret ambiguous Gathic passages.

**Mitigation:** distinguish Old Avestan self-reference from later hagiography.

### RISK-GAT-004 — Oral-transmission romanticism

Strict ritual preservation may tempt investigators to assume verbal perfection.

**Mitigation:** preserve the documented history of phonological, editorial, and scribal changes.

### RISK-GAT-005 — Oral-transmission skepticism

The opposite error is to dismiss the text because surviving manuscripts are late.

**Mitigation:** account for trained liturgical preservation, metrical structure, comparative linguistics, and manuscript stemmatics.

### RISK-GAT-006 — Monotheism/dualism category forcing

Modern binary labels can obscure the actual structure of the text.

**Mitigation:** describe Ahura Mazdā, Spənta Mainyu, Angra/Aka Mainyu, aša, and the divine entities before assigning systematic labels.

## 15. Strongest case for foundational admission

The strongest case is cumulative:

1. **Antiquity:** the Gāthās belong to the oldest Iranian religious literature.
2. **Source distinction:** Old Avestan can be distinguished from later textual layers.
3. **Encounter proximity:** if traditional/substantial Zarathustrian attribution is correct, the hymns are near-primary testimony rather than remote biography.
4. **Moral seriousness:** truth, falsehood, choice, thought, speech, action, and responsibility are central.
5. **Theological depth:** the corpus wrestles with creation, evil, divine wisdom, judgment, and human participation without depending on later systematic theology.
6. **Comparative value:** the Gāthās provide a major non-biblical witness for the project's transcultural testing.
7. **Resilience:** despite transmission complexity and translation difficulty, the central conceptual architecture remains recoverable.
8. **Corrective function:** their inclusion would prevent the eventual foundational corpus from becoming an Abrahamic anthology by default.

---

## 16. Strongest case against foundational admission

The strongest objections are also substantial:

1. **Attribution uncertainty:** single-author Zarathustrian authorship is disputed.
2. **Dating uncertainty:** no secure absolute historical anchor exists.
3. **Transmission distance:** surviving manuscripts are extremely late relative to composition.
4. **Textual alteration:** Old Avestan underwent oral, phonological, editorial, and scribal change.
5. **Philological difficulty:** translations of central passages can diverge materially.
6. **Ritual opacity:** some language may be intelligible primarily within a ritual system that is only partly recoverable.
7. **External corroboration:** the founder's life and revelatory experience lack strong independent contemporary evidence.
8. **Doctrinal ambiguity:** later readers can too easily mistake developed Zoroastrian theology for the Old Avestan witness.

These objections argue strongly against **inerrancy** and against uncritical whole-corpus doctrinal authority. They do not, by themselves, defeat the corpus as a foundational witness candidate.

## 19. Provisional recommendation

### 19.1 Procedural status

**Remain CAND-3 — Evaluated.**

### 19.2 Recommendation after first-cycle comparison

The Gāthās should be treated as a **serious candidate for CAND-4 — Foundational Candidate**.

The future decision should probably be made at two levels:

1. **Gathic corpus-level reception** as an ancient foundational witness to the Zarathustrian/Mazdayasnian encounter;
2. **claim/unit-level authority profiles** distinguishing especially strong material from passages whose translation, attribution, or theological meaning remains unusually uncertain.

### 19.3 What should not happen

Do not:

- canonize all later Zoroastrian doctrine through the Gāthās;
- call the corpus simply “monotheistic” or “dualistic” without qualification;
- equate aša and Vedic ṛta with independent revelation;
- turn later Saošyant doctrine into verbatim Zarathustrian teaching;
- treat surviving medieval manuscripts as if they were the original written text;
- or deny the value of the corpus because its transmission was oral.

## 22. Dossier conclusion

The Gāthās survive the first serious foundational-witness review unusually well, but not because they offer an uncomplicated transcript of an infallible founder.

They survive because a clearly ancient Old Avestan corpus preserves a recoverable religious world in which a speaker or community addresses Ahura Mazdā, gives exceptional weight to aša and truth, treats falsehood as existentially consequential, insists on morally significant human choice, and connects thought, speech, action, and ultimate consequence.

The exact metaphysical interpretation remains contested.

That is not a defect to conceal.

It is precisely why the Gāthās are valuable to a Foundational Witness Corpus designed to preserve high-quality witnesses without pretending that historical confidence, textual confidence, and theological certainty are the same thing.
