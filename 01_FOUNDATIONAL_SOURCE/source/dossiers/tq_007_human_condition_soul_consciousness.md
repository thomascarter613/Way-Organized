# TQ-007 - Human Condition / Soul / Consciousness

**Research question:** What is the human problem, what is a person, and what forms of transformation are warranted before the Way settles soul/afterlife metaphysics?

Across traditions, striking but incomplete convergence appears at the practical level: human beings are finite, vulnerable to suffering, prone to self-deception and harmful desire, shaped by habits and communities, capable of cruelty and compassion, and capable of significant transformation through disciplined practice and relationship. The metaphysical explanations remain deeply different: image-of-God anthropology, sin and grace, fitrah and moral accountability, Buddhist non-self and craving, Sikh haumai/ego, and Hindu models of self, karma, and ignorance are not interchangeable.

## Witness map

| Witness | Source / corpus | Research use |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 1:27 and Jewish traditions | Humanity in the image of God | Supports dignity/vocation interpretations; later Jewish anthropology is diverse. |
| Jesus traditions | e.g., Mark 7:20-23 and related ethical teaching | Locates moral corruption in dispositions/actions arising from within, not merely ritual contamination. |
| Pauline / Christian traditions | Sin, flesh/spirit, grace, new creation | Highly influential Christian anthropology; multiple later theories of original sin and salvation. |
| Qur'an 30:30; 91:7-10 | Fitrah, moral discernment, purification/corruption | Human created disposition and accountability; Islamic anthropology is broader than these verses alone. |
| Early Buddhist texts | dukkha, craving, impermanence, non-self | Frames the problem without a permanent controlling self and emphasizes liberation through insight/practice. |
| Sikh teaching | haumai / ego and truthful living | Ego-centeredness is a key obstacle; remembrance and truthful living are central remedies. |

## Findings

| Claim | Confidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Humans are vulnerable to cognitive bias, self-deception, social influence, harmful desire, and moral failure. | Established | Supported by ordinary experience and multiple religious/psychological traditions; exact mechanisms vary. |
| Humans are also capable of compassion, learning, conscience, repair, self-regulation, and transformation. | Established | A one-sided doctrine of depravity is not warranted by the comparative evidence alone. |
| Human dignity should not depend on religious status, purity, caste, office, wealth, sex, or loyalty to the institution. | Established - Ethical/Institutional | Constitutional commitment of the Way. |
| Humans possess an immaterial immortal soul of a specific type. | Open Question - Metaphysical | Traditions and philosophical theories conflict. |
| No enduring self exists in any meaningful sense. | Open Question - Metaphysical | Early Buddhist non-self analysis is a major challenge to substantial-self theories but requires careful philosophical engagement. |
| Inherited guilt/original sin is the correct universal diagnosis. | Open Question | Distinguish inherited conditions, social transmission, biological propensity, and culpability. |

## Major interpretations

- **Image of God / covenantal personhood:** Human worth and vocation grounded in relation to God.
- **Sin/grace anthropology:** Humanity impaired by sin and dependent on divine grace; many Christian variants.
- **Fitrah/accountability:** Humans created with a primordial disposition toward truth/God yet capable of corruption and repentance.
- **Non-self / craving / ignorance:** Suffering maintained by craving, misperception, and attachment to self; liberation through ethical and contemplative insight.
- **Ego/haumai:** Self-centeredness obscures truthful relation to God and others; remembrance/service reorient the person.
- **Naturalistic psychology:** Human cognition and morality explained through biological, developmental, psychological, and social processes without metaphysical soul assumptions.

## Current Way posture

- **Established:** All leaders and Companions are treated as fallible; governance must assume susceptibility to bias, temptation, dependency, and self-justification.
- **Established:** Every person retains dignity and conscience regardless of religious status.
- **Provisional:** Formation should cultivate attention, honesty, compassion, restraint, service, repair, and intellectual humility because these respond to human vulnerabilities across models.
- **Open Question:** Soul, consciousness after death, rebirth, resurrection, inherited sin, and ultimate mechanism of transformation.

## What would materially change confidence?

- Advances in philosophy/neuroscience of consciousness that strongly favor or undermine specific models of personal identity.
- Credible evidence bearing on survival of consciousness, rebirth, resurrection, or other postmortem claims.
- Better exegesis/source criticism clarifying what the earliest witnesses actually taught about personhood and moral failure.
- Longitudinal evidence on which formation practices produce durable prosocial transformation without coercive harms.
