# TQ-004 - Muhammad / Qur'anic Revelation

**Research question:** What can responsibly be said about Muhammad historically, and how should the Qur'an's revelation claim be evaluated?

Muhammad is historically secure as the seventh-century Arabian leader around whom the early Muslim community formed. The Qur'an is the earliest major source for his proclamation and is far closer to his lifetime than the developed sira and hadith corpora. The theological claim that the Qur'an is divine speech, and the traditional claim of final prophethood, require theological and philosophical assessment in addition to historical source criticism.

## Witness map

| Witness | Source / corpus | Research use |
|---|---|---|
| Qur'an | Earliest major Islamic source | Broad scholarly agreement, though not unanimity, treats its content as substantially deriving from Muhammad's proclamation; it is not arranged as a biography. |
| Early material evidence | Inscriptions, papyri, coins, early Qur'anic manuscripts | Important for dating, community identity, textual transmission, and early Islamic history. |
| Sira literature | Ibn Ishaq tradition preserved through later recensions and related biographies | Indispensable but later than the Qur'an and requires source criticism. |
| Hadith corpora | Later collections with isnad/matn traditions | Central to Islamic law and piety; historical evaluation is report-specific and methodologically complex. |
| Non-Muslim witnesses | Early Christian, Jewish, and other external notices | Potential independent data but often brief, polemical, or difficult to interpret. |

## Findings

| Claim | Confidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Muhammad was a seventh-century Arabian religious and political leader whose movement produced the early Muslim community. | Established | Basic historical conclusion. |
| The Qur'an is the earliest principal textual witness to Muhammad's proclamation. | Established | It remains incomplete as biography. |
| Later sira and hadith should not be given the same source weight as the Qur'an merely because tradition groups them together. | Established - Methodological | Each report requires provenance and transmission analysis. |
| The Qur'an presents Muhammad as messenger and "seal of the prophets." | Established - Textual | The meaning is central to Islamic theology; textual description is distinct from Assembly endorsement. |
| Muhammad was divinely commissioned and the Qur'an is verbatim divine revelation. | Open Question - Theological | History can assess provenance and proclamation; divine origin requires additional inference. |
| Muhammad is necessarily the final possible bearer of revelation for all humanity. | Open Question for the Way | The Qur'anic claim and Islamic interpretation are studied seriously but not adopted automatically. |

## Major interpretations

- **Classical Sunni/Shi'a prophethood:** Muhammad as final prophet/messenger and Qur'an as divine revelation; traditions differ on succession and authority.
- **Historical-critical account:** Muhammad as late antique Arabian religious leader whose proclamation must be studied in its Jewish, Christian, Arabian, political, and linguistic setting.
- **Revisionist historical models:** Some scholarship has challenged aspects of traditional chronology or source reliability; proposals require case-by-case evidence.
- **Interreligious prophetic recognition:** Some non-Muslim thinkers regard Muhammad as a genuine religious reformer or prophetic witness without accepting every Islamic dogmatic claim.

## Current Way posture

- **Established:** Muhammad and the Qur'an are major witnesses that must be studied directly rather than through polemic.
- **Established:** Source classes must remain differentiated: Qur'an, early material evidence, sira, hadith, tafsir, and later doctrine.
- **Open Question:** Divine authorship, inimitability as proof, finality of prophethood, and the normative authority of later hadith for the Way.
- **Safeguard:** No offense or reverence norm may be used to suppress good-faith historical inquiry or to license denigration of Muslims.

## What would materially change confidence?

- New early manuscript/epigraphic evidence bearing on textual history and chronology.
- Stronger source-critical models linking specific biographical reports to early independent strata.
- Arguments for divine revelation that distinguish divine authorship from literary power, historical influence, or sincere conviction.
- Evidence clarifying how earliest Muslims understood prophecy, authority, and Qur'anic collection.
