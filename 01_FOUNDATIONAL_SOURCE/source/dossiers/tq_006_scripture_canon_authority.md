# TQ-006 - Scripture / Canon / Authority

**Research question:** What makes a text scripture, how do canons form, and should the Way have a canon?

The comparative record shows that scripture and canon are historically mediated categories. Jewish authoritative literature developed over time and Second Temple evidence shows graded authority; the New Testament canon formed gradually with core collections and later disputed books; Qur'anic collection and reading traditions have their own early history; Buddhist traditions preserve multiple canons in different languages; Sikh tradition offers a different model in which the Guru Granth Sahib itself holds living scriptural/Guru authority. These mechanisms cannot be collapsed into one theory of inspiration.

## Witness map

| Witness | Source / corpus | Research use |
|---|---|---|
| Jewish textual history | Torah, Prophets, Writings; Dead Sea Scrolls | Shows authority, interpretation, and eventual canonization as historically layered. |
| Christian textual history | Gospels, Pauline letters, catholic epistles, Acts, Revelation, extracanonical literature | Core texts circulated early while disputed boundaries stabilized over centuries. |
| Qur'anic textual history | Recitation, collection, consonantal text, canonical readings | Distinct model centered on a single revealed corpus with oral/written transmission and recognized readings. |
| Buddhist canons | Pali, Sanskrit fragments, Chinese and Tibetan collections | Plural canonical lineages; early-text reconstruction often compares parallels across schools/languages. |
| Sikh scripture | Guru Granth Sahib | Distinctive model of scripture bearing continuing Guru authority within Sikh tradition and containing compositions from multiple voices. |

## Findings

| Claim | Confidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| A text calling itself sacred does not by itself establish its own canon status. | Established | Canon is also a community/institutional reception category. |
| Jewish and Christian canon formation was historically gradual rather than a single universally recognized list from the beginning. | Established | Specific dates/boundaries require tradition-sensitive nuance. |
| Second Temple Jewish evidence can reflect graded levels of authoritative literature. | Well-Supported | Qumran research is particularly important. |
| Multiple Buddhist canons and parallel early texts complicate any claim that one bounded book-list uniquely represents the historical Buddha. | Established | Comparative textual work is essential. |
| The Way presently needs a closed canon. | Rejected - Premature | Closing the source field now would outrun the research and create avoidable authority risks. |

## Major interpretations

- **Inspired bounded canon:** A divinely intended set of books eventually recognized by the faithful community.
- **Community-reception model:** Texts become normative through long use, perceived apostolic/prophetic connection, coherence, liturgy, and communal reception.
- **Scripture-with-living-interpretation:** Textual authority functions inseparably from interpretive community/tradition.
- **Open library / graded authority:** Sources can have different research, formative, liturgical, and doctrinal weights without a single binary canon line.

## Current Way posture

- **Established:** The Assembly will maintain a Library of Witnesses rather than declare an early canon.
- **Established:** Library inclusion means relevance for study, not inspiration or doctrinal endorsement.
- **Provisional:** Sources may receive separate research, formation, liturgical, and doctrinal statuses.
- **Reserved:** Any future canon requires a separate entrenched adoption process and should not occur merely for branding or imitation.

## What would materially change confidence?

- Long-term evidence that a stable bounded corpus is necessary for faithful transmission and can be adopted without suppressing relevant counter-witnesses.
- A mature theology of revelation and inspiration capable of explaining why specific texts have a qualitatively different status.
- Multigenerational reception showing durable communal discernment rather than founder-era preference.
