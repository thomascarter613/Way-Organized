# TQ-003 - Jesus / Christology

**Research question:** What can responsibly be said about Jesus historically, and which claims about his identity remain theological?

Jesus of Nazareth is among the best-attested founders or central figures of antiquity in the sense that multiple early Christian sources preserve traditions about him and external ancient sources attest the movement and his execution context. Historical method can establish a first-century Jewish teacher, a movement, and crucifixion under Pontius Pilate with high confidence. It cannot, by itself, prove incarnation, divine sonship in an ontological sense, miracle causation, or resurrection as a metaphysical event.

## Witness map

| Witness | Source / corpus | Research use |
|---|---|---|
| Pauline letters | Earliest extant Christian writings | Show an already existing movement centered on the crucified and risen Jesus; not biographies. |
| Synoptic Gospels | Mark, Matthew, Luke | Narrative/theological sources preserving earlier traditions; written decades after Jesus and requiring source criticism. |
| Gospel of John | Later canonical Gospel | Distinctive theological portrait; historically valuable but not interchangeable with Synoptic chronology or discourse. |
| Josephus / Tacitus and Roman context | External ancient witnesses | Support the existence of Jesus/Christians and Roman execution context; details and textual issues vary by source. |
| Qur'anic Jesus | Qur'an 4:171; 5:72-75 and related passages | Affirms Jesus as Messiah/messenger while rejecting identification with God and later Islamic interpretation disputes crucifixion. |

## Findings

| Claim | Confidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Jesus was a first-century Jewish figure associated with Galilee/Judaea who attracted followers. | Established | Mainstream historical conclusion. |
| Jesus was crucified under Pontius Pilate. | Established | Oxford scholarship describes this as nearly universally accepted / among the strongest historical points. |
| The Gospels are literary and theological texts as well as historical sources and must be read accordingly. | Established | Their theological aims neither erase nor automatically validate historical content. |
| Jesus preached about God/kingdom and ethical/religious transformation. | Well-Supported | Exact wording and scope of individual sayings vary in confidence. |
| Jesus explicitly claimed ontological identity with God in the later creedal sense. | Open Question - Historical/Theological | The sources and interpretation are contested; historical method cannot simply assume later creeds. |
| Followers very early proclaimed that Jesus had been raised and continued to encounter him as alive. | Established as claim-history | What caused those experiences and whether resurrection occurred metaphysically remain separate questions. |
| Jesus' resurrection as a divine transformation of the dead. | Open Question - Metaphysical/Theological | Historical evidence can establish belief/claims more readily than supernatural causation. |

## Major interpretations

- **Jewish prophetic / eschatological teacher:** Jesus as Jewish teacher announcing God's reign and calling for repentance and transformed life.
- **Messianic claimant or messianically interpreted figure:** Scholars debate how Jesus understood messiahship versus how followers came to interpret him.
- **Incarnate divine Son:** Classical Christian doctrine; a theological claim that goes beyond what historical method alone can establish.
- **Islamic Messiah and messenger:** Qur'anic and Islamic framework honors Jesus but rejects divine identity and commonly interprets crucifixion differently.
- **Nonreligious historical interpretations:** Charismatic teacher, apocalyptic prophet, reformer, wisdom teacher, or combinations.

## Current Way posture

- **Established:** Jesus is a central witness in the Library of Witnesses and indispensable to study of the Way.
- **Established:** His crucifixion under Pilate is taught as history.
- **Well-Supported:** His teachings concerning God, neighbor, mercy, repentance, service, and moral transformation are major formation sources, with source-critical confidence noted passage by passage.
- **Open Question:** Incarnation, Trinity, pre-existence, atonement mechanisms, miracle causation, empty-tomb details, and resurrection metaphysics are not yet Assembly doctrine.
- **Safeguard:** Questioning a Christological interpretation is not grounds for retaliation or exclusion from ordinary public gatherings.

## What would materially change confidence?

- Earlier independent sources materially changing the source map.
- New manuscript or archaeological evidence bearing directly on disputed sayings/events.
- Better historical models of post-crucifixion experiences that explain the rise of resurrection belief.
- Philosophical/theological arguments showing that one Christological model best integrates the historical and experiential evidence.
