# TQ-001 - God / Ultimate Reality

**Research question:** What, if anything, is ultimately real in the religious sense, and what may responsibly be said about God or the Divine?

The evidence supports neither a premature equation of all religious ultimates nor a premature dismissal of them. Jewish and Islamic witnesses emphatically affirm divine unity; Sikh tradition likewise centers one God; Upanishadic traditions develop models of Brahman and self; Buddhist traditions are not organized around a creator God and instead analyze suffering, impermanence, non-self, dependent arising, and liberation. Philosophy of religion itself now treats 'God and other ultimates' as a plural conceptual landscape rather than one uniform idea.

## Witness map

| Witness | Source / corpus | Research use |
|---|---|---|
| Jewish biblical witness | Deuteronomy 6:4-5; Exodus 3:14-15 | Unity/uniqueness of YHWH; covenantal love; divine naming remains interpretively rich. |
| Qur'anic witness | Qur'an 112; 4:171 | God is one, incomparable, not begotten or begetting; Jesus is not a second deity within Qur'anic theology. |
| Sikh witness | Mool Mantar and Sikh teaching tradition | One, creator, timeless, self-existent divine reality; ego is an obstacle to truthful living. |
| Upanishadic witness | Principal Upanishadic traditions | Multiple formulations of Brahman, Atman, nonduality, and ultimate reality; not reducible to one simple doctrine. |
| Buddhist witness | Early Buddhist discourses | Liberation is framed without dependence on a creator-God doctrine; later Buddhist schools develop additional concepts such as emptiness and Buddha-nature. |
| Philosophical witness | Contemporary philosophy of religion | Distinguishes personal-God models, ground-of-being models, nondual ultimates, and non-theistic ultimates; also evaluates naturalism and skepticism. |

## Findings

| Claim | Confidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Religious traditions make materially different ultimate-reality claims. | Established | This diversity must not be erased by saying all traditions teach the same thing. |
| Monotheistic unity is central to Jewish, Islamic, and Sikh witnesses, though their further descriptions differ. | Established | Descriptive claim about source traditions, not an Assembly metaphysical verdict. |
| Religious experience and testimony deserve study but do not uniquely determine one metaphysical interpretation. | Well-Supported | The same kind of human experience can be interpreted differently across traditions. |
| Whether ultimate reality is personal, transpersonal, nondual, plural, or adequately described by naturalism remains unsettled for the Way. | Open Question | No evidentiary shortcut is justified. |
| The problem of suffering and evil is a major test for any model of an all-powerful, all-knowing, perfectly good personal God. | Established | The force of the problem varies with the model of God being evaluated. |

## Major interpretations

- **Classical monotheism:** One personal creator God, maximally powerful/knowing/good; Jewish, Christian, Islamic and Sikh formulations vary.
- **Ground-of-being / panentheistic models:** God as being itself, the ground within which creation exists, or reality transcending ordinary person-language.
- **Nondual models:** Ultimate reality and deepest self/reality understood through forms of nonduality; important in several Hindu philosophical traditions.
- **Non-theistic liberation models:** Religious ultimacy need not be a creator person; many Buddhist systems exemplify this.
- **Naturalism / agnosticism:** No transcendent personal or metaphysical ultimate beyond nature, or insufficient evidence to decide.

## Current Way posture

- **Established:** The Way must distinguish different concepts of ultimacy rather than collapsing them into one.
- **Provisional:** Devotional use of "God" is permitted as ordinary prayer language while metaphysical definition remains open.
- **Open Question:** The Assembly does not yet adopt a binding doctrine of divine personality, Trinity, strict unitarianism, nonduality, panentheism, or naturalism.
- **Institutional safeguard:** No claimant may convert a private certainty about God into unchecked authority over another person.

## What would materially change confidence?

- Compelling publicly assessable evidence of divine action with reliable provenance and alternative explanations addressed.
- Philosophical arguments that substantially outperform rivals on coherence, explanatory power, and moral implications.
- Cross-tradition experiential evidence that discriminates among competing models rather than merely showing that religious experience occurs.
- New evidence bearing on consciousness, cosmology, mind, or religious experience relevant to the metaphysical models.
