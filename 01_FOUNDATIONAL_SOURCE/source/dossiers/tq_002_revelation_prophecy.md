# TQ-002 - Revelation / Prophecy

**Research question:** What counts as revelation, how can revelatory claims be evaluated, and what authority may such claims have?

Religions use 'revelation' for different things: alleged divine speech, divine acts in history, divine self-disclosure, inspired text, prophetic proclamation, awakened insight, or deepened perception of reality. A responsible system therefore evaluates revelation claims in layers rather than asking only whether a claimant is sincere.

## Witness map

| Witness | Source / corpus | Research use |
|---|---|---|
| Hebrew prophetic traditions | Torah and Prophets | Divine speech, covenant, vision, prophetic call, moral indictment, and historical interpretation. |
| Christian traditions | Jesus, apostolic witness, scripture, Spirit | Revelation may be centered in a person/event as well as words; later traditions disagree over continuing revelation. |
| Islamic tradition | Qur'an and prophetic mission | Qur'an is presented as revealed speech delivered through Muhammad; later hadith/sira are distinct source classes. |
| Buddhist traditions | Awakening / insight | The Buddha's authority is typically framed as awakening and insight rather than a creator deity dictating revelation. |
| Sikh tradition | Gurbani / Guru | Revelatory and devotional authority is mediated through the Gurus and Guru Granth Sahib in Sikh self-understanding. |
| Philosophy | Models of divine revelation | Revelation can mean alleged divine speaking, special acts, or broader divine self-disclosure; justification remains a separate question. |

## Findings

| Claim | Confidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sincerity and intensity do not establish divine origin. | Established | They may establish that the person sincerely reports an experience. |
| A report of revelation and the interpretation of that report are distinct evidentiary objects. | Established | This is foundational to the Way's epistemic grammar. |
| Private revelation can rationally matter to the experiencer more than to outsiders. | Well-Supported | Public institutional claims require additional reasons accessible to those affected. |
| History can study revelation claims and their effects but cannot by historical method alone prove a transcendent source. | Established | Metaphysical/theological inference requires additional argument. |
| No contemporary revelation claim should automatically amend doctrine or governance. | Established - Institutional | Rights and lawful amendment processes remain controlling. |

## Major interpretations

- **Propositional revelation:** God communicates truths in words or word-like content.
- **Historical-event revelation:** Divine self-disclosure occurs through events interpreted within a community.
- **Personal revelation:** A person is understood as the decisive self-disclosure of God, as in major Christian Christologies.
- **Illuminative / contemplative revelation:** Insight arises through disciplined perception, contemplation, or awakening.
- **Natural revelation:** Nature, reason, conscience, or the cosmos discloses divine reality apart from special prophecy.

## Current Way posture

- **Established:** Revelation claims are classified as claims before they are classified as authority.
- **Provisional:** The Assembly may receive a personal testimony as spiritually meaningful without adopting its claimed divine interpretation.
- **Open Question:** Whether any historical revelation is genuinely divine, and if so by what mechanism.
- **Institutional safeguard:** No revelation can authorize sexual access, self-compensation, evidence destruction, coercion, rights suspension, or exemption from safeguarding.

## What would materially change confidence?

- Reliable independent corroboration of otherwise inaccessible information under controlled conditions.
- Predictive or explanatory success that survives pre-registration, ambiguity controls, and alternative explanations where such testing is appropriate.
- Historical evidence materially improving the provenance of ancient revelation reports.
- A coherent theology explaining both successful and failed revelation claims without ad hoc immunization.
