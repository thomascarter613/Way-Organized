# Pastoral and Spiritual-Care Ethics Code

- Respect conscience and informed consent.
- State qualifications and scope honestly.
- Do not diagnose or treat medical/mental-health conditions unless licensed and acting within scope.
- Refer to qualified professionals when needs exceed competence.
- Do not discourage lawful medical, psychiatric, psychological, legal, or financial advice.
- Maintain confidentiality subject to safeguarding, mandatory reporting, threats of serious harm, lawful process, and other stated exceptions.
- Never use confession, trauma history, sexuality, finances, family secrets, or spiritual fears as leverage.
- No sexual/romantic relationship during an active pastoral/counseling relationship or other substantial spiritual power imbalance.
- Gifts of material value from counselees require disclosure and, above policy thresholds, supervisory approval or refusal.
- Do not cultivate exclusivity: participants may consult other clergy, traditions, clinicians, advisers, and critics.
- Do not present personal impressions, dreams, visions, or prophetic claims as binding commands over another adult's life decisions.
- Document boundary concerns and seek supervision early.
