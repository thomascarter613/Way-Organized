## Article 6. Members

The Corporation **will not have statutory members** within the meaning of the Tennessee Nonprofit Corporation Act. The bylaws may recognize congregants, participants, affiliates, ministers, volunteers, donors, or covenant participants, but those statuses do not constitute statutory corporate membership unless the charter is lawfully amended.

## Article 7. Purposes

The Corporation is organized and shall be operated exclusively for religious, charitable, and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, including worship, religious formation, scholarship, historical and phenomenological inquiry, charitable service, community support, and activities reasonably related to those purposes.

No substantial part of the activities of the Corporation shall consist of carrying on propaganda or otherwise attempting to influence legislation except to the extent permitted for an organization exempt under Section 501(c)(3). The Corporation shall not participate or intervene in any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public office to the extent prohibited by Section 501(c)(3).

## Article 8. Private Inurement and Private Benefit

No part of the net earnings of the Corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, any director, officer, founder, minister, employee, member, participant, or other private person, except that the Corporation may pay reasonable compensation for services actually rendered, reimburse properly documented expenses, and make payments in furtherance of its exempt purposes.

## Article 9. Foundational Rights and Anti-Coercion Limits

The following principles bind the Corporation and every office created under it:

1. No human being, office, council, claimed revelation, tradition, or institutional act may compel another person's conscience through coercion, threat, manipulation, abuse of spiritual authority, or engineered material dependency.
2. No living founder, leader, teacher, prophet, elder, director, officer, clergy member, council, or successor is infallible, immune from criticism, or beyond investigation, suspension, correction, or removal.
3. Good-faith disagreement, criticism, complaint, reporting of misconduct, refusal of a religious practice, or departure shall not be grounds for retaliation.
4. Participation is voluntary. A person may leave without requiring spiritual release, payment, transfer of property, nondisparagement, confession, or permission.
5. The Corporation shall not require social shunning of a person merely because the person resigns, changes belief, criticizes leadership, or affiliates elsewhere.
6. Internal religious processes shall not be used to obstruct mandatory reporting, criminal investigation, tax obligations, court orders, child-protection duties, or other applicable civil requirements.
7. Institutional reputation, continuity, theology, finances, or loyalty to leadership shall never justify concealment of abuse, destruction of evidence, retaliation, or deception.

These provisions are intended as enforceable governance limitations and not merely aspirational statements.

## Article 10. Board and Independence

Except during the formation transition stated in the bylaws, the board shall consist of not fewer than seven and not more than eleven voting directors. At least two-thirds of the voting directors shall be independent under standards stated in the bylaws. No founder or chief executive may possess unilateral authority to appoint or remove a board majority.

A formation board of five directors may serve for no more than 180 days after incorporation if necessary to organize the Corporation, provided a majority of that board is independent and the board adopts a written plan to reach seven directors before expiration of the transition.

## Article 11. Founder Status

"Founder" is an historical designation only. It creates no ownership interest, veto, life tenure, power of succession, right to compensation, right to appoint a board majority, immunity from investigation, or superior claim to corporate assets. Any founder serving in an office is subject to the same conflicts, safeguarding, discipline, suspension, and removal rules applicable to others, plus any additional recusal rules in the bylaws.

## Article 12. Reserved Integrity Powers

The bylaws shall reserve to the independent board or committees composed solely of disinterested directors final authority over executive appointment/removal, executive compensation, related-party transactions, allegations against senior leadership, safeguarding oversight, independent investigations, external financial review, and amendments that would materially diminish participant rights or board independence.

## Article 13. Entrenched Provisions

Articles 8 through 13 and the dissolution restriction in Article 14 are "Entrenched Provisions." No Entrenched Provision may be suspended by emergency declaration, executive action, spiritual claim, doctrinal decree, or ordinary policy amendment.

An amendment that would repeal, narrow, suspend, or materially weaken an Entrenched Provision requires: (a) written notice describing the rights impact; (b) independent legal review; (c) consideration at two board meetings separated by at least thirty days; (d) approval by at least eighty percent of all voting directors then in office, including at least eighty percent of all independent directors then in office; and a written opinion from an independent external integrity reviewer or qualified nonprofit counsel that the amendment does not materially reduce participant protection without a compelling lawful necessity, with the opinion included in the permanent corporate record. A director with a personal financial or disciplinary interest in the amendment shall not vote.

## Article 14. Dissolution

Upon dissolution, after payment or provision for lawful liabilities, the Corporation's assets shall be distributed solely to one or more organizations then recognized as exempt under Section 501(c)(3) and organized for religious, charitable, or educational purposes, or to a federal, state, or local government for a public purpose. No founder, director, officer, member, participant, employee, or private individual shall receive a distributive ownership share.

## Article 15. Construction

The charter and bylaws should be construed, to the maximum extent lawful, to preserve freedom of conscience, board independence, safe reporting, safe exit, due process, safeguarding, and protection of charitable assets. If a lower-level policy conflicts with these principles, the higher-level protection controls.
