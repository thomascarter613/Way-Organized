# Schedule M - Statutory Voting-Member Model

Use only if the charter states that the corporation will have members.

## 1. Voting membership

Voting membership should be deliberately narrower than ordinary attendance or participation. A voting member must be an adult who voluntarily applies, receives the governing documents and Participant Bill of Rights, completes a reasonable orientation, affirms the mission (not personal loyalty to a leader), and is admitted under a nondiscriminatory process consistent with the organization's lawful religious criteria.

## 2. Rights

Voting members may: elect at least a majority of voting directors from a slate produced by the independent Governance and Nominating Committee; vote on charter amendments and entrenched-rights reductions; vote on dissolution or merger where law or charter requires; inspect records to the extent provided by law; and petition the board under procedures in these bylaws.

## 3. No ownership

Membership creates no property, equity, refund, distributive, or beneficial ownership interest in corporate assets.

## 4. Resignation

A member may resign at any time by any clear communication. Resignation is effective on receipt and requires no interview, explanation, release, payment, or leader approval.

## 5. Discipline / termination

Membership discipline must use written grounds, notice, opportunity to respond, impartial decision maker, proportional action, and appeal. Good-faith disagreement, criticism, external reporting, refusal to donate, refusal of optional religious practice, or resignation from an office are not disciplinary grounds.

## 6. Member meetings

Hold at least one annual member meeting. Provide at least the notice required by Tennessee law and additional notice required for director removal, amendments, merger, dissolution, or entrenched-rights matters. Remote participation should be allowed where lawful and identity/voting integrity can be reasonably protected.

## 7. Quorum and voting

Recommended default: quorum is 20% of eligible voting members or 25 members, whichever is smaller, but never fewer than 10 when the corporation has at least 10 voting members. Ordinary matters are decided by a majority of votes cast unless law, charter, or bylaws require more. Entrenched-rights changes require the charter threshold.

## 8. Member nominations

Members may propose qualified director candidates by petition, but all candidates undergo the same independence/conflict screen. The process shall not permit a founder, family, staff bloc, or donor bloc to evade independence requirements.
