# Founding Board Recruitment Strategy

## Immediate legal minimum and governance target

Tennessee nonprofit law requires at least **three natural-person directors**. The project currently has one identified director: the Founder. Therefore the absolute legal gap is **two additional directors**.

The governance target is stronger: recruit **four additional formation directors**, producing a five-person founding board, then expand to **seven directors within approximately 180 days** of organization if qualified people are available.

## Five-seat formation board

| Seat | Primary function | Independence target | Initial priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Founder / President-Executive Director | Vision, execution, research/religious formation | Not independent | Identified |
| 2. Independent Chair | Board leadership, founder accountability, governance | Independent | **Highest** |
| 3. Secretary / Governance Director | Corporate records, minutes, compliance, document hierarchy | Independent preferred | **Highest** |
| 4. Treasurer / Finance Director | Budget, internal controls, compensation/related-party oversight | Independent | High |
| 5. Safeguarding & Institutional Integrity Director | complaint independence, safeguarding, ethics, anti-retaliation | Independent | High |

## Seven-seat mature-board additions

6. **Community / charitable-program director** - practical service, community needs, nonprofit operations.  
7. **Religious studies / formation / education director** - intellectually serious religious formation without being dependent on the Founder for livelihood or status.

The Board may later add other competencies: law, property/development, HR, cybersecurity/privacy, fundraising, clinical/safeguarding expertise, education, housing, or community development.

## Candidate characteristics

Seek people who can:

- support the organization's charitable/religious purpose without treating the Founder as infallible;
- distinguish loyalty to mission from loyalty to a person;
- read financial statements or learn to do so;
- protect a complainant even when an allegation is embarrassing;
- say "no" to the Founder/President when fiduciary duty requires it;
- tolerate theological uncertainty and good-faith disagreement;
- understand that board service creates legal/fiduciary responsibilities;
- maintain confidentiality without confusing confidentiality with concealment;
- commit enough time to meetings, preparation, training, and committee work.

## Independence red flags

A person is generally a poor choice for an "independent" seat if the person is:

- the Founder's spouse, parent, child, sibling, or other close family member;
- financially dependent on the Founder;
- an employee or direct subordinate of the Founder/President;
- a material vendor, landlord, lender, borrower, business partner, or joint investor;
- dependent on organization-provided housing or substantial support;
- in a pastoral/counseling relationship with the Founder that would inhibit oversight;
- unwilling to contemplate investigating, suspending, or removing the Founder if facts warranted it.

A conflict does not always disqualify a person from all service, but it changes whether the person can count as independent and may require recusals.

## Recruitment order

1. Independent Chair.
2. Separate Secretary who is comfortable with formal records and compliance.
3. Treasurer/finance director.
4. Safeguarding/integrity director.
5. Additional mission/community expertise.

Do **not** recruit merely to satisfy a number. A board filled with people who will automatically agree with the Founder is structurally weaker than a temporarily smaller board that is actively recruiting competent independent directors, subject to Tennessee's legal minimum.
