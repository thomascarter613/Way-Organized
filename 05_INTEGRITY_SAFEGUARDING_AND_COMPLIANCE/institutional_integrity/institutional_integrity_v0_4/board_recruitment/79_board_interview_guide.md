# Board Candidate Interview Guide

## Part A - Explain before evaluating

Give the candidate a short factual briefing:

- the corporation has not yet filed because the final name and founding team are still being assembled;
- Tennessee requires at least three directors;
- the organization is currently designed as a nonmember public-benefit religious corporation;
- the Founder is expected to become President/Executive Director but not Board Chair;
- the Founder has no constitutional veto, life tenure, self-compensation, or immunity from investigation/removal;
- the Board will oversee compensation, money, safeguarding, complaints, investigations, property, and high-dependency programs;
- the organization intends to pursue full 501(c)(3) recognition.

## Part B - Explore judgment

Ask scenario questions:

1. A large donor says funding will stop unless the Board suppresses a substantiated report about a senior leader. What should the Board do?
2. The Founder says a spiritual experience demonstrates that a Board safeguard should be suspended. What should happen?
3. A complaint against the Founder arrives through the independent channel. Who controls intake and investigator selection?
4. The organization wants to hire a Founder's relative. What process is necessary?
5. A member leaves publicly and criticizes the religion. What may the organization do, and what must it not do?
6. The Founder needs a salary to work full time. How should the Board approach the question?
7. Housing becomes available to staff/participants. What new dependency risks arise?

## Part C - Candidate due diligence

- Verify identity/contact information.
- Obtain conflict/independence disclosure.
- Request relevant resume/background information.
- Conduct role-appropriate reference/background checks with consent.
- Discuss availability and term.
- Discuss D&O insurance status/plan.
- Provide governing documents before acceptance.

## Part D - Decision

The Governance/Nominating body should record why the person is qualified, whether they are independent, disclosed conflicts, expected committee assignment, and any onboarding requirements.
