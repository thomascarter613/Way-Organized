# Donation Receipt & Restricted-Gift Standard

## Receipt content
For gifts for which a donor may need a contemporaneous written acknowledgment, the Corporation's receipt process should capture:
- legal name of the Corporation;
- date and amount of cash contribution;
- description, but not donor tax value, of noncash property;
- whether goods/services were provided in exchange;
- if provided, description and good-faith estimate of value, unless an applicable exception is documented;
- if applicable, statement that goods/services consisted entirely of intangible religious benefits.

## Threshold controls
Current IRS guidance states that a donor generally needs a contemporaneous written acknowledgment for a single contribution of $250 or more, and that a charity must provide a disclosure statement for a quid-pro-quo contribution over $75. Configure receipt systems accordingly, while allowing receipts below thresholds as a stewardship practice.

## Restricted gifts
1. No staff member may promise a restriction the Corporation cannot honor.
2. Material restrictions must be recorded in the accounting system.
3. A gift earmarked for an individual is not automatically a charitable restricted fund; tax/legal review may be needed.
4. Donor influence does not purchase governance, doctrine, investigation outcomes, access to minors, spiritual status, or leadership position.
5. Refund/variance requests are documented and handled under law, gift terms, and Board policy.
