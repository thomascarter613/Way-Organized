# Board Reserved Powers Schedule

The board may delegate preparation and administration but retains final authority for these matters:

1. Appointing, evaluating, compensating, suspending, and removing the chief executive.
2. Appointing/removing principal corporate officers.
3. Approving the annual budget and material unbudgeted commitments.
4. Opening/closing bank or investment accounts and establishing signing/payment authority.
5. Borrowing, liens, guarantees, major leases, real-estate transactions, and disposition of substantially all assets.
6. Executive compensation and any material benefit to a disqualified or related person.
7. Related-party transactions and insider loans.
8. Selection of external accountants/auditors and receipt of their reports.
9. Approval of safeguarding policy and appointment/removal of the safeguarding lead.
10. Complaints and investigations involving directors, founder, chief executive, lead clergy executive, safeguarding lead, finance lead, or any person able to compromise the ordinary complaint process.
11. Retention of independent investigators for senior-leadership matters.
12. Final discipline/removal of a director or chief executive.
13. Approval of settlements involving allegations of abuse, retaliation, fraud, or senior-leadership misconduct.
14. Adoption/amendment of the charter, bylaws, Participant Bill of Rights, Founder Covenant, conflict policy, safeguarding policy, complaint policy, investigation protocol, and external-review mandate.
15. Creation/dissolution of board committees and designation of committee authority.
16. Merger, conversion, dissolution, affiliation that transfers control, or change in denominational/canonical control.
17. Approval of an arrangement that materially combines religious authority with participant housing, employment, education, healthcare support, or control of personal assets.
18. Response to an external institutional integrity review and closure of high-risk remediation items.

No person who is the subject or direct beneficiary of a reserved-power decision may control the decision process.
