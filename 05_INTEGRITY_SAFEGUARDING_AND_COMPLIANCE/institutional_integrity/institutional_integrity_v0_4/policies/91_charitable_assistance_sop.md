# Charitable Assistance and Benevolence SOP

## Purpose

Provide food, emergency necessities, limited financial assistance, referrals, and other relief in a manner that serves a genuine charitable class, responds to need/distress, preserves dignity, and prevents donor, founder, clergy, or insider favoritism.

## Governing principles

1. **Charitable purpose.** Assistance exists to relieve poverty, distress, emergency hardship, or comparable charitable need; it is not a benefit of loyalty, membership, donation history, doctrinal agreement, volunteering, or closeness to leadership.
2. **Open-ended charitable class.** Eligibility should describe a sufficiently broad or indefinite class rather than preselecting named beneficiaries.
3. **Needs-based decisions.** Except for immediate emergency necessities where a detailed assessment would be unreasonable, continuing/material aid is based on an objective evaluation of current need and available resources.
4. **No spiritual conditions.** A recipient need not attend worship, convert, confess, accept pastoral counseling, volunteer, donate, sign a loyalty statement, remain in the community, or refrain from criticism.
5. **No earmarked pass-through.** The organization retains control and discretion over donated funds. A donor may support an approved charitable program but may not compel the organization to transfer a deductible charitable gift to a preselected individual.
6. **Insider safeguards.** Assistance involving a director, officer, founder, senior spiritual leader, substantial contributor, family member, or person whose selection could benefit an insider is routed to disinterested reviewers and counsel when material. Ordinary compensation/reimbursement is handled under separate policies, not benevolence.
7. **Privacy with accountability.** Collect the minimum sensitive information needed for decision and records; restrict access; never use recipients' hardship stories for publicity without separate informed consent.

## Eligible forms of assistance

Subject to program design and resources, assistance may include food, clothing, shelter/housing assistance, utilities, transportation, medical or counseling costs, emergency cash or vouchers, temporary lodging, essential household goods, referrals, and other aid reasonably related to the documented need.

## Intake

Record at minimum:

- application/referral date and applicant identifier;
- nature and urgency of need;
- household/context information reasonably necessary to assess need;
- other resources reasonably available, when relevant to non-emergency aid;
- amount/type requested;
- conflicts/insider relationships;
- decision, amount/type approved, approver(s), and payment method;
- follow-up or re-evaluation date if continuing assistance.

Do not require irrelevant religious, medical, immigration, political, or family information merely because the organization has access to it.

## Decision authority

The Board sets annual program budget and delegated approval thresholds. Staff/volunteers may authorize only within written limits. Higher-dollar, unusual, recurring, or conflict-affected requests escalate to the designated disinterested committee/directors.

No single Founder/clergy executive may privately direct a payment outside the approved process.

## Emergency pathway

For immediate basic necessities or urgent safety, a designated responder may provide reasonable emergency aid before a full financial assessment when delay would defeat the charitable purpose. Record what was provided, why immediate action was reasonable, and any later assessment required for continuing aid.

## Prohibited criteria and practices

Do not favor or disfavor applicants based on:

- agreement with a leader's theology or claimed revelation;
- donations to the organization;
- willingness to work/volunteer without ordinary safeguards;
- willingness to keep misconduct confidential;
- withdrawal of a complaint or criticism;
- willingness to join, remain, or return to the religious community.

Assistance must never be threatened or withdrawn as retaliation for protected reporting, departure, or dissent.

## Payments and documentation

When practical, pay a vendor/provider directly or use a traceable payment method. Cash may be used when reasonably necessary but requires a receipt/acknowledgment or contemporaneous record. Records should show amount, purpose, need basis, decision authority, and payment evidence while minimizing unnecessary sensitive data.

## Donor restrictions

Fundraising language describes the charitable class/program, not a promise to transmit tax-deductible funds to a named person. If the organization conducts a separate non-deductible crowdfunding/pass-through activity, it must be clearly separated and reviewed before launch.

## Review

The Board or delegated committee reviews aggregate assistance activity at least quarterly: number served, total aid, exception/conflict cases, repeat/continuing aid, complaints, and whether criteria are producing unfair or coercive outcomes. Individual recipient identities should be omitted from ordinary board reporting unless needed for oversight.

## Source basis

IRS guidance for charities providing emergency/hardship relief emphasizes a charitable class, needs-based selection, and appropriate documentation. IRS private-benefit rules prohibit operating a 501(c)(3) for the creator, family, designated individuals, or other private interests. This policy applies those principles beyond disasters as a conservative internal control; counsel/CPA should conform it to the actual benevolence program before launch.
