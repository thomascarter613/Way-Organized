# Identity & Naming Architecture

## Objective

Choose a name that can serve a serious religious community, research/publication institution, charitable organization, clergy/formation program, and eventually a physical community without sounding denominational, grandiose, authoritarian, or artificially ancient.

## Recommended naming architecture

Use a **same-root two-level architecture** unless a later reason emerges to create separate legal entities:

- **Legal corporation:** `[[PUBLIC_NAME]], Inc.` or another counsel-approved legal form using the same distinctive root.
- **Public/community name:** `[[PUBLIC_NAME]]`.
- **Descriptive line:** a non-legal descriptor such as *A community of worship, inquiry, service, and formation.*
- **Programs:** research institute, publications, benevolence program, retreat program, clergy formation, and eventual residential/community programs can carry subordinate program names.

This is simpler and more transparent than creating a neutral corporation whose relationship to a differently named religious community is hard for donors or participants to understand.

## Name tests

A serious candidate should pass all of the following:

1. **Tradition-transcending:** does not imply that the organization is merely a church, synagogue, mosque, temple, monastery, or a branch of an existing denomination.
2. **Religiously meaningful:** does not sound like a consulting firm, software company, political organization, or academic center only.
3. **Inquiry-compatible:** permits genuine research, historical criticism, doubt, and revision rather than implying possession of final truth.
4. **Community-compatible:** can name people who gather, worship, learn, serve, and care for one another - not merely a website or journal.
5. **Non-authoritarian signal:** avoids language of chosen elites, hidden grades, masters, unquestionable revelation, absolute truth, remnant identity, or founder possession.
6. **Plain pronunciation:** understandable when heard once and easy to spell/search.
7. **Expandable:** still works if the organization later has local assemblies, clergy, publications, schools/programs, retreats, property, and charitable work.
8. **Legally distinct:** Tennessee requires the corporate name to be distinguishable on the Secretary of State's records and prohibits misleading claims of affiliation/sponsorship.
9. **Digitally distinct:** reasonable domain/search availability after final candidate screening.
10. **Trademark-aware:** final selection receives a trademark/conflict check beyond a general web search.

## Terms to use carefully

Terms such as *church*, *synagogue*, *mosque*, *temple*, *ecclesia/ekklesia*, *masjid*, and *sangha* carry valuable histories but also signal a specific tradition or institutional form. They can appear in comparative study or local program language without becoming the umbrella identity.

Words such as *order*, *elect*, *remnant*, *master*, *final revelation*, *one true*, *initiates*, and similar exclusivist/graded-authority terms should not be adopted merely to make a young religion sound ancient or authoritative.

## Naming workflow

1. Select 3-5 finalists from the candidate matrix.
2. Test each aloud in sentences: "I attend ___," "___ published this report," "___ provides pastoral care," "___ is raising funds for land," and "___ removed its founder from office under its bylaws."
3. Run Tennessee entity-name search and reserve only after a finalist is chosen.
4. Run federal/state trademark screening and common-law web search.
5. Check domain/social availability.
6. Conduct a small outside perception test with people who know nothing about the project.
7. Choose legal/public spelling and create a naming-resolution record.

## Tennessee facts relevant to naming

Current Tennessee law requires a nonprofit corporate name to be distinguishable on the Secretary of State's records and restricts language that falsely implies affiliation or authorization. A name can currently be reserved for four months. The Tennessee Secretary of State currently lists a $20 name-reservation fee and a $100 domestic nonprofit charter filing fee. Re-check immediately before filing.
